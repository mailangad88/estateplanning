A living will is a written statement of the medical treatment you want, or do not want, if you are terminally ill or permanently unconscious. A healthcare power of attorney names a person, often called your agent or proxy, to make medical decisions when you cannot. One gives instructions and the other gives a person authority, so they work best together.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | Living will | Healthcare power of attorney |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | States your treatment wishes | Names someone to decide for you |
| Cost to set up | Low, often part of a plan package | Low, often part of a plan package |
| When it applies | Usually end-of-life or permanent unconsciousness | Any time you cannot make or share decisions |
| Who controls it | Your written words | Your chosen agent, guided by your wishes |
| Handles unexpected situations | No, only what it addresses | Yes, your agent can respond to new facts |
| Can you change it | Yes, any time you have capacity | Yes, any time you have capacity |
| Privacy | Shared with your doctors and family | Shared with your doctors and family |
| Goes through probate | No | No |
| Other names | Advance directive, directive to physicians | Healthcare proxy, medical power of attorney |

Names and forms vary by state. Some states combine both into one "advance healthcare directive." See [healthcare directives and living wills](/guides/healthcare-directives-and-living-wills).

## How each one works

A living will speaks for you in your own words. It might say you do not want a breathing machine if there is no reasonable hope of recovery. It might also cover feeding tubes, CPR and pain relief.

A healthcare power of attorney puts a person in the room. Your agent can talk with doctors, review records, weigh options and consent to or refuse treatment. They can handle situations no form could predict, like a surgery decision after a car accident.

## When people choose a living will

People often put special weight on a living will when:

- They have strong views on life support and want them in writing.
- They worry their family would struggle to make the call.
- They want to spare loved ones from guessing.
- They have no one they fully trust to serve as an agent.

Say your father always said he would never want to be kept alive on machines. A living will turns that into a clear instruction. It can take a painful decision off his children's shoulders.

## When people choose a healthcare power of attorney

People often see the healthcare power of attorney as the core document because:

- Most medical decisions are not end-of-life decisions.
- An agent can adapt to facts and treatment options as they change.
- Doctors often want one clear decision-maker to talk with.
- It avoids disputes among relatives with equal say.

Say you have three adult children who may not agree. Naming one as your agent, with a backup, gives the medical team one person to call. It also lowers the chance of family conflict at a hard time.

## Using both together

Most estate plans include both, along with a HIPAA release so your agent can see records. The living will guides your agent. The agent carries out your wishes and fills in the gaps.

These documents sit beside a financial power of attorney, which covers money rather than medicine. See [powers of attorney](/guides/powers-of-attorney). Together they help your family act without going to court if you are incapacitated, which is a core part of [what estate planning is](/learn/basics/what-is-estate-planning).

## Questions to ask an attorney

- Does my state use one combined form or separate documents?
- Who should I name as agent, and who as a backup?
- What witnesses or notary does my state require?
- Should I include a HIPAA authorization?
- How do I make sure my doctors and hospital have copies?
- Will my documents be honored if I am hurt in another state?
