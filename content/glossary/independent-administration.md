Article XXVIII of the Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/28-1 and following) lets an executor or administrator manage the estate with little court involvement. The representative can sell property, pay claims, and make distributions without a hearing for each decision, then closes the estate with a final account or receipts from the heirs. Independent administration is the usual track in Illinois unless the will forbids it or an interested person objects. Any interested person can ask the court to end it and move the case to supervised administration, for example if the representative is not acting properly.

## Example

Tamara is executor of her father's $320,000 estate. Because the case is independent, she lists the house with a broker, pays a $6,500 funeral bill, and sends out the first distribution after the claims period ends, without filing a motion for any of those steps.

## Why it matters

The track saves time and legal fees, but the representative still owes [Fiduciary duty](/glossary/fiduciary-duty) to the heirs. Less court oversight means more reliance on good records.
