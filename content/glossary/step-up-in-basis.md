Basis is generally what you paid for an asset, and gain is the difference between basis and the selling price. When someone dies, most assets they owned, such as stocks, real estate, and some other property, receive a new basis equal to fair market value at the date of death. If heirs sell soon after, little or no capital gain arises. Retirement accounts, like traditional IRAs, do not get a step-up and are taxed as income when withdrawn. Assets given during life do not get one either; the recipient takes the giver's old basis.

## Example

Dale bought stock for $20,000 that is worth $220,000 when he dies. His daughter inherits it with a new basis of $220,000 and sells it for $225,000, owing tax only on $5,000. Had Dale given it to her while alive, she would have owed tax on the $205,000 gain.

## Why it matters

Step-up is why advisors caution against adding children to a deed or giving appreciated property early, since the family may lose a valuable tax benefit. It also means a home held for decades may be sold with little tax. Special rules apply to jointly held and community property.
