Illinois statutes use the older words legacy for personal property and devise for real estate, and a legatee is anyone who takes one of those gifts under a will. A legatee is different from an [Heir](/glossary/heir), who inherits by law when there is no will. In an Illinois probate case the legatees are entitled to notice of the will's admission, may receive copies of inventories and accounts, and can object if the representative mishandles the estate (755 ILCS 5/6-10). A legatee who dies before the testator may leave a lapsed gift.

## Example

Fran's will gives $10,000 to her church, her piano to her niece Bea, and the rest to her son Carl. The church, Bea, and Carl are all legatees, and each receives notice when Fran's will is admitted.

## Why it matters

Knowing who counts as a legatee tells you who must receive notice and who can challenge the will or the executor's work. Legatees are paid after debts and awards.
