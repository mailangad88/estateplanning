A trustee has legal title to the trust assets but must use them for the [beneficiaries](/glossary/beneficiary), not personally. Duties include following the trust document, investing prudently, keeping trust money separate, treating beneficiaries fairly, keeping records, and providing information and accountings. A person can act as their own trustee of a revocable trust. After they are gone, a [Successor trustee](/glossary/successor-trustee) takes over. Choices include a family member, friend, bank trust department, or professional trustee; some trusts name co-trustees.

## Example

Gina's trust names her son Marco as trustee for her daughter's share, to be paid out at ages 25 and 30. Marco invests the money in a diversified account, keeps statements, and sends yearly reports to his sister, who is also a beneficiary.

## Why it matters

A trustee can be sued, surcharged for losses, or removed for breaching duties, so the job should not be given as a prize. Think about time, skill, conflicts of interest, and the strain on family relationships. A professional's fees can be worth the neutrality.
