Section 2-5 of the Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/2-5) says that a gift to an heir during the decedent's life counts against the heir's intestate share only if the decedent declared it an advancement in writing, or the heir acknowledged it in writing. The gift is valued as of the time the heir received it. The rule applies when there is no will, and a will's terms usually control otherwise. Without a writing, a gift is treated as a plain gift, and the heir keeps both the gift and a full share.

## Example

Joe gave his son Tim $30,000 for a down payment and wrote on a note, 'advance on inheritance.' Joe later died intestate with $270,000. Tim's share is computed as if the estate were $300,000, split equally among three children, so Tim receives $70,000 from the estate and each sibling receives $100,000.

## Why it matters

Parents who want gifts to count should say so in writing, and parents who do not want gifts to count should say that too. Siblings often fight over this.
