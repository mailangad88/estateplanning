Fiduciary duties come in several parts. Loyalty forbids self-dealing and conflicts of interest. Prudence or care requires careful, reasonable handling, including sensible investments. Impartiality requires fair treatment of all beneficiaries, even if the fiduciary is one of them. Duties of record keeping, accounting, and informing beneficiaries let others monitor the work. The standard is set by statute and by the governing document, and a [Fiduciary](/glossary/fiduciary) who breaches may be ordered to repay losses, give up profits, lose fees, or be removed.

## Example

Trustee Wes keeps $200,000 of trust cash in a non-interest checking account for two years and also lends $20,000 to his own company. The first is a prudence issue; the second is a loyalty breach. A beneficiary asks the court to surcharge him and replace him.

## Why it matters

These duties are what protect heirs from a careless or dishonest manager. They also warn people asked to serve: good intentions are not a defense. Keeping detailed records and getting advice is the best safeguard.
