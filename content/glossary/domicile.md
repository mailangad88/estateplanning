A person can have many homes but only one domicile. Courts look at where the person lives most of the year, votes, registers cars, holds a driver's license, files taxes, and says home is. Illinois probate is opened in the county of the decedent's domicile (755 ILCS 5/5-1), and an Illinois domicile at death means the Illinois estate tax applies to the estate, with real estate and tangible property located in other states generally excluded from the Illinois calculation. Real estate in another state is typically handled by [Ancillary probate](/glossary/ancillary-probate) there.

## Example

Pat spends winters in Florida and summers in Illinois, but keeps an Illinois driver's license, votes in Illinois, and files an Illinois return. At death, Illinois is likely the domicile and the main probate is in her home county, with a short ancillary case in Florida for her condo.

## Why it matters

Domicile questions arise when retirees move or split time, and they can affect taxes in two states. Document a change with a new license, voter registration, and updated estate documents.
