POLST stands for Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment. Some states call it MOLST, POST, or similar. It is meant for people with serious illness or frailty, not healthy adults. A doctor, and in some states a nurse practitioner or physician assistant, completes it after a conversation with you or your agent. The form is usually brightly colored and kept at home or in the care facility, so paramedics follow it. It covers CPR, level of intervention, and feeding. Unlike a [Living will](/glossary/living-will), it is an actionable medical order.

## Example

Estelle, 84, has advanced lung disease. After discussing her goals with her doctor, she signs a POLST requesting no CPR and no intubation but hospital transfer for comfort. When she collapses, paramedics read the form on her refrigerator and treat her at home.

## Why it matters

A living will may not be visible or specific enough in an emergency, while a POLST is an order that emergency crews are trained to follow. It does not replace a [Healthcare proxy](/glossary/healthcare-proxy), though, and can be changed any time. Review it after changes in health.
