The Illinois statutory short form for property allows the principal to name co-agents and to state whether they must act together or may act separately (755 ILCS 45/3-3). If the form says jointly, both must sign every transaction, which protects against abuse but can slow routine tasks. If it says separately, either can act alone, which is faster but increases risk. Co-agents who disagree can stall a bank account or a sale. Illinois law also holds co-agents responsible for knowing about and reporting misconduct by the other.

## Example

Gina names her two sons, Mark and Eli, as co-agents who must act jointly for any transaction over $5,000 and separately for smaller ones. A $2,000 roof repair is paid by Mark alone, while the sale of her $210,000 condo needs both.

## Why it matters

Co-agents can feel fair to children but may produce stalemates. Many families name one agent and a successor instead. Spell out the rule for disagreements.
