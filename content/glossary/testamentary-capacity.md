The legal standard is lower than for most contracts. In general, the [Testator](/glossary/testator) must understand that they are making a will, know the general nature and extent of their property, and recognize the people who would naturally be expected to receive it. Capacity is judged at the moment of signing, so a person with early dementia may have capacity on a good day. Old age, an eccentric choice, or a diagnosis alone does not defeat capacity, but evidence of confusion at signing can.

## Example

Rita has mild Alzheimer's. Her attorney meets her in the morning when she is sharpest, asks her to describe her property and family in her own words, and has a doctor write a note the same day. Years later, the note helps defeat a challenge by a disinherited nephew.

## Why it matters

Lack of capacity is a leading ground for contesting wills. If you or a parent has cognitive decline, acting sooner rather than later and documenting capacity makes the plan more secure. This is separate from [Incapacity](/glossary/incapacity) for finances, though the facts overlap.
