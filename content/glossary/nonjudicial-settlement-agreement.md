Section 111 of the Illinois Trust Code (760 ILCS 3/111) allows trustees, beneficiaries, and other interested persons to sign a binding agreement on matters such as approving a trustee's accounting, appointing or resigning a trustee, setting trustee pay, or interpreting a trust term. The agreement may not violate a material purpose of the trust or add terms a court could not approve. Any interested person can ask a court to approve the agreement, which can bind beneficiaries who did not sign, such as minors, when properly represented.

## Example

Three siblings are beneficiaries of a $600,000 trust. They agree in writing that their sister Ona may serve as successor trustee at $4,000 a year, and that the prior trustee's accounting is approved. A court filing is not needed.

## Why it matters

A settlement agreement can save thousands of dollars in court costs when everyone agrees. If one beneficiary refuses or cannot sign, the court route may be required. It does not replace the duty to inform beneficiaries, discussed in [Illinois trustee duty to inform](/learn/illinois/illinois-trustee-duty-to-inform-beneficiaries), and trustees should still keep records as a [Trustee](/glossary/trustee).
