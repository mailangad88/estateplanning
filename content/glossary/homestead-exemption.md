Under 735 ILCS 5/12-901, each Illinois homeowner may protect up to $15,000 of equity in a residence from judgment creditors, and a married couple who both own the home may each claim it. The exemption continues for a surviving spouse and minor children who live in the home (735 ILCS 5/12-902). It does not stop a mortgage, a tax lien, or claims for the cost of the home itself. Do not confuse it with the property tax homestead exemptions, such as the general homestead and senior exemptions, which are claimed through the county assessor.

## Example

Rita dies owning a $200,000 house with a $120,000 mortgage. Her widower Sam, who lives there, can claim up to $15,000 of the $80,000 equity against unsecured creditors, while the mortgage lender is paid in full.

## Why it matters

The amount is modest, so it protects less than many people think. Estate plans should not rely on it for large exposures. See [Illinois homestead exemption after a death](/learn/illinois/illinois-homestead-exemption-after-a-death) and how title changes can affect the property tax side.
