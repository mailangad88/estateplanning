The Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/24-1 and following) requires the representative to account for the estate. The account starts with the inventory value and lists receipts, such as interest and sale proceeds, then disbursements, such as funeral costs, taxes, claims, fees, and distributions, and ends with the balance remaining. Under independent administration, the representative may close by delivering the account to heirs and obtaining their written receipts and releases. Under supervised administration, the court reviews the account and issues an order approving it and discharging the representative.

## Example

Pilar closes her mother's estate. Receipts total $188,400 and disbursements total $41,900, including $24,000 in claims and funeral costs and $6,500 in fees. She distributes the remaining $146,500 equally to her three siblings and herself, each of whom signs a receipt.

## Why it matters

A clear account protects the executor from later complaints. Keep statements and receipts for every entry.
