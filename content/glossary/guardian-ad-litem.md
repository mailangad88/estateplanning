In an adult guardianship case the court may appoint a guardian ad litem to interview the person, review the petition and medical report, and tell the judge what is needed (755 ILCS 5/11a-10). The GAL is not the person's guardian and does not make decisions for them. In probate, courts also appoint a GAL for minors, unknown heirs, or persons under disability when their rights could be affected. The GAL files a report and may testify, and the court usually orders the fee paid from the estate or the ward's funds.

## Example

Hal's daughter petitions for guardianship over him. The court appoints a GAL, who visits Hal twice, talks with his doctor, and reports that Hal can manage daily tasks but needs help with money, supporting a limited order.

## Why it matters

The GAL is the court's eyes and a check against hasty orders. Families should speak openly with the GAL. See how the process works in [Illinois guardianship](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship) and the differences from a [Guardian](/glossary/guardian) who actually takes over decisions.
