The word has more than one meaning. In everyday use, your estate is all of your property and debts at death. In probate, the [Probate estate](/glossary/probate-estate) is the narrower set of assets that need a court-supervised process to transfer. For federal tax purposes, the gross estate is broader still: it includes life insurance you own, retirement accounts, and your share of jointly held property, even if none of it goes through probate. A person's estate also exists as a legal entity after death, with its own tax ID, that the executor manages.

## Example

Naomi dies owning a $500,000 house, a $200,000 IRA, a $50,000 car and savings, and a $100,000 mortgage. Her whole estate is about $650,000 net, but only the house and savings, which lack a beneficiary, are in her probate estate. The IRA passes by form to her daughter.

## Why it matters

Estate size affects which taxes may apply (the federal exemption is $15,000,000 per person in 2026, and some states tax estates at far lower levels) and which probate procedures are available. Making a list of assets, who gets each, and how each is titled is the first step in any plan.
