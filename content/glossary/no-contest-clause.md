The clause works as a deterrent: leave a child $10,000, but say the gift is forfeited if the child sues to invalidate the will. It has teeth only if the person has something to lose, so a token gift or no gift makes it meaningless. States differ widely. Some enforce the clause strictly. Others refuse to enforce it when the challenger had probable cause or good faith, and a few do not enforce them at all. Wording matters, because a clause may not reach every type of dispute, such as asking a court to interpret an unclear term.

## Example

Lena's will leaves her daughter Cora $50,000, with a clause forfeiting it if Cora contests. Cora believes her brother pressured Lena, but the evidence is thin. She weighs a $50,000 certain gift against a risky lawsuit and drops the challenge.

## Why it matters

A no-contest clause can discourage family fights where one heir is receiving less than another, but it will not stop a determined or well-founded challenge. Better protection comes from careful drafting, medical evidence of capacity, and clear explanations.
