The Illinois Power of Attorney Act (755 ILCS 45) prints one form for property (section 3-3) and one for health care (section 4-10). Each uses short headings, such as banking, real estate, and tax matters, that carry detailed powers defined by the statute. A property form must be signed by the principal, witnessed by one qualifying witness, and notarized. The form can be changed with added instructions and limits, such as gift authority. A bank that refuses a properly signed statutory form can face consequences under the Act.

## Example

Sonja signs the statutory form naming her daughter Beth as agent. She initials the powers she wants and strikes the line for gifts. A bank teller reads the plain title, recognizes it, and lets Beth manage her $52,000 account.

## Why it matters

The form's status speeds acceptance, but only a properly signed one counts. Free online forms may miss witness or notary details. Have an attorney review any form against your goals.
