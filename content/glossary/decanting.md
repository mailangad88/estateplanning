The name comes from pouring wine from one bottle to another. Illinois recognizes decanting under the Illinois Trust Code (760 ILCS 3), which lets a trustee with discretion to distribute principal move assets to a second trust for the same beneficiaries, subject to fiduciary limits. Article 12 of the Code (760 ILCS 3/1201 and following) sets the rules. Common reasons include fixing outdated terms, changing the trustee, adding special needs protections, or moving the trust's governing law. The trustee must act in good faith, consider the beneficiaries' interests, and give notice as the statute requires. Decanting cannot be used to add new beneficiaries.

## Example

Gloria's father left a trust that pays her nothing until age 40 but allows the trustee to distribute principal for her health. Gloria now needs a special needs trust for her son. The trustee decants $180,000 into a new trust with supplemental needs terms.

## Why it matters

Decanting gives an old irrevocable trust a path to change without a court case, but it carries tax and fiduciary risks.
