In Illinois, intestate succession runs through the spouse, descendants, parents, siblings, and more distant kin (755 ILCS 5/2-1). Only if no kindred can be found does the estate escheat, and the Probate Act sends it to the county. The path to escheat is rare, since the law reaches distant cousins and descendants of grandparents before it does. Money owed to the estate, or held by a bank, may instead turn up as unclaimed property that the state treasurer holds for the owner or heirs without limit of time.

## Example

Ned, a bachelor with no known relatives, dies with $64,000 in the bank. A genealogy search in probate finds a first cousin once removed living in Ohio, who inherits. Without that search, the money could have gone to the county.

## Why it matters

Naming a will beneficiary, even a friend or charity, prevents escheat.
