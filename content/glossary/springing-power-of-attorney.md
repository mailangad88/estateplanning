Instead of giving the [Agent (power of attorney)](/glossary/agent) authority from the moment of signing, a springing document says the power begins only when a named trigger occurs. The trigger is usually a written finding of [Incapacity](/glossary/incapacity) by one or two physicians. Some people like it because they feel safer handing over control only when needed. The downside is that proving the trigger can delay access, banks may demand extra proof, and disagreements about whether the trigger has occurred can lead to court. A springing power should also be made durable.

## Example

Nina signs a springing power of attorney that requires two doctors to certify incapacity. After a fall and a brain injury, her daughter collects both letters, but the bank takes two weeks to review them before honoring the document.

## Why it matters

Springing documents sound cautious, but the delay can arrive precisely when bills are due and care decisions are pending. Many attorneys prefer an immediate durable power with a trusted agent and safeguards. Compare them in [springing vs. immediate power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/springing-vs-immediate-power-of-attorney) and see [what to do when a bank refuses](/learn/power-of-attorney/bank-refuses-power-of-attorney).
