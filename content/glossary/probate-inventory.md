Under 755 ILCS 5/14-1, the representative must prepare an inventory of the real and personal estate within 60 days after letters are issued, listing each item and its value at the date of death. Under independent administration the inventory is generally not filed with the court, but it must be given to any interested person who asks. Under supervised administration it is filed. Heirs and legatees rely on it to see what the estate holds, and it becomes the starting point for the final account.

## Example

Ilse, executor, lists a house at $215,000, a car at $9,000, a checking account at $27,300, and furniture at $2,500, a total of $253,800. She gets the house value from an appraiser and dates each value to the date of death.

## Why it matters

A good inventory prevents later disputes and supports the tax basis of inherited property under [Step-up in basis](/glossary/step-up-in-basis). It lists only the [Probate estate](/glossary/probate-estate), not assets with named beneficiaries. See how to value assets in [probate inventory and estate accounting](/learn/probate/probate-inventory-and-estate-accounting).
