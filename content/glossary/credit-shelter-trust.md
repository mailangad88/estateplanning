At the first spouse's death, assets equal to an exemption amount go to the trust rather than outright to the survivor. The survivor can receive income and sometimes principal, but the trust is not part of the survivor's estate. The federal exemption is $15,000,000 per person in 2026, and federal [Portability](/glossary/portability) can carry unused federal exemption to the survivor. Illinois has no portability, and its exclusion is $4,000,000 per person, so a credit shelter trust is mainly used in Illinois to avoid state tax (see 35 ILCS 405).

## Example

Hank and Ivy own $7,000,000, mostly in joint names. At Hank's death, a $4,000,000 credit shelter trust is funded for Ivy and their children. When Ivy dies later, only about $3,000,000 plus growth counts toward her Illinois estate tax computation.

## Why it matters

A trust can save state tax, but it adds paperwork and can lose the step-up in basis at the second death. Many couples below the exemptions skip it.
