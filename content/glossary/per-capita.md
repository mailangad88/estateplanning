"Per capita" is Latin for "by heads." Under a simple per capita distribution, an estate is split equally among those individuals who qualify and are alive when the gift takes effect. A deceased relative's descendants receive nothing unless the document says otherwise. Variations exist, such as "per capita at each generation," which shares with next-generation descendants only when a whole level is gone. Compare [Per stirpes](/glossary/per-stirpes), which divides by family branch.

## Example

Walt's will leaves his estate "to my children, per capita." His daughter died before him, leaving two kids. Walt's two surviving sons split everything half and half, and the daughter's children get nothing, though a per stirpes clause would have given them her share.

## Why it matters

The label controls how fairly or unevenly an estate is divided when a family member dies early, so it should be chosen deliberately. Beneficiary forms and trusts often include a default; read it. A surprise here can cut a grandchild out. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will) for default intestacy shares and [trusts for children](/learn/trusts/trusts-for-children).
