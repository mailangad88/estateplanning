The date is stated in the published and mailed notice to creditors. It must be at least six months after the first publication, or at least three months after a known creditor receives notice, whichever is later (755 ILCS 5/18-3). Claims filed late are barred. Separately, Illinois bars most claims two years after death even if no notice was ever published (755 ILCS 5/18-12). A few claims, such as those secured by a lien on estate property, are not wiped out by the bar date.

## Example

Tony, the executor, publishes notice on March 4 and mails it to a hospital on March 10. He sets the bar date at September 15, six months after publication. A credit card company that files in October finds its claim barred.

## Why it matters

The bar date lets the executor distribute with less fear of surprise bills. It is no shield for an executor who skips notice to known creditors.
