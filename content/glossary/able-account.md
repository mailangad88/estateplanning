Created by federal law, ABLE accounts let eligible people save money that grows tax free when used for qualified disability expenses such as housing, education, transportation, health, and employment support. Eligibility generally requires that the disability began before a certain age, currently 26 and scheduled to rise to 46 in 2026. SSI usually ignores the first $100,000 in an account, and family and friends can contribute up to the annual limit, which matches the [Annual gift tax exclusion](/glossary/annual-exclusion). At death, a state may claim the balance for Medicaid costs.

## Example

Priscilla's parents open an ABLE account for her, deposit $5,000 a year, and she uses the debit card to pay for rent, transit passes, and a therapy app. Her SSI checks continue because the account is below the $100,000 threshold.

## Why it matters

An ABLE account gives the person day-to-day control that a trust usually does not, and is easier to open. It has limits on size and eligible age, so it often works best alongside a [Special needs trust](/glossary/special-needs-trust). See [ABLE accounts](/learn/special-needs/able-accounts) and [special needs trusts](/learn/special-needs/special-needs-trust).
