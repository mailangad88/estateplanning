The principal is the person whose affairs are being handled. In a [Power of attorney](/glossary/power-of-attorney), the principal decides what the [Agent (power of attorney)](/glossary/agent) may do, whether authority begins now or later, and when the document ends. A principal must have mental capacity at the time of signing, meaning enough understanding to know what the document does and who the agent is. A principal can generally revoke the document at any time while competent, by written notice to the agent and the institutions that hold copies. The principal's authority returns to the estate's [Executor](/glossary/executor) at death.

## Example

Frances, as principal, signs a financial power of attorney naming her daughter and listing the powers she approves, including paying bills and managing accounts but excluding gifts. Later she revokes it in writing after moving closer to another child who becomes her new agent.

## Why it matters

Because the principal remains the legal owner of the assets, an agent must follow the document's limits and the principal's known wishes. Some principals add safeguards, such as requiring annual accountings or a co-agent for large transactions.
