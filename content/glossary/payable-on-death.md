POD is the name given to the designation on bank accounts and certificates of deposit; investment accounts use [Transfer-on-death (TOD)](/glossary/transfer-on-death) for the same idea. You fill in a form at the bank naming one or more beneficiaries. While you are alive, the beneficiary has no rights to the money, and you can spend it or change the form freely. At death, the beneficiary shows the bank a death certificate and ID and receives the balance. Because the account passes by contract, the will has no say over it.

## Example

Gloria names her grandson Eli as POD beneficiary on her $30,000 savings account, and her will leaves everything to her daughter. After Gloria dies, Eli collects the savings by showing the bank a death certificate, and the daughter gets the rest of the estate.

## Why it matters

POD is a simple way to skip probate for small accounts, but it can undercut a will's equal shares if forgotten, and a minor beneficiary may need a guardian to receive funds. Name a backup, and review it as family changes.
