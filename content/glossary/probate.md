Probate takes place in the county court where the deceased lived, and sometimes in other states (see [Ancillary probate](/glossary/ancillary-probate)). Steps usually include filing the will and a petition, getting the executor appointed, giving notice to heirs and creditors, inventorying assets, paying valid claims and taxes, and distributing what is left. Only assets in the [Probate estate](/glossary/probate-estate) go through it. Timelines depend on the state and the estate, but simple cases often take around six to twelve months and contested ones much longer. Many states offer shortcuts for small estates.

## Example

After Edwin dies, his daughter files his will with the county court. A judge appoints her executor, creditors have a few months to file claims, and after the inventory and bills are done, the court approves a final distribution to Edwin's three children.

## Why it matters

Probate costs money and time, is generally public, and can delay inheritances. It also gives structure, protects against fraud, and cuts off creditor claims after a deadline. How much it matters depends on how you own your assets.
