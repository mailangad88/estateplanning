Section 15-1 of the Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/15-1) also protects children. If there is no surviving spouse, each minor or dependent child who lived with the decedent is entitled to a reasonable award for nine months of support, with a statutory floor of $10,000 per child. When there is a surviving spouse, the children's share is folded into the spouse's award instead. A court sets the amount if the parties disagree, and the award is paid to the child's guardian or other person responsible for the child's care.

## Example

Elena, a widow, dies with an estate of $90,000 and two minor children, ages 9 and 12. The court allows a child's award of $10,000 each, paid to their guardian before unsecured creditors and before any other beneficiary receives anything.

## Why it matters

A child's award can make a real difference when a small estate faces many debts. It is a statutory claim, so it appears in the claims order discussed in [how creditor claims work in an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/how-creditor-claims-work-in-an-illinois-estate). A parent with minor children should also name a [Guardian](/glossary/guardian) in a will.
