An ILIT is a trust that the insured cannot change or control. The trustee buys the policy or receives it as a gift, pays premiums with gifts from the insured, and distributes the proceeds under the trust terms after death. Because the insured holds no incidents of ownership, the death benefit is generally outside the estate for federal and Illinois estate tax. If the insured transfers an existing policy and dies within three years, the proceeds are pulled back into the estate (IRC 2035). Gifts to the trust can qualify for the annual exclusion if beneficiaries get withdrawal rights.

## Example

Vera, 64, has a $1,500,000 term-to-permanent policy and an Illinois estate near $4,500,000. She sets up an ILIT, and each year gifts $19,000 per child to the trust to pay premiums.

## Why it matters

The Illinois exclusion of $4,000,000 is far below the federal amount, so insurance can push an Illinois estate over the line even when federal tax is not an issue. The trust is permanent and needs careful administration. See [ILIT basics](/learn/estate-tax/irrevocable-life-insurance-trust) and the [Annual gift tax exclusion](/glossary/annual-exclusion).
