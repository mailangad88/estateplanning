Illinois taxes estates above a $4,000,000 exclusion amount (35 ILCS 405). An estate whose gross value, plus adjusted taxable gifts, exceeds that amount must file Form 700 with the Illinois Attorney General, generally within nine months of the date of death, with an extension available. The tax is computed using a state calculation that links to the federal tax credit, and it is not the same as the federal Form 706. An estate that owes no tax can still need to file if it crosses the filing threshold.

## Example

Nell dies with a gross estate of $4,650,000, including a house, investments, and life insurance. Her executor files Form 700 within nine months and pays tax, which is calculated on the amount above the exclusion using the Illinois formula.

## Why it matters

Illinois has no portability, so a married couple can lose a $4,000,000 exclusion at the first death without planning. Gross value, not just probate value, counts.
