Property that passes outright to a surviving spouse, or into certain trusts that give the spouse enough control, qualifies for a deduction equal to its full value. That defers the tax until the second spouse dies. A [QTIP trust](/glossary/qtip-trust) lets you use the deduction while still controlling who ultimately gets the property. If the spouse is not a U.S. citizen, special rules apply, including a qualified domestic trust. The deduction postpones tax rather than erasing it, since assets left to the survivor become part of the survivor's estate.

## Example

Henry leaves his $9,000,000 estate entirely to his wife, Irene, so his estate owes no federal estate tax. Irene's own estate will be counted when she dies, so the couple also files to preserve Henry's unused exclusion through [Portability](/glossary/portability).

## Why it matters

Careless use can waste an exemption or push a large estate into taxation at the second death, and "everything to my spouse" may not protect children from a prior marriage. Many couples combine the deduction with trusts and portability. See [portability for married couples](/learn/estate-tax/portability-for-married-couples) and [QTIP and marital trusts](/learn/blended-families/qtip-and-marital-trusts).
