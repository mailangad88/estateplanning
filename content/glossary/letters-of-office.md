In Illinois, the circuit court issues letters of office once a will is admitted to probate and the executor qualifies, or once an administrator is appointed when there is no will (Probate Act of 1975, 755 ILCS 5). The word covers both [Letters testamentary](/glossary/letters-testamentary) and letters of administration. The clerk gives the representative a signed, sealed copy that shows the name, the date, and the court. Banks, brokers, and title companies ask to see a certified copy before they release money or retitle anything, and many want one dated recently. The letters end when the estate closes or the court removes the representative.

## Example

After Carol's death, her son Dev is appointed executor of a $210,000 estate. He orders five certified copies at a few dollars each, gives one to the bank holding her $48,000 checking account, one to the brokerage, and keeps the rest for the car title and the county recorder.

## Why it matters

Without letters, nobody can lawfully move an estate asset, even with a will in hand. Order extra certified copies up front, because each institution keeps its own.
