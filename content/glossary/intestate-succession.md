Each state has its own formula, but the pattern is similar. A surviving spouse comes first, sometimes taking everything, and sometimes sharing with the deceased's children, especially children who are not also the spouse's. If no spouse, children and their descendants inherit, often under [Per stirpes](/glossary/per-stirpes) rules. Next come parents, siblings, and further relatives such as grandparents and cousins. If no relatives can be found, property goes to the state, which is rare. Unmarried partners, stepchildren, friends, and charities receive nothing, and assets with beneficiary forms are not affected.

## Example

Cal, divorced, dies with no will and two adult children. His state's statute splits his probate property equally between them. His girlfriend of fifteen years, who shared his home, has no claim unless the house was jointly owned.

## Why it matters

The rules reflect a typical family, not yours. They can force a spouse and stepchildren to share, give shares to estranged relatives, or leave minors' money to a court-supervised process. A will changes all of this. See [what happens if you die without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will) and [Intestate](/glossary/intestate) and [Heir](/glossary/heir).
