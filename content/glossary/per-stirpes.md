The phrase is Latin for "by branch." The estate is first divided into equal shares at the level of your children, one share for each child who is living and one for each child who has died leaving descendants. A deceased child's share is divided equally among that child's children. This can continue down generations. Some institutions apply slightly different versions, so the exact wording on a form matters. Contrast with [Per capita](/glossary/per-capita), which divides equally among those living.

## Example

Mina leaves her estate "to my three children, per stirpes." One son dies before her, leaving two daughters. The estate is split in thirds: each surviving child gets a third, and the two granddaughters split their father's third, each receiving one-sixth.

## Why it matters

Per stirpes protects grandchildren from being skipped when a parent dies early, and keeps branches of a family equal. It can also give a very different result from a per capita clause with a large family. Many beneficiary forms let you choose, so make a conscious choice.
