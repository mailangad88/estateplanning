Section 2-6 of the Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/2-6) treats a person who intentionally and unjustifiably causes the death of another as if the person had died first, for purposes of inheriting from the victim by will, intestacy, or survivorship. The bar reaches property that would pass by will, by intestacy, or by survivorship. A criminal conviction is strong proof, but a civil court can decide the question on a lower standard of proof. Illinois also has a related bar, in section 2-6.2, for financial exploitation, abuse, or neglect of an elderly person or a person with a disability.

## Example

Derek is convicted of killing his mother, Alma. Her will leaves her $300,000 estate to Derek and his two children. The court treats Derek as having died first, so his share passes as if he were gone, and the children may take in his place.

## Why it matters

The rule keeps wrongdoers from profiting, and it can reroute an estate in surprising ways. Interested persons should raise it early in the probate case.
