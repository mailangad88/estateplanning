QTIP stands for qualified terminable interest property. The trust, created at the first spouse's death, must pay the surviving spouse all income at least annually. It can also allow principal payments for the spouse's needs. Because the spouse receives enough benefit, the trust qualifies for the [Unlimited marital deduction](/glossary/marital-deduction), delaying estate tax until the surviving spouse dies. The key feature is that the first spouse decides who receives what is left, and the surviving spouse cannot change that. An independent trustee is often chosen to balance interests.

## Example

Gary remarries and has two adult children from his first marriage. At his death, a QTIP trust funds income for his wife, Susan, for life. When Susan dies, the remaining assets go to Gary's two children, not to Susan's relatives.

## Why it matters

QTIP trusts balance two goals that often clash in blended families: support for a new spouse and certainty for children. The tradeoff is that the survivor has limited control and may feel dependent on a trustee. Poorly drafted terms can invite conflict.
