Each person has a federal basic exclusion of $15,000,000 in 2026. If the first spouse to die uses only part of it, the unused amount, called the DSUE, can be "ported" to the survivor, who then has their own exclusion plus the DSUE; together a couple can shelter up to $30 million. To elect portability, the executor files Form 706 on time, even if no tax is owed. Portability applies to federal tax only and generally does not carry over to state estate taxes or the generation-skipping tax exemption.

## Example

Roy dies in 2026 and leaves everything to his wife, so no tax is due. His estate files Form 706 and elects portability, locking in his unused $15,000,000 exclusion. Later, when she dies with a $24,000,000 estate, her own and Roy's exclusions together cover it.

## Why it matters

Skipping the filing can forfeit a large exemption, and portability does not protect growth after the first death the way a bypass trust can. Many couples with moderate wealth rely on it for simplicity. Ask when filing is due, since late-election relief is limited.
