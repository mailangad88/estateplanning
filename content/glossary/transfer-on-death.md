TOD is the securities-account counterpart of [Payable-on-death (POD)](/glossary/payable-on-death) designations at banks. Under a law adopted in most states, an owner can register stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and brokerage accounts in TOD form with a beneficiary. Some states also let vehicles carry a TOD title. During life, the beneficiary has no right to the assets, and the owner can sell or change the designation. At death, the brokerage retitles assets after receiving a death certificate and claim forms. Real estate uses a separate tool, the [Transfer-on-death deed](/glossary/transfer-on-death-deed).

## Example

Sandra registers her $120,000 brokerage account as TOD, naming her niece and nephew in equal shares. When Sandra dies, the brokerage splits the account into two accounts for them within weeks, and her will plays no part.

## Why it matters

TOD is free or nearly free to set up and avoids probate for that account. Problems come from forgetting it: it can override equal gifts in a will, leave the estate short of cash to pay debts, or point at someone who has since died. Review forms regularly.
