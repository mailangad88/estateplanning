An agent, sometimes called an attorney-in-fact, steps into your shoes for the powers listed in a [Power of attorney](/glossary/power-of-attorney). The person who signs the document is the [Principal (power of attorney)](/glossary/principal). An agent need not be a lawyer. Financial agents pay bills, manage accounts, and file taxes; healthcare agents consult doctors and make treatment decisions. Agents are [fiduciaries](/glossary/fiduciary), so they must act in the principal's interest, keep their own money separate, and keep records.

## Example

Walter names his niece Dana as agent under his [Durable power of attorney](/glossary/durable-power-of-attorney). When Walter is hospitalized for weeks, Dana signs in as his agent to pay his mortgage and renew his homeowner's insurance. She keeps a ledger and never moves his funds into her own account.

## Why it matters

The agent you pick matters more than any clause in the document, because an agent can often move large amounts of money with little oversight. Choose someone organized and honest, and name a backup. An agent acts only while the principal is alive and cannot change the principal's will.
