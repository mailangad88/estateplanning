Under the Uniform Transfers to Minors Act, in force in nearly every state, anyone can give assets to a minor through an account run by a custodian. The gift is irrevocable, and the money must be used for the child's benefit. When the child reaches the age of termination, 18 to 21 in most states and up to 25 in a few, the child takes full control. Gifts count toward the [Annual gift tax exclusion](/glossary/annual-exclusion). Parents can also name a custodian in a will to receive an inheritance for a minor.

## Example

Grandparents put $15,000 into a UTMA account for their grandson Leo, with his aunt as custodian. She uses part to pay for a laptop and summer program. At 21, Leo gets the entire balance and can spend it as he chooses.

## Why it matters

UTMA accounts are simple and cheap, but the child gains full control at a fixed age whether or not they are ready, and the balance can affect financial aid. For larger sums, a trust allows later ages and conditions. See [leaving money to minors](/learn/guardianship/leaving-money-to-minors) and [trusts for children](/learn/trusts/trusts-for-children).
