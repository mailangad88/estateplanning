Under 755 ILCS 5/15-1, a surviving spouse is entitled to a reasonable award for support for nine months after death, in a manner suited to the standard of living before death. The statute sets a floor of $20,000, plus $10,000 for each minor or dependent child who lived with the spouse. The award is paid from the estate whether or not the will mentions it, and it ranks ahead of most unsecured debts. The spouse may waive it, for example in a prenuptial agreement.

## Example

Malik dies leaving his wife Joan and a 15-year-old son, with an estate of $140,000. Joan is entitled to at least $30,000 as the award, which comes out of the estate before credit card creditors and before the residuary heirs.

## Why it matters

The award is separate from the [Elective share](/glossary/elective-share) and from the right to renounce a will, and it can be claimed together with them. It also affects timing, since the representative must plan for it before distributing. See [Illinois spouse rights at death](/learn/illinois/illinois-spouse-rights-at-death) for how the pieces fit.
