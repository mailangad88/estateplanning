The federal privacy law known as HIPAA restricts what providers may tell others about your health. A HIPAA authorization lets you name who may receive information, what may be shared, and for how long. It is separate from a [Healthcare proxy](/glossary/healthcare-proxy), though a proxy often gets access once the proxy takes effect. A standalone authorization can take effect right away, which helps if you need someone to talk with your doctor before a formal incapacity finding is made. Many estate planners include a broad authorization in every plan.

## Example

Frank's mother signs a HIPAA authorization naming Frank and his sister. When she is admitted with confusion, the hospital explains her condition and treatment plan to both children immediately, and the siblings can obtain records to decide whether she needs a care facility.

## Why it matters

Without an authorization, relatives may be told nothing, and a healthcare agent may need to prove that you are incapacitated before the proxy applies, which can take days. That delay is especially painful in an emergency or with dementia. Keep one signed copy with each provider.
