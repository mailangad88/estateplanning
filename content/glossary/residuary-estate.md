A will usually lists particular gifts, then includes a "residuary clause" that says who gets everything else. That leftover property is the residuary estate. It includes any asset not mentioned and gifts that failed, for example because a beneficiary died. A good residuary clause names more than one level of backup. If a will has none, or if the residuary gift fails, the leftover property passes under state intestacy rules, even though the will otherwise stands.

## Example

Cheryl's will gives her car to her nephew, $10,000 to a charity, and "the rest" to her son. After debts and the gifts, $180,000 is left; that is the residuary estate and goes to her son. If he died first with no backup named, it would pass under state law.

## Why it matters

The residuary clause is the most important part of many wills because it controls the bulk of the estate, including things nobody foresaw. Debts and expenses are usually paid from the residue first, which can shrink the share of the person who receives it.
