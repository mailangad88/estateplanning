The Illinois Power of Attorney Act (755 ILCS 45) allows the principal to name one or more successor agents, who act in the order listed. The statutory property form has space for successors and says each successor serves only if the previous agent dies, becomes incapacitated, resigns, or refuses to act. A successor has the same powers as the first agent unless the form says otherwise. The same approach applies to health care agents under the health care power of attorney part of the Act. Without a named successor, the principal may need a guardianship.

## Example

Cal names his wife Dee as agent and his daughter Rae as successor. When Dee has a stroke, she resigns in writing and delivers it to Rae and to the bank, which then accepts Rae's authority.

## Why it matters

A document without a successor can fail at the worst time. Name at least one and tell them. Choose someone who lives nearby and is willing to take it on.
