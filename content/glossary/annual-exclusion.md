Each calendar year, you can give any number of people a gift up to the annual limit, currently $19,000 per recipient as of 2026, without any reporting. The limit applies per giver, so a married couple can together give $38,000 to one person. Gifts above the limit are not taxed right away; they require a Form 709 gift tax return and reduce your lifetime [estate and gift tax](/glossary/estate-tax) exemption, which is $15,000,000 per person in 2026. Payments made directly to a school for tuition or to a provider for medical care are generally outside these limits.

## Example

Priya and her husband give each of their three children $38,000 in 2026 by splitting gifts, for $114,000 total. No return is needed and their $15,000,000 exemptions are untouched. If Priya instead gave one child $50,000 alone, she would file Form 709 for the $31,000 above the limit.

## Why it matters

Annual gifts can move wealth to heirs over time and reduce a taxable estate, but the recipient generally takes your original cost basis, so there is no [Step-up in basis](/glossary/step-up-in-basis). That can cost an heir capital gains tax later. Gifts to qualify for Medicaid may also trigger a penalty under the [look-back rules](/glossary/look-back-period). Compare the tradeoffs in [the annual exclusion guide](/learn/estate-tax/gift-tax-annual-exclusion).
