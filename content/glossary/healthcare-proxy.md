The name varies by state: healthcare proxy, healthcare power of attorney, medical power of attorney, or healthcare surrogate designation. The person you name, your healthcare agent, speaks with doctors, reads your records, and makes treatment choices consistent with your values once you cannot. Unlike a [Living will](/glossary/living-will), which covers specific situations in writing, a proxy covers situations nobody predicted. Most proxy forms are part of an [Advance directive](/glossary/advance-directive), and most require witnesses or a notary.

## Example

Anita names her husband as proxy and her sister as backup. After a car accident leaves her unconscious, her husband tells the surgeon she always said she would not want long-term ventilator support, and the care team follows his lead.

## Why it matters

Without a proxy, hospitals follow state surrogate rules that rank relatives, which can put the wrong person in charge, especially for unmarried partners, stepfamilies, or estranged relatives. Pair the proxy with a [HIPAA authorization](/glossary/hipaa-authorization) so your agent can see records. Talk to your agent in advance about your wishes.
