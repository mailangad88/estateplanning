The Illinois Real Property Transfer on Death Instrument Act (755 ILCS 27) lets an owner name who receives real estate at death while keeping full control during life. The owner signs with testamentary capacity before two credible witnesses and a notary, then records the instrument with the county recorder before death. The beneficiary has no rights until then, and the owner can sell, mortgage, or revoke by a later recorded instrument. An unrecorded TODI is ineffective, and the transferred property remains open to creditor claims.

## Example

Gus, a widower, signs a TODI naming his daughter Alma for his $230,000 house and records it in July. When he dies the next spring, Alma records a notice of death affidavit and the house avoids probate.

## Why it matters

A TODI works much like a [Transfer-on-death deed](/glossary/transfer-on-death-deed) in other states. Errors in signing or recording can void it, and it does not handle taxes or debts. Study the details in the [Illinois TODI guide](/learn/illinois/illinois-transfer-on-death-instrument).
