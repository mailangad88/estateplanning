In a land trust, the deed names a trustee, often a title company or bank, as the title holder, and a trust agreement names the owner as beneficiary. The beneficiary keeps the right to occupy, rent, direct a sale, and assign the beneficial interest. Illinois treats that interest as personal property, which affects probate and can keep the owner's name off the public deed. A land trust is not the same as a living trust. It does not hold other assets, and unless it names successor beneficiaries or is assigned to a living trust, the interest may pass through probate.

## Example

Bruno holds his $340,000 two-flat in a land trust with a Chicago title company as trustee. His trust agreement names his daughter Sol as successor beneficiary. At his death she gives the trustee a copy of the death certificate and takes over.

## Why it matters

Land trusts offer privacy and flexible transfer, but they leave the rest of an estate plan untouched. Many owners assign the beneficial interest to a [Living trust](/glossary/living-trust) too.
