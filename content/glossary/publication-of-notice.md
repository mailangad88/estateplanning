Under 755 ILCS 5/18-3, the representative publishes the notice once a week for three successive weeks in a newspaper in the county where the estate is open. The first publication starts the clock for the claims period. The notice gives the decedent's name, the representative's name and address, the attorney's name, and the claims bar date. The representative also mails or delivers the notice to each known or reasonably ascertainable creditor and files proof of publication with the clerk. Publication costs vary by newspaper.

## Example

Omar is appointed executor of his aunt's estate and arranges a three-week run in the county newspaper at about $90 total. He mails copies to her credit card company, her landlord, and a hospital, then files the proof with the clerk.

## Why it matters

Skipping publication leaves the estate open to claims for two years after death rather than a few months. It is a small cost for a large protection.
