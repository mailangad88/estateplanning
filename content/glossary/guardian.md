For children, parents can nominate a guardian in their wills to raise their minor children if both parents die; a judge makes the final appointment, guided by the child's best interests. For adults, a court may appoint a guardian after finding the person cannot make safe decisions (in some states this is called a [Conservatorship](/glossary/conservatorship), or the guardian handles personal care while a conservator handles money). A guardian of a child's person is separate from whoever manages the child's inheritance, who may be a [Trustee](/glossary/trustee) or a custodian.

## Example

Mark and Dee name Dee's brother as guardian of their six-year-old in their wills and name a friend as trustee of the money they leave. After a tragic accident, the court appoints the brother to raise the child, and the friend manages the money for the child's benefit.

## Why it matters

If you have young children and no named guardian, a judge decides who raises them, which can include a custody dispute among relatives. This choice is often the most important decision in a parent's plan. Pick someone whose values you share and name an alternate. See [how to choose a guardian](/learn/guardianship/how-to-choose-a-guardian) and [what if no guardian is named](/learn/guardianship/what-if-no-guardian-named).
