Under 755 ILCS 5/2-8, a surviving spouse may file a written renunciation with the court within seven months after the will is admitted to probate. The spouse then receives one-third of the entire estate after claims if the decedent left descendants, or one-half if there are none. The renunciation covers the probate estate, not assets that pass by beneficiary form or joint tenancy. If the spouse misses the seven-month window, the will controls unless the court extends the time for cause.

## Example

Walt leaves his $400,000 estate to his two children from a first marriage and gives his wife Rosa $20,000. Rosa renounces within seven months and receives one-third, or about $133,000, minus her share of claims.

## Why it matters

Renunciation is Illinois's version of the [Elective share](/glossary/elective-share) idea. It can reshape a plan that favors children or others over a spouse, and a prenuptial agreement can waive it.
