The Illinois Health Care Surrogate Act (755 ILCS 40) sets a priority list for who decides when a patient cannot and has no agent named. In general the order runs from a court-appointed guardian, to the spouse or civil union partner, adult children, parents, adult siblings, adult grandchildren, a close friend, and finally the guardian of the estate. The attending physician determines that the patient lacks decisional capacity, and some decisions, such as forgoing life-sustaining treatment, apply only for certain qualifying conditions. The surrogate must follow the patient's known wishes where they are known.

## Example

Morris, 79, suffers a stroke with no health care power of attorney. His wife Peg acts as surrogate and consents to surgery. When Peg later falls ill, his son Ravi is next on the list.

## Why it matters

The default list may not match who a person would pick, and it can leave out an unmarried partner who is not a close friend in the legal sense. Naming an agent in a [Healthcare proxy](/glossary/healthcare-proxy) avoids the question. See [who decides under the Illinois Act](/learn/illinois/illinois-health-care-surrogate-act-who-decides).
