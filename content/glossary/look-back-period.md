When a person applies for Medicaid long-term care coverage, the state reviews financial records for the previous 60 months, in most states. Transfers for less than fair value, such as gifts to children, can trigger a penalty: a period of ineligibility calculated by dividing the amount given by the state's average monthly nursing home cost. The penalty clock typically starts only once the person is otherwise eligible and in care. Some transfers are exempt, for example to a spouse or to a disabled child. California handles this differently, so rules should be checked locally.

## Example

Pam, 78, gives her grandson $60,000 for college, then needs nursing home care 18 months later. When she applies for Medicaid, the transfer is caught in the review. If her state's average monthly cost is $10,000, she would face about six months of ineligibility.

## Why it matters

The look-back is why long-term care planning is best started years before it is needed. Moving assets into certain trusts or making permitted transfers early may protect resources, while last-minute gifts often backfire. Ordinary gifts under the [Annual gift tax exclusion](/glossary/annual-exclusion) are not exempt from the look-back.
