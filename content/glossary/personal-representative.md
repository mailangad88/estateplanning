Many states use "personal representative" as the single, modern term for both [Executor](/glossary/executor) and [Administrator](/glossary/administrator). The role is identical: gather and protect assets, pay debts and taxes, keep records, and distribute property to heirs. The title also signals that the person represents the estate, not their own interests, and is a [Fiduciary](/glossary/fiduciary). Authority starts only when the court issues [letters](/glossary/letters-testamentary). Some states require a bond, and nonresident personal representatives may face added requirements.

## Example

Isaac dies in Florida with a will naming his sister as personal representative. The clerk issues letters, and she hires a probate attorney, opens an estate account, notifies creditors, and later sends a final accounting to the heirs.

## Why it matters

The title matters when reading forms and court notices, because they may use it exclusively. Whoever serves has legal exposure, as duties include accurate record keeping and fair treatment of all heirs, so serious errors can mean personal liability. Pick someone able to do the work.
