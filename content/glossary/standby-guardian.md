Under 755 ILCS 5/11-5.3, a parent can name a standby guardian by written designation, and the person can take over on triggering events such as the parent's death, mental incapacity, or debilitation with consent. The designation is helpful for parents with a serious illness or a single parent who wants to avoid a gap. The standby guardian then asks the court to confirm appointment, usually within a short window set by statute. A standby designation is more flexible than a will clause because it can take effect while the parent is still alive.

## Example

Lorna, a single mother with advanced cancer, designates her sister Joy as standby guardian for her 8-year-old son. When Lorna is hospitalized for weeks, Joy has authority to enroll him in school and consent to care.

## Why it matters

Parents often assume a will covers this, but a will only speaks at death. A standby designation gives clarity during illness.
