Some digital assets have money value (crypto, online businesses, domain names); some have sentimental value (photos, messages); some are simply access points to financial life (email, banking logins). Most states have adopted a version of the Revised Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act, which lets an [Executor](/glossary/executor) or [Agent (power of attorney)](/glossary/agent) request access, but companies often limit what they share, such as the contents of messages, unless you gave permission through their own online tools. Terms of service may bar transfer of some accounts entirely.

## Example

Joel dies with a Bitcoin wallet, a cloud photo library, and an online store. His executor finds a password list and a letter of instruction, accesses the store and photos, and uses a hardware key to move the Bitcoin. Without those, the coins would be lost for good.

## Why it matters

Missing access can mean lost money, unclosed accounts, identity theft, and lost family photos. List accounts, store passwords securely, use each platform's legacy contact or inactive account tool, and authorize your fiduciary in your documents.
