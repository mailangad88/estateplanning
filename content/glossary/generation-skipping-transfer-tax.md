The tax exists to prevent families from avoiding estate tax by skipping a generation. It applies to "skip persons" such as grandchildren, and to trusts that benefit them. The rate is the top estate tax rate, 40 percent, and each person has a GST exemption that matches the basic exclusion amount, $15,000,000 in 2026. Annual-exclusion gifts and direct payments for tuition or medical bills are generally excluded. The exemption is not portable between spouses, so each spouse must use their own. Proper allocation is reported on gift or estate returns.

## Example

Mabel leaves $2,000,000 in a trust for her grandchildren. She allocates part of her GST exemption on her estate tax return, so no GST tax is due. If her estate exceeded $15,000,000 in total generation-skipping transfers, the excess would face a 40 percent tax.

## Why it matters

Most families fall far below the exemption and never owe it, but those leaving large sums to grandchildren, or to long-term "dynasty" trusts, should plan for it. Allocating exemption by mistake or missing a filing can waste it. See [the federal estate tax exemption](/learn/estate-tax/federal-estate-tax-exemption) and [Estate tax](/glossary/estate-tax).
