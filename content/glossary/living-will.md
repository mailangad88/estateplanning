Despite the name, a living will has nothing to do with a will that distributes property. It speaks only while you are alive. It usually applies when two doctors find that you have a terminal condition or are permanently unconscious, and it tells your care team whether you want CPR, a ventilator, tube feeding, or dialysis. It guides your agent and your doctors but is not a medical order, unlike a [Do-not-resuscitate order (DNR)](/glossary/dnr) or a [POLST](/glossary/polst). Many states provide a statutory form, and most combine it with a [Healthcare proxy](/glossary/healthcare-proxy).

## Example

Robert writes in his living will that if he is terminally ill, he wants pain relief and comfort care but no ventilator or feeding tube. When pneumonia puts him in intensive care with no hope of recovery, his wife shows the document and the team transitions him to comfort care.

## Why it matters

A living will spares your family from guessing at hard choices and from fighting over them. It works best when it is specific, discussed with your agent, and kept where the hospital can find it. Update it if your health or views change. Read [what a living will should say](/learn/healthcare-directives/living-will) and [DNR orders and POLST forms](/learn/healthcare-directives/dnr-and-polst).
