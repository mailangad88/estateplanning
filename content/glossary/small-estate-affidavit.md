Most states let heirs skip formal probate when the probate estate is below a set dollar threshold, which differs widely, from a few thousand dollars to several hundred thousand in some states. After a waiting period, the heir signs an affidavit stating that the estate qualifies, that debts will be paid, and that the heir is entitled to the property, and then presents it, with a death certificate, to the bank or agency holding the asset. Some states require filing it with a court, and real estate often has separate rules. Check the current limits in your state.

## Example

Lou dies with a $15,000 checking account in his name alone and no real estate. His daughter waits the required period, signs the affidavit, and the bank releases the funds to her without a court case.

## Why it matters

The process can save thousands of dollars and months of time, but affidavit signers take responsibility for accuracy, and an affiant who ignores debts or other heirs can be liable. Banks may reject incomplete forms.
