The trust document lists the protector's powers, which often include replacing the trustee, approving changes to tax or trust law, moving the trust's situs, and consenting to certain distributions. The Illinois Trust Code has rules for directed trusts, where someone other than the trustee holds a power to direct or protect the trust (760 ILCS 3), and Illinois courts may treat a protector as a fiduciary to the extent of those powers. The protector should not be the grantor or a beneficiary who could be taxed because of the powers. The role is usually unpaid or paid hourly, and the document should name a successor.

## Example

Abe sets up an irrevocable trust for his children worth $900,000. He names his accountant, Iris, as protector with power to remove the trustee on 30 days' notice. Years later the family bank trustee raises fees, and Iris replaces it.

## Why it matters

A protector gives a long trust a way to adjust without a court case, but a poorly chosen one can create conflict or tax problems. Specify limits in writing.
