Not everything a person owns goes through probate. Assets with a named beneficiary (life insurance, retirement accounts, [Payable-on-death (POD)](/glossary/payable-on-death) accounts), assets held in a trust, and property owned in [Joint tenancy with right of survivorship](/glossary/joint-tenancy) with survivorship pass outside of it. What is left, typically solely owned real estate, bank accounts, vehicles, and personal property with no beneficiary, forms the probate estate, and the will controls it. The size of the probate estate often decides whether a state's small estate shortcut is available.

## Example

Priya's net worth is $900,000: a $500,000 house in a living trust, a $300,000 401(k) with her son named, and $100,000 in a checking account in her name only. Her probate estate is just the $100,000 checking account.

## Why it matters

Knowing what is in your probate estate shows how much a will actually controls and where costs are likely. Many people are surprised that their will does not govern retirement accounts. Review ownership and beneficiaries on everything you own.
