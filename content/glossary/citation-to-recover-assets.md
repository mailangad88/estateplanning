Section 16-1 of the Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/16-1) allows the representative, or any interested person, to petition for a citation to a person who may hold property of the decedent or have information about it. The court can order the person to appear, answer questions under oath, and deliver the property. The proceeding covers items such as a hidden bank account, a relative who took jewelry, or an agent under a power of attorney who has not accounted. The judge can order return of the property and, in some cases, award costs.

## Example

After Sue dies, her executor learns that her neighbor Ed has her $14,000 savings bond and a ring. A letter goes unanswered, so the executor files a citation. At the hearing Ed returns both items.

## Why it matters

Citations are a tool for recovering what belongs to the estate without a separate lawsuit. They also serve heirs when an executor does nothing. A citation hearing is formal and usually needs a lawyer.
