In Illinois the affidavit is signed under oath before a notary by a person with personal knowledge, often a relative or long-time friend. It states the date and place of death, whether there was a will, the marriage and children of the decedent, and who survives. Title companies and some institutions accept a recorded affidavit as part of the chain of title. It does not by itself pass title, and it does not replace a court finding of heirship, which is made in a probate case under the Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/5-3).

## Example

Lena's grandfather died in 1998 owning a lot in his own name, with no probate ever opened. She and her cousin sign an affidavit listing his four children, two of whom have also died, so a title company can see who holds an interest.

## Why it matters

The affidavit can reduce a title problem but cannot fix one that needs a court order. If the facts are disputed, a court proceeding may be needed.
