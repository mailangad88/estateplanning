Article XII of the Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/12) governs bonds. The court sets the amount as a multiple of the personal estate's value, and a corporate surety usually issues it for an annual premium that the estate pays. The bond protects heirs and creditors, not the representative, and the surety can seek repayment from a representative who causes a loss. A will can waive surety, and when there is no will the court may require it unless all interested persons agree otherwise.

## Example

Dale is appointed administrator of an estate with $120,000 in personal property and must post a bond. The surety company charges a premium of about $600 a year, which is paid from the estate and listed on the final account.

## Why it matters

A bond adds cost and some underwriting, and a person with poor credit may have trouble getting one. If a will waives surety, the process is faster.
