The Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/11a-12) directs the court to appoint a limited guardian, with powers listed in the order, when the adult is able to make some but not all decisions. A limited order may cover only medical consent, only large financial transactions, or only choice of residence. The adult retains the rest, including the right to vote, marry, and manage a bank account unless the order says otherwise. The court favors this approach because Illinois law calls for the least restrictive alternative. It can later be expanded or ended on petition.

## Example

Dana, 34, has an intellectual disability, works part time, and pays her own rent. Her family asks for a limited guardianship giving her mother authority over medical consent and any contract over $5,000 and leaves everything else to Dana.

## Why it matters

The limited form protects dignity and still addresses real risks. Some families can avoid guardianship altogether with a signed [Durable power of attorney](/glossary/durable-power-of-attorney) or a supported decision-making agreement.
