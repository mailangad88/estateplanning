Terminology varies by state. In some states a conservator manages finances and a [Guardian](/glossary/guardian) makes personal and medical decisions for an adult; in others, "guardianship" covers both. Either way, a judge must find, after a hearing and often a medical evaluation, that the person lacks capacity. The conservator then reports to the court, usually files periodic accountings, and may need court permission for major actions such as selling a home.

## Example

Albert, 82, has advanced dementia and never signed a power of attorney. His bank will not talk to his son. The son petitions the court, a judge reviews doctors' letters and appoints him conservator, and from then on he files annual reports on every dollar he spends.

## Why it matters

A conservatorship takes away rights, costs legal fees, is public, and can take weeks or months. A signed [Durable power of attorney](/glossary/durable-power-of-attorney) plus a healthcare proxy usually lets a family avoid it entirely, while the person still has capacity. If the person has already lost capacity, though, a conservatorship may be the only path.
