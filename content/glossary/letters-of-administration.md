When a person dies intestate, an heir or other interested person petitions the circuit court in the county of the decedent's residence to appoint an administrator. The Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/9-3) sets a preference order that generally starts with the surviving spouse, then children, then other relatives. The court usually requires a surety bond and gives the person letters of administration once the oath and bond are filed. Some versions, such as letters of administration with the will annexed, apply when a will exists but names nobody able to serve.

## Example

Ruben dies without a will, leaving a house worth $190,000 and a $30,000 savings account. His wife Inez files the petition, posts a bond, and receives letters of administration. The bank then lets her open an estate account.

## Why it matters

An administrator has the same basic duties as an [Administrator](/glossary/administrator) named in any other setting: collect assets, pay debts, and distribute by Illinois intestacy law. The bond adds a cost that a will can often waive.
