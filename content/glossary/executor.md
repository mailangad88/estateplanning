The [Testator](/glossary/testator) chooses an executor in the will, and the court confirms the choice and issues [letters testamentary](/glossary/letters-testamentary) giving legal authority. The job runs for months to years. The executor inventories property, notifies creditors, pays valid debts and taxes, files the decedent's final returns, and distributes the rest according to the will. Many states use "[Personal representative](/glossary/personal-representative)" for the same role. An executor is a [Fiduciary](/glossary/fiduciary) and can be held personally liable for mistakes.

## Example

Ben names his friend Alicia as executor. After his death she files the will with the county court, gets letters, opens an estate account, pays his last credit card and funeral bill from it, files his final tax return, and then splits the remainder among his three nieces.

## Why it matters

The role demands time, organization, and patience, so the best choice is not always the closest relative or eldest child. Name a backup in case your first choice cannot serve, and consider whether a professional should share the work. Executors can usually be reimbursed and paid a fee under state law.
