**How to use this checklist.** If you already have a buy-sell agreement, pull it out and check each item against the actual text. If you do not, treat each section as a topic to decide with your co-owners. A buy-sell agreement is a contract among owners. Its job is to set, in advance, who may buy a departing owner's share, at what price and how it is paid.

## Why it matters

Without one, a co-owner's death can leave you in business with their spouse or heirs, who may not want to work or may want cash. The heirs may not be able to find a buyer, and a deadlocked company can lose value fast. With one, there is a plan for price, payment and control.

## Step 1: Choose the structure

| Structure | Who buys | Common use |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-purchase | The remaining owners buy the share | Few owners |
| Entity redemption | The company buys the share | More owners |
| Hybrid | The company has first option, owners second | Flexible cases |

- [ ] We chose a structure on purpose.
- [ ] We know who owns any insurance policies under that structure.
- [ ] We asked a tax advisor how the choice affects basis and taxes.

## Step 2: List the triggers

- [ ] Death of an owner
- [ ] Disability, with a definition and a waiting period
- [ ] Retirement
- [ ] Divorce, where a spouse might gain an interest
- [ ] Bankruptcy or creditor claim against an owner
- [ ] Owner wants to sell to an outsider (right of first refusal)
- [ ] Owner is fired or leaves
- [ ] Loss of a required professional license
- [ ] Deadlock between owners

For each trigger, write whether the purchase is required or optional.

## Step 3: Decide how to set the price

| Method | How it works | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed price in the agreement | A number everyone agrees to | Gets stale quickly |
| Formula | A multiple of earnings or revenue | Can mislead if business changes |
| Appraisal | An independent appraiser sets it | Costs money, takes time |
| Negotiated at the time | Owners agree later | Often fails at the worst moment |

- [ ] The agreement names a method, not just "fair value."
- [ ] If the price is fixed, we update it at least once a year in writing.
- [ ] We decided whether to apply discounts for minority or lack of marketability.
- [ ] We know how the appraiser is chosen if owners disagree.
- **Date of last valuation:** ____________________
- **Current agreed value:** ____________________

An old fixed price is the most common failure. A business bought for a price set ten years ago may leave a family badly underpaid, or leave the buyers unable to pay.

## Step 4: Plan the funding

The agreement is only as strong as the money behind it.

- [ ] Life insurance covers each owner for an amount tied to current value (see the [Life Insurance Needs tool](/tools/life-insurance-needs)).
- [ ] Disability buyout coverage exists if disability is a trigger.
- [ ] Policy owners and beneficiaries match the agreement.
- [ ] Premiums are paid and someone checks annually.
- [ ] If there is no insurance, the agreement says how payment is made, such as a promissory note over years.
- [ ] A cash reserve or sinking fund covers any gap.

## Step 5: Control and transfer limits

- [ ] The agreement bars transfers to outsiders without owner consent.
- [ ] It limits transfers to a family member or trust, if any are allowed.
- [ ] It covers what happens if an owner's will leaves shares to a spouse or child.
- [ ] A spouse's consent or waiver is on file where state law may give spouses a claim.
- [ ] It deals with licenses or regulations that limit who can own shares.

- **Note term and interest rate:** ____________________
- **Security for the seller (pledge of shares, personal backing of the note):** ____________________
- **Release of the departing owner from company debts and personal backing of loans:** ____________________
- [ ] The agreement addresses loans and leases you signed personally and leases.

## Step 7: Keep it alive

- [ ] The agreement is signed by every owner.
- [ ] It matches the operating agreement or bylaws.
- [ ] Each owner's will or trust works with it.
- [ ] We review it every year, and after major changes in value, owners or law.
- [ ] Original copies are with the company's attorney.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You have co-owners and no signed agreement.
- Your agreement is more than a few years old or the business has changed.
- An owner is uninsurable or the insurance is out of line with the price.
- The business is a licensed practice with ownership limits.
- A divorce, death or dispute is already in view.
