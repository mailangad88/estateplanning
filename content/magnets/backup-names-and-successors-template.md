## How to use this template

Every plan names people. People die, move, fall ill or say no. A backup turns that from a court problem into a paperwork step. Fill in the table, tell each person, and review it once a year. This is general education, and the exact outcome depends on your documents and state law.

## What happens when a named person cannot serve

| Situation | If you named a backup | If you did not |
|---|---|---|
| Executor dies before you | The backup steps in | Court appoints an administrator, often a family member, with a bond and added cost |
| Executor refuses | Backup steps in | Court appoints someone, may ask for a bond |
| Executor is a minor | A minor cannot serve. Backup or the court acts. | Court appoints someone |
| Trustee dies or cannot serve | The trust names the next trustee | The trust may allow beneficiaries to choose, or a court names one |
| Agent under a POA dies | Backup agent acts | The POA may end and a guardianship may be needed |
| Guardian cannot serve | Backup is nominated | The court chooses, and may consider family in conflict |
| Bank named as trustee no longer exists | A successor by merger usually takes over | The trust may need a court or amendment |
| Charity no longer exists | A gift can lapse or be redirected to a similar charity, depending on the document | Court may apply a cy pres rule |
| Beneficiary dies before you | Gift passes under your fallback instruction | State law may pass it to the beneficiary's descendants, or the gift fails |
| Beneficiary dies after you, before payment | The share generally goes to their estate or heirs | Depends on survival terms in the document |
| Your only child dies before you | The plan's fallback names others | Intestacy may pass to grandchildren or other relatives |

In Illinois, rules for trustees and agents appear in the Trust Code (760 ILCS 3) and the Powers of Attorney Act (755 ILCS 45). <!-- verify: sections on successor trustee and successor agent --> Other states differ.

## Survival clauses

Wills and trusts often say a beneficiary must survive you by a set number of days (commonly 30 or more). That prevents property from passing twice in a short time. Check your documents for one.

## If you have no one to name

- [ ] A professional trustee or corporate trust company
- [ ] A trusted friend, a faith group or a mentor
- [ ] A co-agent arrangement with a named tie breaker
- [ ] A care manager for medical or housing decisions
- [ ] A named charity as backup beneficiary

Say why you chose them in a letter. See [choosing an executor worksheet](/checklists/choosing-an-executor-worksheet).

## Questions for each person

- [ ] Are you willing and able to serve?
- [ ] Do you live close enough?
- [ ] Do you get along with the others?
- [ ] Are you comfortable with money and paperwork?
- [ ] Do you know where the documents are?

## Common mistakes

- Naming only one person per role
- Naming a spouse as primary and nobody after
- Naming a bank without checking it still exists
- Naming two people who do not get along as co-roles
- Not telling the backup

## When to talk to an attorney

- Your trust names a bank or advisor that has changed
- Your executor, trustee or agent has died
- You are considering naming a child who may be too young
- You want to name co-roles or a tie breaker
- Your guardian choice is contested
