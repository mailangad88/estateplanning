## How to use this chart

If you cannot decide for yourself and you never named a health care agent, someone still has to decide. In Illinois, a law called the Health Care Surrogate Act sets an order of people who can step in. It is found at 755 ILCS 40. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 40 is the Illinois Health Care Surrogate Act --> The law is a safety net, not a plan. This chart shows how it works. Other states use different lists. It is general education, not legal advice.

## When the law applies

The surrogate law is used when:

- The patient lacks decision-making capacity for the decision at hand
- There is no valid health care power of attorney, or the named agent cannot act
- There is no court-appointed guardian

A doctor decides whether a patient lacks capacity, often with a second opinion for major decisions. <!-- verify: Illinois requirements for who certifies lack of capacity -->

## Order of surrogates

| Order | Person | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Court-appointed guardian of the person | Where one exists |
| 2 | Spouse or civil union partner | Spouse is first in the family line |
| 3 | Adult child | If several children, a majority generally decides |
| 4 | Parent | |
| 5 | Adult sibling | |
| 6 | Adult grandchild | |
| 7 | Close friend | Often must be someone who knows the patient's wishes |
| 8 | Guardian of the estate | |

If no one is available or willing, the hospital may ask a court to appoint a guardian.

## What this means for real families

### Spouse

A spouse can act under the statute, without a separate form, in the order above. But a spouse has no right to financial authority. Banks do not accept a hospital surrogate role.

### Unmarried partner

An unmarried partner is not on the main list in many states. Illinois includes civil union partners as spouses. A long-term unmarried partner may have to rely on the "close friend" level, or on a health care power of attorney they were named in. If you are not married, do the paperwork. A written directive is far stronger than a statutory order.

### Adult children

Adult children can serve as surrogates, but disagreement among them can stall a decision. A named agent ends that argument.

### A friend

A friend ranks last. They can serve only if no one higher is available and willing. A friend you want to speak for you needs to be named in writing.

## If this, then that

| If this happens | Then this |
|---|---|
| A married patient with no documents | Spouse is the surrogate, with the doctor |
| Siblings disagree about a parent's care | The law's order and majority rules apply among those in the same rank; a hospital ethics committee or court can be asked |
| A named agent is out of reach | The alternate agent steps in if one is named. If not, the surrogate order may apply. |
| A DNR is requested for a patient with no agent | The surrogate may be able to consent to one, with doctor review and extra steps. |
| A patient with dementia has no documents | The patient may still have capacity for a limited document. If not, the surrogate order is used, or a guardianship. |
| Surrogate and patient wishes conflict | Doctors look to the patient's known wishes. Written ones carry the most weight. |

## Why a named agent is better

| Surrogate statute | Health care power of attorney |
|---|---|
| You do not choose | You choose |
| Order is fixed by law | Alternate agents are your pick |
| Can be contested by family | Written authority is clear |
| Does not cover wishes about specific treatments | Can state your wishes |

Even if you are married, a document lets you pick the person best suited and name a backup. Married people often assume their spouse covers everything. A spouse can be hurt, ill or not nearby at the wrong moment.

## Common mistakes

- [ ] Assuming a spouse can handle finances too
- [ ] Not naming an alternate agent
- [ ] Assuming a partner or friend has authority
- [ ] Waiting until a diagnosis, when signing may be challenged
- [ ] Never telling the person they were chosen

## When to talk to an attorney

- Family members disagree about who should decide
- A loved one lacks capacity and has no documents
- You are not married and want a partner or friend to decide
- A guardianship may be the only route
