## How to use this guide

We are sorry for your loss. Property records can be messy, and old paperwork is common. Use the research log to build a picture, then match any problem you find to the table of fixes. This is general education for Illinois, and a short consult with an Illinois attorney can confirm the route.

## Key terms

- **Clear title** means the record shows who owns the property and no one else has a valid claim against it.
- **Cloud on title** is anything in the record that makes ownership unclear: an old lien, a missing heir, a mistake.
- **Title search** is a review of the public record, done by a title company or attorney, to trace ownership and claims.
- **Quiet title action** is a court case asking a judge to declare who owns the property.

## Step flow

1. **Find the property.** Search the county recorder or assessor records by the person's name, and look at tax bills and mail.
2. **Get the current deed.** Copies are available from the county recorder of deeds, sometimes online.
3. **Check how it was owned.** Sole name, joint with survivorship, in a trust or land trust, or with a TOD instrument.
4. **Look for liens and taxes.** Mortgages, judgments, unpaid taxes.
5. **Decide the route.** Probate, small estate affidavit (personal property only), affidavit of heirship, or a court case.
6. **Record the new paper.** The final transfer document must be recorded in the county.

## Does the person own real estate?

| Where to look | What it shows |
|---|---|
| County recorder of deeds | Deeds, mortgages, liens recorded in the person's name |
| County assessor and treasurer | Who is billed for property taxes |
| Old mail and tax returns | Property tax bills, mortgage interest forms |
| Illinois State Treasurer unclaimed property search | Money held in the person's name, not land |
| A title company | A formal search for a fee |

In Illinois each county keeps its own records. In Cook County, the County Clerk's office now holds the recording function. <!-- verify: Cook County recording office name and online search -->

## How the property was held

| Held as | What happens at death | Probate? |
|---|---|---|
| Sole name | Goes under the will or intestacy | Usually yes |
| Joint tenancy with survivorship | Passes to the surviving owner | No, but record a death certificate |
| Tenancy in common | The deceased share goes under the will or intestacy | Often yes |
| Revocable trust | Successor trustee follows the trust | No |
| TOD instrument, 755 ILCS 27 | Passes to the named beneficiary | No, if recorded before death |

## Common title problems and fixes

| Problem | Usual fix |
|---|---|
| Deed lost | Get a certified copy from the recorder. The recording is the proof, not the paper |
| Typo in name | Corrective deed or affidavit, signed and recorded |
| Wrong legal description | Corrective deed. A survey may be needed |
| Deed names someone who has died | Their estate or heirs must sign, which may require probate |
| House still in a relative's name from decades ago | Open an estate (even years late), or an affidavit of heirship plus court action |
| No will | Heirs are set by intestacy. Illinois order is in 755 ILCS 5/2-1 |
| Old mortgage never marked paid | Request a recorded release from the lender |
| Lien from a judgment | Pay, negotiate, or contest before a sale |
| Unknown or missing heirs | Court case to determine heirs |

## Illinois heirship tools

Illinois has an affidavit of heirship form for some situations, but it often does not by itself give a buyer or title company clear title to real estate. See the [Illinois heirship affidavit worksheet](/free/illinois-heirship-affidavit-worksheet). For real estate left by a person who died without a will, a probate case often supplies the order a title company can rely on. <!-- verify: when an Illinois heirship affidavit is enough for real estate -->

## When a quiet title action fits

- A cloud cannot be fixed by signature
- Heirs cannot be found
- A claim has been on the record for many years
- A seller needs a court ruling to close

It is a court case, so it is slower and costs more than a corrective deed.

## Removing a deceased owner from a deed

You do not edit the old deed. Instead you record a new document, such as:

- A death certificate and affidavit, when the owner was a joint tenant with survivorship
- Executor's deed under a probate case
- Trustee's deed under a trust
- A recorded TOD instrument with the beneficiary's acceptance and death certificate

## Unclaimed property

The Illinois State Treasurer runs a free search at I-Cash for money held in a person's name. Heirs can claim it with proof of death and relationship. Do not pay a "finder" a percentage.

## When to talk to an attorney

- The deed names someone who has died
- There is no will and several heirs
- A lien or judgment is on the record
- A buyer or title company will not close
- Heirs cannot be found
