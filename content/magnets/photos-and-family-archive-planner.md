**How to use this planner.** Go through the parts in order. Part 1 finds what you have, Part 2 sets up backups, Part 3 decides who gets what, and Part 4 writes it down. You do not have to organize every photo. You only need to make sure the collection survives and someone knows it exists.

## Part 1: Find everything

Photos are scattered. List every place yours live.

| Location | Approx. size or count | Account or device | Backed up elsewhere? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone camera roll | | | |
| Cloud service (iCloud, Google, Amazon, other) | | | |
| Computer folders | | | |
| External hard drives | | | |
| Memory cards and old cameras | | | |
| Social media posts | | | |
| Printed albums and boxes | | | |
| Old film, slides, videotapes | | | |
| Family documents (letters, certificates, recipes) | | | |

- [ ] I checked old email and texts for photos sent to me.
- [ ] I listed accounts under spouse or parent names that hold family pictures.
- [ ] I noted which cloud plan I pay for and when it renews.

## Part 2: Backups that last

A common guideline is three copies, on two kinds of storage, with one copy somewhere else. It is easy to remember and works at any size.

- [ ] Copy one: the original on phone or computer.
- [ ] Copy two: a separate drive at home, or a second cloud.
- [ ] Copy three: kept off site, such as a drive at a relative's house or a second cloud.
- [ ] I will check a few files each year to make sure they open.
- [ ] I replace external drives every few years rather than waiting for them to fail.

What to watch for:

- A cloud account can lapse if a payment fails or if the owner dies and the card stops. Photos may then be deleted after a time set by the provider.
- Phone "sync" is not a backup. A deleted photo can vanish everywhere.
- File formats age. Convert odd formats to common ones such as JPEG or MP4 when you can.
- Old tapes and film degrade. Digitize the ones you care about.

## Part 3: Who inherits what

Photos are property, but they are also emotional. Fights over them can be sharper than fights over money. Decide on purpose.

- **Person who keeps the master archive:** ____________________
- **Backup person:** ____________________
- **Who receives copies (not originals):** ____________________
- **Items I want to go to a specific person:** ____________________
- **Photos or videos I want deleted or kept private:** ____________________
- **Paper items (albums, letters) and who gets them:** ____________________

Consider the tie-breakers. Some families give one person the originals and make copies for everyone else. This avoids splitting a collection. Others hold a family meeting and let people choose in rounds.

Copyright is a separate issue. In general, the person who took a photo owns the copyright, and it can pass through the estate. Sharing copies in the family is normally fine, but if you want to publish an archive or a book, ask an attorney.

## Part 4: Instruction page

Write this and keep it with your letter of instruction.

- **Where the archive lives:** ____________________
- **Account email and cloud service:** ____________________
- **Where passwords are kept (not the passwords):** ____________________
- **How the folders are organized:** ____________________
- **Who is allowed to access it:** ____________________
- **What I want done with it:** ____________________
- **Photos that identify people (names of people in old pictures):** ____________________

Names are the part families lose first. Spend one afternoon with a parent or grandparent and write names on the back of prints or in the file names. That small step makes the collection far more valuable to the next generation.

## Part 5: Social media and shared albums

- [ ] I decided whether my accounts should be kept up, memorialized or closed.
- [ ] I set a legacy contact where the platform offers one (see the [Online Accounts Legacy Contacts Checklist](/free/online-accounts-legacy-contacts-checklist)).
- [ ] I downloaded the posts and pictures I want kept.
- [ ] I told shared album members who will manage the album later.

## Part 6: Simple naming system

- Folder per year, then per event: 2024 / 2024-07 Beach trip.
- Keep the original, and put edited versions in a subfolder.
- Add a short text file in each year folder listing people and places.

## Part 7: Put it in your plan

- [ ] My will or trust says who receives digital photos and family records.
- [ ] My executor is allowed to access cloud accounts and devices.
- [ ] I stored the instruction page where the right person can find it.
- [ ] I will review this planner once a year.

## When to talk to an attorney

- Siblings might disagree on who gets originals.
- Your photos or recordings have commercial value or copyright concerns.
- Family records involve a business, a famous person or an adopted child's records.
- You want a trust to hold the archive or pay for its upkeep.
- A cloud company will not release an account to your executor.
