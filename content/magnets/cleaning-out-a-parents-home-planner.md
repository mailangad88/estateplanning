## How to use this planner

We are sorry for your loss. Clearing a parent's home is physical work, and it is also a lot to feel. There is no deadline for the feelings. There may be a deadline for the house, such as rent, a sale or a lease. Go slowly where you can. Do not throw anything away or give anything away until you have read the first section, and until the person with legal authority says it is fine. Before an executor is appointed, rules about who may remove property vary, so ask an attorney if you are unsure.

## Step 1: Agree on ground rules

Most family arguments about a home come from a missing agreement, not from greed.

- [ ] Decide who is in charge of the clearing, usually the executor
- [ ] Tell every sibling and close relative the plan and the dates
- [ ] Agree that nothing is removed without being written in the log
- [ ] Agree that valuable items are not taken early, even by agreement
- [ ] Take photos of each room before touching anything

## Step 2: Look for the important things first

Look here before anything is donated or discarded. Paper is often hidden in ordinary places.

- [ ] Original will, trust papers, and powers of attorney
- [ ] Deeds, car titles, and mortgage papers
- [ ] Bank, brokerage and retirement statements
- [ ] Insurance policies, including life insurance and funeral policies
- [ ] Tax returns from recent years and unfiled returns
- [ ] Birth, marriage, military and Social Security papers
- [ ] Safe deposit box key or a bank notice about one
- [ ] Digital devices, passwords and phones. See [digital accounts after death](/free/digital-accounts-after-death-checklist)
- [ ] Cash, coins, gift cards, checks never deposited

Common hiding places: desk drawers, freezers, books, coat pockets, mattresses, tool boxes, filing cabinets, and under the stairs. Check pockets of coats and the lining of bags.

Put everything you find in one labelled box and give the executor an inventory.

## Step 3: Set aside valuables

Not everything valuable looks valuable.

| Category | Examples | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Jewelry | Rings, watches, loose stones | Photograph, then appraisal |
| Art and collectibles | Paintings, coins, stamps, dolls | Appraisal before any sale |
| Firearms | Handguns, rifles | Ask an attorney and local law enforcement about legal transfer. Do not mail them |
| Medication | Prescriptions and controlled drugs | Use a take-back program |
| Vehicles | Cars, boats, trailers | See the [vehicle transfer checklist](/free/vehicle-transfer-after-death-checklist) |
| Sentimental items | Photos, letters, recipes | Scan before dividing |

## Step 4: Make a room-by-room plan

Work one room at a time. Use five piles, with labels on the floor or on boxes.

1. **Keep:** belongs to the family
2. **Share:** goes into the keepsake round (see Step 5)
3. **Sell:** goes to a dealer, online or an [estate sale](/free/estate-sale-planner)
4. **Donate:** goes to a charity
5. **Discard:** recycling and trash

Tackle paper-heavy rooms like the office last. Documents deserve more time than furniture.

## Step 5: Divide keepsakes fairly

A rotation avoids fights.

- Each sibling lists, privately, the five things they most want.
- Anything only one person wants goes to that person.
- Where two people want the same item, take turns choosing, starting with the oldest or by a draw.
- If the will says who gets a certain item, follow the will first.
- Items of real value are treated as part of the estate, not as keepsakes.

Photos can be scanned so everyone gets a copy. If feelings run high, ask a neutral person to run the process. See [when heirs disagree](/free/when-heirs-disagree-guide).

## Step 6: Donate and dispose

- [ ] Ask charities what they accept, and book pickups
- [ ] Ask for a written receipt listing items donated
- [ ] Shred papers with account numbers or Social Security numbers
- [ ] Remove personal data from computers and phones before donating or recycling
- [ ] Hire a hauler for heavy items and ask for a quote in writing
- [ ] Dispose of paint, chemicals and batteries through local programs

## Step 8: Final walk-through

- [ ] Check every cupboard, closet and attic corner
- [ ] Look behind and under large furniture
- [ ] Confirm the utilities, locks and insurance
- [ ] Take final photos
- [ ] Return keys and sign off with the landlord, buyer or agent

If the house is to be sold, read the [selling a house in probate checklist](/free/selling-a-house-in-probate-checklist).

## When to talk to an attorney

- No executor has been appointed yet and someone wants to start clearing
- You find a will, trust or other paper you do not understand
- Siblings disagree about who gets valuable items
- You find firearms, large amounts of cash or unusual assets
- The house must be cleared quickly because of a lease or sale deadline
- You suspect that someone has already taken things
