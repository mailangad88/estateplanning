## How to use this guide

Illinois follows the federal rules for special needs trusts, then adds its own agencies, forms and courts. This guide covers what is Illinois-specific and what to bring to a lawyer. Other states differ, so a family that moves should have the plan reviewed. Where a figure or rule needs checking, you will see a marker.

## Trust rules in Illinois

Illinois has no separate statute that creates special needs trusts. They are written under the Illinois Trust Code and the federal Medicaid law. A first-party trust must be set up before the person turns 65, and pays back HFS at death. <!-- verify: Illinois HFS payback and age 65 handling --> A third-party trust funded by parents or others generally does not repay.

Illinois courts can supervise guardians. A trust is private unless a dispute reaches court.

## Pooled trusts in Illinois

Illinois has nonprofit pooled trusts. They can suit a family without a trustee or a person with a smaller amount. Ask each one about fees, minimum deposits, what happens at death, and whether they accept people over 65. <!-- verify: current list of Illinois pooled trusts and their rules -->

## PUNS and waiver waiting lists

Illinois offers home and community services through Medicaid waivers. The state keeps the PUNS list so that families register a person's needs. How long a person waits depends on urgency and funding. No honest figure fits everyone, so ask DHS or the local Independent Service Coordination agency for current estimates. <!-- verify: current PUNS wait information -->

- [ ] Register on PUNS if not done
- [ ] Update PUNS if needs change

## Medicaid look-back and transfers

Medicaid reviews transfers made within 60 months before applying for long-term care. <!-- verify: Illinois look-back period for this program --> Transfers to a trust for the sole benefit of a disabled person under age 65 are often exempt from the penalty, but strict rules apply. Moving a house or savings to a disabled child can fall under similar exceptions. Do not move assets before asking. See /tools/medicaid-lookback-date.

## What the work costs

Costs vary by firm and by complexity. No honest single number fits every family, so this table shows what drives it.

| Cost driver | What raises it |
|---|---|
| Drafting a third-party trust | Complex family, real estate, multiple beneficiaries |
| First-party or pooled trust | Court or agency review, settlement terms |
| Funding the trust | Retitling accounts, deeds, beneficiary forms |
| Annual upkeep | Tax returns, accounting, trustee fees, bank fees |
| Minimum to start | Depends on the trust type; some pooled trusts have minimums, and a third-party trust can start with little <!-- verify: pooled trust minimums --> |

Ask any lawyer for a written fee quote that lists what is included. See /free/estate-plan-cost-guide.

## Do you need a lawyer? Online option?

A form from the internet may name the right words, but a special needs trust fails quietly if it is wrong. The money may count against SSI or Medicaid, and the person finds out after benefits stop. Families commonly work with a lawyer for the document and use online tools only for storing information. This is general education, not a rule.

## Planning attorney vs financial planner

| | Planning attorney | Financial planner for special needs |
|---|---|---|
| Drafts the trust and wills | Yes | No |
| Advises on benefits law | Yes | Some |
| Projects lifetime costs, invests | Sometimes | Yes |
| Can represent in court | Yes | No |

The best setup is often both, with a letter of intent as the shared document. See /free/letter-of-intent-special-needs-template.

## How to find a lawyer in Illinois

1. Ask your local ARC chapter or a disability advocacy group
2. Ask your Independent Service Coordination agency
3. Check the Illinois State Bar Association or county bar referral line
4. Ask whether the lawyer handles special needs trusts, benefits and guardianship regularly
5. Interview two before choosing

## Prepare before the meeting

- [ ] Diagnosis and doctor names
- [ ] Benefits list: SSI, SSDI, Medicaid, waiver, housing
- [ ] Copy of current will or trust
- [ ] Account and property list
- [ ] Draft letter of intent
- [ ] Names of possible trustees and guardians
- [ ] Your goals and fears in two sentences

## Questions to ask

- How many special needs trusts have you drafted?
- Will you review my current documents first?
- How do you handle funding and retitling?
- What will annual upkeep involve?
- What happens if the trustee dies or resigns?
- What are your fees and what is included?
- Do you work with a tax adviser and a financial planner?

## When to talk to an attorney

- Your child has benefits and you have no trust
- You hold assets in your child's name
- You want to move a house or savings and are worried about Medicaid
- You moved to Illinois from another state
- A settlement or inheritance is coming
