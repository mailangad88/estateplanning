## How to use this guide

Form 706 is the federal estate tax return. Many families never file one. Some should file even when no tax is due. Use the filing test, mark the dates on the calendar, then use the records table. Figures change. Check the IRS instructions for the year of death. This is general education, not tax or legal advice.

## Filing test

| Situation | Form 706 |
|---|---|
| Gross estate plus certain gifts exceed the federal filing threshold for the year of death (about $15 million per person in 2026) <!-- verify: 2026 basic exclusion amount --> | Required |
| Estate is below the threshold, spouse survived, and the couple may want to keep the unused exemption | Optional: file to elect portability |
| Estate is below the threshold, no surviving spouse | Usually not needed |
| Illinois resident with an estate above the Illinois threshold | Illinois return may be due even if the federal return is not |

Illinois has its own estate tax with a much lower exclusion than the federal one, and no portability for state purposes. See the [Illinois estate tax guide](/free/illinois-estate-tax-guide).

## What portability does

Portability lets a surviving spouse add the late spouse's unused federal exemption to their own. It is not automatic. The estate has to file a complete, timely Form 706 and elect it. A couple whose combined estate is well below the exemption may not need it today, but laws and values change.

| Choice | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Elect portability | Keeps unused exemption for the survivor, costs less than a bypass trust to set up | Does not shield growth, does not apply to the GST exemption, and the return must be prepared |
| Skip it | Saves return costs | Unused exemption is lost |

Portability does not carry over the generation-skipping transfer exemption. Only the late spouse's own estate can use that. If the survivor remarries and the new spouse also dies, the exemption of the last deceased spouse is the one that counts.

## Deadlines

1. **Nine months after death:** the Form 706 and any tax are due.
2. **Extension:** an automatic six-month extension to file is available by filing Form 4768 before the due date. It extends time to file, not time to pay.
3. **Late portability:** Revenue Procedure 2022-32 allows a simplified late election if the return is filed by the fifth anniversary of the death and the estate was not otherwise required to file. <!-- verify: Rev. Proc. 2022-32 conditions -->
4. **Penalties:** filing late or paying late adds penalties and interest unless there is reasonable cause.

## Who prepares it

The executor signs. Preparation can be done by the executor, an attorney or a CPA with estate tax experience. Doing it yourself is possible for a simple estate, but valuation schedules, elections and the portability worksheet are easy to get wrong. Cost depends on how many assets need appraisals and how complex the estate is. Ask for a flat-fee quote and what it includes.

## Who pays

The estate pays any federal estate tax, from estate assets. The executor can be personally liable if the estate is distributed before the tax is paid. Heirs who receive property may be liable to a degree. Without a will, the same rules apply: the administrator handles it, and state law decides who inherits.

## Paying over time and other elections

| Election | What it does | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| Section 6166 | Defers part of the tax for a closely held business interest, with annual payments over a period of years, interest applies | IRC section 6166 <!-- verify: eligibility threshold of 35 percent of adjusted gross estate --> |
| Section 2032 alternate valuation | Values assets six months after death, only if it lowers both the gross estate and the tax | IRC section 2032 |
| Special use valuation | Values farms and closely held business real estate at their use, not highest and best use | IRC section 2032A |
| Installment on reasonable cause | Extension of time to pay | Form 4768 |

Alternate valuation applies to the whole estate, not asset by asset.

## Form 8971

If Form 706 is required, the executor also sends Form 8971 to the IRS and a Schedule A to each beneficiary. It reports the final estate tax value so heirs can use it as their basis. Filing a Form 706 only to elect portability does not trigger the same requirement.

## Valuation and records

| Asset | What to get |
|---|---|
| House | Appraisal by a licensed appraiser as of date of death |
| Closely held business | Business valuation report |
| IRA or 401(k) | Statement as of date of death |
| Art and collectibles | Qualified appraisal |
| Cryptocurrency | Date-of-death price from a documented source |
| Life insurance | Form 712 from the insurer |

Keep these for as long as the return could be examined and as long as heirs hold the assets.

- [ ] Death certificate
- [ ] Will and any trusts
- [ ] Prior gift tax returns (Form 709)
- [ ] Account statements as of date of death
- [ ] Appraisals
- [ ] Debts, funeral costs, legal and administration costs for deductions
- [ ] Marital and charitable deduction proof

## Common mistakes

- Assuming no return is needed because the estate looks small
- Missing the late portability window
- Distributing assets before checking tax
- Treating the extension to file as an extension to pay

## When to talk to an attorney

- The estate or the gifts made during life are near the exemption
- There is a closely held business or a farm
- A spouse died and the survivor is unsure about portability
- The estate has trouble paying the tax
- A beneficiary is not a U.S. citizen
