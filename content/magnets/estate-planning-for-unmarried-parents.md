**How to use this guide.** Marriage gives a couple a bundle of legal rights without any paperwork. Unmarried parents get some of those rights only through documents and legal steps. Work through the parts below. Rules vary by state, and an Illinois example is given only where it helps.

## Part 1: Is each parent legally recognized?

A child's legal parent matters for inheritance, benefits, health decisions and custody. Mothers are generally recognized at birth. For fathers who are not married to the mother, recognition often requires a step.

- [ ] A signed acknowledgment of paternity, usually offered at the hospital or through a state agency
- [ ] A court order establishing parentage
- [ ] An adoption order, including a second-parent or stepparent adoption where available
- [ ] For same-sex couples or those using assisted reproduction, the state's parentage rules or a court judgment of parentage

An acknowledgment signed in one state is generally respected in others, but check the form. A birth certificate listing a name may not by itself prove legal parentage in every state.

- **Child's legal parents confirmed by:** ______________________
- **Document and date:** ______________________
- **Where the original is:** ______________________

Illinois, for example, has a voluntary acknowledgment of paternity process through the state agency, and the form is usually signed at the hospital. Other states have equivalents.

## Part 2: What happens if one parent dies

Surviving parents with legal parentage are generally the child's guardian. A father who was never legally established may need a court to confirm his role, and a court may be asked to decide. A non-parent partner, such as a stepparent, has no automatic rights.

Choose guardians in case both parents die, and write down why.

- **Guardian:** ______________________
- **Backup guardian:** ______________________
- **Reason:** ______________________
- **Has this person agreed?** ______________________
- **Do both parents agree?** ______________________

Use the [choosing a guardian worksheet](/checklists/choosing-a-guardian-worksheet) and read [guardianship for minor children](/guides/guardianship-for-minor-children). If parents disagree about a guardian, it is better to find out now.

## Part 3: How children inherit

Children generally inherit from a parent under intestacy rules if parentage is established. A child of an unmarried father may need proof of paternity to inherit from him. A will with a clear gift avoids doubt.

Also:

- A minor cannot manage property directly. A court-supervised arrangement may be needed unless you name an adult to manage the money, or set up a trust. See [leaving money to minors](/guides/leaving-money-to-minors).
- Life insurance and retirement accounts pass by beneficiary form, so naming a child directly can cause the same problem. Name a trust or a custodian under your state's Uniform Transfers to Minors Act, with an age you choose.
- Child support obligations and life insurance are often linked in separation agreements.

## Part 4: What a partner does not get automatically

Unmarried partners usually have no automatic right to:

- Inherit if there is no will
- Make medical decisions, unless named, though some states list a close friend or partner among surrogates
- Serve as executor
- Receive Social Security survivor benefits (though a child may qualify for benefits)
- Claim spousal exemptions from the federal estate tax or inheritance tax
- Stay in the home, if it is owned by the other partner
- Make funeral decisions

Also consider that some benefits can be claimed by the children, not the partner.

| Gap | Document or step that helps |
|---|---|
| Inheritance | Will or trust naming partner |
| Medical decisions | Health care power of attorney |
| Money decisions during incapacity | Financial power of attorney |
| Shared home | Joint ownership with survivorship, or a trust, with agreement on what happens |
| Funeral and remains | Written authorization where state law allows |
| Account access | Joint accounts or beneficiary designations |
| Children's care | Guardian named in will, plus agreement between parents |

If one partner owns the home and the other contributes, the contribution may not give the partner any rights. A written agreement or a deed change can help. Some states allow a cohabitation agreement. Gift and tax rules apply when one partner puts the other on a deed, and transfers to a non-spouse do not get the marital deduction. Ask a lawyer before retitling.

## Part 6: Your weekend checklist

- [ ] Confirm parentage paperwork for each parent
- [ ] Choose a guardian and a backup
- [ ] Sign a will naming guardian, executor and a plan for children's money
- [ ] Sign powers of attorney for finances and health care, naming each other if that is your wish
- [ ] Review every beneficiary form
- [ ] Consider term life insurance for each parent, with a trust or custodian for the child
- [ ] Decide what happens to the home
- [ ] Write a letter of instruction that includes the child's routines, doctors and school
- [ ] Tell the guardian where documents are
- [ ] Set a reminder to review after any move, birth or change in the relationship

## If the relationship ends

Estate plans should be updated after separation. Named partners remain in forms until you change them. Parentage and custody orders do not change when the relationship ends, so keep paperwork current. A court order for custody or support may also affect who can serve as guardian.

## Questions to discuss together

- Would I want my partner to be guardian if I died and the other parent were unable?
- What if the other parent and my partner disagree?
- How much should go to our child and when?
- Who handles money for the child until then?
- What do we want to happen to our home?

## When to talk to an attorney

- Parentage has not been legally established for one parent.
- You are in a dispute over custody or guardianship.
- One partner owns the home and the other does not.
- You have children from other relationships.
- You or your partner is not a citizen, or lives in another state.
