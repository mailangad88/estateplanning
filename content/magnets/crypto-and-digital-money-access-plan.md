**How to use this planner.** Fill it in once, store it somewhere safe and separate from your keys, and tell your executor where it is. It lists what you own and how to proceed. It never contains passwords, seed phrases or private keys.

## The problem to solve

Crypto held in your own wallet can only be moved by someone with the private key or recovery phrase. If no one has it, the coins can be lost for good. Crypto held on an exchange is easier: the exchange holds it and will usually deal with an executor who has court papers. Your plan needs to cover both, and to avoid creating a second risk: a note anyone could use to take the money.

Prices swing, so your executor also needs to know the date of death value matters for taxes. Keep that in mind when you read the executor steps below.

The email and phone matter. Many exchanges send codes to them, so if those die with you, the account can be hard to reach.

- **Person who should handle the crypto:** ____________________
- **Backup person:** ____________________
- **Do they understand crypto?** ____________________
- **Will they be named as executor, or work for the executor?** ____________________

Naming someone to help does not give them legal power. Legal authority comes from your will or trust and, for a will, the court. Say in your will or trust that your executor may access digital assets, and that you want them to hire a technical helper if needed.

## Step 3: Pick how recovery information is protected

Never put a seed phrase in your will. A will becomes public after probate in many places. Never email, text or photograph recovery words either.

| Method | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Written phrase in a fireproof safe or safe deposit box | Hardware wallets | The executor needs a way to open the box |
| Metal backup plate in a sealed envelope | Fire and water damage | Needs a trusted holder |
| Split storage (parts in two places) | Reducing theft risk | Easy to lose one part |
| Password manager emergency access | Exchange logins | Does not replace wallet recovery words |
| Multisignature wallet with a trusted co-signer | Large holdings | Complex, so get help setting it up |

- **Where the recovery information lives:** ____________________
- **Who knows where it is:** ____________________
- **How the executor can legally reach it (for example, the safe deposit box is jointly held or named in the will):** ____________________

If you are not sure which method suits you, pick the simplest one you will actually maintain.

## Step 4: Write the access instructions

Put these in a document separate from your will. A letter of instruction works well. See the [Letter of Instruction Outline](/checklists/letter-of-instruction-outline).

- **Summary:** "I own crypto in the places listed on page 1."
- **First action I want taken:** ____________________
- **What to do with hardware wallets (do not throw out old devices):** ____________________
- **Wallet apps on my phone and computer (do not wipe devices until checked):** ____________________
- **Whether I want holdings kept, sold or divided in kind:** ____________________
- **Who is allowed to see the recovery information:** ____________________

## Step 5: Executor steps after a death

Give your executor this order.

- [ ] Get certified copies of the death certificate and court letters, if probate is required.
- [ ] Secure phones, computers and hardware wallets. Do not delete apps or reset anything.
- [ ] Find exchange accounts using the inventory and email searches.
- [ ] Contact each exchange with court papers. Do not log in as the deceased unless the terms allow it.
- [ ] Record the dollar value of each holding on the date of death for tax reporting.
- [ ] Do not trade, send or "test" a wallet before talking to an attorney or tax preparer.
- [ ] Beware of "recovery" services that ask for upfront fees or phrases. Scams target grieving families.
- [ ] Keep records of every transfer, including the dates and amounts.

Crypto is generally treated as property for federal tax purposes. Heirs often receive a basis equal to the date of death value, but the details depend on how the asset was held. Confirm with a tax professional.

## Step 6: Check yourself

- [ ] Someone other than me knows this plan exists.
- [ ] I could pass a test: if I disappeared, could my executor find every exchange account?
- [ ] My will or trust gives my executor or trustee digital asset authority.
- [ ] I will update this plan every year or after any new wallet.

## When to talk to an attorney

- Your holdings are large or spread over many wallets and platforms.
- You want crypto held in a trust or to go to different people.
- You hold crypto in a business or partnership.
- You want to name a trusted co-signer or share access during your life.
- Your state's digital asset law is unclear to you.
