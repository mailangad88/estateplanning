**How to use this cheat sheet.** Print the page for each document you plan to sign. Line up your witnesses and notary before signing day, then tick the signing-day list at the end. Illinois signing rules are strict and a missed step can make a document ineffective. This is educational, and an Illinois attorney can confirm what applies to you.

## Will

- **Who must be present:** You, and at least two witnesses who watch you sign. You need to be 18 or older and of sound mind and memory.
- **Witnesses:** Two or more credible witnesses sign in your presence. Choose adults who receive nothing under the will. A witness who is a beneficiary, or married to one, can lose the gift to the extent it exceeds what that person would get without the will.
- **Notary:** Not required. A witness attestation clause lets the court admit the will without calling the witnesses. Attorneys often add a notarized witness affidavit as well.
- **Special rules:** Handwritten wills without two witnesses are not valid in Illinois. Your spouse can still renounce the will and take one-third of the estate if you leave descendants, or one-half if you do not.
- **Where to store the original:** In a fireproof place your executor can reach, or with your attorney. A person holding a will must file it with the circuit court clerk of the county where the deceased lived within 30 days after learning of the death. Illinois also lets you deposit a will with the clerk for safekeeping, so ask the clerk about the process and fee.<!-- verify -->
- **Fill in:** Original is kept at ____________________

## Statutory short form power of attorney for property

- **Who must be present:** You, at least one witness and a notary public, together.
- **Witnesses:** The form says the power of attorney is not effective unless at least one witness signs and your signature is notarized. The witness cannot be your agent or a successor agent. The witness cannot be your parent, sibling or descendant, or the spouse of any of them, or the same relatives of any agent. The witness cannot be your attending physician or mental health provider or a relative of either. The witness cannot be an owner or operator of a health care facility where you live, or a relative of one.
- **Notary:** Required. The notary cannot also serve as the witness.
- **Special rules:** Choose your agent and a successor with care, because the agent can reach your money. Banks sometimes ask for a copy of the power of attorney and may ask to review it first.
- **Where to store the original:** Somewhere your agent can find it. Give copies to your agent and your financial institutions. If you use it for real estate, ask the county recorder about recording a copy.
- **Fill in:** Agent is ____________________ Successor is ____________________

## Power of attorney for health care

- **Who must be present:** You and one witness who is 18 or older and sees you sign, or hears you confirm the signature is yours.
- **Witnesses:** The witness cannot be your agent or a successor agent. The witness cannot be related to you, your agent or a successor agent by blood, marriage or adoption. The witness cannot be your physician, advanced practice nurse, dentist, podiatrist, optometrist or psychologist, or a relative of any of them. The witness cannot be an owner or operator of the facility where you are a patient or resident, or a relative of one.
- **Notary:** Not required.
- **Special rules:** Electronic signatures are allowed under the Act. Talk with your agent about your wishes first.
- **Where to store the original:** Keep the original at home, not in a safe deposit box. Give copies to your agent, your doctor and the hospital you use.
- **Fill in:** Agent is ____________________ Doctor given a copy on ____________________

## Living will

- **Who must be present:** You and two witnesses who are 18 or older. Someone else may sign for you at your direction while you watch.
- **Witnesses:** Each witness sees you sign or hears you confirm the signature. Avoid anyone who would inherit from you, anyone named in your documents, anyone responsible for paying for your care and your attending physician.<!-- verify -->
- **Notary:** Not required.
- **Special rules:** A living will speaks to life-prolonging treatment. It does not name an agent, so most people sign it with the health care power of attorney.
- **Where to store the original:** At home. Give copies to your agent, your physician and your hospital.
- **Fill in:** Witnesses are ____________________

## HIPAA authorization

- **Who must be present:** You. Federal law does not require witnesses for this form.
- **Witnesses:** Not required by federal law, though some hospitals ask for one. Many people sign it alongside the health care power of attorney.<!-- verify -->
- **Notary:** Not required.
- **Special rules:** Name each person who may see records and say how long the permission lasts. Many Illinois health care forms already include this language.
- **Where to store the original:** With your health care documents. Give copies to doctors and to the people named.
- **Fill in:** People named are ____________________

## Transfer on death instrument for a home

- **Who must be present:** You, at least two witnesses and a notary public. You need the same mental capacity as for a will.
- **Witnesses:** Two or more credible witnesses sign to say you executed the instrument in their presence, voluntarily and appearing of sound mind. Choose adults who will not receive the home.
- **Notary:** Required. The witnesses' signatures and yours are acknowledged before a notary.
- **Special rules:** The instrument must be recorded with the recorder of the county where the property sits before you die, or it does not work. In Cook County, recording goes through the Cook County Clerk's Recordings Division.<!-- verify --> Revoking it later also takes a recorded instrument, not a note on the original or a will provision. It covers residential property of one to four units.
- **Where to store the original:** The recorder returns a recorded copy. Keep it with your will and tell the person named where to find it.
- **Fill in:** Recorded on ____________________ County ____________________ Document number ____________________

## Guardian nomination for minor children

- **Who must be present:** You and two witnesses. If you place the nomination in your will, follow the will rules.
- **Witnesses:** Two or more credible witnesses, 18 or older. Neither can be the person you name as guardian.
- **Notary:** Not required.
- **Special rules:** A court still approves a guardian, and it looks at the child's interests. Name a backup, and talk to the people you pick first.
- **Where to store the original:** With the will. Give a copy to the guardian.
- **Fill in:** Guardian is ____________________ Backup is ____________________

## Signing-day checklist

- [ ] Names and spellings match your photo ID on every document.
- [ ] Two witnesses are lined up for the will, living will, guardian nomination and transfer on death instrument.
- [ ] One witness is lined up for each power of attorney, with no relatives, agents or doctors.
- [ ] A notary public is booked for the property power of attorney and transfer on death instrument.
- [ ] No witness or notary is named in the documents as a beneficiary or agent.
- [ ] You sign each document where the form directs, with witnesses watching.
- [ ] Every page is complete and no blank lines are left that someone could fill in later.
- [ ] You collected the originals and made copies for agents and doctors.
- [ ] The transfer on death instrument is on its way to the county recorder, with the fee.
- [ ] You wrote down where the originals are kept and told your executor and agents.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You are unsure whether a witness qualifies.
- You own a home and want to record a transfer on death instrument.
- You have a blended family or a child with a disability.
- You are signing for a parent who may have lost capacity.
- Your documents came from another state or a template.
