**How to use this guide.** Read the powers table, then walk the fallback ladder and fill in your own choices at the end. Guardianship law is state law. Illinois is the example here, and other states differ. A named guardian is a nomination that a court must approve, so the choice is a request, not an order. This is general education, not legal advice.

## What a guardian of a child decides

| Area | Usually the guardian's call? | Common limits |
|---|---|---|
| Where the child lives | Yes | Court oversight, and moves may need notice or approval |
| School | Yes | Court can step in if a choice harms the child |
| Religion | Often yes | Parents can state wishes in a letter, which a court may weigh |
| Medical care | Yes, routine and emergency | Some major decisions may need court approval |
| Daily rules and discipline | Yes | Must stay within the law |
| Managing the child's money | Only if appointed guardian of the estate | Reports and a bond are common |

Guardian of the person raises the child. Guardian of the estate manages money and property for the child. One person can hold both, or you can split the roles. Many parents name different people when they want a check on money management.

## Guardianship, custody and adoption

| Feature | Guardianship | Custody | Adoption |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who has it | A non-parent appointed by a court | A parent, set in a family case | A new legal parent |
| Parents' rights | Parents' rights can continue | Parents' rights continue | Ends birth parents' rights |
| Ends when | Child turns 18, or by court order | Child turns 18, or by order | Permanent |
| Inheritance rights from the guardian | No automatic right | Not applicable | Child inherits like a birth child |
| Court review | Yes | Yes | Final decree |

Guardianship is not adoption. A child under guardianship stays the legal child of the parents.

## Surviving parent, ex and unfit parent

If one parent dies and the other is alive and fit, the surviving parent generally has the right to raise the child. A guardian named in a will usually matters only when both parents have died or the survivor cannot serve. See Illinois law at 755 ILCS 5/11-5. <!-- verify: Illinois rules for a guardian named in a will when one parent survives -->

| Situation | What generally happens |
|---|---|
| Other parent alive and fit | Child lives with that parent, guardian named in your will is not used |
| Other parent alive, you worry about fitness | Court can decide what is best for the child, based on evidence |
| Parents are divorced, you hold custody and die | The other parent often has the first claim |
| Other parent has lost rights or is absent | The guardian you named can ask the court to act |

You cannot simply write a will to cut an ex out of a child's life. A court looks at the child's best interests. Naming a guardian does not remove a living parent's rights. If you have real concerns, such as abuse or neglect, keep records and ask an attorney about custody orders now, since a will cannot fix this.

## Best interests of the child

Courts weigh facts about the child and the caregiver. Illinois courts use a list of factors, and judges have room to decide. <!-- verify: Illinois best interest factors for guardianship of a minor -->

Common things a court looks at:

- The child's age, needs and bond with the person
- Stability of the home and income
- Health and safety of the adults in the home
- Wishes of the parents, and of an older child
- Keeping siblings together
- The proposed guardian's history and ability

## If the first choice cannot serve

3. **Third guardian:** ______________________
4. **Guardian of the estate, if different:** ______________________

If the first choice says no, dies or becomes unable, the will's backup takes the role. If all named people decline, the court appoints someone, often a relative who steps forward. If no relative can serve, the court can use another suitable adult or a public or professional guardian. Ask each person if they will serve, and ask again every few years.

## Pay, oversight and removal

- **Pay:** A guardian of the person is generally not paid for raising a child, but the child's support can come from the child's funds, insurance or trust. A guardian of the estate may be paid reasonable fees from the estate, with court approval. <!-- verify: Illinois guardian compensation rules -->
- **Reports:** Court-supervised guardians usually file reports on the child and on money.
- **Removal:** A court can remove a guardian who neglects or mistreats the child or mismanages money. Anyone with an interest can ask.

Money held in a trust avoids many of these steps. See [trust for minor children planner](/free/trust-for-minor-children-planner).

## Moving, school and religion

A guardian may want to move across state lines or change schools. Courts often require notice or approval for moves. A letter of wishes can say where you hope the child will go to school or worship, and a judge may consider it, but it is not binding in most states. Use the [letter to your children's guardian template](/free/letter-to-your-childrens-guardian-template).

## Common mistakes

- Naming a guardian without asking
- No backup
- Assuming the will controls a living parent's rights
- Naming a couple and then divorce or death
- Putting a large sum in the guardian's hands with no oversight

## When to talk to an attorney

- The other parent is a concern
- You have a child with special needs
- You want different guardians of the person and estate
- You live apart from your children's other parent
- You are a single parent with no obvious choice
