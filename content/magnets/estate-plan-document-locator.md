**How to use this workbook.** Start with Page 1 and finish it in one sitting. Then fill in one page a week. This organizer is not a legal document and does not replace a will, trust or power of attorney.

## Read this first

**Security rule.** This organizer will hold sensitive information. Do not write full account numbers, passwords, PINs or crypto seed phrases here. Write where they live instead, for example "password manager, emergency kit in the fireproof box in the hall closet." Keep the finished organizer somewhere safe and locked, and tell your executor or successor trustee where it is. Do not email it or upload it to a public cloud folder. Do not store it only in a safe whose combination only you know.

**Update rhythm.** Review once a year, and after any major change: marriage, divorce, a move, a new account, a new child, or the death of someone named in your plan.

**Why it matters.** Families often spend months searching for accounts and policies. The NAIC and state treasuries offer locator tools for lost policies and accounts because people do lose track. See Sources.

Under the Revised Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act (RUFADAA, adopted in most states), your explicit settings in an online tool, such as Google's Inactive Account Manager or Apple's Legacy Contact, generally control access. Next comes what your will or trust says, then the provider's terms of service. Set the tools up. A will alone may not solve it.

## Page 11: Letter of instruction (non-binding)

Write in your own words. Prompts:

- **Who I want called first:** ______________________
- **Funeral or memorial wishes and music:** ______________________
- **Where to scatter or bury:** ______________________
- **Personal messages:** ______________________
- **Instructions about pets, home and belongings:** ______________________
- **What my family should know about accounts, debts or ongoing commitments:** ______________________
- **Who has permission to speak for the family:** ______________________
- **Photos or letters to find:** ______________________

This letter is not a legal document and does not override a will or trust. Do not use it to make gifts of property, since your will or trust governs distributions.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You discover there is no will, or the documents are more than five years old.
- A beneficiary on an account is someone you no longer want.
- A house is still in your name even though you have a trust on paper.
- You own a business with no succession plan.
- You want to bring the finished organizer to an attorney to speed up planning and keep costs down.
