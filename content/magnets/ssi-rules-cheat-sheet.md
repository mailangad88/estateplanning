## How to use this sheet

Benefit rules decide how a trust, a gift or a marriage plays out. Read the table first, then sort your family's assets. Figures change, so each one carries a check marker. This is general education, not advice about your case.

## SSI, SSDI and Medicaid side by side

| | SSI | SSDI | Medicaid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who runs it | Social Security Administration | Social Security Administration | State agency with federal funds |
| Based on | Need (low income and few resources) | Work history, or a parent's record for adult children | Need, plus program category |
| Asset limit | Yes | No | Yes for most categories |
| Health coverage | Often leads to Medicaid | Medicare after a waiting period | Itself |
| Family income counted? | For children under 18 at home | No | Depends on program |

SSI and SSDI are different programs even though one agency pays both. A person can receive both if SSDI is small. Medicaid is a separate application in some states. Rules vary by state, so ask your state agency.

## What SSI counts

The SSI resource limit is $2,000 for one person and $3,000 for a married couple. <!-- verify: current SSI resource limits as of 2026 --> The limit applies on the first moment of each month.

### Resource sorter

| Item | Counts toward the limit? |
|---|---|
| Checking and savings | Yes |
| Cash in a wallet or home | Yes |
| Stocks, bonds, extra property | Yes |
| The home the person lives in | No, if it is the principal residence |
| One vehicle used for transportation | No, any value <!-- verify: vehicle exclusion --> |
| Personal belongings and household goods | Generally no |
| Burial plot or funeral set-aside | Generally no, within limits |
| ABLE account balance | No up to a cap, over the cap it counts <!-- verify: ABLE SSI exclusion cap --> |
| Money in a properly written third-party special needs trust | Generally no |

### Income

Income is anything received in cash or in kind that helps meet food or shelter. Wages, gifts of cash, an inheritance in the month received, and shelter paid by others are the common ones. SSA excludes a small amount each month. <!-- verify: general income exclusion amount -->

## The deeming rule

For a child under 18 who lives at home, part of the parents' income and resources is counted as the child's. This is called deeming. It can reduce or block SSI for a child in a household with moderate income.

At 18, deeming usually stops. A new SSI application as an adult is then judged on the adult's own money. Many families apply in the months around the 18th birthday. See /free/adult-child-with-disability-turning-18-checklist.

## Marriage

SSI pays couples less than two individuals, and a spouse's income and resources count. People call this the marriage penalty.

Some people with disabilities keep benefits after marriage. Others lose SSI, or Medicaid. SSDI on a parent's record (DAC) can end at marriage, with exceptions. Check before the wedding.

- [ ] Ask SSA how the spouse's income and savings would count
- [ ] Ask whether Medicaid continues
- [ ] Ask if DAC benefits are affected, and if exceptions apply
- [ ] Update any special needs trust and ABLE account plans

## When a parent dies

An adult who became disabled before age 22 may receive **childhood disability benefits** (also called disabled adult child or DAC benefits) on a parent's Social Security record. The parent must be retired, disabled or deceased. <!-- verify: DAC eligibility rules and age of onset -->

If a parent dies, SSI for the adult may change because of lost support, new income or an inheritance. DAC benefits, if available, can replace SSI and may come with Medicare. Medicaid eligibility should be rechecked so coverage does not lapse.

## Disability definition

For SSI and many trust provisions, disability means the person cannot do substantial work because of a physical or mental condition expected to last at least a year or result in death. <!-- verify: SSA adult disability definition wording --> A first-party special needs trust under federal Medicaid law uses the SSA definition. A third-party trust usually lets the document define who qualifies.

## Applying for an adult child

1. Gather identity, birth proof, citizenship or immigration papers
2. Collect medical records, doctors, therapies and test results
3. List all income, bank accounts and where the person lives
4. Call SSA or start online, then schedule the interview
5. Keep copies of everything sent and the date
6. Appeal a denial within the stated deadline <!-- verify: SSA appeal deadline -->

## When to talk to an attorney

- A denial letter arrives and you are unsure about an appeal
- A marriage is planned and benefits are at stake
- A parent has died and DAC or SSI changes are unclear
- A trust or ABLE account is being set up
- The person has savings close to the limit
