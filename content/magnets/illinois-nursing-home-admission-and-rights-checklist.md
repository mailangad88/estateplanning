## How to use this checklist

Admission day is rushed. A stack of forms arrives while a hospital wants the bed. This checklist slows that moment down. Illinois nursing homes are licensed under the Nursing Home Care Act, 210 ILCS 45, and federal law adds resident rights. <!-- verify: 210 ILCS 45 is the Illinois Nursing Home Care Act --> Other states differ. This is general education, not legal advice.

## Step flow

1. Ask for the admission packet a day or more before signing, if possible.
2. Read each item in the table below.
3. Mark the lines you do not understand.
4. Ask the facility to explain in writing.
5. Sign only the parts that are required for admission.
6. Keep a full copy.

## What a facility can and cannot demand

| Issue | Plain answer |
|---|---|
| Guarantor | Federal law generally bars a nursing home that takes Medicare or Medicaid from requiring a third party to personally back the bill as a condition of admission. A family member can sign as the resident's agent without taking on a personal debt. |
| Children's liability | A child is not automatically responsible for a parent's bill. Illinois has an old filial responsibility law, which is rarely used. <!-- verify: 750 ILCS 65/15 and how courts apply it to nursing care --> |
| Advance directive | A facility must ask whether you have one. It may not require you to sign one as a condition of care. |
| Arbitration clause | Often optional. You can usually refuse it and still be admitted. |
| House | A nursing home cannot take a house for a debt without a court judgment. Medicaid estate recovery is a separate process after death. |
| Medicaid | A facility may be able to limit the number of Medicaid beds. Ask how many and whether a private pay resident who runs out of money keeps their bed. |
| Discharge | Residents have notice and appeal rights. Nonpayment can be a reason, but the process has steps. |

## Agreement review

- [ ] The resident is named as the responsible party, not a child personally
- [ ] You signed as "agent for" or "power of attorney for" the resident
- [ ] No personal-backing or "jointly liable" language
- [ ] Daily rate and what it includes are written down
- [ ] Extra charges are listed: laundry, therapy, supplies, bed hold
- [ ] Rate increase notice period is stated
- [ ] Policy if private pay money runs out is stated
- [ ] Policy on Medicaid conversion is stated
- [ ] Arbitration clause is marked optional or refused
- [ ] A copy of resident rights was given

## Private pay vs Medicaid

| | Private pay | Medicaid |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | The resident or family | The state, plus the resident's income less an allowance |
| Daily rate | Usually higher | Set by the state, usually lower |
| Room choice | Often more | Often shared room |
| Risk | Savings run down | Facility may have limited Medicaid beds |

Residents on Medicaid typically contribute most of their monthly income, such as Social Security and pensions, and keep a small personal needs allowance. <!-- verify: Illinois personal needs allowance amount -->

If you must pay privately while a Medicaid application is pending, ask the facility about pending-status billing. Keep payments in writing and keep records.

Ask each facility for its most recent inspection report. Also visit once at a mealtime and once on a weekend.

## Money while in care

- **Social Security:** Payment continues. Medicaid residents typically direct most of it to the facility. Spouses at home keep their own benefits.
- **Bank account:** The resident's account stays theirs. Large withdrawals will be looked at for Medicaid. See the transfer penalty workbook on this site.
- **House:** Stays in the resident's name unless transferred. Not seized by the facility.

## Reporting abuse or neglect in Illinois

1. In an emergency, call 911.
2. Report to the Illinois Department of Public Health nursing home hotline. <!-- verify: current IDPH complaint hotline number -->
3. Call the Illinois Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program through the Department on Aging. <!-- verify: ombudsman contact -->
4. For financial exploitation or abuse in a home or community setting, the Illinois Adult Protective Services program takes reports. <!-- verify: APS hotline number -->
5. Write down dates, names and what you saw. Take photos if allowed.
6. Do not wait for proof. The agencies investigate.

## Common mistakes

- [ ] Signing as a guarantor because the form says "responsible party"
- [ ] Not asking what happens when private pay money runs out
- [ ] Skipping the inspection report
- [ ] Waiting to apply for Medicaid until savings are almost gone
- [ ] Not keeping a copy of the agreement

## When to talk to an attorney

- The agreement contains a personal payment promise, arbitration clause or personal liability line
- You received a discharge notice
- A Medicaid application is pending and bills are building
- You suspect neglect and want to know your options
- A parent's house is involved
