## How to use this guide

A sudden illness can leave a hospital asking, "Who speaks for this patient?" Illinois has an answer if you did not choose someone, but it may not be who you would pick. This guide shows the default order, what changes if you sign a directive and what families do when a parent will not sign. It is general education about Illinois, and other states differ.

## The short version

| Your situation | Who usually decides |
|---|---|
| You signed a health care power of attorney | Your agent |
| You signed no document, but you have close family | A surrogate decision maker chosen by a statutory order |
| You have no one | A court guardian, or the hospital's ethics process |
| You signed a living will | Your written wishes guide care, and an agent or surrogate carries them out |

## Who decides with no agent: the Illinois order

The Illinois Health Care Surrogate Act (755 ILCS 40) sets an order of people who may make decisions for a patient who lacks decision making capacity and has no agent. <!-- verify: order of surrogates in 755 ILCS 40/25 --> In outline:

| Order | Person |
|---|---|
| 1 | A guardian of the person, if one exists |
| 2 | The patient's spouse or civil union partner |
| 3 | An adult child (all adult children, if more than one, by majority) |
| 4 | A parent |
| 5 | An adult sibling |
| 6 | An adult grandchild |
| 7 | A close friend |
| 8 | The guardian of the estate |

Check the statute for the current order and conditions. The Act may also set limits on certain decisions, such as end-of-life choices. <!-- verify: limits on surrogate authority for life-sustaining treatment -->

## Scenarios

| What happens | What usually follows |
|---|---|
| Coma or unconscious | Doctors look for an agent, then a surrogate. Urgent care starts regardless. |
| Stroke with no plan | Same process. A spouse usually leads. |
| No HIPAA release | Providers may share with those involved in care, but you may be refused details. Sign a release. |
| Hospital cannot reach family | The hospital searches, and may use an ethics committee or ask the court for a guardian. |
| No family at all | A friend may qualify under the Act. Otherwise a guardian or a public guardian. |
| Family disagrees | The surrogate order decides who controls. If they tie, the hospital may seek a court. |
| Spouse becomes incapacitated | You may act as surrogate for health. For money, you may need a power of attorney or a guardianship. |

## Money is separate

A health care agent does not manage bank accounts. Illinois uses a separate document, the statutory power of attorney for property. Without it, a family may need to ask a court to appoint a guardian of the estate. See [power of attorney vs guardianship](/learn/power-of-attorney/power-of-attorney-vs-guardianship) and the [Illinois power of attorney for property guide](/free/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property-guide).

## If a parent has dementia and no power of attorney

1. **Check capacity.** Ask the doctor whether the parent can still understand and sign. If yes, act soon.
2. **Gather what exists.** Look for old powers of attorney, trust documents, a living will.
3. **Use the surrogate order for health.** Doctors will use it without court.
4. **For money, consider guardianship.** A court appoints a guardian. It takes time and costs money.
5. **Plan the next stage.** See [dementia legal checklist](/free/dementia-diagnosis-legal-checklist).

## If a parent refuses to sign

| Reason they give | Gentle response |
|---|---|
| "I don't want to talk about dying" | Frame it as who speaks if they have a stroke |
| "You just want my money" | Name an outside agent or share it among siblings |
| "I already have a will" | A will does not cover medical decisions |
| "I'll do it later" | Offer a date and an appointment |
| "I don't trust the choice" | Let them pick the agent |

You cannot force an adult with capacity to sign. See the [aging parent conversation kit](/free/aging-parent-conversation-kit).

## When to talk to an attorney

- A family member has lost capacity and no document exists
- Relatives are fighting about treatment
- A guardianship may be needed
- A parent is signing under pressure
- You have no family or no trusted person to name
