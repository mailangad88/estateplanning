**How to use this workbook.** Get a three-ring binder, ten tab dividers and sheet protectors. Work through one tab per sitting. A binder does not replace your attorney's file, but it means someone in a crisis does not have to search drawers. Keep the original signed documents where your attorney or state rules advise, and put copies in the binder, marked "copy."

## The cover page

Print this and put it in front.

- **Name:** ____________________
- **Date binder last updated:** ____________________
- **Where original will and trust are kept:** ____________________
- **Attorney name and phone:** ____________________
- **Executor or successor trustee:** ____________________
- **Agent for property (power of attorney):** ____________________
- **Agent for health care:** ____________________
- **Who knows this binder exists:** ____________________
- **Location of the binder:** ____________________

## Tab 1: Legal documents

- [ ] Will (copy), with a note on where the original is
- [ ] Revocable trust and any amendments (copy)
- [ ] Durable power of attorney for property
- [ ] Health care power of attorney and living will
- [ ] HIPAA releases
- [ ] Prenuptial, postnuptial or divorce agreements that affect your plan

Add a sticky note on each with the date signed and the attorney who prepared it. See [how to make a will](/guides/how-to-make-a-will) for what makes the original valid.

## Tab 2: Health and care

- [ ] A list of doctors, medications and allergies
- [ ] Insurance cards and Medicare details (copies)
- [ ] Written wishes about treatment, organ donation and where you want care
- [ ] Contact list for the agent and family

## Tab 3: Money snapshot

- [ ] Account inventory with type, institution and who the beneficiary is
- [ ] Recent statements for one month, not years
- [ ] List of debts and monthly payments
- [ ] Safe deposit box location and who has access

Use the [asset and account inventory](/checklists/asset-and-account-inventory). Account numbers can stay off the page; list the last four digits only.

## Tab 4: Beneficiary forms

- [ ] A copy of each retirement and life insurance beneficiary confirmation
- [ ] A one-page table of beneficiaries by account
- [ ] Date each was last checked

| Account | Primary | Contingent | Last checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | |
| | | | |

See the [beneficiary designation audit](/checklists/beneficiary-designation-audit).

## Tab 5: Property and titles

- [ ] Deeds (copies) and where originals are recorded
- [ ] Vehicle titles
- [ ] Mortgage and home equity details
- [ ] Insurance policies for home, auto, umbrella and valuables
- [ ] Appraisals for jewelry, art or collections

## Tab 6: Business and side income

- [ ] Operating agreement or partnership papers
- [ ] Buy-sell agreement
- [ ] Names of the business attorney, accountant and key contacts
- [ ] Who would run it in an emergency

Skip this tab if you have no business. See [business succession planning](/learn/business-owners/business-succession-planning) if you do.

## Tab 7: Tax and insurance

- [ ] Last three years of tax returns
- [ ] Life insurance policies and the beneficiary on each
- [ ] Long-term care or disability policies
- [ ] Name and number of your tax preparer

## Tab 8: Digital life

- [ ] Instructions for the password manager, not the master password itself unless the binder is locked up
- [ ] Phone, computer and tablet unlock details, held securely
- [ ] A list of email, cloud, payment and social accounts

The [digital assets inventory](/checklists/digital-assets-inventory) provides a table.

## Tab 9: Final wishes

- [ ] Funeral and burial or cremation preferences
- [ ] Any prepaid arrangements with the funeral home
- [ ] A letter of instruction
- [ ] Notes on pets, keepsakes and personal messages

See the [letter of instruction outline](/checklists/letter-of-instruction-outline) and [funeral and burial wishes](/checklists/funeral-and-burial-wishes).

## Tab 10: Home and household

- [ ] Utilities, internet, and who to call
- [ ] Maintenance providers and warranties
- [ ] Spare keys, alarm codes and garage openers
- [ ] Pet care instructions

## Who gets what

| Person | Role | Gets a copy of | Knows where the originals are? |
|---|---|---|---|
| | Executor or trustee | | |
| | Property agent | | |
| | Health care agent | | |
| | Family member | | |

Be careful with copies of your whole plan. Share the cover page widely and share the full contents only with people who need them. A health care agent needs tab 2 and 9 now, not your tax returns.

## Keeping it current

Set a yearly reminder with the [plan review reminder](/tools/plan-review-reminder). In 15 minutes:

- [ ] Swap in new statements
- [ ] Check the beneficiary table against what accounts show
- [ ] Update contacts that have changed
- [ ] Note any life event: birth, death, marriage, divorce, move, new job, new business
- [ ] Update the date on the cover page

## Common mistakes

- Putting the binder in a safe nobody can open. Make sure at least one person can.
- Leaving out the health care papers, which are the ones needed first.
- Keeping unsigned drafts mixed in with signed documents. Mark drafts clearly or leave them out.
- Letting it go stale after a divorce, move or birth.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You do not have signed documents to put in Tab 1.
- A document in the binder was signed in another state or years ago.
- Beneficiaries do not match what your will or trust says.
- You own a business or rental property.
- A family member has special needs or may challenge the plan.

## Next step

Bring your binder and cover page to a review. [Book a consult](/plan-finder) to confirm that every document in it still works.
