**How to use this checklist.** Go through it once with your business in front of you. A sole proprietorship has no separate legal existence, so there is no board, partner or operating agreement to take over. If you are unreachable, nothing happens unless you set it up. Work from top to bottom and tick what is done.

## Why solos are different

When you are the business, the business ends or stalls when you do. Your executor inherits your tools, your invoices and your debts, but not your customers' trust. Your clients may have data on your laptop that a stranger should not see. Planning now keeps the business worth something and protects your family from a mess.

## Step 1: Inventory business assets

| Asset | Where it is | Value (rough) | Who should get it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equipment and tools | | | |
| Inventory | | | |
| Vehicle used for business | | | |
| Accounts receivable (money owed to me) | | | |
| Business bank account | | | |
| Domain names and website | | | |
| Intellectual property (designs, code, writing, photos) | | | |
| Customer lists | | | |
| Licenses and certifications | | | |

- [ ] I keep business money in a separate account, not mixed with personal.
- [ ] Domains and websites are registered in my own name and a trusted person knows the registrar.
- [ ] Intellectual property is documented, including who owns work done for clients.

## Step 2: Protect client files

- **Where client files are kept (cloud, laptop, paper):** ____________________
- **Who can access them if I cannot:** ____________________
- **Kind of confidential information (health, tax, legal, financial):** ____________________
- **Retention period I follow:** ____________________

- [ ] Confidential files are encrypted or password protected.
- [ ] A trusted person knows how to find them without reading them all.
- [ ] My client agreements say what happens to files if I am unavailable.
- [ ] Some professions have rules about client files. I checked mine.

## Step 3: Licenses and permits

| License or permit | Issuer | Renewal date | Transfers on death? |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | |
| | | | |

Most personal licenses do not transfer to heirs. Some can be held temporarily by an executor, and others end with the holder. Contact each issuer, or ask them what happens when the holder dies.

## Step 4: Accounts and access

- [ ] Business email has a legacy or recovery plan.
- [ ] Payment platforms (card processors, invoicing tools) list a trusted contact if available.
- [ ] Accounting software can be viewed by a bookkeeper or accountant.
- [ ] Phone number for the business is not tied only to my personal phone.
- [ ] A password manager has emergency access (see the [Password Manager Handoff Guide](/free/password-manager-handoff-guide)).

## Step 5: Name who winds down

- **Person to wind down or run the business short term:** ____________________
- **Backup:** ____________________
- **Do they know the business?** ____________________
- **Person to talk to clients:** ____________________
- **Preferred outcome (sell, transfer clients, close):** ____________________

Sample client notice: "Thank you for your work with [business name]. [Name] has taken over wrapping up current projects. Your files are secure and will be returned or transferred as you direct."

## Step 6: Legal paperwork to line up

- [ ] Will names an executor and covers business assets.
- [ ] Durable power of attorney covers business matters while I am alive but unable to act (see [Powers of Attorney](/guides/powers-of-attorney)).
- [ ] A health care directive is in place.
- [ ] Business assets are titled so they do not have to go through probate, if that is the goal. Ask about a trust or transfer-on-death options.
- [ ] Beneficiaries are named on business retirement accounts, such as a solo 401(k) or SEP IRA.
- [ ] Insurance reviewed: liability, disability, life and business interruption.

## Step 7: Money and taxes

- [ ] Estimated tax payments have a plan.
- [ ] Debts and loans you signed personally are listed.
- [ ] A final business tax return and any sales tax filings will be needed after death, and someone knows who prepares them.
- [ ] Records are saved for the period tax rules require.

## Step 8: Dividing the business value

If you want the business to have value for your family:

- [ ] A trusted professional could buy or take over clients.
- [ ] A written transition agreement exists with a colleague.
- [ ] The business has documented processes someone else could follow.

## When to talk to an attorney

- Your work is in a licensed field with rules about client files.
- You own valuable intellectual property or a brand.
- You want assets to bypass probate.
- You have employees or contractors.
- You are thinking of forming an LLC to separate liability and ease transition.
