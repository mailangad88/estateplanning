## How to use this planner

We are sorry for your loss. An estate sale sells most of a home's contents over a few days, usually before the house goes on the market. Done well, it turns a hard task into a manageable one. Done in a hurry, it can sell things the family wanted, or valuable items for too little. Work through the steps in order. Check state and local rules, since some places require permits for sales, and an executor may need court authority before selling estate property. If you are not sure, ask an attorney.

## Step 1: Confirm you may sell

- [ ] Find out who has legal authority to sell the contents
- [ ] Check the will for gifts of specific items, such as "my piano to my niece"
- [ ] Get written agreement among heirs on what is being sold, if you can
- [ ] Confirm that no item belongs to someone else, such as a loaned painting

Complete the [family keepsake round](/free/cleaning-out-a-parents-home-planner) first. Sell only after that.

## Step 2: Decide who runs the sale

| | Do it yourself | Hire an estate sale company |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Your time, signs, supplies | A commission or fee, often a share of sales |
| Effort | High: sorting, pricing, staffing | Lower: the company does most of it |
| Pricing skill | Depends on research you do | Often better with antiques and collectibles |
| Control | Full | Shared, set by the contract |
| Security | Your helpers and your rules | Company staff and procedures |
| Good fit | Small sale with ordinary goods | Full house, antiques, limited family time |

You can also hold a mixed approach: sell ordinary goods yourself and send certain items to an auction house or dealer.

## Step 3: Hold back what must not sell

Before anyone arrives, remove the following and store them safely.

- [ ] Wills, trust papers, financial records, and tax returns
- [ ] Jewelry, coins, cash, gift cards and collectibles you have not had appraised
- [ ] Firearms and medication
- [ ] Computers, phones, tablets and any device with personal data
- [ ] Family photographs and albums
- [ ] Items promised in the will or by family agreement
- [ ] Anything the home buyer or the estate may need, such as built-in appliances in a sale contract

Use coloured tape or a locked room for hold-back items. Tell the company in writing which areas are off limits.

## Step 4: Get valuables looked at

Some items are worth more than they look: old furniture, rugs, art, silver, watches, tools, vinyl records and vintage toys. A short appraisal can prevent an expensive mistake.

- [ ] Photograph the items you do not understand
- [ ] Ask a local appraiser or auction house for an opinion
- [ ] Ask the sale company how it will handle high-value pieces

## Step 5: Interview companies and read the contract

Talk with at least two companies. Ask for references and look at past listings.

Questions to ask:

1. What is your commission, and what is it based on?
2. Which costs come out of the sale, such as advertising, staff, cleanup or credit card fees?
3. What happens to items that do not sell?
4. How do you price items, and do you share the price list before the sale?
5. Are you licensed and insured, and can I see proof?
6. How do you protect small valuables during the sale?
7. When will I be paid, and will I get a written report of every sale?
8. May I end the contract, and what would it cost?

Terms to look for in the contract:

- [ ] Commission rate and exactly what it covers
- [ ] Any minimum fee or advertising charge
- [ ] Dates and hours of the sale
- [ ] List of excluded rooms and items
- [ ] Payment deadline and how proceeds are reported
- [ ] Treatment of unsold goods, and who pays for haul-away
- [ ] Insurance and responsibility for damage or theft

Do not sign a contract the same day you meet the company. Take it home. An attorney can read it quickly if the estate is large.

## Step 6: Prepare the house

- [ ] Take photos of every room before sale
- [ ] Make a list of any damaged items
- [ ] Clean paths and clear stairs so visitors are safe
- [ ] Confirm that the homeowner insurance covers visitors and tell the insurer about the sale
- [ ] Arrange parking and neighbour notice
- [ ] Switch off hazardous items, and lock away anything breakable

## Step 7: Sale day

- [ ] Keep the family away if emotions are high, or ask one person to represent the estate
- [ ] Check that locked rooms stay locked
- [ ] Keep two people near the cash or payment point
- [ ] Do not allow buyers to go to upper floors alone
- [ ] Take photos at close of each day

## Step 8: After the sale

- [ ] Get a written itemized sales report and the payment
- [ ] Deposit the proceeds into the estate account
- [ ] Record the sale in the [estate accounting ledger](/free/estate-accounting-ledger-template)
- [ ] Decide what to do with unsold goods: donate with a receipt, haul away or sell online
- [ ] Do a final walk-through and photographs
- [ ] Prepare the house for the market. See [selling a house in probate](/free/selling-a-house-in-probate-checklist)

Estate sale proceeds are normally income to the estate or part of the estate's value, and tax treatment can depend on what was sold and when. Ask a tax professional.

## When to talk to an attorney

- It is unclear who has authority to sell
- The will names specific items or heirs disagree on who gets what
- You find items that may be very valuable or may belong to someone else
- The company's contract has terms you do not understand
- Items go missing before or during the sale
- The estate may have a tax filing to report the proceeds
