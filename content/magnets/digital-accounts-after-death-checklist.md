## How to use this checklist

We are sorry for your loss. A person's online life is scattered across phones, email, subscriptions and apps. Some of it carries real money, and some of it carries memories you will want. Federal and state law limits what a company may share with family, so the steps below favor requests through official channels over guessing passwords. Many states have adopted a uniform law on fiduciary access to digital assets, but details vary. Do not log into someone's account without checking that you are allowed to.

## Step 1: Start with the phone and the email

The phone and the main email account are usually the keys to everything else, because password resets go to them.

- [ ] Keep the phone charged and in a safe place
- [ ] Do not delete messages, photos or apps yet
- [ ] If you know the passcode, back up the phone's photos and contacts
- [ ] Do not cancel the phone number until you have finished the other accounts, since text-message codes may still be needed
- [ ] Turn off any auto-reply or automated payment that might cause harm

## Step 2: Money comes before memories

Check online money first.

- [ ] Banks, credit cards, and brokerage accounts. See the [first 30 days checklist](/checklists/first-30-days-after-a-death)
- [ ] Payment apps and balances, such as peer to peer apps and online wallets
- [ ] Online shopping accounts holding gift cards or store credit
- [ ] Cryptocurrency exchanges and wallets, if any
- [ ] Airline miles and hotel points, which some programs allow to be transferred
- [ ] Domain names, websites and online stores that earn income

Cryptocurrency can be lost if the key or recovery phrase is missing. If you find signs of crypto, do not move funds. Tell the executor and ask an attorney.

## Step 3: Find the accounts

- [ ] Search the inbox for words like "receipt", "welcome", "statement", "subscription" and "password"
- [ ] Look at recent bank and card statements for recurring charges
- [ ] Check a password manager if one was used
- [ ] Look for a written list of accounts. A [digital assets inventory](/checklists/digital-assets-inventory) may exist
- [ ] Check browser saved logins only with legal authority

## Step 4: Social media and email

Each company has its own process. Most offer a choice of memorializing or deleting.

| Account type | Common options | What companies often ask for |
|---|---|---|
| Social network | Memorialize, remove, or in some cases a legacy contact | Death certificate, proof of relationship |
| Email and cloud storage | Close, or request data in limited cases | Death certificate and legal authority, sometimes a court order |
| Photo sharing | Download photos, or close the account | Proof of death |
| Streaming and subscriptions | Cancel | Account details and death notice |
| Gaming and app stores | Cancel, sometimes transfer in limited cases | Contact support |

Before closing any account, download what the family wants: photos, videos, documents and messages. Closed accounts are often deleted for good after a set time.

Some people choose a legacy contact or an inactive account manager while they are alive. If your loved one did, follow it. If not, use each company's help pages, which usually have a "deceased user" form.

## Step 5: Subscriptions and recurring bills

- [ ] List every recurring charge from the last three statements
- [ ] Cancel streaming, music, news, software, gym and meal-kit services
- [ ] Ask for refunds of unused periods
- [ ] Cancel phone and internet service last
- [ ] Keep paying any service the estate needs, such as the electric or home internet at a house being sold

## Step 6: Sample request to a company

You can adapt this wording.

"I am writing to report the death of [full name], who held an account with your company under the email address [email]. I am the [executor, spouse, child] and I am enclosing a certified death certificate and proof of my authority. Please tell me what steps are needed to [close the account / memorialize the account / provide a copy of the data]. Please do not delete content until I have confirmed. Contact: [name, phone, email]."

## Step 7: Protect against fraud

Closed accounts and old profiles can be used by thieves. See the [identity theft after death checklist](/free/identity-theft-after-death-checklist).

- [ ] Change the mailing address on accounts that stay open
- [ ] Do not post the full date of birth, address or funeral time on social media
- [ ] Be careful with emails that claim to be from a company about the deceased account
- [ ] Never pay a fee to someone who claims to recover accounts

## Planning for your own accounts

This list is easier when someone has planned ahead. Keep an inventory of accounts and say who can act. See [digital assets and estate planning](/guides/digital-assets-estate-planning).

## When to talk to an attorney

- A company will not release information without a court order
- The accounts hold money, cryptocurrency or business income
- A device is locked and holds important documents
- Family members disagree about what to keep or delete
- You suspect fraud or identity theft
- The person lived in another state or country
