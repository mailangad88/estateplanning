**How to use this checklist.** A home is usually the largest asset a first-time buyer owns, and the deed decides who gets it. Use Part 1 before closing, since the title company prepares the deed from what you tell them. Use Parts 2 to 5 in the 90 days after. For a deeper look at how title types work, see the [new homeowner title and deed guide](/free/new-homeowner-title-and-deed-guide). Rules vary by state.

## Part 1: Before closing, decide how to take title

Tell the closing agent how names should appear. Do not accept the default without reading it.

- [ ] I know the names that will appear on the deed, and they match my intentions.
- [ ] If buying with a partner or friend, we know whether we hold as joint tenants with survivorship or as tenants in common, and why.
- [ ] If married, I know whether my state offers tenancy by the entirety or community property options.
- [ ] If I am single, I have asked whether the lender or state allows a transfer on death deed or a trust at closing.
- [ ] I have asked the title company how the deed will read and I have read the draft.

| If you are | Common starting point | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Single, no children | Sole name, with TOD or a will | Who gets it if I die? |
| Single parent | Sole name, with trust or TOD | Who would manage it for minors? |
| Married | Joint with survivorship, or entireties | What if we both die together? |
| Unmarried couple | Survivorship or a trust | What do I want to protect for my family? |
| Buying with a friend or relative | Tenants in common, with a written agreement | What if one wants to sell? |

Write the plan:

- **Names on the deed:** ______________________
- **How we hold title:** ______________________
- **Who inherits if I die:** ______________________
- **Backup if that person has died:** ______________________

## Part 2: Transfer on death deed, trust or will

Only some states allow transfer on death deeds. Where they are allowed, they can pass a home without probate and cost little to record. A revocable living trust offers more control, for example for minor children or blended families, but costs more to set up.

- [ ] I have read the [comparison of a transfer on death deed and a trust](/compare/transfer-on-death-deed-vs-trust).
- [ ] I know whether my state allows a TOD deed and how it must be recorded.
- [ ] I have confirmed with my lender that a TOD deed or trust transfer will not trigger the loan. Federal law protects some trust transfers of a primary home, but ask in writing.
- [ ] I know that any deed or trust must be signed, notarized and recorded to work.
- [ ] I have a will that deals with everything the deed does not.

## Part 3: Life insurance and mortgage protection

A mortgage does not disappear at death. If someone co-signed or lives in the home, the payments continue. Heirs who inherit can often keep paying, but a loan comes due if nobody does.

Ways to cover the loan:

| Option | How it works | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Term life insurance | Pays a lump sum to your beneficiary for a set number of years | Choose a term that outlasts the mortgage |
| Mortgage life insurance from the lender | Pays the lender | Often costs more, and the benefit usually shrinks as the loan does |
| Employer group coverage | Low cost | Ends if you leave the job |
| Savings or an emergency fund | Covers months of payments | Does not cover the full loan |

- [ ] I know the monthly payment, the loan balance and the end date.
- [ ] I have used the [life insurance needs tool](/tools/life-insurance-needs) to size a policy.
- [ ] I compared at least two quotes and read what is excluded.
- [ ] Beneficiary on each policy is a named person, with a backup, and not a minor.
- [ ] If I have minor children, I considered naming a trust as beneficiary.

## Part 4: Documents to update within 90 days

- [ ] **Will.** Add the home, name an executor and a guardian if I have children.
- [ ] **Durable power of attorney.** Name someone who could sign for me, for example to refinance or sell if I am incapacitated.
- [ ] **Health care directive.** Name an agent who can speak with my doctors.
- [ ] **Beneficiary forms.** Check retirement accounts and bank accounts. A home does not replace them.
- [ ] **Homeowners insurance.** Make sure every owner is named on the policy.
- [ ] **Home warranty and utilities.** Keep a folder with account numbers.

## Part 5: Keep records together

An executor or successor will not know where things are unless you tell them.

- **Closing documents are kept at:** ______________________
- **Mortgage servicer and loan number:** ______________________
- **Homeowners insurance agent and policy number:** ______________________
- **Property tax bill and county parcel number:** ______________________
- **Recorded deed (copy) is at:** ______________________
- **Who knows where these are:** ______________________

## Common mistakes

- Adding a parent or child to the deed to "avoid probate." It can create a gift tax filing, expose the home to the other person's debts and lose a tax benefit on later sale. Ask first.
- Leaving a spouse or partner off the title because it was easier at closing.
- Assuming a will controls a home held in joint tenancy. The survivor takes it first.
- Skipping a backup beneficiary on a TOD deed.
- Forgetting that a trust must be funded. Signing a trust document does nothing for a home still titled in your own name.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You are buying with someone who is not your spouse.
- You have children from a prior relationship.
- You want to place the home in a trust and are not sure the lender will allow it.
- You plan to rent part of the home or run a business from it.
- You are using a gift from family for the down payment.
- You live in one state and are buying in another.
