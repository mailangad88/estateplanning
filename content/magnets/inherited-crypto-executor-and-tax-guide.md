## How to use this guide

Crypto is easy to lose. If no one has the keys, no company can recover it. This guide is for the person who must find out whether it exists and deal with it. Follow the steps in order, keep notes, and do not rush any transfer. It is general education, not tax or legal advice.

## Step flow

1. **Do not touch anything yet.** Do not log in, move coins or reset passwords until you have a plan.
2. **Search for signs.** Use the checklist below.
3. **Secure devices and papers.** Keep phones, computers and any hardware wallets safe.
4. **Get legal authority.** An executor needs letters of office to deal with an exchange.
5. **Record values on the date of death.**
6. **Transfer or sell** with advice, then report on taxes.

## Did the person own crypto?

- [ ] Emails from exchanges (Coinbase, Kraken, Gemini and similar), including tax forms
- [ ] Bank or card statements showing transfers to exchanges
- [ ] Apps on the phone or computer
- [ ] A small device that looks like a USB stick (a hardware wallet)
- [ ] Papers with 12 or 24 random words (a seed phrase)
- [ ] Past tax returns showing sales of digital assets
- [ ] Browser extensions for wallets

If the person had a hardware wallet but no seed phrase, funds may not be recoverable. If they used an exchange, the exchange holds the coins and can verify an executor.

## Exchange versus self-custody

| Where crypto sits | Who can unlock it | What the family needs |
|---|---|---|
| Exchange account | The company, after verifying the executor | Death certificate, letters of office, forms, sometimes a lawyer's letter |
| Software wallet on a phone | Whoever has the seed phrase | The seed phrase |
| Hardware wallet | Whoever has the device and PIN or seed phrase | Device plus PIN or seed phrase |
| Multi-signature wallet | Several keys together | A set of keys held by named people |

A seed phrase is the master key. Anyone who has it can take the funds, so keep copies private.

## If an exchange refuses

- [ ] Ask what documents they accept and read the response in writing
- [ ] Send certified copies and letters of office
- [ ] Ask for a named contact in their deceased accounts team
- [ ] If they delay, ask an attorney to send a letter
- [ ] Do not pay "recovery" services that approach you

## Executor duties and risk

An executor generally has a duty to protect estate assets. Losing crypto through carelessness, such as sharing a seed phrase or sending funds to the wrong address, can raise a claim from heirs. The best protections are:

- Keep written notes of every step
- Send a small test amount first when moving funds
- Ask for professional help if you do not understand it
- Get heirs' written agreement before selling

An executor can usually sell crypto if the will or law allows sales, but selling is a decision about value and tax. Do not sell in a panic just because prices move.

## Valuation and basis

| Question | Common approach |
|---|---|
| What value goes on the estate tax return? | Fair market value on the date of death, documented with exchange price records |
| What is the heir's basis? | Often the date-of-death value (a step-up, or step-down if lower) |
| If heirs sell right away? | Gain or loss is small, because it is measured from the stepped-up value |
| Is it taxed as income when received? | Inheritance is generally not income. Later sales can create gain |

Take screenshots of the price at several exchanges on the date of death. Rules on valuing crypto are developing. Ask a tax adviser.

## NFTs

An NFT is a digital token that points to an item such as art. It is held in a wallet, so the same key problems apply. Valuation can be hard because few sales exist. Many NFTs also depend on a platform's terms. Basis follows the same idea as other property: the date-of-death value, supported by an appraisal when large.

## Reporting

- [ ] The estate tax return, if the estate is large enough to need one
- [ ] The person's final income tax return, with crypto sales to date of death
- [ ] The heirs' returns when they sell
- [ ] Estate income tax return if the estate holds crypto for a time

The tax form asks about digital assets, so answer honestly.

## If the executor does not understand crypto

Hire someone who does. A crypto-literate accountant, an attorney and perhaps a custodian can work together. The cost is usually less than a mistake.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You cannot find keys or an exchange
- An exchange will not release funds
- The balance is large or the heirs disagree
- There is no will, or the will is silent on crypto
- Someone contacts you offering to "recover" coins
