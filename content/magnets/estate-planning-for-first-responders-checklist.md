## How to use this checklist

Your family may depend on benefits that pass by forms, not by a will, and some of them only apply in specific circumstances. Work through the sections with your spouse or partner and mark each line. Benefit rules vary by agency, pension system and state, so confirm each with your department's benefits office. This checklist is educational, not advice for your situation.

## Section 1: The documents

- [ ] Will with executor, guardian and backup guardian
- [ ] Financial power of attorney
- [ ] Health care power of attorney and HIPAA release
- [ ] Living will or directive
- [ ] Trust for minor children, or trust terms in the will
- [ ] Funeral and burial wishes, including whether the department may hold a service with honors
- [ ] A letter of instruction for the first days

Many first responders sign a will at the academy and never touch it again. Review it after every marriage, birth, divorce, promotion or move.

## Section 2: Line-of-duty benefits

Some benefits are triggered only when a death or disability is connected to duty. Examples include:

| Benefit | Where to ask | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Federal Public Safety Officers' Benefits (PSOB) | U.S. Bureau of Justice Assistance | Provides death and disability benefits and education help for eligible officers and their survivors. Claims are filed through a web-based process, and your agency usually helps |
| State or local line-of-duty death benefit | State or department | Varies widely by state and department |
| Pension duty death or duty disability benefit | Your pension board | Often more generous than a non-duty benefit, but rules differ |
| Workers' compensation death benefit | Employer and state agency | Pays survivors in many states |
| Union or association death benefit | Your union | Often paid to a named beneficiary |
| Group life and accidental death coverage | HR and insurer | Check if there is a higher payout for duty deaths |

A line-of-duty determination can take time. Families do better when they know the claim exists and who to call.

- **My agency's benefits contact:** ______________________
- **My pension system and contact:** ______________________
- **My union or association contact:** ______________________

## Section 3: Pension survivor options

Pension systems for police and fire often differ from general employee plans. Ask:

- [ ] What does my spouse receive if I die in service, in the line of duty and not?
- [ ] What do my children receive, and until what age?
- [ ] What does my spouse receive if I die after I retire?
- [ ] Which survivor options do I choose at retirement, and is the choice permanent?
- [ ] What happens to the survivor benefit if my spouse remarries?
- [ ] What if I am divorced, and does a court order affect my pension?
- [ ] Is there a death benefit or refund payable to a named beneficiary?
- [ ] Is there a retiree health benefit that a survivor can keep?

Get answers in writing, and keep them with your plan. Rules for who counts as a survivor, and when benefits end, differ from one system to the next.

## Section 4: Beneficiary forms

- [ ] Pension or death benefit beneficiary
- [ ] Deferred compensation (457) or 401(k) beneficiary
- [ ] Group life and accidental death beneficiary
- [ ] Union or association policy beneficiary
- [ ] Personal life insurance beneficiary
- [ ] Bank account payable-on-death designations

Do not name a minor child directly. A trust, or a trustee named in your will, can hold the money until a safe age. Use the [beneficiary designation audit](/checklists/beneficiary-designation-audit) to track each one.

## Section 5: Guardianship and children

You may work nights, holidays and extended overtime. If both parents work in emergency services, an event that affects one can affect both.

- **Guardian:** ______________________
- **Backup guardian:** ______________________
- **Short-term caregiver who can be at the house within an hour:** ______________________
- **Who knows the children's school, doctors and routines:** ______________________

Read the [guardianship guide](/guides/guardianship-for-minor-children). A guardian chosen in a will is not always in place the first night, so name a short-term person on a medical and school authorization if your state allows it.

## Section 6: Health care wishes

You may have seen the worst day of many families. Write down what you want.

- [ ] Agent named, with a backup
- [ ] Wishes about CPR, ventilation, feeding tubes and pain control written down
- [ ] Wishes about organ donation recorded
- [ ] Copies with my agent, doctor and spouse

See the [guide to healthcare directives](/guides/healthcare-directives-and-living-wills). After a critical incident or injury, your agent may be called quickly. Make sure they have a copy.

## Section 7: Access and security

Many first responders limit what they share about home addresses and family. Plan for that.

- [ ] Documents stored where a spouse or executor can get them without you
- [ ] A trusted person knows how to reach the department's benefits office
- [ ] Phone, email and key accounts have a digital access plan
- [ ] Firearms, equipment and department property have written instructions for return or storage under state law
- [ ] Privacy concerns, such as keeping addresses off public records, have been discussed with an attorney

## Section 8: Family instruction sheet for the first 72 hours

- **Who calls the pension system and union:** ______________________
- **Where the will, directives and insurance papers are:** ______________________
- **Who has the children:** ______________________
- **Where the safe, passwords and keys are:** ______________________
- **Funeral or honors wishes:** ______________________
- **Debts and mortgages and who to call:** ______________________

## Section 9: Debts, home and cash

- [ ] Life insurance sized to cover the mortgage, debts and children's costs
- [ ] A cash source that does not require probate, such as a payable-on-death account
- [ ] Home ownership reviewed with an attorney (joint ownership, trust or transfer on death)

## When to talk to an attorney

- You have children and no guardian or trust named
- You are close to retirement or choosing a pension survivor option
- You are divorced, remarried or have children from more than one relationship
- A trust or minor child is a beneficiary of a pension, 457 or insurance policy
- A line-of-duty claim has been filed, denied or delayed
- Your family has privacy or security concerns that affect how documents are recorded
