## How to use this guide

We are sorry for your loss. This guide covers estates for people who lived in DuPage County, which is the 18th Judicial Circuit of Illinois. It is meant to take some of the mystery out of the first visit and the first filings. Court practices change, so confirm each detail with the clerk or an attorney. This is education, not legal advice.

## Does the estate belong in DuPage County?

Illinois sends a probate case to the county where the person had a known place of residence (755 ILCS 5/5-1). If the person lived in DuPage County, in towns such as Naperville, Wheaton, Downers Grove or Elmhurst, the case belongs here. Note that Naperville and some other towns reach into Will County, so check the county on the address record. If the person lived outside Illinois, the rules look to where their Illinois real estate or personal property sits, so ask an attorney.

Next, ask whether a court case is needed at all. A small estate affidavit may work when personal property is $150,000 or less for a death on or after August 15, 2025. Real estate in one person's name, or a family dispute, usually means a court case. See the [small estate affidavit guide](/free/illinois-small-estate-affidavit-guide).

## Where to go

The 18th Judicial Circuit Court sits at the DuPage County Judicial Center, 505 North County Farm Road, Wheaton, IL 60187. The Illinois Courts directory lists the court's hours as 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and its phone as (630) 407-8901. The Circuit Court Clerk is at the same address. Its civil department number is (630) 407-8700, and the clerk's site offers civil e-filing and case lookup.

## Local notes

- **Parking and rules:** The courthouse lists free parking in two garages and several lots. The clerk's page also says weapons, outside food and drink, and recording devices, including camera phones, are not allowed. Check the posted rules before you go, and plan to put your phone away.
- **Filing:** Probate papers are generally filed electronically through the clerk. The original will is different. Ask the clerk's civil department how it wants the original delivered.
- **Forms:** The clerk's site posts probate forms, such as petitions for letters, a notice of hearing and a citation to discover assets. DuPage has a form for summary administration in some cases. Ask which forms the court expects before you start.
- **Small estates:** If the estate qualifies for the affidavit, you may never need the courthouse. Banks decide whether to accept it, so call each one first.

## The Illinois steps that apply here

These steps come from the state Probate Act, so they are the same in every county.

| Step | What happens | Rule to know |
|---|---|---|
| File the will | The person holding the original gives it to the circuit clerk | Do it promptly. Hiding a will for 30 days after learning of the death can be a felony (755 ILCS 5/6-1) |
| Notice to heirs | Heirs and legatees get a copy of the petition and the order | Within 14 days after the order (755 ILCS 5/6-10) |
| Notice to creditors | Published once a week for three weeks in a county newspaper, and mailed to known creditors | Claim date is the later of six months from first publication or three months from mailing (755 ILCS 5/18-3) |
| Inventory | A verified list of assets is filed | Within 60 days after letters issue (755 ILCS 5/14-1) |
| Outer claim limit | Late claims are cut off | Two years after death (755 ILCS 5/18-12) |

For family members, an interested person can ask for formal proof of the will within 42 days after it is admitted (755 ILCS 5/6-21). A will contest must be filed within six months after admission (755 ILCS 5/8-1). Our [Illinois probate checklist](/free/illinois-probate-checklist) walks through every stage.

## Before you file

- [ ] Original will found and ready to deliver to the clerk
- [ ] Certified death certificates ordered
- [ ] Heirs listed with addresses (see the [Illinois intestacy chart](/free/illinois-intestacy-chart) if there is no will)
- [ ] Call to the clerk's civil department to confirm forms and e-filing steps
- [ ] Asset list started, with date-of-death values
- [ ] Attorney contacted if the estate has real estate

## When to talk to an attorney

- The will names no executor, or the person named cannot serve
- A relative may contest the will or disagrees about who should serve
- The estate owns real estate, a business or property in another state
- You are not sure the small estate affidavit is allowed
- A creditor sends a claim you do not understand
- You are asked to sign court papers and are not sure what they do
