## How to use this planner

Many parents help an adult child with rent, a down payment, debt or a business, and later wonder how it should affect the will. This planner helps you decide what to do and write it down so that no one is guessing. Complete the sections in order. Rules vary by state and tax situation, so bring your results to an attorney and tax professional.

## Step 1: Decide how much you can afford

- **Savings and investments:** ______________________
- **Amount needed for my own retirement and care:** ______________________
- **Amount I can give or lend without strain:** ______________________

Give yourself a margin. Helping a child at the cost of your own security can leave the child responsible for you later. If you may need long-term care within a few years, ask about [look-back rules](/guides/medicaid-and-long-term-care-planning), since gifts can affect eligibility.

## Step 2: Compare your options

| Option | How it works | Good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gift now | Money given with no repayment | Down payment, tuition, emergency | Cannot be taken back, may be treated as an advance |
| Loan | Written, with repayment terms | Larger sums, business | Interest rules, collection awkwardness |
| Pay a bill directly | Pay tuition or medical costs to the provider | Large costs | Must go to the provider to avoid gift tax |
| Co-sign a loan | You promise to pay if they do not | Rare | You are liable, credit impact |
| Let them live with you | Housing support | Short transitions | Tax and ownership questions |
| Leave it at death | Gift in will or trust | Fairness among children | Child waits, plan may change |
| Trust for the child | Money held with conditions | Child with debt, divorce risk or addiction | Cost, trustee needed |

## Step 3: Know the tax basics

- The annual gift tax exclusion is $19,000 per recipient for 2026. A married couple can give $38,000 to each child without a gift tax return, if both agree.
- Gifts over that amount usually require a Form 709, but tax is typically not due because the lifetime exemption is $15,000,000 per person for 2026.
- Tuition and medical bills paid directly to the school or provider are generally excluded and do not count against the annual limit.
- A loan with little or no interest may be treated partly as a gift. Ask a tax professional about the rate rules.
- Gifts are generally not income to the child.

## Step 4: Keep a ledger

Track every dollar you give or lend. Siblings often remember differently.

- **Total given to Child 1:** ______________________
- **Total given to Child 2:** ______________________
- **Total given to Child 3:** ______________________

Decide for each line whether it is truly a gift, or an advance against inheritance, which means the amount will be counted when your estate is divided. Write it down at the time. A short letter works.

## Step 5: Three ways to equalize

Equal does not always mean fair, but if you want the same total for each child, you have these options.

1. **Adjust the will or trust.** Say that amounts given earlier are deducted from that child's share. Name the amounts or attach the ledger.
2. **Give the others the same now.** Matching gifts avoid the need to adjust later.
3. **Use a loan forgiveness clause.** The will says unpaid loans are forgiven, or deducted from the child's share.

Sample wording for your attorney to adapt: "Any amount listed on the attached schedule shall be treated as an advancement and charged against that child's share."

Without clear words, state law may or may not count lifetime gifts. Many states presume that gifts to children are not advances unless documented. Ask your attorney about your state.

## Step 8: Protect against common problems

- [ ] A child's divorce: money mixed into joint property may be divided. Ask about keeping gifts separate.
- [ ] A child's creditors: gifts can be reached by lenders. A trust may offer protection.
- [ ] Benefits: money given to a child with a disability can affect benefits. See [special needs trusts](/guides/special-needs-trusts).
- [ ] Unequal need: one child needs more help. Decide whether to compensate the others.
- [ ] Your own plan: update your will after each large gift. See [how to make a will](/guides/how-to-make-a-will).

## When to talk to an attorney

- You plan to lend a large sum or help buy a home
- You want to treat gifts as advances in your will
- A child has debts, a troubled marriage or an addiction
- You may need Medicaid-funded care within five years
- You are making gifts above the annual exclusion
