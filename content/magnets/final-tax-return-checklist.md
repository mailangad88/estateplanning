## How to use this checklist

A final return is prepared much like any other. It covers income from January 1 up to the date of death. Take it a step at a time. Many tax preparers are used to this return, and you can ask one for help.

## Step 1: Work out who files

- [ ] **A court-appointed executor or administrator** files and signs for the decedent.
- [ ] **A surviving spouse** can file a joint return for the year of death, in most cases. Joint filing is generally allowed unless the survivor remarries in that year.
- [ ] **No spouse and no appointed representative?** A person in charge of the property may need to file, particularly to claim a refund. Ask a tax professional.
- [ ] Write down who is responsible: ____________

## Step 2: Tell the IRS who you are

- [ ] Send Form 56 (Notice Concerning Fiduciary Relationship) once you are the personal representative.
- [ ] Get the decedent's date of death, Social Security number and last address ready.
- [ ] Request prior year transcripts if you are unsure earlier returns were filed (Form 4506-T).

## Step 3: Know the deadline

The return is due when it would have been due if the person had lived. For someone who used the calendar year, that is April 15 of the year after death, even if the death was in January. If you need more time, you can request an extension in the usual way.

- **Due date:** ____________
- **Extension requested?** [ ] Yes [ ] No

## Step 4: Gather documents

- [ ] Wage statements (W-2) and pension or retirement statements (1099-R)
- [ ] Social Security statement (SSA-1099). Benefits for the month of death are generally not kept by the family, so expect an adjustment.
- [ ] Interest and dividend statements (1099-INT, 1099-DIV)
- [ ] Brokerage sales statements (1099-B)
- [ ] Prior year return for reference
- [ ] Medical bills paid before and after death
- [ ] Property tax and mortgage interest statements
- [ ] Records of estimated tax payments
- [ ] Death certificate (one copy for refund claims)
- [ ] Court letters of office if one was issued

Income received after death belongs on the estate's return (Form 1041) or the beneficiary's, not the final Form 1040.

## Step 5: Prepare the return

- [ ] Use the same Form 1040 as for a living person.
- [ ] Write "Deceased," the person's name and the date of death across the top.
- [ ] Take the standard deduction in full, with no reduction for the short year.
- [ ] Consider medical expenses paid within one year of death. They may be claimed on the final return or on the estate tax return, in some cases.
- [ ] Review tax credits and any carryover losses.

## Step 6: Sign correctly

| Situation | How to sign |
|---|---|
| Executor files for a single person | Executor signs, adds title "Executor" or "Personal representative" |
| Joint return with a surviving spouse and an executor | Both sign |
| Surviving spouse, no executor | Spouse signs and writes "Filing as surviving spouse" |

Sample wording for the signature line: **"Jordan Lee, Personal Representative for Pat Lee, deceased."**

## Step 7: Claim any refund

A refund is owed to the estate or to the heirs, not to the person who died.

- [ ] **Surviving spouse filing a joint return:** Form 1310 is not required.
- [ ] **Court-appointed executor filing the original return:** Form 1310 is not required if you attach a copy of the court certificate showing you were appointed.
- [ ] **Everyone else claiming a refund:** attach Form 1310, and bring proof of your relationship.
- [ ] **Amended returns:** Form 1310 is attached to Form 1040-X claims even if it was sent earlier.

## Common stumbles

- Paying bills from the estate account before checking whether the estate is solvent.
- Filing the return too early, before all 1099s arrive.
- Leaving out income earned from January 1 to the date of death.
- Forgetting the state return.

## When to talk to an attorney

- There is no appointed executor and a refund is expected.
- The decedent owed tax, or returns were not filed for earlier years.
- The estate has little money and many debts.
- Family members disagree about who should file.
- The decedent had income from a business or from out of state.
