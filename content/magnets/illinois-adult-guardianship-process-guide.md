**How to use this guide.** Read the alternatives first, since guardianship is usually the last tool. If a court case is still the route, follow the flow and fill in the checklist. Illinois adult guardianship is under Article XIa of the Probate Act, 755 ILCS 5/11a-1 and following. Other states use other names and steps. This is general education, not legal advice.

## What adult guardianship is

A guardian is a person a court appoints to make decisions for an adult the court finds cannot make or communicate responsible decisions because of a disability. In Illinois the law prefers the least restrictive route, and a court may limit a guardian's powers to what the person cannot do. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 5/11a-3 standard and limited guardianship preference -->

There are two parts:

| Role | Covers |
|---|---|
| Guardian of the person | Where the person lives, medical care, daily needs |
| Guardian of the estate | Money, bills, property |

A court can appoint one person for both, or two people.

## Try these first

Guardianship takes away rights. Ask if a signed document already does the job.

| Tool | Works if | Covered in |
|---|---|---|
| Power of attorney for property | The person still has capacity to sign it | [Illinois power of attorney for property guide](/free/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property-guide) |
| Healthcare power of attorney | The person can still name an agent | [Illinois health care POA guide](/free/illinois-health-care-poa-guide) |
| Supported decision-making, joint accounts, representative payee | Needs are narrow | [guardianship alternatives guide](/free/guardianship-alternatives-guide) |

If the person has already lost capacity and has no documents, guardianship may be the only route.

## The Illinois process step by step

1. **Gather the facts.** Collect diagnoses, doctor names, bank details, property and family names and addresses.
2. **Get the medical report.** A licensed doctor, psychologist or other qualified professional completes a report on the person's condition and abilities. The law sets a time limit on how recent the report must be. <!-- verify: medical report timing and form under 755 ILCS 5/11a-9 -->
3. **File a petition** in the circuit court of the county where the person lives. The petition names the person, the proposed guardian and the reasons.
4. **Notice.** The person and close relatives must be told. The person must get notice of the hearing and the right to attend and to a lawyer.
5. **Guardian ad litem.** The court usually appoints one to meet the person and report back. See below.
6. **Hearing.** The judge weighs the evidence. The person can object, bring witnesses and ask for a jury.
7. **Order.** The court names the guardian, lists the powers and may set a review date.
8. **Letters of office and bond.** The guardian receives the official document needed to act, and the court may require a bond for the estate.
9. **Reports.** The guardian files reports on a schedule.

Court forms and fees are published by the circuit court clerk, and local rules vary by county. See [Cook County probate guide](/free/cook-county-probate-guide) for one example county.

## Medical evidence checklist

- [ ] A current written report from a qualified professional
- [ ] The diagnosis and how it affects decision making
- [ ] What the person can and cannot still do
- [ ] A statement of the person's ability to attend court
- [ ] Records from recent hospital stays or nursing home care
- [ ] Notes on the person's wishes if known

A diagnosis alone does not prove the need. The court looks at function.

## Guardian ad litem

A guardian ad litem is a lawyer or other person appointed by the court to look into the case and tell the judge what is best for the person. It is not the person's own lawyer. Costs can be charged to the estate or the petitioner. The person also has the right to ask for their own attorney.

## Emergency and temporary guardianship

Illinois allows a temporary guardian when waiting would harm the person. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 5/11a-4 temporary guardian time limits and notice -->

| Feature | Emergency or temporary order | Plenary or limited guardianship |
|---|---|---|
| Time frame | Short, with set limits | Long term, reviewed |
| Notice | Can be shorter, still required | Full notice and hearing |
| Powers | Limited to the emergency need | Based on evidence |
| When used | Sudden crisis, such as a hospital discharge with no decision maker | Ongoing incapacity |

You do not become guardian just by asking. The judge decides after hearing evidence.

## Rights and limits

A guardian's powers depend on the order. A court can give the guardian authority over living arrangements, medical choices and money. A person under guardianship may lose control of money, the right to sign contracts and to choose where to live, depending on the order. Some rights, such as voting and marriage, are specific questions. <!-- verify: which rights are retained or lost under Illinois guardianship -->

Major actions such as selling a home or moving a person to a care facility can require court approval or notice. Ask the order's exact words.

## Reports

An adult's guardian generally must file reports with the court, including an annual report on the person's condition and living situation and an accounting of money for a guardian of the estate. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 5/11a-17 and 11a-18 report schedule and contents -->

- [ ] Keep every receipt
- [ ] Keep funds separate from your own
- [ ] Log visits, doctor appointments and decisions
- [ ] Ask the court for a form or the local rule

## Lawyer or no lawyer

Some people file without a lawyer. Courts publish forms and self-help centers exist. The case is still a court case with notice, a medical report, a hearing and rules on reports. A person with strong objections or a complicated estate may need one. The person at the center has the right to counsel.

## If you disagree

| Your position | Options |
|---|---|
| You are the person, or you believe a guardianship is not needed | Object, ask for your own attorney, and ask for a hearing. After an order, ask the court to change or end it |
| Sibling files for your parent | Ask the clerk for the notice, attend, tell the court your view, propose yourself or a different plan |
| A guardian misuses money or neglects the person | Write down what you saw and tell the court. A guardian can be removed or ordered to account |

Guardianship can be contested at the start and later. A judge can limit or end it if the person improves or a guardian fails.

## Costs

Costs include filing fees, a medical report, a guardian ad litem and attorney fees. Expect to ask the clerk for the current schedule. <!-- verify: Illinois filing fee schedule -->

## When to talk to an attorney

- A parent lost capacity and has no power of attorney
- A hospital is pushing for a decision maker fast
- Family members disagree about who should serve
- You worry a guardian is misusing funds
- You are the person facing guardianship
