**How to use this checklist.** A payable-on-death (POD) designation on a bank account and a transfer-on-death (TOD) registration on a brokerage account let the account pass directly to the person you name. No probate is needed for those accounts. The beneficiary usually shows a death certificate and ID. This checklist helps you set up each account one at a time. For background, see the [guide to POD and TOD](/guides/transfer-on-death-and-payable-on-death). Rules and forms vary by institution and state.

Accounts that do not use POD or TOD, and why:

- IRAs, 401(k)s and life insurance have their own beneficiary forms, not a POD form.
- Joint accounts with survivorship pass to the surviving owner first. The POD applies after the last owner dies.
- Accounts held in a trust follow the trust, not a POD.
- Real estate usually uses a deed, not an account form. Some states allow a transfer on death deed.

## Step 2: Decide who gets what

- **Primary beneficiary:** ______________________
- **Share (percent or equal):** ______________________
- **Second primary, if sharing:** ______________________
- **Backup if the first has died:** ______________________
- **Backup share:** ______________________

Questions to ask:

- [ ] Do my accounts, my will and my beneficiary forms agree with each other?
- [ ] If I have two or more children, do I want equal shares? Use percentages that add up to 100.
- [ ] If a child dies before me, do I want the child's children to receive that share? Many forms handle this only if you pick the right wording, such as "per stirpes."
- [ ] Is any beneficiary a minor? If so, I will not name the child directly. I will ask about a custodian under a state gifts-to-minors law, or a trust.
- [ ] Does any beneficiary receive government benefits, such as SSI or Medicaid? A direct payment could cause them to lose benefits. I will ask an attorney about a special needs trust.
- [ ] Would I rather name a charity for part of an account?

## Step 3: Check the form

For each institution, ask for the POD or TOD form, or find it online.

- [ ] I am the owner of the account, and the name matches the account exactly.
- [ ] I wrote each beneficiary's full legal name, date of birth and relationship.
- [ ] I did not write "my children" or "my family" in place of names.
- [ ] I did not name "my estate" unless I want the money to go through probate.
- [ ] The shares total 100 percent.
- [ ] I named a backup, or checked the box for lineal descendants if that is what I want.
- [ ] I signed and dated where required. Some banks require a witness or a notary.
- [ ] Brokerage forms may separate TOD "per stirpes" from "per capita." I asked what each means.

## Step 4: Submit and confirm

- [ ] I sent the form by the method the institution asks for: branch, mail or secure message.
- [ ] I asked for written confirmation, and I saved it.
- [ ] I logged in a month later to check the beneficiary shows correctly on the account.
- [ ] I kept a copy of each form.

## Step 5: Fit it into the whole plan

POD and TOD accounts are non-probate. They avoid the will, which means they must be thought about together.

| Problem | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Unequal results | Child A is on three accounts and Child B on none, while the will says equal | Align shares, or leave a note and adjust the will |
| Not enough cash for the estate | Everything is POD, so the executor has none to pay bills | Keep an account for the estate, or make a plan to share costs |
| Taxes or debts | Heirs of POD accounts may be liable in some states if the estate cannot pay | Ask an attorney about your state |
| A new life event | Divorce, marriage, a birth or a death | Review within 30 days |
| An older form | The bank switched systems and lost the record | Ask the bank to confirm every few years |

## Common mistakes

- Setting up POD names and never telling the beneficiary where to look.
- Naming a person who has died, with no backup.
- Naming a minor directly.
- Forgetting that a divorce may not remove an ex from a bank form in every state.
- Leaving a minor child's share to the guardian by assumption. The form only pays who is named.
- Not updating after the bank merges or the account is moved.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You want unequal shares or conditions, such as paying out over time.
- A beneficiary is a minor, has a disability or receives public benefits.
- You have a trust and want accounts to flow into it.
- You own accounts jointly and are not sure how they interact with POD.
- You are divorcing, remarrying or blending a family.
- Your state allows a transfer on death deed and you are weighing it for your home.
