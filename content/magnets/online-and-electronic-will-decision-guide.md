**How to use this guide.** Answer the situation questions first, then check the signing rules for your state. Online tools are not all alike, and the law on e-signing and remote witnessing changed quickly in the past few years and still differs by state. This is general education, not legal advice.

## What an online will can and cannot do

An online will is a form you fill in on a website. The site produces a document you print, sign and have witnessed, or in some states sign electronically. Whether it is valid depends on how it is signed, not on where it was made.

A will made online is generally valid if it meets your state's signing rules. The form does not decide that. The signing does.

| Part of the process | Online tool | Attorney |
|---|---|---|
| Producing a document from your answers | Yes | Yes |
| Checking your answers for legal conflicts | Rarely | Yes |
| Advising on beneficiary forms and joint titles | Rarely | Yes |
| Supervising the signing | No | Yes |
| Handling a family with special needs or a business | Not well | Yes |
| Price | Lower | Higher |

## Decide with this table

| If this is you | Then consider |
|---|---|
| Single, no children, simple assets, clear wishes | An online will plus careful signing may be enough |
| Married with young children and a home | Attorney help, or at least an attorney review |
| Blended family, second marriage, or children from other relationships | Attorney |
| Child or adult with a disability | Attorney, to protect benefits |
| Business or rental property | Attorney |
| Property in more than one state | Attorney licensed where the property sits |
| Large estate near a tax threshold | Attorney, with a tax advisor |
| Short on money and simple needs | Low cost options below, then an attorney check |

## E-wills, electronic signing and Zoom

An electronic will is one signed and stored in digital form. A growing number of states allow them. Illinois has an electronic wills statute at 755 ILCS 6/, which also covers remote witnesses. <!-- verify: Illinois Electronic Wills and Remote Witnesses Act citation, effective date and requirements -->

| Method | Typical rule | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Print, sign with ink, two in-person witnesses | Accepted in every state | Witnesses present, not beneficiaries |
| E-signature on a will | Allowed only where state law permits | State statute on e-wills |
| Witnessing over video (Zoom) | Allowed in some states, with conditions | Recording, identity check and statute |
| Remote online notarization | Allowed in many states for some documents | Whether it applies to wills and which state's notary |
| Signing a will by text or email | Not a valid will | Treat as a draft |

Remote rules for wills are narrower than for contracts. A website's "e-sign" button for a contract does not mean a will is valid that way.

Documents that often work well electronically include powers of attorney, healthcare directives in states that allow it, and beneficiary forms. Check each document's rule. See [Illinois signing requirements cheat sheet](/free/illinois-signing-requirements-cheat-sheet).

## Is an online will valid in my state?

- [ ] I know my state's signing rule (number of witnesses, notary, e-signing)
- [ ] The service says it supports my state
- [ ] I will sign in the exact way my state requires
- [ ] Witnesses are adults who are not named in the will
- [ ] I added a self-proving affidavit or notarization if my state offers it
- [ ] I stored the original, not only a PDF
- [ ] I will update the will after major life events

If you move, check again. Read [moving states estate plan checklist](/free/moving-states-estate-plan-checklist).

## Vetting an online service

Ask these before you pay:

1. Who owns the company and where is it based?
2. Does it say what it does not do?
3. Does it list the states it supports?
4. Is there a way to reach a licensed attorney for questions?
5. What happens to my data and my documents?
6. Is the price a one time fee or a subscription?
7. Can I download the documents and keep them?
8. How do I update, and what does it cost?

Red flags: promises that a will "avoids probate", no state-specific signing instructions, and pressure to buy add-ons you do not understand. See the [DIY will red flags checklist](/free/diy-will-red-flags-checklist).

## Common mistakes

- Treating a PDF as the signed will
- Signing with only one witness or with a beneficiary as witness
- Ignoring the beneficiary forms on retirement accounts and insurance
- Using a kit written for another state
- Never updating the will
- Using a will where a trust is the better tool

## When to talk to an attorney

- Your family or assets are not simple
- The online service cannot answer a question about your state
- You are signing remotely and the rules are unclear
- You already used a template and want it reviewed
- You have a deadline, such as surgery or travel
