## How to use this kit

We are sorry you are making these decisions now. Funeral choices come fast, and cost is often the hardest part. This kit covers who decides, who pays, and where help may come from. It is general information for Illinois, not legal advice.

## Who decides

Illinois has a law on the disposition of remains: the Disposition of Remains Act, 755 ILCS 65. <!-- verify: act citation and priority list order --> It sets an order of people who have the right to control the arrangements, unless the person left written directions. The order is commonly:

| Order | Person |
|---|---|
| 1 | The person named in the deceased's written directions or a power of attorney for health care |
| 2 | The surviving spouse or civil union partner |
| 3 | Adult children, a majority if more than one |
| 4 | Parents |
| 5 | Adult siblings |
| 6 | Other relatives, then a guardian or others |

Check the statute for the exact order. Other states have their own lists.

## If the family disagrees

- [ ] Look for written wishes: a will, a letter, a prepaid funeral contract
- [ ] Ask the funeral home which person signs the contract
- [ ] Offer two or three options that cover religious, cost and location concerns
- [ ] Agree on cremation or burial before talking about the service
- [ ] If the dispute stays stuck, a court can decide, which is slow and expensive

Say this in the group chat: "Dad's wishes come first. Does anyone know if he wrote anything down?"

## Who pays

| Situation | Usually who pays |
|---|---|
| Estate has money | The estate pays from probate assets. Funeral costs are paid early in the order of claims. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 5/18-10 classes --> |
| Prepaid plan or burial policy | The plan pays, check how to claim |
| Whoever signed the contract | The signer is liable to the funeral home until paid back |
| Family contributions | Agreed informally, put it in writing |
| Nobody | County or local assistance may help, see below |

Relatives are not automatically liable for a funeral bill unless they signed. Ask before signing anything.

## Paying and getting reimbursed

1. **Save every receipt.** Funeral home, flowers, cemetery, obituary.
2. **Ask the executor in writing.** The estate can usually reimburse reasonable costs.
3. **If there is no estate account yet,** pay with your own funds and ask for repayment when the account opens.
4. **Reasonable is the word.** Costs far above the family's standard may be questioned.
5. **Funeral costs rank high** among claims, but only if the estate has money.

## Help when there is no money

- **Local government:** counties and townships in Illinois may have an indigent burial program. Ask the county or township office. <!-- verify: Illinois county burial assistance process -->
- **Funeral home payment plans:** many offer them
- **Crowdfunding and community:** churches, unions, employers
- **Body donation:** some programs cover cremation
- **Limited time:** low-cost direct cremation can be arranged within days

Ask about help before signing a contract, not after.

## Benefits that may help

| Benefit | What to know |
|---|---|
| Social Security death benefit | A small one-time payment, available to a spouse or child who qualifies. The amount is set by law. <!-- verify: current lump sum amount --> Apply through Social Security. |
| VA burial benefits | Veterans may qualify for burial in a national cemetery and an allowance. <!-- verify: current VA burial allowance --> Ask the VA or funeral director. |
| Medicare | Does not pay funeral costs. |
| Life insurance or burial policy | Check the policy and claim with a death certificate |
| Union, employer, fraternal groups | Ask their member services |

The funeral home commonly reports the death to Social Security. You should still call to confirm.

## When to talk to an attorney

- Family members are in open conflict about arrangements
- You are being asked to sign a large contract with no estate money
- The estate may be insolvent and you want to know if you will be repaid
- Someone is blocking access to the body or the service
