**How to use this planner.** Trust administration has a rhythm: quiet first weeks, then a long middle of gathering and paying, then a careful ending. Timing depends on the trust's size, whether real estate must be sold, tax filings and whether anyone disputes things. The ranges below are common patterns, not promises. Fill in your own dates as you go.

## Phase 1: Immediate steps (first month)

Goal: secure, notify, organize.

- [ ] Death certificates ordered
- [ ] Trust document located and read
- [ ] Beneficiaries identified and notified in writing (many states set a deadline; Illinois uses 90 days)
- [ ] EIN obtained and trust account opened
- [ ] Property secured and insured

## Phase 2: Gather and value (months 1 to 3)

Goal: know what is in the trust, and what it is worth.

- [ ] Inventory every asset with date-of-death value
- [ ] Order appraisals for real estate or business interests
- [ ] Identify assets that need probate because they were never retitled
- [ ] Gather debts and unpaid bills
- [ ] Choose a tax preparer

## Phase 3: Pay and protect (months 2 to 9)

Goal: settle valid claims, keep assets safe, hold steady.

- [ ] Final income tax return for the person who died
- [ ] Creditor claims reviewed (state rules vary on whether and how a trust handles claims)
- [ ] Expenses paid from trust funds with receipts
- [ ] Ongoing home costs paid
- [ ] Decisions about selling or keeping real estate made, with written reasons

What slows this phase: a house that is hard to sell, an out-of-state asset, a business with no manager, or a lost document.

## Phase 4: Taxes and reserves (months 6 to 15)

Goal: file what is due and hold back enough to cover it.

- [ ] Trust income tax return (Form 1041) prepared
- [ ] Estate tax return if needed (federal exemption in 2026 is $15,000,000 per person; some states tax at lower levels)
- [ ] Reserve set aside for taxes, final fees and any open claims
- [ ] Question for tax preparer: should we use a fiscal year or calendar year for the trust?

**Reserve I plan to hold back:** ______

## Phase 5: Distribution (months 9 to 18, often longer)

Goal: give each beneficiary what the trust says, in the right order.

- [ ] Specific gifts distributed (items, set sums)
- [ ] Remaining property divided as the trust directs
- [ ] Interim distributions considered only if funds are enough for open claims
- [ ] Receipts signed by each beneficiary
- [ ] Trusts for minors or children set up if the trust calls for continued trusts

Sample receipt line:

> I, [Name], received [description or amount] from the [Name] Trust on [date] as part of my distribution. I understand it is part of the final settlement.

## Phase 6: Closing

Goal: finish cleanly so no one can say later that anything was missed.

- [ ] Final accounting or summary prepared for beneficiaries
- [ ] Beneficiaries given time to review it
- [ ] Release or consent signed where appropriate
- [ ] Final tax return filed
- [ ] Trust bank account closed
- [ ] Records kept (many advisors suggest keeping them for several years; ask yours)

## Pace and patience

It is normal for administration to take a year or more. Beneficiaries sometimes want faster payment. Send brief updates every month or two, even when nothing has changed. Updates cut down on worry and complaints.

## When to talk to an attorney

- A beneficiary wants to contest the trust or asks for a full accounting.
- The trust owns real estate in another state.
- Creditor claims exceed assets, or you are unsure whether the trust is solvent.
- The trust contains ongoing trusts for children or a person with a disability.
- You are unsure whether to distribute before taxes are settled.
- You need help preparing the final accounting.
