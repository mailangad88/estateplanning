## How to use this guide

An agent under a power of attorney holds a position of trust. Most agents do the job honestly. When one does not, families often wait too long because they are unsure what counts as misuse. This guide covers Illinois, where powers of attorney are governed by the Illinois Power of Attorney Act, 755 ILCS 45. Other states differ. It is general education, not legal advice.

## Duties of an Illinois agent

| Duty | What it means |
|---|---|
| Act in good faith | Use the power for the principal's benefit |
| Follow the document | Stay within the powers granted |
| Keep records | Track receipts, payments and transactions |
| Keep money separate | Do not mix the principal's money with the agent's |
| Avoid conflicts | Do not benefit from deals without clear permission |
| Account when asked | Provide records when the law or the document requires |

<!-- verify: 755 ILCS 45/2-7 duties of agents and 755 ILCS 45/2-8 duty to account -->

The principal, a guardian, a successor agent and others named in the document can usually ask for an accounting. Whether family members who are not named have a right to information is a different question. Often they do not, until a court says so.

## Warning signs

- [ ] Bills go unpaid and utilities are shut off
- [ ] The agent has a new car, trip or home improvement
- [ ] The principal is isolated from family and friends
- [ ] The principal's accounts show big withdrawals or transfers to the agent
- [ ] The agent refuses to show records
- [ ] A deed, will or beneficiary change appears that the principal did not understand
- [ ] The agent has taken on all decisions and pushed others out
- [ ] Documents the principal signed recently are out of character

One sign alone may have an innocent reason. A pattern is a reason to ask questions.

## Step flow

1. **Ask politely.** Request an accounting for the past period in writing.
2. **Get records.** Ask the bank or plan administrator what you are entitled to see.
3. **Check the document.** Read what the agent may do, who may receive an accounting, and whether a monitor was named.
4. **Report.** If funds are missing, report to Adult Protective Services and, if it appears criminal, to the police. <!-- verify: Illinois APS reporting process and hotline -->
5. **Freeze if you can.** If you are the principal or have authority, revoke the power and give written notice to institutions.
6. **Ask the court.** Illinois law allows an interested person to petition the court to review an agent's conduct, require an accounting, or remove the agent. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 45/2-10.3 petition for review and relief -->

## Court options

| Goal | Court step |
|---|---|
| See what happened | Petition for an accounting |
| Stop the misuse now | Request an emergency order |
| Replace the agent | Ask for removal, and confirm who becomes successor |
| Get money back | Seek restitution and, in some cases, attorney fees |
| Replace the power of attorney with oversight | Petition for guardianship |
| Cancel documents signed under pressure | Challenge for lack of capacity or undue influence |

An agent who breaks the law can be responsible for losses and can face criminal charges. Agents can also be sued for honest mistakes if they were careless, though a document may limit that. <!-- verify: Illinois standard of liability for agents and exculpation clauses -->

## Siblings who disagree

A sibling who is not the agent may disagree about money, care or the house. That alone is not misuse. Disagreement becomes actionable when there is evidence of self-dealing, neglect or acting outside the document.

Before going to court:

- [ ] Ask the agent to share records voluntarily
- [ ] Hold a family meeting with a neutral facilitator
- [ ] Review the document together
- [ ] Ask whether a successor or co-agent is named

If the principal still has capacity, the best answer is often for the principal to make changes. A principal can revoke and sign a new document naming someone else.

## Safeguards to build into a new power of attorney

| Safeguard | What it does |
|---|---|
| Named monitor | A person who receives copies of statements or an annual accounting |
| Second signature | Large transactions need a co-signer, such as a bank or another trusted person |
| Dollar limits | Caps on gifts or sales without approval |
| Co-agents | Two people who must act together |
| No self-gifts | A clear ban on gifts to the agent |
| Report duty | Agent gives a summary to named family each year |
| Successor agent | A named backup, so a removal does not leave a gap |

Illinois allows the principal to include many of these. Ask your drafter to confirm how each is worded. <!-- verify: Illinois statutory support for monitors and co-signature requirements -->

## When to talk to an attorney

- Money is missing or a deed or beneficiary has changed
- The agent refuses to share records
- The principal lacks capacity and cannot revoke
- You want to remove an agent or ask for a guardian
- You are the agent and have been accused
