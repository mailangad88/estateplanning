## How to use this kit

We are sorry for your loss. Searching a loved one's papers is slow work. This kit puts the places to look in order, with Illinois steps for the court. Fill in the log as you go. It is general information, not legal advice. Other states use different offices and rules.

## Part 1: Find the will

An original will matters. Illinois law requires anyone who has a will to file it with the circuit court clerk in the county where the person lived within 30 days after the death is known (755 ILCS 5/6-1). <!-- verify: current 30-day wording and penalties --> So the court file is one of the best places to look.

### Search order

1. **The home.** Fireproof boxes, desks, files, a folder labeled "will" or "important."
2. **The attorney.** Check old bills, letters, checks to a law firm, or an email. Call and ask whether they hold an original.
3. **The circuit court clerk.** Ask whether a will or estate case is on file. Search by name.
4. **The safe deposit box.** See Part 2.
5. **Family and friends.** Executor, a trusted friend, a faith leader.
6. **Financial advisor or accountant.** Sometimes holds a copy.
7. **Illinois Secretary of State.** A will can be deposited there for safekeeping. <!-- verify: Illinois Secretary of State will deposit program --> Ask whether one exists.

### Who may see a will

- **Before filing:** the person holding it must file it. Nobody is entitled to a private copy, but an executor must act on it.
- **After filing:** it is part of the court record and generally public. Anyone can ask the clerk for a copy, for a fee.
- **Beneficiaries and heirs:** are entitled to notice of the case, which includes the will. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 5/6-10 notice contents -->

If an executor will not share the will, ask the clerk first. If the will was never filed, an attorney can ask the court to order the holder to file it. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 5/6-1 enforcement -->

### What if no will turns up

- [ ] Search twice, a few weeks apart
- [ ] Put a notice in the mail forwarding and check mail for law firm letters
- [ ] Ask the clerk to check again later
- [ ] If none is found, the estate generally passes by intestacy, see [Illinois intestacy chart](/free/illinois-intestacy-chart)

A copy sometimes helps, but proving a lost will requires a court process. Talk to an attorney.

## Part 2: Safe deposit boxes

| Question | Usual answer |
|---|---|
| How do I know there is a box? | Look for keys, rental bills, bank statements, tax deductions |
| Can I just open it? | No. Banks generally require legal authority. |
| Who can get in? | A co-renter, or an executor with letters, or a court order. |
| Illinois shortcut | A person may be allowed to look for a will only, with a bank officer present. <!-- verify: Illinois safe deposit box statute for will search --> |
| What if I have no letters yet? | Ask the bank about its procedure for a will search |

Script: "My mother died on ____________. I think she had a safe deposit box. I would like to know whether one exists and how to look for a will, with a bank employee present."

## Part 3: Find the accounts

| Where to look | What you may find |
|---|---|
| Mail from the last 12 months | Statements, 1099 forms, bills |
| Tax returns | Interest and dividends point to accounts |
| Checkbook registers and bank statements | Regular payments to other institutions |
| Email and phone apps | Statements, account alerts |
| Credit report | Loans and cards, not bank accounts |
| Employer | Final pay, retirement plans, insurance |
| Unclaimed property | See below |

Request a credit report for the person who died through the credit bureaus, and place a deceased flag. See [identity theft after death checklist](/free/identity-theft-after-death-checklist).

## Part 4: Life insurance

- [ ] Check the file for policies and premium notices
- [ ] Ask the employer about group life insurance
- [ ] Check bank statements for premium payments
- [ ] Ask the insurance agent or financial advisor
- [ ] Search the National Association of Insurance Commissioners policy locator <!-- verify: NAIC policy locator service -->
- [ ] Search unclaimed property

## Part 5: Unclaimed property

Illinois holds unclaimed money through the State Treasurer. Search the deceased's name and old addresses at the Illinois I-Cash site. <!-- verify: I-Cash site and claim process for deceased owners --> Many states have their own program, and multi-state searches exist. The claim for a deceased owner usually needs a death certificate and proof you are the executor or heir.

## Common mistakes

- Throwing away mail before sorting it
- Assuming no will because the home has none
- Trying to open accounts or a box with no authority
- Assuming a joint account or beneficiary form was never set up

## When to talk to an attorney

- A will cannot be found and the family disagrees
- Someone is holding a will and not filing it
- A bank refuses to talk to you
- You find assets in other states
- The size of the estate is unclear
