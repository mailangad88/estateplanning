## How to use this guide

There are two different questions here. One is whether the person who died had creditors. The other is whether the person who inherits has them. The answers differ, and so do the tools. This guide covers both and shows where Illinois law helps. It is general education, and exemption statutes get amended, so check each cite before you rely on it.

## Creditors of the person who died

Assets with a named beneficiary pass outside the estate. Creditors of the estate usually go after probate assets first. But beneficiary payments are not always out of reach.

| Asset | Reachable by the decedent's creditors? |
|---|---|
| Life insurance with a named person | Usually not, when a person is named |
| Retirement account with a named person | Usually not, though taxes can still be owed |
| Probate property | Yes, in priority order |
| Joint accounts | Sometimes, depending on who contributed |
| Estate named as beneficiary | Yes. It becomes a probate asset |
| Revocable trust | Often yes, if the estate lacks funds to pay valid debts |

In Illinois, a living trust is not an automatic shield. Claims against an estate can reach a revocable trust's assets in some cases. <!-- verify: Illinois statute on revocable trust liability for settlor's debts, 760 ILCS 3/505 -->

Can an executor claw back a payout? Usually not from a person who was properly named on a life insurance or retirement form. But if the estate owes taxes, or if a transfer was a fraudulent one, a court can order it back. A joint account's balance is more open to claims.

## Creditors of the person who inherits

Once the money is in a beneficiary's hands, their own debts matter.

| Asset received | Protection from the beneficiary's creditors |
|---|---|
| Cash in a bank account | None. A judgment creditor can seek it |
| Life insurance proceeds | Illinois protects some insurance proceeds, with conditions. See 215 ILCS 5/238 <!-- verify: scope of 215 ILCS 5/238 for beneficiaries --> |
| Inherited traditional or Roth IRA | Not protected in federal bankruptcy (the Supreme Court held this in 2014). Illinois state law differs; check 735 ILCS 5/12-1006 <!-- verify: whether 12-1006 covers inherited IRAs --> |
| Spouse who rolls the IRA over | Usually treated like the spouse's own IRA |
| Inheritance held in a spendthrift trust | Often protected while it stays in the trust |

Child support and back taxes rank differently. A support agency or the IRS may reach assets that other creditors cannot. A trust with a spendthrift clause may still be reached for support. <!-- verify: Illinois spendthrift trust exceptions, 760 ILCS 3/503 -->

## Divorce

Inherited money is generally treated as the beneficiary's separate property in Illinois, so long as it is kept separate. Mixing it into a joint account or putting the spouse on a deed can turn it into marital property that a court may divide. A decree can also divide an inherited IRA in some situations. <!-- verify: Illinois treatment of inherited property in 750 ILCS 5/503 -->

Ways families reduce exposure:

1. Leave the money in trust and not outright
2. Name the trust itself as the account beneficiary where appropriate
3. Tell the beneficiary to keep inherited funds in a separate account
4. Avoid putting a spouse's name on a deed or title

## How a spendthrift trust works

A spendthrift clause says the beneficiary cannot sell or pledge their interest, and creditors cannot attach it before the trustee pays it out. The trustee controls distributions. Once money is paid out to the beneficiary, it can be reached.

| Feature | Outright gift | Trust with spendthrift clause |
|---|---|---|
| Creditor can attach it | Yes | Often no until paid out |
| Divorce | Possible claim if mixed | Better protection while held in trust |
| Beneficiary controls timing | Yes | Trustee decides, or follows terms |
| Cost and effort | Low | Higher: trustee, tax filing |

## Checklist before you finalize

- [ ] Beneficiary forms name people or trusts, not the estate
- [ ] A contingent beneficiary is listed on every form
- [ ] Any trust receiving retirement money meets the IRS see-through tests
- [ ] A beneficiary who is disabled has a special needs trust. See the [special needs trust guide](/guides/special-needs-trusts)
- [ ] You told the trustee, not the beneficiary, about the spendthrift terms

## Common mistakes

- Naming a vulnerable beneficiary directly and hoping for the best
- Naming the estate and exposing the account to creditors
- Putting the money in a joint account with a child who has debts
- Assuming a trust protects the grantor's own assets. A self-settled trust often does not

## When to talk to an attorney

- A beneficiary has a pending lawsuit, bankruptcy, or support order
- You are leaving an IRA to a trust
- You own a business or have significant assets that could draw claims
- A beneficiary is in a marriage that is in trouble
- You are not sure whether a debt could reach what you are leaving
