## How to use this guide

We are sorry you are facing this. Kane County is the 16th Judicial Circuit of Illinois, and probate is heard in Geneva. This guide shows the main steps and the two places you may need to visit. Details change, so confirm them with the clerk or an attorney. This is education, not legal advice.

## Does the estate belong in Kane County?

Illinois sends a probate case to the county where the person had a known place of residence (755 ILCS 5/5-1). If the person lived in Kane County, in a town such as Aurora, Elgin, St. Charles or Geneva, the case belongs here. Aurora and Elgin reach into other counties, so check the county of the home address. If the person lived outside Illinois, the rules look to where their Illinois real estate or personal property sits, so ask an attorney.

Next, ask whether a court case is needed at all. A small estate affidavit may work when personal property is $150,000 or less for a death on or after August 15, 2025. Real estate in one person's name, or a family dispute, usually means a court case. See the [small estate affidavit guide](/free/illinois-small-estate-affidavit-guide).

## Where to go

The Kane County Courthouse is at 100 South Third Street, Geneva, IL 60134. The Illinois Courts directory lists its phone as (630) 208-5145 and its hours as 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and says it houses most civil courts, including probate. The Clerk of the Circuit Court's site gives a records office at 719 South Batavia Avenue, Building B, Geneva, IL 60134, phone (630) 232-5950. Confirm with the clerk which address handles probate filings and the original will.

## Local notes

- **Case search online:** The clerk's site links to an online court records portal. You can search by case number or party name and filter by case type. It helps you check that a filing was received and see the next hearing date.
- **Probate records:** The clerk keeps probate records, which cover estates, wills and guardianships. Records of an estate case are part of the public file.
- **Local court rules:** The 16th Circuit has local rules that apply on top of state law. Ask the clerk or your attorney where to find the probate section before you file.
- **Filing:** Probate papers are generally filed electronically. The clerk's site does not spell out the will deposit steps, so call before you carry the original in.

## The Illinois steps that apply here

These steps come from the state Probate Act, so they are the same in every county.

| Step | What happens | Rule to know |
|---|---|---|
| File the will | The person holding the original gives it to the circuit clerk | Do it promptly. Hiding a will for 30 days after learning of the death can be a felony (755 ILCS 5/6-1) |
| Notice to heirs | Heirs and legatees get a copy of the petition and the order | Within 14 days after the order (755 ILCS 5/6-10) |
| Notice to creditors | Published once a week for three weeks in a county newspaper, and mailed to known creditors | Claim date is the later of six months from first publication or three months from mailing (755 ILCS 5/18-3) |
| Inventory | A verified list of assets is filed | Within 60 days after letters issue (755 ILCS 5/14-1) |
| Outer claim limit | Late claims are cut off | Two years after death (755 ILCS 5/18-12) |

For family members, an interested person can ask for formal proof of the will within 42 days after it is admitted (755 ILCS 5/6-21). A will contest must be filed within six months after admission (755 ILCS 5/8-1). Our [Illinois probate checklist](/free/illinois-probate-checklist) walks through every stage.

## When to talk to an attorney

- The will names no executor, or the person named cannot serve
- A relative may contest the will or disagrees about who should serve
- The estate owns real estate, a business or property in another state
- You are not sure the small estate affidavit is allowed
- A creditor sends a claim you do not understand
- You are asked to sign court papers and are not sure what they do
