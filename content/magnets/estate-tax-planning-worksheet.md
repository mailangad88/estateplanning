## How to use this worksheet

This worksheet gives you a rough estimate, not a tax return. Work in round numbers. Use today's values, then repeat the exercise at a higher value to see how growth could change the answer. For a quick calculation you can also use the [estate tax estimator](/tools/estate-tax-estimator).

## Step 1: Know the two taxes

The federal estate tax applies only to estates above the exemption. For 2026 the federal basic exclusion amount is $15,000,000 per person. It is adjusted for inflation in later years.

Some states add their own estate tax or an inheritance tax. Their thresholds are often far lower than the federal figure, and rules vary by state. Illinois, for example, taxes estates above a much lower level than the federal one. Check your state before you decide you are safe.

## Step 6: Married couples and portability

A married couple has two exemptions. If the first spouse to die does not use all of theirs, the unused amount can be carried to the survivor. This is called portability. It is elected by filing a federal estate tax return (Form 706) for the first spouse's estate, even when no tax is due. The IRS describes the election on its estate tax pages.

Two cautions. Portability applies to the federal exemption only, and many states do not allow it, so a state-level exemption can be lost if the first spouse's share is not planned for. Portability also does not carry forward the unused exclusion from the generation-skipping tax.

## Common mistakes

- Counting only the house and forgetting life insurance and retirement accounts.
- Assuming the federal threshold also protects you from a state tax.
- Skipping the portability election because "no tax was due."
- Using purchase prices instead of current values.
- Forgetting that beneficiary-designated assets still count toward the estate.

## When to talk to an attorney

- Your net estate is within reach of a federal or state threshold.
- You live in, or own property in, a state with its own estate or inheritance tax.
- You own a business, farmland or life insurance policies with large death benefits.
- You are widowed and your late spouse's estate never filed a return.
- You plan large gifts to children or trusts.
