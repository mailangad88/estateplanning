## How to use this planner

An inheritance rarely has to be decided in a week. The most expensive mistakes come from fast moves: spending, mixing funds, or accepting money that costs you a benefit. Use the tables to check what your inheritance touches. This is general education. Rules vary by state and by program.

## First 30 days: do and wait

- [ ] Put any money in a separate account in your own name
- [ ] Do not commingle it with a joint account if you are married or may divorce
- [ ] Ask the executor or trustee for a written list of what you will receive
- [ ] Keep every letter and statement
- [ ] Wait before large purchases, loans to family or investments
- [ ] Tell a benefits caseworker if you receive Medicaid or SSI, before the money arrives

## What is coming, and when

Estates are usually not closed quickly. Distribution often waits for creditor claim periods and tax filings, so expect many months at least. Executors can sometimes make a partial early distribution, but are cautious because they can be personally responsible if they pay too early.

| Source | How you learn the amount | Typical wait |
|---|---|---|
| Will through probate | Ask the executor, or look at the court file | Until debts, claims and taxes are handled |
| Trust | Ask the trustee for the trust terms and an accounting | Depends on the trust terms |
| Beneficiary designation (life insurance, IRA, POD) | Contact the company with a death certificate | Often weeks, not months |
| Joint account | You already own it | Immediate with a death certificate |

## Effects check

| If this applies | What can happen | What to do first |
|---|---|---|
| You get Medicaid | Cash can count as a resource and may end eligibility or create a penalty | Report it and ask about spending down or a trust |
| You get SSI | Resource limit is very low, so a lump sum can suspend benefits | Ask about an ABLE account or special needs trust before receiving |
| You are in a divorce | Inheritance is often treated as separate property in many states, but mixing it can change that | Keep it separate, tell your attorney |
| You pay or receive child support or alimony | Income from an inheritance can be counted in some states | Ask whether it counts in your state |
| You owe creditors | Creditors can sometimes reach it once it is yours | Ask about timing before you accept |
| Your own estate | The money adds to your estate | Update your plan |

## Keep, sell, pay debt or disclaim

| Choice | Fits when | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Keep the asset | It has value to you and carrying costs are affordable | Taxes, insurance, upkeep |
| Sell | The asset is a house you cannot afford, or investments that do not fit your plan | Step-up in basis often means little tax right away |
| Pay off high-interest debt | The rate is high and the payoff is certain | Do not drain your emergency savings |
| Invest | You have no pressing debts | Wait for an unhurried plan |
| Disclaim | You do not want it or it would harm a benefit or invite creditors | Strict rules: you must act in time and cannot accept it first |

## Refusing an inheritance (disclaiming)

A disclaimer is a signed, written refusal. Most states, and federal tax law, require it within a set time, often nine months from the death. <!-- verify: federal nine-month disclaimer rule --> The key rules are:

1. You must not have accepted the property or its benefits.
2. The refusal must be in writing and delivered as the law requires.
3. It cannot direct who gets it. It passes as if you had died first.
4. Some benefit programs treat a disclaimer as a transfer, so check first.

Illinois recognizes disclaimers under 755 ILCS 5/2-7. <!-- verify: Illinois disclaimer statute and deadlines --> Other states differ.

## Questions to ask

- [ ] What exactly am I receiving, and in what form?
- [ ] What debts or taxes could reduce it?
- [ ] When will it likely arrive?
- [ ] Does the estate expect to need money back?
- [ ] Do I need a release or receipt when I get paid?

## When to talk to an attorney

- You receive Medicaid, SSI or other means-tested help
- You are going through a divorce or owe large debts
- You are considering a disclaimer
- The inheritance includes a business, house or retirement account
- You think the executor is paying early or late
