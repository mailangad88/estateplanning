**How to use this template.** Part 1 is a single page for the fridge, a bag or a folder by the door. Part 2 is the fuller packet. Parts 3 and 4 are the wallet card and the babysitter sheet. Use pencil or a laptop so you can update easily. Review every six months and after any move or change.

## What this does, and does not do

An emergency sheet helps people act fast in the first hours. It is not a will, a trust or a power of attorney. It cannot appoint a guardian or give anyone legal authority. Those need signed documents. Rules vary by state, so ask an attorney licensed in yours.

Do not write passwords, full Social Security numbers or full account numbers on any of these pages. Write where they are kept.

## Part 1: The one-page sheet (fridge version)

Keep to what fits on a single page. Use large print.

Place a second copy where emergency responders may look: in your wallet, the glove box or a marked envelope. Some people also put a note on the inside of the front door saying "emergency info is on the fridge."

Without any paperwork, caregivers may have trouble getting medical care or school records for a child. Some parents sign a signed authorization for a short-term caregiver. Ask an attorney in your state whether that works there. See the [guardianship guide](/guides/guardianship-for-minor-children).

## Part 3: Wallet card

Cut out along the lines and fold. Fill in with a pen that does not smear. Replace if anything changes.

**FRONT**

```
+--------------------------------------------+
| IN CASE OF EMERGENCY                       |
| Name: ____________________________________ |
| DOB: _____________  Blood type: __________ |
| Allergies: ______________________________ |
| Conditions: _____________________________ |
| Medications: ____________________________ |
+--------------------------------------------+
```

**BACK**

```
+--------------------------------------------+
| CALL: ______________________  ____________ |
| CALL: ______________________  ____________ |
| Doctor: _____________________ ____________ |
| Healthcare agent: ___________ ____________ |
| Directive is at: _________________________ |
| Children at home: [ ] yes  [ ] no          |
| Pets at home:     [ ] yes  [ ] no          |
+--------------------------------------------+
```

Keep the card next to your photo ID. Many phones also have an emergency information screen you can fill in. Check yours and make sure it matches the card.

## Keeping it current

- [ ] Review every six months, such as when clocks change
- [ ] Update after a move, birth, divorce, new medication or new pet
- [ ] Tell two people where it is
- [ ] Remove old copies so no one finds outdated numbers
- [ ] Store a photo of each page in a locked phone album or secure cloud folder

### Common mistakes

- Listing only one contact, who may be on the same trip
- Leaving out medication doses
- Keeping the sheet in a drawer no one will open
- Assuming a babysitter or neighbor can legally make medical decisions for a child

## When to talk to an attorney

- You want to name a guardian for children in a will
- You want a healthcare power of attorney or a living will that meets your state's rules
- You want a signed authorization for a short-term caregiver
- You have a child with special needs or a medical condition
- You are an unmarried partner and want to be sure your partner can act for you
