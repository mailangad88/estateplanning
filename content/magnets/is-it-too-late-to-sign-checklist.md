## How to use this checklist

Illness does not end the right to plan. The test is what the person understands at the moment of signing, not the diagnosis. Work through the sections in order. Rules vary by state, so the details below describe how many states approach it. Ask an attorney licensed in your state before relying on any of it.

## The short answer by document

| Document | What the person generally must understand | Often still possible late in life? |
|---|---|---|
| Will | Who their family is, roughly what they own, who they are leaving it to | Yes, on a clear day |
| Revocable trust | The same as a will, plus that a trustee will hold property | Yes, but assets must be retitled |
| Power of attorney | Who they are choosing and what that person can do | Yes, if they understand the grant |
| Healthcare directive | Who will decide and what treatments they care about | Yes, often fastest to arrange |
| Beneficiary change | That they are changing who receives the account | Yes, if the company allows it |

Capacity can come and go. A person with dementia may have clear hours. Ask whether the person understands the document today.

## Is it too late? Five quick questions

- [ ] Is the person alive and able to communicate in some way (speech, writing, nod, device)?
- [ ] Can they say who their close family is?
- [ ] Can they describe, even roughly, what they own?
- [ ] Can they say who they want to receive it, and why?
- [ ] Are they signing freely, without someone pressuring them?

Five yeses is a good sign. A no on any one is a reason to call an attorney, not a reason to stop.

## Special situations

| Situation | What commonly applies |
|---|---|
| Blind | Allowed. The document should be read aloud and the reading noted. |
| Cannot read | Allowed. Read aloud, in the presence of the witnesses. |
| Cannot write | Allowed in many states. A mark or an assisted signature may do, with witnesses. |
| Cannot speak | Allowed. Writing, a communication board or yes and no answers can show understanding. |
| Strong medication | Allowed if clear at the moment of signing. Pick the time of day the person is most alert. |
| Mental illness | The diagnosis is not the test. Understanding at signing is. |
| Guardianship or conservatorship | Sometimes limits contracts. A will may still be allowed. Check the court order. |
| Previously declared incompetent | Ask the court order what was removed. A later clear period may support new documents. |
| Dementia | Possible during clear periods. Document how the person did. |

## Signing in a hospital

1. **Call an attorney early.** Do not wait for the last day.
2. **Pick the best hour.** Ask nurses when the person is most awake.
3. **Bring disinterested witnesses.** Not family members who gain from the document.
4. **Use simple documents.** A will, a power of attorney and a healthcare directive are enough in a short window.
5. **Record the process.** Who was there, the time, how the person answered questions.

## If the person has already lost capacity

- **Will:** no one can sign a will for them. Without one, state intestacy law decides.
- **Power of attorney:** if the person cannot understand, a family member may need a court guardianship. See [power of attorney vs guardianship](/compare/power-of-attorney-vs-guardianship).
- **Healthcare:** many states have a default list of family decision makers.
- **Trust:** an existing trust can continue under the successor trustee.

## What can and cannot be fixed after a death

| Problem | After death |
|---|---|
| No will | Cannot be fixed. State law decides who inherits. |
| Beneficiary never named | Cannot be changed. Account terms or state law decide. |
| Wrong beneficiary named | Cannot be changed. Possible court action only in narrow cases. |
| A will with a typo | Sometimes a court can interpret it. Rewriting is not allowed. |
| Property not in a trust | Sometimes a court petition can move it into the trust. |
| An heir disclaims | Can redirect the share within strict deadlines. |

## Common mistakes

- Waiting for the person to "feel better" first
- Letting the person who benefits arrange everything
- Using a form kit with no witnesses
- Assuming a diagnosis means no capacity
- Doing nothing because the will would be contested

## When to talk to an attorney

- The person has any memory loss or has been in a hospital
- A family member wants to be present for the signing
- There is a guardianship or conservatorship order
- Large property or a business is involved
- Family members are already arguing
