**How to use this worksheet.** Guardianship is a state court process, and no two states use exactly the same words, forms or timelines. Start with the term decoder, then fill in the lookup sheet for your state from the court's own website. The starting notes are general and may be out of date, so confirm each one with the court or an attorney licensed in that state. This is general education, not legal advice. Illinois readers can use the [Illinois adult guardianship process guide](/free/illinois-adult-guardianship-process-guide).

## Term decoder

| Term you may see | Usual meaning | Where it varies |
|---|---|---|
| Guardian of the person | Decides care, housing and health for a child or adult | Called a guardian in nearly every state |
| Guardian of the estate | Manages money and property | Some states call this a conservator |
| Conservator | Manages money, and sometimes personal care | In California, conservatorship is the adult proceeding |
| Guardian ad litem | Investigates for the court | Some states use a visitor, investigator or evaluator |
| Minor guardianship | For a child under 18 | Often a separate statute and court |
| Adult guardianship | For an adult found unable to manage affairs | Rules about proof and limits differ a lot |
| Limited guardianship | Court gives only the powers needed | Some states prefer it by law |
| Temporary or emergency guardianship | Short order for a crisis | Time limits and notice rules differ |

Guardian and conservator are not always different. In some states one person with one title does both jobs. In others the roles are split by name. Always read the order, not the title.

## Starting notes for 14 states

Use these as search terms, not as answers. <!-- verify: each state row against the current statute or court website before reliance -->

| State | Names and court to search for | Notes to confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Arizona | Guardianship and conservatorship, superior court | Courts may use investigators and visitors |
| California | Guardianship (minors) and conservatorship (adults), superior court probate division | Different tracks for general and developmental disability cases |
| Florida | Guardianship under Chapter 744, circuit court | An examining committee and a court-appointed attorney are common |
| Georgia | Guardianship and conservatorship, probate court | Medical evidence and a hearing are usual |
| Massachusetts | Guardianship and conservatorship, Probate and Family Court | Medical certificate and forms from the court |
| Michigan | Guardian and conservator, probate court | Court review each year |
| New Jersey | Guardianship, Superior Court, Chancery Division, Probate Part | Two physician certifications are common |
| New York | Article 81 of the Mental Hygiene Law for adults, with a separate route for some developmental disability cases | Hearing and court evaluator; court prefers least restrictive option |
| North Carolina | Guardianship through the Clerk of Superior Court | A multi-step proceeding, with a guardian ad litem |
| Ohio | Guardianship, probate court | Medical statement and court investigator |
| Pennsylvania | Guardianship, Orphans' Court | Medical evidence of incapacity and a hearing |
| Texas | Guardianship under the Estates Code, county or probate court | Alternatives, including supported decision-making, must be considered |
| Virginia | Guardian and conservator, circuit court | Evaluations and a hearing |
| Washington | Guardianship, superior court | Often a court visitor and registry rules |

Medical evidence, notice and report schedules change by state. Local rules also vary by county, so call the clerk.

## If the person lives in a different state

A guardian appointed in one state may need to start or transfer a case when the person moves. Many states use uniform laws on this point, but not all. Ask the clerk in the new state. Then compare with [moving states estate plan checklist](/free/moving-states-estate-plan-checklist).

## Using your sheet

1. Fill in the lookup sheet.
2. Check the court's own website for forms.
3. Make a short list of facts and records.
4. Review the alternatives in the [guardianship alternatives guide](/free/guardianship-alternatives-guide).
5. Book time with an attorney licensed in that state.

## When to talk to an attorney

- The person is in a different state from you
- A hospital or facility needs a decision maker now
- You find conflicting answers on a court's website
- Family members disagree about who should serve
- The person owns property in more than one state
