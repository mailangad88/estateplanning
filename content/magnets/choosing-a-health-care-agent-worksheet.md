**How to use this worksheet.** A health care agent, sometimes called a proxy or health care power of attorney, speaks for you when you cannot. The job differs from choosing an executor or a trustee. It happens in hospitals, often at night, often under stress. Fill in the scorecard for each candidate, hold the conversation, then give your choice to your attorney to put in the form your state recognizes. Terms and rules vary by state.

## What the agent actually does

- Talks with doctors and asks questions
- Decides about treatments when you cannot, guided by your wishes
- Chooses or changes doctors, hospitals and care facilities, within the document's limits
- Reads your medical records
- Makes sure your living will is followed
- Speaks to family so that decisions do not turn into arguments

The agent does not have to be the person you love most. The agent has to be the person who can do this.

## Step 1: Criteria scorecard

Score each person from 1 (not at all) to 5 (strongly). Use the notes column for evidence, such as "stayed calm when Dad was in the ICU."

- **Candidate 1:** ______________________
- **Candidate 2:** ______________________

A high score does not decide it alone. Read your totals as a prompt for a conversation.

## Step 2: Common traps

- **Naming the oldest child by default.** Birth order says nothing about temperament.
- **Naming a spouse who may fall apart.** Many couples name each other, which is often right. Ask whether your spouse could handle the hardest decision, and name a backup who could help.
- **Naming two agents at once.** Joint agents who disagree can freeze a decision. Many people name one agent and a backup. Ask your attorney whether joint agents are allowed and wise.
- **Choosing someone far away.** Distance can be handled with phone calls and travel, but think about who is nearby and able to be at the bedside.
- **Choosing someone who benefits from your estate.** It is not forbidden, but think about conflict.
- **Not asking.** Some people agree, then discover they cannot do it. Others are honored and prepared.

## Step 3: Conversation prompts

Use these when you talk with your first choice. Check each when done.

- [ ] "I would like to name you as my health care agent. Would you be willing?"
- [ ] "Here is what matters to me" (use your [living will worksheet](/guides/healthcare-directives-and-living-wills) answers if you have them).
- [ ] "Here is what I would want if I could not recover."
- [ ] "If it is a close call, I would rather you lean toward: more time / comfort / ask me first" (choose one).
- [ ] "Is there anything about this that worries you?"
- [ ] "Could you make a decision you disagreed with, if it was what I asked?"
- [ ] "Who else should know?"

Write what they said:

- **Willing?** Yes / No / Needs time
- **Concerns they raised:** ______________________
- **Anything I will change after this conversation:** ______________________

## Step 4: Backup agent

Backups matter because the first agent can be unavailable, unwilling or unable.

- **Backup agent:** ______________________
- **Relationship:** ______________________
- **Phone and email:** ______________________
- **Lives:** ______________________
- [ ] Backup has been asked and agrees.
- [ ] Backup has a copy of the documents.

When does the backup step in? Common wording: if the first agent is unavailable, unwilling or unable to act. Ask your attorney how to write it.

## Step 5: Questions for your attorney about the document

- [ ] Does my state use a specific form or witness rule?
- [ ] Can the agent make decisions about mental health treatment or admission to a care facility?
- [ ] Should I add instructions on organ donation, autopsy, funeral arrangements or hospice?
- [ ] How does the document work with my living will?
- [ ] Should I name a person to be my guardian of the person, in case a court ever needs one?
- [ ] Is a HIPAA release included?
- [ ] What happens if I move to another state?

## Step 6: A one-page letter to your agent

Write this letter and keep it with your documents. Fill in the blanks.

> Dear ____________,
>
> Thank you for agreeing to speak for me if I cannot. These are the things that matter most to me: ______________________.
>
> The most important treatment wishes I have are: ______________________.
>
> My doctors are: ______________________. My pharmacy is: ______________________.
>
> The people who should be kept informed are: ______________________.
>
> If you are unsure what to do, please ask ______________________ for support. I trust your judgment.

## Step 7: After you choose

- [ ] Sign the document with the formalities your state requires.
- [ ] Give copies to your agent, backup, doctor and hospital.
- [ ] Tell your family who your agent is.
- [ ] Put a note in your wallet or phone: "My health care agent is [name], [phone]."
- [ ] Review every few years, after major illness, divorce, relocation or a death in the family.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You want the document to meet your state's rules and be accepted in other states.
- You are considering joint agents or want special limits.
- Family members are likely to dispute your choice.
- You have a serious diagnosis and want choices recorded quickly.
- You do not know whom to name, and want to talk through options.
