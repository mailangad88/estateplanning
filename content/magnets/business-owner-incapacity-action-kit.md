## How to use this kit

A business does not pause when its owner is in the hospital. Payroll runs, rent is due, and customers still call. Work through the sections in order and fill in each blank. Hand a copy of the finished kit to the person you name as your agent. Rules vary by state, so confirm details with an attorney in yours.

## What happens with no plan

If you cannot act and no one has legal authority, banks and landlords often will not take instructions from family. A court may have to appoint a guardian or conservator, which takes time and is public.

| Business type | Who can act with no paperwork | What usually goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | No one. The business is you | Accounts frozen, no one can sign |
| Single-member LLC | No one, unless the agreement names a successor manager | Bank requires the member's signature |
| Multi-member LLC or partnership | Other owners may keep operating, but cannot act for your share | Votes stall, your share is exposed |
| Corporation | Officers and directors keep operating | Your personal shares have no voice |

## Three documents that carry the load

1. **Durable power of attorney for property.** Covers your personal assets, and can cover business ownership interests if drafted to. In Illinois, see 755 ILCS 45/. <!-- verify: Illinois Power of Attorney Act citation --> Read the [Illinois property power of attorney guide](/free/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property-guide).
2. **Operating agreement or bylaws with a succession clause.** This names who steps in as manager and what authority they have, so the company is not relying on your personal power of attorney alone.
3. **A revocable trust holding the business interest.** A successor trustee can act without a court, and the trust can tell them how to run things.

## What a business power of attorney should say

- [ ] Names the business by legal name and, if applicable, the entity type
- [ ] States whether the agent may sign contracts, hire and fire, and borrow
- [ ] States whether the agent may sell or transfer the ownership interest
- [ ] Names a second agent if the first cannot serve
- [ ] Says when it starts (right away, or only when a doctor certifies incapacity)
- [ ] Is accepted by your bank in advance (ask which form they prefer)

## Choosing the agent or successor manager

Pick someone who can run the business, not just someone you trust. They may be different people.

| Role | Skills it needs | Candidate | Backup |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agent under the power of attorney | Honest, organized, trusted with money | | |
| Interim manager | Knows operations and staff | | |
| Financial signer | Comfortable with payroll and taxes | | |
| Customer contact | Credible with clients | | |

Appointing a successor manager for an LLC usually means amending the operating agreement and signing a written consent. Some agreements require other members to vote. Check yours.

## Disability insurance and buyouts

Disability income insurance replaces part of your pay if you cannot work. Ask whether it pays the owner or the company, and how long the wait is before benefits begin.

A disability buyout provision in a buy-sell agreement lets the other owners buy your share if you are disabled past a stated period. Without one, you stay an owner but cannot work, and the business may struggle to pay you while also paying someone else to do your job.

| Decision | Options | Your answer |
|---|---|---|
| How long before the buyout triggers? | Often a number of months set in the agreement | |
| Who certifies disability? | Two doctors, or the insurer's definition | |
| How is it paid? | Lump sum or installments | |
| Is there insurance to fund it? | Buy-sell disability policy | |

## A 30-day continuity plan

1. Week one: agent signs the bank forms and notifies key vendors.
2. Week one: interim manager takes over daily operations.
3. Week two: payroll and taxes confirmed for the next quarter.
4. Week two: customers receive a short, calm message.
5. Week four: owners and advisers meet to decide the next step.

## When to talk to an attorney

- Your bank will not recognize your power of attorney
- You have partners and no written buyout terms
- The business holds a license that cannot pass to an agent
- You want the business in a trust
- A diagnosis is already in hand
