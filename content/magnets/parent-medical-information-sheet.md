**How to use this worksheet.** Sit with your parent, ideally at a calm time with their pill bottles and insurance cards on the table. Fill in the sheet in pencil, then type or rewrite it neatly. Keep a copy in your parent's wallet, one on the refrigerator, one in your phone and one with the person who holds the health care power of attorney. Update it after every appointment that changes something. A medical information sheet is a convenience. It is not a legal document, and a doctor will still verify details.

## Part 4: Keeping it current

- [ ] Add a date at the top of the sheet each time it changes: "Updated: ______".
- [ ] Update after every appointment, new prescription or hospital stay.
- [ ] Review the whole sheet every three months with a pill bottle check.
- [ ] Replace old copies, in the wallet and on the refrigerator, whenever a new one is printed.
- [ ] Photograph the sheet and the insurance cards, and store the photos in a secure folder, not in a shared chat.
- [ ] Bring the sheet to every appointment and hospital stay, and hand a copy to staff.
- [ ] Keep a list of questions for the next doctor visit on the back.

## Before an emergency room visit

Take these in a folder or bag by the door:

- [ ] The sheet and current pill bottles, or a photograph of each label
- [ ] Insurance cards and a photo ID
- [ ] Copies of the health care power of attorney and directive
- [ ] Glasses, hearing aids and dentures
- [ ] A phone charger
- [ ] A short note on baseline behavior: "Mom is usually sharp. Today she is confused."

## Privacy

This sheet contains sensitive details. Share it with people who need it and avoid email attachments that others can forward. Do not put Social Security numbers or full account numbers on it. If a provider will not talk to you, a HIPAA release or the health care agent's authority is often the reason. Ask the office what they need.

## When to talk to an attorney

- Your parent has no health care power of attorney, or the agent named has died or is unwilling.
- Your parent is losing capacity and has not signed documents.
- Providers refuse to share information with the family.
- Siblings disagree about treatment decisions.
- You are thinking about long-term care and how to pay for it.
