## How to use this checklist

Farm succession in Illinois has the same family questions as anywhere, plus a state estate tax that begins far below the federal level. Work down the list in order and date each box. For the people questions, see the [farm and ranch succession guide](/free/farm-and-ranch-succession-guide). This checklist is educational, not advice for your situation, and farm families usually need a lawyer, a tax professional and a lender.

## Step 1: Inventory what you have

- [ ] List every parcel: legal description, acres, county, PIN and how title is held
- [ ] Note which parcels are owned, leased in or leased out, and the lease terms
- [ ] Collect the deeds, mortgages, easements, drainage and crop-share agreements
- [ ] List equipment and livestock, with year, serial numbers and who owns each item
- [ ] List grain, inputs and accounts receivable
- [ ] Gather the last three years of tax returns and farm financial statements
- [ ] List loans, lines of credit and co-signers

- [ ] Whether the farming heir would buy, rent or inherit

## Step 3: Choose a structure for land and operation

- [ ] Keep land personally owned and lease it to the operator
- [ ] Place land in a revocable trust
- [ ] Consider an LLC or family entity for land, equipment or both, with an operating agreement
- [ ] Use a land trust, if that is how title is already held
- [ ] Write a lease with rent, term, who pays taxes, drainage and improvements
- [ ] Put buy-sell or option terms in writing

Any entity needs a written agreement that covers death, disability, divorce and sale. See the [Illinois small business succession guide](/free/illinois-small-business-succession-guide) and the [Illinois land trust guide](/free/illinois-land-trust-guide).

## Step 4: Check the Illinois estate tax

The federal estate tax exemption is $15,000,000 per person in 2026. Illinois has its own estate tax, which starts when an estate's gross value passes $4,000,000, according to the Illinois Attorney General. The Attorney General's instructions say Illinois Form 700 is due nine months after death, and an estate above the threshold must file even if no federal return is required.

Farmland values can push a family farm over that line even when the family has little cash. The Attorney General's instructions mention elections such as alternate valuation and special use valuation for qualifying property. Whether your farm can use them is a question for a tax professional. For the full rules, read the [Illinois estate tax guide](/free/illinois-estate-tax-guide).

- [ ] Add up the value of land, equipment, livestock, grain, accounts and life insurance
- [ ] Compare the total with the Illinois threshold
- [ ] Ask whether life insurance, gifts during life or a trust could change the result
- [ ] Plan how heirs will pay any tax without selling the land

## Step 5: Ask about USDA Farm Service Agency programs

The Farm Service Agency (FSA) lends to farmers who may not qualify for ordinary credit. Its farm ownership loans can be direct loans or loans from a commercial lender backed by FSA. A down payment program for beginning farmers finances part of a purchase after a small down payment from the buyer. FSA describes a beginning farmer as someone who has not operated a farm for more than 10 years. Check current limits and rules with your county FSA office.

- [ ] Ask your local FSA office what a next-generation operator might qualify for
- [ ] Ask what records an applicant needs, including farm financials and a business plan
- [ ] Tell FSA about any change in who operates or owns a farm, so program records stay correct

## Step 6: Plan for long-term care

Nursing home costs can force a farm sale. Illinois Medicaid looks back 60 months at transfers. HFS says certain farmland and homestead property may be non-countable, and it can waive estate recovery when property is a family farm providing primary income. These rules turn on facts. Read the [Illinois Medicaid guide](/free/illinois-medicaid-long-term-care-guide) before giving or selling land.

- [ ] Make a long-term care plan before a health crisis
- [ ] Do not transfer land to children without legal advice

## Step 7: Sign the core documents

- [ ] Will or revocable trust that matches the farm plan
- [ ] Financial power of attorney that names someone who can sign farm papers
- [ ] Health care power of attorney
- [ ] Beneficiary forms on retirement accounts and insurance
- [ ] Written leases and any buy-sell agreement

- [ ] Invite spouses and those who do not farm
- [ ] Share the goals and the inventory, not just the plan
- [ ] Write down what was agreed and who will follow up

## When to talk to an attorney

- Your estate may exceed the Illinois estate tax threshold.
- You want to give or sell land to one child and balance it for the others.
- You own land through a partnership, LLC, land trust or several owners.
- A spouse or parent may need nursing home care.
- There is a lease, a mortgage or a drainage district question that affects the transfer.
