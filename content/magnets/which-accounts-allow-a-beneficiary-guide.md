## How to use this guide

A beneficiary form moves money outside a will, usually without probate. Not every asset offers one. Use the table to see what is possible, circle the assets you own, then read the notes for each. Rules vary by state and by company, so treat this as a starting point.

## Quick table

| Asset | Beneficiary allowed? | How it is done |
|---|---|---|
| Checking or savings | Yes | A payable-on-death (POD) designation at the bank |
| Brokerage account | Yes | A transfer-on-death (TOD) registration |
| IRA, 401(k), 403(b) | Yes | Beneficiary form with the custodian |
| Life insurance | Yes | Policy beneficiary form |
| Annuity | Yes | Contract beneficiary form |
| HSA | Yes | Beneficiary form with the HSA custodian |
| 529 plan | Yes (successor owner) | Successor account owner form |
| Pension | Often | Plan terms control; spouse rights apply |
| Safe deposit box | No | Access is set by the bank's own rules |
| Social Security | No | Federal rules decide who may claim |
| Vehicle | Depends on state | Some states allow a TOD title; Illinois has its own procedure |
| House | Depends on state | A TOD deed, or a trust |
| Cash at home, jewelry, furniture | No | Will, trust or a written list |

## Notes on the "no" and "depends" rows

### Safe deposit box

The box itself has no beneficiary. Banks decide who may enter after a death, and some need a court order or a small estate procedure. Name a co-renter, keep a list of the contents with your will and do not store the only original will inside.

### Social Security

You cannot name someone. Federal rules decide who gets survivor benefits. A surviving spouse, a minor child or a dependent parent may qualify. The agency pays them directly, not through your estate. Tell your family to report the death to Social Security so overpayments are not later recovered. A one-time lump-sum death payment exists for qualifying survivors. <!-- verify: current amount and who qualifies -->

### HSA

A spouse named as beneficiary can generally keep it as their own HSA. Any other person gets the account value, and it is generally taxable to them. If no beneficiary is named, the account usually goes to the estate.

### Pension

Many pensions pay a surviving spouse automatically unless the spouse signs a waiver. A single-life pension may pay nothing after you die. A joint-and-survivor option pays the survivor a share. Ask the plan which option you chose.

### Vehicles and houses

A car often passes by a simple procedure at the Secretary of State. In Illinois, real estate can pass at death through a recorded transfer on death instrument under 755 ILCS 27. <!-- verify: Illinois TOD instrument statute cite and vehicle procedure --> Other states use TOD deeds or lady bird deeds. See the [Illinois TOD instrument guide](/free/illinois-tod-instrument-guide).

### 529 plans

The account owner can name a successor owner. That person controls the account if the owner dies. The student beneficiary can be changed to another family member.

## Beneficiaries that are not people

Yes, you can name:

- **A charity, church or university.** Use the legal name and tax ID number.
- **A trust.** Use the trust's full name and date, and the trustee's name.
- **Your estate.** This sends the money through probate. Usually it is a poor choice.

A pet cannot be a beneficiary because it cannot own property. Use a pet trust instead. See the [pet trust planning guide](/free/pet-trust-template-guide).

| If you want this... | Wording to consider |
|---|---|
| A charity | "[Full legal name], tax ID [number]" |
| A trust | "Trustee of the [Name] Trust dated [date]" |
| All your children equally | "My children in equal shares, per stirpes" |
| One child, with a backup | Primary: [Name]. Contingent: [Name]'s children |

## If you cannot name a beneficiary

When an asset has no beneficiary option, other tools can fill the gap:

1. A living trust that owns the asset
2. A will, which means probate
3. Joint ownership, which has its own risks
4. A small estate affidavit, if the estate is small

## Fill-in record

- **Account and company:** ______________________
- **Primary beneficiary:** ______________________
- **Contingent beneficiary:** ______________________
- **Form last updated:** ______________________
- **Confirmation received (yes / no):** ______

Repeat for each account. Keep copies with your will.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming a will controls an account with a named beneficiary. It does not
- Naming minors outright, which can lead to a court-supervised guardianship of the money
- Using nicknames or leaving out a contingent
- Assuming a safe deposit box is a transfer tool

## When to talk to an attorney

- The asset is a house, a business or a large pension
- You want a trust to receive an IRA or insurance
- A beneficiary is a minor, disabled or receives benefits
- You are unsure whether a form conflicts with your will
