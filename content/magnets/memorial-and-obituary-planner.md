## How to use this planner

There is no right way to remember someone. This planner breaks the work into small pieces so you can hand some of them to others. Do the parts that matter to you and skip the rest. If your loved one left wishes, start there. A written plan, or the [funeral and burial wishes checklist](/checklists/funeral-and-burial-wishes), may already exist.

Take your time where you can. Ask a friend to hold the notebook.

## Step 1: Gather what is known

- [ ] Any written wishes, such as a letter, a prepaid plan or a note in a will
- [ ] Whether a prepaid funeral contract exists, and with whom
- [ ] The funeral home chosen, if any
- [ ] Cemetery or plot information
- [ ] Faith or cultural traditions the family wants to follow
- [ ] Who is making decisions, and who has the legal right in your state

Wishes in a will may not be read in time. Look for them in the first days.

## Step 2: Choose the shape of the service

| Option | What it is | Things to think about |
|---|---|---|
| Funeral with the body present | Service, often followed by burial | Needs more advance coordination |
| Memorial service | Service without the body, any time later | Allows time for travelers to gather |
| Graveside only | Short service at the cemetery | Simple, less cost |
| Celebration of life | Informal gathering, often with stories | Can be at home or in a hall |
| Private family gathering | Small, intimate | Can pair with a larger event later |

A direct cremation or burial with a service later is a choice some families make to give themselves time.

## Step 3: Write the obituary

An obituary tells people the person died, shares who they were and gives service details. Many families write a short notice for the paper and a longer remembrance for the funeral home page or social media. Newspapers often charge by the line, so ask about cost before you submit.

Answer these in your own words:

- **Full name, and name used by family:** ______________________
- **Age, date and place of death:** ______________________
- **Where they were born and grew up:** ______________________
- **Work, service or calling:** ______________________
- **What they loved:** ______________________
- **A saying, a habit or a story people will recognize:** ______________________
- **Survived by (spouse, children, grandchildren, siblings):** ______________________
- **Preceded in death by:** ______________________
- **Service details:** ______________________
- **Where to send donations or flowers:** ______________________

A simple structure:

1. Announcement: name, age, date and place of death.
2. Life story in two or three short paragraphs.
3. Family: who survives and who died earlier.
4. Service details and donations.

Sample paragraph: "Ruth was born in Peoria and worked as a school secretary for thirty-one years. She kept a drawer full of stickers for any child sent to the office with a scraped knee."

Before publishing, ask someone to read it for names, dates and spelling. Consider what you share online. Full birth dates and the names of every relative can give identity thieves useful details, and a service time and address can make a home look empty. A funeral home can help you think it through.

## Step 4: Timeline

| When | Tasks |
|---|---|
| First days | Contact the funeral home, choose the service type, call close family, order death certificates |
| Days 2 to 4 | Write the obituary, choose clothing and photos, confirm the date and place, ask people to speak |
| Days 4 to 7 | Send the obituary to papers, confirm flowers and music, arrange food and travel |
| Service day | Assign someone to greet people, a guest book and a ride for elderly relatives |
| Week after | Send thank-you notes, return borrowed items, collect donation lists |
| Weeks after | Note memorial gifts, plan a later gathering if needed |

## When to talk to an attorney

- Family members disagree about burial or cremation
- You are unsure who has the legal right to decide
- The person left written wishes that differ from what the family wants
- There may be a dispute about who pays
- A prepaid funeral contract is unclear
