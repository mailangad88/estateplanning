## How to use this worksheet

Funeral prices differ from one provider to the next, and families who are grieving often have little time to compare. This worksheet lets one person make calls while others rest. You can also use it years ahead to plan. Bring a friend to appointments if you can, someone who is not as close to the loss.

It is perfectly fine to ask for prices and to compare. A good funeral home expects it.

## Your rights under the FTC Funeral Rule

The Federal Trade Commission enforces the Funeral Rule. In summary, funeral providers must:

- **Give prices by phone.** You can ask for pricing information without giving your name or contact details.
- **Give you a General Price List (GPL).** It itemizes every service and product. You should be handed it when you start discussing arrangements in person.
- **Let you buy separate items.** You can choose only the goods and services you want, and you do not have to buy a package.
- **Accept a casket or urn bought elsewhere.** They cannot refuse it or charge you a fee for using it.
- **Offer alternatives for cremation.** Providers must tell you that alternative containers, such as cardboard or fiberboard, are available.
- **Explain embalming.** The FTC states that no state law requires routine embalming for every death. Refrigeration is often an option, and some services, such as a public viewing, may call for it.
- **Give an itemized statement** of everything you chose, with prices and the total, before you pay.

Rules vary by state on some points, such as when embalming may be required for certain circumstances, so ask the provider to show you the law if they say something is required.

If you feel a provider has not followed these rules, the FTC accepts complaints.

## Before you call

- [ ] Decide the rough type of arrangement: burial, cremation or a donation of the body
- [ ] Find any prepaid contract or written wishes
- [ ] Choose three providers, including one that is not the closest
- [ ] Ask each for the GPL by email or phone
- [ ] Ask about cemetery fees separately, since they are often not part of the funeral home's bill

## Questions to ask each provider

- [ ] Is the GPL available by email? May I keep a copy?
- [ ] What does the basic services fee include?
- [ ] What is your lowest-cost casket or container?
- [ ] Do you charge for a casket I buy elsewhere? (They should not.)
- [ ] What is the total for direct cremation, with every fee?
- [ ] Which charges are paid to others, such as the cemetery or crematory, and what are they?
- [ ] What happens if I need to change the date?
- [ ] How and when is payment due?

## Watch for

- Packages that include items you do not want
- Pressure to decide at once, or to buy a more costly item as a sign of respect
- Statements that something is required by law, which you can ask to see
- A prepaid plan with unclear terms for refunds or transfers

## Prepaying in advance

Some people prepay. Terms vary by state, and some contracts are not refundable or transferable if you move. If you prepay, keep the contract with your estate documents and tell your executor where it is.

## When to talk to an attorney

- Family members disagree about burial or cremation
- You are unsure who has authority to sign the contract
- A prepaid plan or insurance policy has unclear terms
- You believe a provider charged more than the price list or broke the rules
- You are unsure whether the estate or a family member should pay
