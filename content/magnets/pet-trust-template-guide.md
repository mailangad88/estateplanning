## How to use this guide

In the law of most places, pets are property. A pet can't inherit money or enforce a promise. A pet trust solves that by giving someone legal duties and a fund. Rules vary by state, so use this guide to organize your decisions before you meet an attorney. For a lighter planning sheet, see the [pet care plan](/free/pet-care-plan). This guide goes further into the trust itself.

## Will gift or pet trust?

| | Gift to a person in a will | Pet trust |
|---|---|---|
| Money for the pet | Goes to the person outright | Held by a trustee for the pet |
| Is the person required to use it for the pet? | Not legally | Yes, the trustee must follow the terms |
| Who can enforce it? | Nobody, usually | A person named in the trust, or a court-appointed one |
| Works during your incapacity | No, a will only works at death | Yes, if made in a living trust |
| Cost and setup | Low | Higher, since a trust is drafted |
| Fits | One animal, low cost, a trusted friend | Several animals, higher costs, a long lifespan |

Many states, including Illinois, have a statute allowing a trust for the care of animals. In Illinois, such a trust lasts for the life of the animals covered. Someone named in the trust can enforce it, and if nobody is named, a court can appoint a person. A court may also reduce a fund that is far larger than needed, and the excess goes to the people the trust names for leftovers.

## Step 1: Choose the roles

A pet trust has three jobs, and they work better when one person does not hold all three.

- **Caretaker:** lives with the pet day to day.
- **Trustee:** holds and pays out the money.
- **Enforcer:** checks that the caretaker follows your instructions.

- **Caretaker:** ____________
- **Backup caretaker:** ____________
- **Second backup:** ____________
- **Trustee:** ____________
- **Enforcer (or court appointment if none):** ____________

Ask each person before you name them. Talk about the pet's habits, age and health.

Funding options include cash, a life insurance policy payable to the trust, or a share of the residuary estate. An overly large fund can invite challenges from family, and courts may trim it.

## Step 3: Write the care instructions

- [ ] Feeding schedule and food brand
- [ ] Vet name, phone, and records location
- [ ] Medical conditions and medicines
- [ ] Spending limits for treatment, and who decides
- [ ] Exercise, training and boarding preferences
- [ ] Microchip number and registration
- [ ] Where the pet should never go (for example, no breeder or shelter)
- [ ] When to consider euthanasia, and who decides
- [ ] What happens to remains (burial, cremation, other)

State clearly how much choice the caretaker has. Over-detailed instructions can be impractical years later.

## Step 4: Name what happens at the end

- **When the pet dies, leftover funds go to:** ____________
- **If caretaker cannot continue:** ____________
- **Reports to the trustee (how often):** ____________

## Sample clauses to discuss

**Purpose.** "This trust is for the care of my dog, Biscuit, and any other pet I own at my death."

**Caretaker.** "My trustee shall deliver Biscuit to Alex Rivera as caretaker. If Alex Rivera cannot or will not serve, to Sam Cho."

**Payments.** "My trustee shall pay for Biscuit's reasonable food, veterinary care, grooming and boarding, and shall reimburse the caretaker for these costs on receipt of invoices or receipts."

**Check-ups.** "The caretaker shall take Biscuit to a veterinarian at least once a year and send my trustee the record."

**Remainder.** "When the last pet covered by this trust dies, my trustee shall pay the remaining funds to Riverside Animal Shelter."

## Step 5: Make it findable

- [ ] Tell the caretaker and the trustee where the trust document is.
- [ ] Keep a short note in your wallet and on your phone naming a person to call about your pet.
- [ ] Put the care sheet where an emergency contact can find it.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You have several animals, or an animal with a long life, such as a horse or parrot.
- Your pet has an expensive condition.
- You want the trust to start during incapacity, not only death.
- A family member may object to the fund.
- You own animals as part of a business, such as breeding or farming.
