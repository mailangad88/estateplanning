**How to use this kit.** Hold one meeting to start, not to decide everything. Send the agenda a week ahead, use the ground rules, and end with a list of next steps. If tensions run high, ask a neutral facilitator, such as a family business advisor or your attorney, to lead. Nothing said in the meeting is a legal commitment until it is written into documents.

## Before the meeting

- [ ] Decide who is invited: owners, spouses, adult children, key non-family managers (separately, if sensitive).
- [ ] Collect basic facts: business value estimate, ownership, debts, owner's income needs.
- [ ] Decide what you will share and what stays private for now.
- [ ] Pick a neutral place and set 90 minutes.
- [ ] Pick a note taker who is not the owner.

## Ground rules to read aloud

1. Everyone speaks and everyone listens.
2. No decisions today unless all agree.
3. We separate three questions: who leads, who owns, and who benefits.
4. "Fair" does not always mean "equal." We will talk about what fair means for us.
5. What is said here stays here until we agree otherwise.

## The 90-minute agenda

**1. Opening and purpose (10 minutes).** Owner explains why now, in a few sentences. Sample: "I want the business and the family to be okay when I step back. I want to hear what each of you wants before I decide anything."

**2. Facts (15 minutes).** What the business does, how it is owned, what it is worth in rough terms, what the owner plans to live on. Do not hand out the owner's personal finances unless the owner chooses to.

**3. Everyone's hopes and worries (25 minutes).** Go around the table.

- **What do I want for the business?** ____________________
- **What do I want for myself?** ____________________
- **What worries me?** ____________________
- **What I would not accept:** ____________________

**4. Leadership, ownership and income (20 minutes).** Use the grid below.

**5. Fairness (10 minutes).** Discuss children who work in the business and those who do not.

**6. Next steps (10 minutes).** Who does what by when.

## Leadership, ownership and income grid

Fill in options for each. Keep the three questions apart.

| Question | Options on the table | Who is interested | Concerns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who will run the business? | | | |
| Who will own it? | | | |
| Who will receive income from it? | | | |
| Who will own the real estate? | | | |
| Who has votes and who does not? | | | |

Leadership should usually be based on skills, willingness and fit. Ownership can be broader. Voting rights can be separated from economic shares in many entities, so some children can have income without control.

## Fairness for inside and outside children

The child who works in the business has put in time, perhaps for lower pay. The child who does not may want an equal share of family wealth. Consider these approaches:

- **Equalize with other assets.** Leave the business to those in it and other property, life insurance or cash to the rest.
- **Voting and non-voting shares.** Working children get voting shares, all children get economic shares.
- **Buy-out terms.** Working children buy out siblings over time on a note.
- **Compensation for past work.** Pay or credit for the extra work of the inside child, written and agreed.
- **Right to sell.** A path for outside children to sell their shares.

Questions to talk over:

- **What would "fair" mean to each of us?** ____________________
- **What will we do if the business loses value?** ____________________
- **What if someone wants out?** ____________________
- **How are in-laws treated?** ____________________
- **Who sits on a family council or board?** ____________________

## Talking about a delicate topic

Here are some lines that help.

- To the child who works in the business: "I want to hear what you want, and I want it to be your choice, not only mine."
- To the child outside it: "You are not being left out. I want to find a way that treats you fairly too."
- To the owner: "What would make you comfortable stepping back?"

## After the meeting

- [ ] Write a short thank-you and a summary within a week.
- [ ] Arrange a valuation if none exists.
- [ ] Ask an attorney to review how the ideas fit the law and tax rules. The federal estate exemption for 2026 is $15,000,000 per person, but some states tax estates at lower levels. See the [Estate Tax Estimator](/tools/estate-tax-estimator).
- [ ] Hold a second meeting for decisions.
- [ ] Update wills, trusts and beneficiary forms.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You want to treat children differently, and need to document why.
- There is a dispute or a child feels excluded.
- You need an agreement on voting, buyouts or transfer limits.
- The business is large enough that taxes could affect the plan.
- You have a blended family or a child with special needs.
