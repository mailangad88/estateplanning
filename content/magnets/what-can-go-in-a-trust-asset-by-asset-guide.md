## How to use this guide

A trust only controls what it owns or what names it as beneficiary. Read the table, mark each asset you have, then fill in the tracker. Rules and lender policies vary, so call each company before you retitle anything. This is general education, not legal advice.

## Asset table

| Asset | In the trust? | How |
|---|---|---|
| House | Usually yes | Deed to the trustee, recorded with the county |
| Mortgaged house | Usually yes | Same deed, tell the lender if it asks |
| Checking and savings | Often yes, or POD | Retitle, or add a POD to the trust |
| Brokerage account | Usually yes | Retitle to the trustee, or TOD |
| Stocks held directly | Yes | Transfer agent or broker changes title |
| Cash at home | Yes in theory | Keep a list and an assignment |
| Car | Often no | Use the state's transfer process or leave it out |
| IRA, 401(k), 403(b) | No | Name the trust or a person as beneficiary instead |
| Annuity | Usually no | Beneficiary designation |
| Life insurance | Usually no | Beneficiary designation, or an ILIT |
| HSA | No | Beneficiary designation |
| Timeshare | Maybe | Check the contract and any transfer fee |
| Cryptocurrency | Yes | Assignment, and a plan for keys |
| Firearms | Special rules | Federal and state transfer laws apply |
| Social Security | No | Cannot be held in trust, but paid benefits can be deposited into trust accounts |
| Business interest (LLC or stock) | Yes | Assignment, check operating agreement |
| Rental property | Yes | Deed, update insurance and leases |
| Art, jewelry, furniture | Yes | Assignment of personal property |
| Digital accounts | Limited | Access plan, usually not retitled |
| Out-of-state land | Yes | Deed under that state's rules |

## House, mortgage and refinance

You can put a mortgaged house in a trust and still live in it. Federal law (the Garn-St Germain Act) limits a lender's use of the due-on-sale clause when a borrower transfers a home to a revocable trust and stays in it. <!-- verify: Garn-St Germain, 12 U.S.C. 1701j-3(d)(8) conditions --> Many people send the lender a short notice. Keep paying the loan.

Refinancing: lenders may ask you to deed the house out of the trust for closing, then back in. Some lend directly to a trust. Plan for this before you refinance and make sure the deed goes back afterward. Lenders, title insurers and insurance carriers have their own rules. Ask your title insurer whether coverage continues. Ask your homeowner's insurer to add the trustee as an insured.

In Illinois, a deed into your own revocable trust is generally exempt from transfer tax, but the recorder still needs the exemption language. <!-- verify: Illinois transfer tax exemption 35 ILCS 200/31-45 --> See the [house in trust deed worksheet](/free/house-in-trust-deed-worksheet).

## Retirement accounts and annuities

Do not retitle an IRA or 401(k) to a trust. That can be treated as a cash-out and trigger income tax. Name the trust as beneficiary only when there is a reason, such as protecting a young or vulnerable beneficiary, and the trust is drafted to qualify under the IRS rules. Otherwise name people. See the [inherited IRA guide](/free/inherited-ira-10-year-rule-guide).

An annuity works the same way: the owner stays the owner, the beneficiary form does the work.

## Car

Many people leave a car out. A car usually has a simple transfer process, and putting it in a trust can complicate insurance and registration. If you own several valuable cars, ask about it. Keep in mind that a car is often outside the Illinois small estate affidavit count.

## Stocks, cash, crypto

Stocks and brokerage accounts move easily. Cash can go in a trust bank account. Cryptocurrency can be assigned to the trust, but the trustee needs the keys. Record where they are kept. See the [crypto access plan](/free/crypto-and-digital-money-access-plan).

## Timeshares and firearms

A timeshare transfer may require approval or fees, and some resorts restrict who can inherit. See the [timeshare checklist](/free/timeshare-and-vacation-club-exit-checklist). Firearms can be assigned to a trust on paper, but transfers to a beneficiary follow federal and state law. See the [firearms checklist](/free/gun-owners-estate-checklist).

## Business interests

A trust can own shares of a corporation, a membership interest in an LLC or a partnership share. Check the operating agreement for transfer limits. The trustee has to be able to act for the business. See the [business succession guide](/learn/business-owners/business-succession-planning).

## What happens to anything left out

Assets not in the trust and without a beneficiary pass under the will, which usually means probate. A pour-over will sends them into the trust, but only after the court process. A small estate affidavit may work for small amounts of personal property.

## Common mistakes

- Retitling an IRA to the trust
- Leaving the house out of a trust after signing
- Forgetting new accounts opened after signing
- Failing to tell the insurance company

## When to talk to an attorney

- The house has a mortgage, a reverse mortgage or is in another state
- A business or rental property is involved
- A retirement account will benefit a trust
- You are not sure how a lender will respond
