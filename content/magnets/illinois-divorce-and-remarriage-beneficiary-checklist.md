## How to use this checklist

A divorce decree ends a marriage. It does not always change the forms on file with your insurer, your employer or your bank. Go account by account, mark what you find and send in new forms. This is general education, and Illinois law on this point has changed in recent years, so confirm the current rule for your situation.

## What Illinois does after a divorce

Illinois has a statute that revokes a former spouse's designation on certain accounts when a judgment of dissolution is entered. It is found in the Illinois Marriage and Dissolution of Marriage Act, 750 ILCS 5/503(b-5). <!-- verify: scope and effective date of 750 ILCS 5/503(b-5) --> A will works differently: 755 ILCS 5/4-7 generally treats gifts to a former spouse as revoked after divorce. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 5/4-7(b) wording -->

Do not rely on the statute alone. Other states differ, and some accounts follow different rules.

| Account type | Does divorce usually reset the beneficiary? | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Will | Often yes in Illinois for gifts to the former spouse | Sign a new will anyway |
| Revocable trust | Depends on the trust wording | Amend the trust |
| Individual life insurance | Illinois statute may revoke, insurers still want a form | Send a change form |
| Bank POD account | Not reliably | Change at the bank |
| Employer 401(k) or pension under ERISA | No. The plan paperwork controls | File a new form, check with the plan |
| IRA | Not reliably | File a new form |
| TOD brokerage account | Not reliably | File a new form |
| Joint property | Ends as joint with right of survivorship only if retitled | Retitle |

Federal law governs most employer plans, and federal law can override a state statute. A former spouse named on an old 401(k) form has sometimes collected even though the divorce decree said otherwise. A decree is not a beneficiary form.

## Step flow after a divorce

1. **List every account.** Use the table below.
2. **Request a copy of the form on file.** Companies often keep the original.
3. **Decide the new beneficiary and the backup.** Name a contingent too.
4. **File the change.** Get written confirmation.
5. **Check the decree.** It may require you to keep an ex-spouse as beneficiary of life insurance for children's support. Do not remove them without reading it.
6. **Update the will, the trust and your powers of attorney.**

## After remarriage

Marrying again does not remove a prior beneficiary. If you named your first spouse and then remarry, the first spouse stays on the form until you change it.

- [ ] Decide whether the new spouse should be named
- [ ] Check whether a 401(k) or pension requires your new spouse's written consent to name someone else. Federal rules often require it for employer plans
- [ ] Update contingent beneficiaries for children from the first marriage
- [ ] Review any prenuptial agreement that promised accounts to either side

A surviving spouse often has automatic rights in a 401(k). That is why a plan asks a spouse to sign when someone else is named. IRAs, bank accounts and life insurance usually do not need spousal consent in Illinois, because Illinois is not a community property state. Community property states, such as California and Texas, add spousal claims to some accounts.

## After a spouse dies

- [ ] Find every account that named your late spouse as primary or contingent
- [ ] Name a new primary and contingent for your own accounts
- [ ] Remove the late spouse from joint titles, deeds and vehicle titles
- [ ] Update your will, trust and powers of attorney, which may name your late spouse

If a beneficiary dies before you and nobody is named after them, the money often goes to the estate, which can mean probate.

## Unmarried partners

You can name a partner on life insurance, IRAs, bank accounts and many other accounts, whether or not you are married. A spouse with spousal rights in an employer plan has to approve it in writing. An old divorce settlement may also limit your choices.

## When to talk to an attorney

- A decree and a beneficiary form seem to say opposite things
- You are still paying support that is tied to life insurance
- A 401(k) or pension is involved and you remarried
- You own accounts in more than one state
- Someone died and an ex-spouse is still the named beneficiary
