## How to use this planner

An ABLE account is a tax-advantaged savings account for a person with a disability. It is also called a 529A account. Work through each part, fill in the lines and bring the page to your attorney or the plan you choose. Details vary by state plan and change over time, so confirm current numbers before you act.

## Part 1: Is the person eligible?

Check each item.

- [ ] The disability began before a set age. That age was 26. Starting in 2026, it is 46, so more adults now qualify.
- [ ] The person receives SSI or SSDI, or can show a qualifying condition by a doctor's certification
- [ ] There is a single ABLE account owner. The person with a disability is the owner, even if a parent or agent manages it.

Each person generally can have one ABLE account. The disability onset age matters, not the age today.

## Part 2: What it does for benefits

- **SSI:** Social Security does not count the first $100,000 in the account toward the $2,000 SSI resource limit. If the balance goes above $100,000, SSI payments are suspended until it falls back under, but Medicaid generally continues.
- **Medicaid:** Rules vary by state. Ask your state Medicaid office.
- **Taxes:** Earnings grow tax deferred, and withdrawals are tax free when used for qualified disability expenses.

## Part 3: How much can go in

- Total yearly contributions from everyone, including the owner, are tied to the annual gift tax exclusion, which is $19,000 for 2026. Confirm the current figure with your plan.
- A working owner who is not covered by certain employer retirement plans may add more, up to a federal poverty-line amount. Ask the plan how to claim this.
- Contributions are not deductible on the federal return, though some states give a deduction.
- Money in a 529 college plan can sometimes be rolled to an ABLE account, within the yearly limit.

## Part 4: What the account can pay for

The test is a "qualified disability expense": a cost related to the disability that helps health, independence or quality of life. The list is broad. Examples:

| Usually fits | Ask first |
|---|---|
| Education and job training | Anything that could be both personal and disability related |
| Housing, including rent and utilities | Large gifts to others |
| Transportation and vehicle changes | Cash withdrawals |
| Assistive technology | Items with no receipt |
| Health, dental and therapy costs | Spending the plan did not approve |
| Personal support services | |
| Legal fees and financial management | |

Housing spending is treated specially by SSI. Spending on housing can reduce the monthly SSI check by a set amount, though the money stays tax free. Ask a benefits counselor before you rely on it.

- [ ] Keep receipts for every withdrawal
- [ ] Spend from the ABLE account for the item, not the person's bank account
- [ ] Review spending yearly

## Part 5: The payback rule

When the owner dies, a state may claim what remains in the account to repay Medicaid it paid for the owner after the account was opened. Funeral and burial costs can often be paid first. States differ. If a payback would take most of the account, consider whether a different tool fits.

## Part 6: ABLE account or special needs trust?

| Question | ABLE account | Special needs trust |
|---|---|---|
| Who controls it? | The owner, or an agent for them | A trustee |
| Size limit | Yearly cap and $100,000 SSI cap | No set cap |
| Can hold an inheritance or settlement? | Only up to the yearly limit | Yes |
| Medicaid payback | Possible | Depends on type |
| Cost and effort | Low, online | Legal drafting |
| Housing spending | Counted for SSI | Rules differ |

Many families use both. The trust holds large sums, and the ABLE account covers day to day spending. See [special needs trust vs ABLE account](/compare/special-needs-trust-vs-able-account).

## When to talk to an attorney

- The person may receive a settlement, inheritance or large gift
- You want to know whether a trust or an ABLE account fits their situation
- A payback claim is a concern
- Housing and Medicaid waiver rules are unclear in your state
- You are naming a trustee or successor for the account
