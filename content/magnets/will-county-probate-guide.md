## How to use this guide

We are sorry for your loss. This guide is for estates of people who lived in Will County, Illinois, in the 12th Judicial Circuit. It explains the usual path and leaves room to record what the clerk tells you. Practices change, so confirm each detail with the court or an attorney. This is education, not legal advice.

## Does the estate belong in Will County?

Illinois sends a probate case to the county where the person had a known place of residence (755 ILCS 5/5-1). If the person lived in Will County, in a place such as Joliet, Bolingbrook, Plainfield or Frankfort, the case belongs here. Several of these towns sit on the county line, so check the county on the property tax bill or the voter record, not just the mailing city. If the person lived outside Illinois, the rules look to where their Illinois real estate or personal property sits, so ask an attorney.

Next, ask whether a court case is needed at all. A small estate affidavit may work when personal property is $150,000 or less for a death on or after August 15, 2025. Real estate in one person's name, or a family dispute, usually means a court case. See the [small estate affidavit guide](/free/illinois-small-estate-affidavit-guide).

## Where to go

The Will County Courthouse is at 100 West Jefferson Street, Joliet, IL 60432. The county's courts site lists the main courthouse phone as (815) 727-8592. It also lists a Court Annex at 57 N. Ottawa Street in Joliet, a limited-service branch, and the River Valley Justice Center at 3208 West McDonough in Joliet. The courts site does not say which building takes probate filings. Call the main courthouse number or the Circuit Clerk before you travel, and ask which building and which window you need.

## Local notes

- **Probate guardians ad litem:** The courts site has a "Find A Probate GAL" page. A guardian ad litem is a lawyer the court may appoint to look out for someone's interests, for example a minor or a person with a disability. It matters if an heir is a child or cannot act for themselves.
- **Forms:** The Circuit Clerk and the courts site post court forms. Ask the clerk which probate forms the court expects.
- **Questions about procedure:** The Office of the Chief Judge lists (815) 727-8540 on its information page. It is a place to ask about eFiling and will handling when the clerk is not sure.
- **Law library:** The courthouse has a law library with self-help resources. Librarians can point to books, though they cannot give legal advice.
- **Original will:** Ask how the clerk wants the original delivered.

## The Illinois steps that apply here

These steps come from the state Probate Act, so they are the same in every county.

| Step | What happens | Rule to know |
|---|---|---|
| File the will | The person holding the original gives it to the circuit clerk | Do it promptly. Hiding a will for 30 days after learning of the death can be a felony (755 ILCS 5/6-1) |
| Notice to heirs | Heirs and legatees get a copy of the petition and the order | Within 14 days after the order (755 ILCS 5/6-10) |
| Notice to creditors | Published once a week for three weeks in a county newspaper, and mailed to known creditors | Claim date is the later of six months from first publication or three months from mailing (755 ILCS 5/18-3) |
| Inventory | A verified list of assets is filed | Within 60 days after letters issue (755 ILCS 5/14-1) |
| Outer claim limit | Late claims are cut off | Two years after death (755 ILCS 5/18-12) |

For family members, an interested person can ask for formal proof of the will within 42 days after it is admitted (755 ILCS 5/6-21). A will contest must be filed within six months after admission (755 ILCS 5/8-1). Our [Illinois probate checklist](/free/illinois-probate-checklist) walks through every stage.

## When to talk to an attorney

- The will names no executor, or the person named cannot serve
- A relative may contest the will or disagrees about who should serve
- The estate owns real estate, a business or property in another state
- You are not sure the small estate affidavit is allowed
- A creditor sends a claim you do not understand
- You are asked to sign court papers and are not sure what they do
