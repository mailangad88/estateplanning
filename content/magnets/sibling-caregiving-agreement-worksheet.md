**How to use this worksheet.** Print one copy per sibling. Each person fills in Part 1 alone, then meet (video is fine) and complete Parts 2 to 6 together. The goal is a one-page agreement everyone signs. It is a family agreement, not a court order, and it does not replace the powers of attorney or other documents your parent signed. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Part 1: Each sibling's honest inventory

Fill in privately, then read aloud.

- **My name:** ______________________
- **Distance from Mom or Dad:** ______________________ miles
- **Hours a week I can give, realistically:** ______________________
- **My job or family limits (shifts, small children, health):** ______________________
- **Things I do well (money, paperwork, driving, cooking, medical talk):** ______________________
- **Things I cannot or will not do (personal care, nights, lifting):** ______________________
- **What I can contribute in money, if anything, each month:** ______________________
- **What I worry about most:** ______________________

Honesty matters here. A sibling who says "I cannot do hands-on care but I can pay bills and handle insurance calls" is more useful than one who promises and fades.

Fill in a name for each task. Several tasks can share one person, and some can rotate.

| Paying bills and keeping records | | | |

Tip: give paperwork to the sibling who lives far away. Distance is a handicap for hands-on tasks and not for phone calls, bills and online accounts.

- **Respite: each caregiver gets at least this much time off per month:** ______________________
- **How to ask for a swap, and how much notice:** ______________________
- **Who is the emergency contact to the facility, doctor and pharmacy:** ______________________
- **Backup plan if the main caregiver is sick or burned out:** ______________________

## Part 4: Money rules

Money is where sibling agreements most often break down. Decide in advance.

### Parent's money and siblings' money

- **Parent's money pays for:** ______________________ (care, medicine, home changes, transport, paid helpers)
- **Who may sign checks or use cards for the parent, and under what authority:** ______________________ (a power of attorney, a joint account or other)
- **Siblings pay for their own travel and time unless agreed:** Yes / No
- **Receipts: every purchase for the parent is logged with receipt and date.** Agreed by: ______________________
- **A shared spreadsheet or folder where records live:** ______________________
- **Review of the account by a second sibling each:** month / quarter

### Paying a sibling for care

Some families pay a sibling who provides a lot of care. Rules vary by state and by benefits. If a parent may need Medicaid for long-term care within the coming years, payments to family without a written agreement can create problems with look-back rules. Talk to an attorney before any payments start. A written agreement signed in advance is usually treated more favorably than money handed over after the fact. See the [guide on Medicaid and long-term care planning](/learn/elder-care/medicaid-planning).

- **Will any sibling be paid?** Yes / No
- **Rate, hours and who approves:** ______________________
- **A written care agreement is signed before payments:** Yes / No
- **Taxes: payments to a family caregiver may count as wages.** Ask an accountant or attorney.

### Gifts and loans

- **No sibling takes a gift or loan from the parent without telling the others:** Agreed by: ______________________
- **Tracking of earlier help (down payments, tuition):** ______________________

## Part 5: Decision rules

- **Who holds the health care power of attorney, and the financial power of attorney:** ______________________
- **Everyone has a copy, and knows where the originals are:** Yes / No. See the guide to [powers of attorney](/guides/powers-of-attorney).
- **Decisions the agent makes alone (small, routine):** ______________________
- **Decisions the agent discusses first (a move, a major medical choice, selling the home):** ______________________
- **How we reach a decision: consensus, majority, agent decides after listening:** ______________________
- **Emergency rule: the nearest sibling acts, then calls the others within hours.** Agreed by: ______________________
- **Parent's own wishes come first.** If the parent can still speak for themselves, we ask them.
- **A neutral person we ask if we are deadlocked (doctor, social worker, elder care manager, mediator):** ______________________

## Part 6: Keeping the peace

- [ ] A monthly 30-minute call, with notes sent to everyone.
- [ ] A shared group chat for urgent updates and a separate one for chat.
- [ ] A standing rule: no criticism of how a task was done without offering to do it.
- [ ] Gratitude is said out loud each month.
- [ ] Every six months, review this agreement and change what is not working.
- [ ] If someone cannot keep a commitment, they say so early.
- [ ] When tension rises, bring in a neutral third party before it becomes personal.

## The one-page agreement

Copy the final answers here.

- **Parent's name:** ______________________
- **Main tasks and owners:** ______________________
- **Money rules, in one sentence:** ______________________
- **Decision rule, in one sentence:** ______________________
- **Next review date:** ______________________

Sample closing wording: "We, the undersigned children of [parent], agree to share their care as described above. We will treat our parent's wishes first, share information openly, and revisit this agreement every six months."

- **Signatures and dates:** ______________________  ______________________  ______________________

## When to talk to an attorney

- A sibling will be paid, or someone is already receiving money from the parent.
- Your parent may need Medicaid for long-term care.
- There is no power of attorney or health care directive, and your parent may be losing capacity.
- Siblings disagree about the parent's capacity, or someone limits others' access.
- You suspect financial abuse or undue influence.
