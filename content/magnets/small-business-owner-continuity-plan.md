**How to use this planner.** Fill in each part in one sitting if you can. It is a working document, not a legal one, so keep it where a trusted person can find it. The aim is simple: if you were unreachable tomorrow, the business should be able to pay people, serve customers and protect its value while the legal steps get done.

## Part 1: What the business is

- **Legal name and any trade names:** ____________________
- **Entity type (sole proprietorship, LLC, partnership, corporation):** ____________________
- **State of formation:** ____________________
- **Owners and percentages:** ____________________
- **Governing documents (operating agreement, bylaws, buy-sell) and where kept:** ____________________
- **Registered agent:** ____________________
- **Federal tax ID location:** ____________________

The entity type changes what happens next. A sole proprietorship does not exist apart from you, so its assets may go through your estate. An LLC or corporation continues, but someone must have authority to act for your ownership share.

## Part 3: Money and signing authority

- [ ] Someone other than me is a signer on the business bank account or can be added fast.
- [ ] I have a durable power of attorney that covers business matters (see [Powers of Attorney](/guides/powers-of-attorney)).
- [ ] My operating agreement or bylaws say who steps in when an owner dies or is disabled.
- [ ] Payroll can run without me.
- [ ] Tax payments and filings have a named person.
- [ ] Credit cards and loans are listed, with notes on any you signed personally.

Personal signatures matter. If you signed a lease or loan personally, your family may face the debt even if the company closes.

## Part 6: The 30-day plan

Write this for your backup person. Edit it to fit your business.

**Days 1 to 3**
- [ ] Confirm who is in charge and tell the team and key clients in a short message.
- [ ] Secure the premises, cash and inventory.
- [ ] Contact the business attorney and accountant.
- [ ] Call the insurance agent to report the event and check business interruption or key person coverage.

**Days 4 to 10**
- [ ] Get the bank to allow a named signer. Banks usually need court papers or governing documents.
- [ ] Keep payroll, rent and vendor payments on schedule.
- [ ] Review which contracts include a change of control clause.
- [ ] Note any filing and tax deadlines.

**Days 11 to 30**
- [ ] Decide with the family whether to keep running, sell, or wind down.
- [ ] Get a valuation before accepting any offer.
- [ ] Reach out to key customers in person.
- [ ] Meet with the estate attorney to plan next steps.

## Sample message to staff

"I want you to hear from us directly. [Name] is not able to work. [Person] will lead day-to-day operations. Your pay and benefits continue as usual. Please send questions to [contact]. We will update you every [period]."

## Part 7: Gaps to close

Check any that apply, then bring the list to an attorney.

- [ ] No buy-sell or succession agreement (see the [Buy-Sell Agreement Checklist](/free/buy-sell-agreement-checklist)).
- [ ] No power of attorney covering the business.
- [ ] Ownership held in my own name when a trust might be better.
- [ ] No key person insurance.
- [ ] No written instructions for the successor.
- [ ] No plan for what my family wants: keep, sell, or close.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You have co-owners and no buy-sell agreement.
- You want ownership to pass to family or key staff.
- Your business holds licenses that limit who can own it.
- You have loans or leases you signed personally, or large debts.
- You are considering putting ownership in a trust.
