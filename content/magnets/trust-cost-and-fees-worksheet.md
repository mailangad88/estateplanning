## How to use this worksheet

Prices for trusts vary by state, by lawyer and by how complicated your family is. We do not quote prices here because they change and would mislead. Instead, this worksheet lists every place a cost can hide so you can compare quotes on the same terms. Fill in each line, add them up and compare to probate. This is general education, not legal advice.

## What moves the price

- The number of people, and whether anyone has special needs
- Whether a married couple needs one joint trust or two
- Real estate, especially in more than one state
- A business interest or rental properties
- A tax plan, such as a bypass trust
- How much funding help is included

Ask: "What is not included?" and "Is funding included, or is it my job?" Many families pay for a trust that never gets funded.

## Costs that depend on the trust type

| Trust | Why cost differs |
|---|---|
| Revocable living trust | A common starting point, one set of documents |
| Joint trust for a couple | One document for two, fewer deeds |
| Irrevocable trust | More drafting, tax returns, an EIN |
| Special needs trust | Benefit rules, a trustee who understands them, often more drafting |
| ILIT | Drafting plus yearly gift letters and tax filing |
| Pet trust | Short, often added to a plan |

For a state-by-state price, ask for a written quote. Prices in New York, California, Florida and Texas, and in Illinois, differ, and these pages do not publish a price list.

## Ongoing costs

| Cost | When | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| Annual fee to the lawyer | Rare for a revocable trust | You, if any |
| Tax return | Irrevocable and after death | The trust |
| Bank or brokerage account fees | Account level | Trust |
| Investment management fee | If used | Trust |
| Trust protector or accountant fees | If used | Trust |
| Updates and amendments | When life changes | You |

A revocable living trust normally has no separate annual filing while the creator is alive. There is no court registration fee for a private trust. An irrevocable trust files its own income tax return every year.

## Trustee pay

Can a trustee be paid? Yes. Most states, including Illinois, allow reasonable compensation unless the document says otherwise. <!-- verify: Illinois trustee compensation standard, 760 ILCS 3/708 -->

| Trustee | Typical way to pay |
|---|---|
| Family member | Often unpaid, or paid hourly or a modest percentage, reimbursed for expenses |
| Bank or trust company | A published fee schedule, often a percentage of assets plus minimums |
| Professional trustee | Hourly or a percentage |

How much does a trustee get paid? There is no single answer. Ask a corporate trustee for its fee schedule in writing, and ask about minimum annual fees, charges for real estate and charges for special tasks.

## Cost of changing a trust

A small change can be a short amendment. A big change can need a restatement. Amendments cost less than a new trust, but fees depend on the work. See the [amendment vs restatement guide](/free/trust-amendment-vs-restatement-guide). A few small changes may be done by a lawyer at a modest charge, so ask about the policy for updates.

A trust is not always cheaper. It can cost more upfront and save at death, especially with real estate in more than one state. A small estate may do fine with a will and beneficiary forms. Illinois has a small estate affidavit for personal property. See the [small estate affidavit guide](/free/illinois-small-estate-affidavit-guide).

## Is there a minimum amount?

There is no legal minimum to have a trust. The question is whether the cost is worth it for your facts. People with a house, real estate in several states, a blended family, a minor child or a family member with special needs often find it more useful than people with only bank accounts that have beneficiaries.

## Questions to ask for any quote

- [ ] What exactly is included, and is funding included?
- [ ] Is the price flat, and what changes it?
- [ ] Who drafts the documents, and are they licensed in my state?
- [ ] What does an update cost later?
- [ ] What happens if I move?
- [ ] Is there any ongoing fee?

## When to talk to an attorney

- You are comparing quotes and the scope differs
- A corporate trustee wants a minimum fee
- An irrevocable trust or special needs trust is involved
- You are unsure whether a trust or a will fits
