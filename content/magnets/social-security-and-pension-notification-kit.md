## How to use this kit

Government and pension payers need to know about a death. Some will learn it on their own, and some will not. This kit puts the calls in order, gives you words to use and keeps a record. Take it one payer at a time. Rules and forms change, so confirm each step with the payer.

## Before you call

- [ ] Several copies of the death certificate
- [ ] The person's Social Security number, full name and date of birth
- [ ] The date and place of death
- [ ] Your own ID and your relationship to the person
- [ ] Recent statements showing deposits, or a list of pensions and annuities
- [ ] A notebook, or the log at the end of this kit

## Social Security

A funeral home will usually report the death to Social Security if you give it the Social Security number. Ask the funeral director whether they did. If not, report it yourself by calling the Social Security Administration. Social Security says a funeral home will usually tell them, so you may not need to report it, but it is worth confirming.

The agency does not need you to report to several offices. One report is enough.

Points to know:

- **Payments after the month of death.** Social Security benefits are paid a month behind. In general, the payment for the month a person died is not payable, so a payment received after death usually needs to be returned. If it was deposited directly, the agency can take it back from the account. Do not spend it.
- **Lump sum.** A one-time $255 death payment may be available to a surviving spouse or child who qualifies.
- **Survivor benefits.** A surviving spouse, former spouse, child or dependent parent may qualify for monthly benefits based on the person's work. These require an application, and they do not start automatically.
- **Medicare.** The death ends the person's coverage. A surviving spouse should ask how their own coverage is affected.

> Script: "I am calling to report the death of [name], Social Security number ending in [last four]. The date of death was [date]. I am the [relationship]. Has the death been reported, and what do I need to do about a payment for [month]?"

## Pension and retirement plans

Employers and pension administrators often keep paying until they learn of a death. A payment that arrives after death can be demanded back, sometimes with a penalty. A pension may also offer a survivor benefit to a spouse, depending on the option chosen at retirement.

> Script: "I am calling to report the death of [name], who received a pension from [plan]. The date of death was [date]. What documents do you need, and does the plan pay a survivor or death benefit?"

Ask each plan:

- [ ] Was a survivor option chosen, and who is named?
- [ ] Is there a one-time death benefit?
- [ ] Is a final payment owed or should one be returned?
- [ ] Is any life insurance attached through the employer or a union?
- [ ] What are the tax forms for the year of death?

## Veterans Affairs and military retirement

If the person was a veteran or received military retired pay, notify the VA and the military pay office. Some benefits end with the death. Others may be available to survivors, such as burial allowances, a headstone or marker, or survivor compensation. Eligibility depends on service and circumstances, so confirm with VA directly.

> Script: "I am calling to report the death of veteran [name]. Service number or Social Security number ending in [last four]. I would like to know which benefits stop and which survivor benefits may be available."

## Annuities and insurance

Annuity and life insurance companies need a claim, not just notice. Ask for a claimant's statement and send a certified death certificate. If a beneficiary is named, the company usually pays that person directly. If none is named, the payment may go to the estate.

## Return or keep

| Payment | Usual handling |
|---|---|
| Social Security after the month of death | Not payable, expect it to be taken back |
| Pension paid after death | Often reclaimed by the plan |
| Final paycheck or accrued benefits | May be owed to the estate |
| Life insurance with a named beneficiary | Paid to the beneficiary, not the estate |
| Refund of a premium or overpayment | Ask the payer |

If you are unsure, hold the money in the account and ask. Returning a payment is easier than explaining a spent one.

## Tax records to keep

Keep every statement, form 1099 and letter. The executor or a tax preparer will need them for the final income tax return. Do not guess about amounts paid in the year of death.

## When to talk to an attorney

- A payer says the estate must repay a large amount
- You are not sure whether a pension survivor option applies
- A beneficiary is missing, or two forms disagree
- A second marriage or former spouse raises a question about survivor benefits
- You received a letter you do not understand
