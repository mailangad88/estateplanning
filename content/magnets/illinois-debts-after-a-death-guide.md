## How to use this guide

We are sorry you are dealing with this. The first thing to know is that debts belong to the person who died, not automatically to the family. Most debts are paid, if at all, from the estate. This guide shows who is responsible, in what order the estate pays and how to stop collectors from pushing you. It covers Illinois. Other states differ. This is general education, not legal advice.

## Who is responsible

| Debt | Who generally pays | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Credit card, in the deceased's name only | The estate | Children and relatives are generally not responsible |
| Credit card, joint account | The surviving joint holder | An authorized user is not the same as a joint holder |
| Medical bills | The estate, and sometimes a spouse | See [medical debt after death guide](/free/medical-debt-after-death-guide). Illinois has a family expense law that can reach a spouse. <!-- verify: Illinois Family Expense Act 750 ILCS 65 --> |
| Federal student loans | Usually discharged at death | Some private loans with a cosigner continue with the cosigner |
| Private student loans | Depends on the contract | Cosigners may owe |
| Car loan | The lender keeps the lien | Heirs who keep the car must keep paying |
| Mortgage | The lender keeps the lien on the house | See [mortgage after death guide](/free/mortgage-after-death-guide) |
| Reverse mortgage | Generally due when the last borrower dies | Heirs may repay, refinance or sell. Time limits apply. <!-- verify: HUD HECM repayment timeline --> |
| Taxes | The estate | Final returns are due |
| Child support owed | The estate, as a claim | Support ending at death depends on the order |

Collectors sometimes call family members as if they owe money. You do not have to agree to pay. Ask for the request in writing and say you are not the executor.

## The order Illinois pays claims

Illinois ranks claims by class (755 ILCS 5/18-10). <!-- verify: classes and current wording of 18-10 --> In outline:

1. Funeral and burial costs, and the costs of administering the estate
2. A surviving spouse's or child's award
3. Debts owed to the United States
4. Certain medical and similar expenses of the last illness, up to a limit
5. Debts owed to the state or a local government
6. Everything else, including credit cards and personal loans

Within a class, claims are generally paid in full or shared proportionally. A creditor in a lower class gets paid only if money remains.

## Do creditors come before heirs

Yes. Heirs and beneficiaries are paid after debts and taxes. An executor who gives out money first can be personally responsible if the estate falls short. For that reason many executors wait until the claim period ends before a final distribution.

## Step flow: claims and notices

1. **Open the estate.** An executor or administrator gets letters from the court.
2. **Publish notice.** The executor publishes a notice to creditors in a local newspaper and mails notice to known creditors. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 5/18-3 publication and mailing requirements -->
3. **Claim period.** Creditors have a set time to file a claim, at least six months from first publication. <!-- verify: exact claim deadline from first publication and from mailing -->
4. **Review claims.** The executor accepts or objects. A disputed claim may go to the court.
5. **Outer limit.** A claim is generally barred two years after death, even if no notice was given. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 5/18-12 two-year bar -->
6. **Pay in order.** Pay what is valid, in rank.
7. **Distribute.** After claims, taxes and costs.

How long an executor has depends on the case. There is no one-size deadline for paying debts. Expect to wait until at least the claim period ends.

## If the estate cannot pay everything

An estate is insolvent when debts and costs exceed what it holds.

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Stop | Do not pay heirs or lower-ranking creditors first |
| List | Make a list of assets and claims with dollar amounts |
| Rank | Pay in the statutory order until the money is gone |
| Keep records | An executor must account for every dollar |
| Heirs | Heirs generally get nothing, and do not have to pay the rest |
| Ask for help | An attorney can guide the order and the court filing |

Remaining debts are usually written off. Creditors cannot collect from the family just because the estate cannot pay, unless the family member is a cosigner or joint holder.

## What is not available to creditors

Assets that pass outside probate are often shielded from most creditors, including life insurance with a living beneficiary, retirement accounts with a named beneficiary and some property in a living trust. <!-- verify: Illinois protection of exempt assets from decedent's creditors --> Medicaid estate recovery is a separate topic. ## Common mistakes

- Paying a collector from your own account out of guilt
- Distributing assets before the claim period ends
- Ignoring a creditor's claim without a written response
- Letting a mortgage or car loan go into default
- Closing accounts before the estate has the records

## When to talk to an attorney

- Debts look larger than the assets
- A creditor has filed a claim you think is wrong
- A reverse mortgage or business debt is involved
- A collector is threatening you personally
- You are an executor who has already paid heirs
