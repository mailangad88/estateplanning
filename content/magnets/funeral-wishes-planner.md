**How to use this planner.** Fill in what you know and mark the rest "family to decide." Wishes do not need to be elaborate. Even a few clear lines help a grieving family make choices in a short time. Share a copy with the people most likely to carry it out.

## Why record wishes, and where

Funeral decisions often must be made within days. Without a record, relatives can disagree or choose something costly out of guilt. A written plan gives them direction.

Do not rely on your will alone. A will is often found and read days or weeks after the funeral. Keep your wishes in several places that are easy to reach (see Part 8).

Compare this with the shorter [funeral and burial wishes checklist](/checklists/funeral-and-burial-wishes).

## Part 5: Prepaid arrangements

Prepaying can reduce some decisions later, but terms vary a great deal. Ask these questions before signing, and get the answers in writing.

- [ ] Is the plan with a funeral home, a cemetery, or a third-party insurer?
- [ ] Where is the money held, and who protects it if the seller closes or is sold?
- [ ] Is it refundable? If so, how much, and by when?
- [ ] Can I transfer it to another funeral home or another state? What fees apply?
- [ ] Is the price locked in, or can it rise?
- [ ] What does it cover and what is left out (cemetery fees, clergy, flowers, death certificates)?
- [ ] What happens if I need Medicaid or move away?
- [ ] What if I pay in installments and stop paying?
- [ ] Who gets the paperwork and the contract when I die?

Record what you have:

- **Provider:** ______________________
- **Contract or policy number:** ______________________
- **Amount paid and date:** ______________________
- **Paperwork is kept at:** ______________________
- **Who knows about it:** ______________________

A prepaid plan with no one aware of it may never be used. Tell your executor and family.

## Part 6: Who decides

Who has authority over your remains varies by state. Many states give priority to a person you name in writing, often through a specific form or a healthcare power of attorney. If you name no one, the law usually ranks relatives, often starting with a spouse, then adult children, then parents.

- **The person I want to carry out my wishes:** ______________________
- **Backup:** ______________________
- **People who may disagree, and my thoughts on that:** ______________________
- **Does my state have a written form to name an agent for final arrangements?** ______________________

If your wishes differ from what the law would assume (for example, you want a friend rather than a relative to decide), ask an attorney licensed in your state how to make that clear and enforceable.

## Part 7: Cost awareness

Funerals cost much less when families know their options. The FTC Funeral Rule is a federal rule that requires funeral homes to give you an itemized general price list when you ask in person, and to quote prices by phone. It also says you may choose only the goods and services you want in many cases, and that a funeral home generally cannot refuse a casket bought elsewhere.

Questions to use when comparing providers:

- [ ] May I have the general price list to take home?
- [ ] What is the basic services fee, and what does it cover?
- [ ] What are the costs of a direct cremation or immediate burial?
- [ ] Which items are optional (embalming, viewing, hearse, flowers)?
- [ ] What third-party charges are passed through (cemetery, clergy, death certificates)?
- [ ] Do you charge to handle a casket or urn I bring?
- [ ] What happens to the plan if I change my mind?

Compare at least two or three providers and write down each answer. Ask them to explain any fee you do not understand.

- [ ] A copy with my executor

- [ ] A copy in my home file, in an easy-to-find folder
- [ ] A note on the fridge or in my wallet saying where it is kept
- [ ] A copy with my attorney, if I have one

A safe deposit box may be sealed or hard to open quickly. If you use one, keep a copy elsewhere too.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You want a specific person, not a relative, to control arrangements
- Family may disagree, such as between a spouse and children from earlier marriages
- You have a prepaid contract and are unsure of its terms
- You live in or may move to a state with different rules
- You want funeral costs covered through a trust or a Medicaid-compatible plan
