## How to use this checklist

We are sorry you are doing this. Being named executor means a lot of paperwork at a hard time. You do not have to do it all at once. Work from the top, check what you finish, and write down dates as you go. This is general information, not advice for your estate. Your county and your attorney can tell you what applies.

Most Illinois estates use independent administration. This means the executor can handle most steps without asking the court to approve each one. The court is still involved at the start and the end.

## First week

- [ ] Get several certified copies of the death certificate
- [ ] Find the original will and keep it safe
- [ ] Secure the home, vehicles and valuables, and keep insurance in force
- [ ] Forward the mail and note incoming bills
- [ ] Make a list of family members, with phone numbers and addresses

- **Date of death:** ____________________
- **Original will found at:** ____________________

## File the will and decide whether to serve

Illinois law says anyone holding the original will must file it with the circuit court clerk in the proper county immediately after death. Hiding or destroying a will for 30 days after learning of the death can be a felony. If you are named executor, the statute gives you 30 days after you learn of your appointment to start a probate case or to decline. You may also hire an attorney to start the case.

- [ ] Decide whether you will serve, and tell the family and the attorney
- [ ] File the original will with the clerk
- [ ] Petition for letters of office, asking for independent administration

- **County and case number:** ____________________
- **Date the will was filed:** ____________________
- **Date letters of office were issued:** ____________________

## Deadlines table

| Task | Illinois timing |
|---|---|
| File the will with the clerk | Immediately after the death |
| Decide whether to serve | Within 30 days after learning you are named |
| Send notice to heirs and legatees | Within 14 days after the order admitting the will or naming the representative |
| Publish notice to creditors | Once a week for 3 successive weeks |
| Mail notice to known creditors | After letters issue |
| Claims deadline | At least 6 months after first publication, or 3 months after mailing to a known creditor, whichever is later |
| Outer limit on claims | 2 years after the date of death |
| Surviving spouse may renounce the will | Within 7 months after the will is admitted |
| Will contest period | 6 months after the will is admitted |
| Illinois estate tax return, if required | 9 months after death |
| Closing objection period | 42 days after notice of the closing report |

Courts can extend some of these dates. Ask the clerk or your attorney before relying on a date.

## Notify the people who must be told

Within 14 days after the court order, send notice to heirs and legatees. The notice tells them about the estate and their rights.

- [ ] List every heir and every person named in the will, with addresses
- [ ] Mail the required notice and keep proof of mailing
- [ ] Give a copy of the will where the statute and court require

## Open an estate account and take inventory

- [ ] Get a tax identification number for the estate
- [ ] Open an estate checking account, and deposit every incoming check there
- [ ] List each asset and its value at the date of death
- [ ] Do not mix estate funds with your own money

The Probate Act sets a 60-day inventory rule for estates in general. Under independent administration, you do not file the inventory with the court. You mail or deliver it to interested persons. Your attorney can tell you the dates for your case.

- **Date inventory sent to heirs:** ____________________

## Creditors and claims

- [ ] Publish notice in a county newspaper once a week for three weeks
- [ ] Mail notice to creditors you know about
- [ ] Review each claim before paying
- [ ] Keep a log of claims received, allowed and paid

- **First publication date:** ____________________
- **Claim deadline set in the notice:** ____________________

Claims are paid in an order the statute sets. Funeral and burial expenses, administration expenses and fees come first. Awards to a surviving spouse or child come second. Debts due the United States are third. Medical and nursing home bills from the last year, and some wages, are fourth. Do not pay heirs first. If the estate cannot cover everything, talk with an attorney before paying anyone.

## Taxes

- [ ] File the decedent's final federal and state income tax returns
- [ ] File estate income tax returns if the estate earns income
- [ ] Check whether an Illinois estate tax return is needed, using the [Illinois estate tax guide](/free/illinois-estate-tax-guide)
- [ ] Keep every receipt and bank statement

## Distribute and close

- [ ] Pay valid claims, expenses and taxes
- [ ] Prepare an accounting for the heirs
- [ ] Distribute what remains as the will directs
- [ ] File the closing report with the court
- [ ] Wait out the objection period, then ask for discharge

Under the Probate Act, the closing report states that notice was given, that the inventory and accounting were mailed or delivered, that claims, taxes and expenses were paid, and that the remaining assets were distributed. After a 42-day objection period, the court enters an order discharging the independent representative and closing the estate.

## Take care of yourself too

- Ask for help with the paperwork. It is a normal request.
- Write down what you decide and why.
- Take one task at a time.

## When to talk to an attorney

- The will is unclear, or family members disagree.
- The estate owes debts, taxes or has a business to wind down.
- There is real estate in more than one state.
- Someone threatens a will contest.
- You are unsure whether to serve, or whether you will be paid.
