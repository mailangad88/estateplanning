**How to use this checklist.** Take it one section at a time. It is meant to make life easier for you now and for the people who will help you. If you are an adult child using this with a parent, read each section aloud and let your parent decide. Rules vary by state.

## Simplify accounts

Every account is a task for someone later and a statement to watch now.

- [ ] List all bank, brokerage, retirement and old workplace accounts
- [ ] Close accounts you no longer use, after moving money with the paper trail kept
- [ ] Keep one main checking account and one savings account where practical
- [ ] Put payable-on-death beneficiaries on bank accounts that do not already pass another way
- [ ] Combine old IRAs when it does not trigger a tax or lose a useful feature
- [ ] Set up paperless statements to a trusted email, and put key mail on a calendar

## Required distributions and taxes

Retirement accounts that were tax-deferred generally require annual withdrawals once you pass a set age, and the penalty for missing one can be large. At this age it is easy to miss one when accounts have been scattered.

- [ ] Confirm which accounts carry a required minimum distribution each year
- [ ] Ask each custodian to automate it, or set one account to cover the total where the rules allow
- [ ] Decide whether to give part of the distribution directly to charity, which some taxpayers can do
- [ ] Keep a folder of tax papers a family member can find

## Power of attorney readiness

A signed document is not the same as one that works. Banks and brokerages sometimes refuse older or unfamiliar forms.

- [ ] Reread the power of attorney. Does it name a backup agent?
- [ ] Does it allow gifting, handling taxes, retirement accounts and real estate if you want it to?
- [ ] Take a copy to each bank and ask whether they will accept it, or ask for their own form
- [ ] Make sure the agent has met your adviser and attorney
- [ ] Review a health care power of attorney and living will, and confirm agents have copies

- **Who I would call first in an emergency:** ____________________
- [ ] Review home safety: stairs, lighting, bathrooms, medication handling
- [ ] Price home care in your area and assisted living for comparison
- [ ] Know how Medicare, any long-term care insurance and savings fit together
- [ ] Learn how a Medicaid look-back works before any large gift or transfer
- [ ] Write down medical wishes and share them with your agent and doctor

Use the [Medicaid look-back date tool](/tools/medicaid-lookback-date) and see [caring for aging parents](/life-events/caring-for-aging-parents).

## Gifting on purpose

Gifts can be a pleasure, but they affect taxes, Medicaid eligibility and your own security.

- [ ] Choose an amount you can afford to give no matter what care costs
- [ ] Keep records of each gift, date and recipient
- [ ] Know the annual gift exclusion, $19,000 per recipient for 2026, and that gifts above it generally require a gift tax return, even if no tax is due
- [ ] Pay medical or tuition bills directly to the provider or school, which are treated differently from gifts
- [ ] Never give away a home or large asset without asking about Medicaid, tax basis and your own housing

## Make the paper easy to find

- [ ] A one-page list of documents and where they are
- [ ] Spare keys, alarm codes and passwords in a place the agent knows
- [ ] Funeral wishes written down
- [ ] Pet care and household instructions noted
- [ ] Names of the attorney, adviser, CPA and doctors on one page

## When to talk to an attorney

- You think you may need paid care within the next few years.
- You want to give away property or move it into a trust.
- Your powers of attorney are old or a bank rejected them.
- Someone is pressuring you for money or access to accounts.
- You have a disabled family member or a blended family.
- You are unsure whether you still have capacity to sign, and want it documented.
