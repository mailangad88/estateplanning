## How to use this guide

Taxes on a business depend on its value, who gets it and when. Read the sections that match your question and fill in the worksheet at the end. Figures change, so treat anything here as a starting point for an adviser.

## Who decides the value

At death, the value is generally what a willing buyer would pay a willing seller, known as fair market value. The executor reports it on the estate tax return if one is required. The IRS can challenge it. For a business with no market, a written appraisal by a qualified appraiser is the usual support.

## When to get a valuation

| Situation | Valuation helps because |
|---|---|
| Starting estate planning | You learn whether the business drives your tax picture |
| Setting a buy-sell price | Owners can agree on a formula that is not stale |
| Gifting shares to children | A gift tax return needs a supportable value |
| An owner dies | The estate needs a date-of-death value for tax and basis |
| One child gets the business | The others' share needs a number to equalize |

Ask what kind of report you need. A formal appraisal costs more than a broker's opinion of value, and the tax return may require the formal kind.

## Federal estate tax

For 2026 the federal estate tax exemption is $15 million per person, indexed for inflation after that. <!-- verify: federal estate tax exemption, 2026 --> Most families are below it. If the business plus other assets are below the exemption, no federal estate tax is due. Some states have a lower threshold, including Illinois. Use the [state death tax checker](/tools/state-death-tax-checker).

Executors of estates where a closely held business is a large part may be able to spread the estate tax payment over time under a deferral rule in the tax code. The rules are technical and have thresholds. Ask a tax attorney whether it applies.

## Step-up in basis

Basis is the starting number used to compute gain when something is sold. When an owner dies, heirs commonly receive a new basis equal to the date-of-death value (a step-up). That can remove much of the taxable gain on a later sale.

| Transfer | Basis the child commonly receives |
|---|---|
| Inherited at death | Value at date of death |
| Gift during life | The giver's basis (carryover) |
| Purchased | Price paid |

Partnership interests and S corporation shares follow the same idea, with added details. For partnerships, a section 754 election can adjust the partnership's basis in its assets after a partner dies. Ask the partnership's accountant.

## Selling an inherited business

If heirs sell soon after death and the sale price is close to the stepped-up value, the gain is often small. If they hold and the value grows, the later gain is taxed. Sales of assets inside a company can produce different results than a sale of the company's shares.

## Valuation discounts

Minority interests and shares that are hard to sell are sometimes valued at a discount. The IRS scrutinizes these.

| Discount | Idea | Common challenge |
|---|---|---|
| Lack of control | A minority owner cannot direct the company | Is the discount reasonable? |
| Lack of marketability | No ready buyer for the shares | Supported by comparable data? |
| Family entity structure | An LLC or limited partnership holds assets | Must have a real business reason |

Discounts are not automatic. A qualified appraiser documents them.

## Buy-sell price and estate tax

A buy-sell price may or may not be accepted as the estate tax value. The IRS looks at whether the agreement is a genuine business arrangement, with a price comparable to arm's-length deals. An outdated or one-sided price can be ignored. Review the price on a schedule.

## Gift tax on shares

Giving LLC interests or shares to children is a gift. If the value is above the annual exclusion, a gift tax return is generally filed, and the excess uses part of the lifetime exemption. The exclusion was $19,000 per recipient for 2025. <!-- verify: annual exclusion for 2026 -->

- Your estate is close to or above a state or federal threshold
- You plan to gift shares
- A buy-sell agreement sets the price
- An owner has died and a return is due
- You want to claim a discount
