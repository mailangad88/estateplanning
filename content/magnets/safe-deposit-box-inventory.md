**How to use this worksheet.** Take the next trip to the bank with this printed out, a pen and your ID. Open the box, list what is inside, and decide for each item whether it stays. Do this once, then repeat every year or two. Bank and state rules differ on who can open a box after a death and what is allowed, so call your bank with the questions at the end.

## Good candidates for a box

- [ ] Original deeds, titles and property records
- [ ] Birth, marriage, adoption and military discharge papers
- [ ] Stock or bond certificates that exist on paper
- [ ] Valuable jewelry, coins or collectibles, with a photo inventory kept at home
- [ ] Spare keys to a vacation home, if appropriate
- [ ] Appraisals and proof of ownership for valuables
- [ ] A copy of your will or trust, with the original kept where your attorney says

## What does not belong in a box

Some things are needed fast, at night or on a weekend, when the bank is closed.

- [ ] **Health care power of attorney and living will.** Keep copies at home, with your agent and with your doctor. A hospital cannot wait for a bank to open.
- [ ] **Burial and funeral wishes.** A family may need them before they can enter the box.
- [ ] **Your only original will.** Some banks limit access after a death, and a court may need the original. Ask your attorney. Many keep originals in their own vault.
- [ ] **Passwords and recovery codes.** Better in a password manager with a sharing plan.
- [ ] **Cash.** A box is generally not insured against loss in the way a deposit account is, so check what the bank covers and what your homeowner's policy says.
- [ ] **Anything you need weekly.** Passports you travel with, for example.

## Does your insurance cover it?

Banks usually do not insure what is inside. Homeowner's or renter's policies often cover contents only up to a low limit, or exclude some items. Ask your insurer.

## Access plan

A box only helps if the right person can open it.

- **Primary person who can open it:** ____________________
- **Backup person:** ____________________
- **How they will know it exists:** ____________________
- **Where the key and box number are written:** ____________________

Options include adding a co-renter, naming a deputy or agent on the bank's form, or leaving instructions with your attorney. Each has trade-offs. A joint renter can generally enter on their own while you are alive, which carries risk if trust is thin. A power of attorney may or may not satisfy the bank, and a power of attorney usually ends at death.

## What banks commonly do at death

Rules vary by state and bank, so confirm yours. In general:

- The bank may seal a box when it learns of a death, or limit entry to an inventory with a representative present.
- A surviving co-renter may or may not be able to open it.
- The executor or personal representative usually needs a court appointment, a death certificate and ID.
- Some states allow a limited search for a will, burial instructions or insurance policy, with the contents listed and handed over.
- If the key is lost, drilling the box can cost a fee.

See [how probate works](/guides/how-probate-works) for when the court gets involved.

## Questions to ask your bank

- [ ] Who can enter after my death, and what documents will they need?
- [ ] Can my executor open it before they have formal court papers?
- [ ] Is there a list or search procedure for wills?
- [ ] What happens if the fee is not paid or the key is lost?
- [ ] Will the bank accept my power of attorney form for access?
- [ ] Is a box mailed notices at my current address?

## Yearly check

- [ ] Confirm the fee is paid and the address on file is current
- [ ] Compare the box with this list
- [ ] Remove anything that is now out of date
- [ ] Add the new valuables, photos or titles
- [ ] Update the people who know

## When to talk to an attorney

- You hold the only original of your will or trust in the box.
- Your box is in a different state than you live in.
- You want a family member added, and are unsure how it affects ownership.
- You are an executor and the bank will not open the box.
- You own coins, art or other items that may be taxable or hard to value.
