**How to use this template.** Copy the sections below into a document or print them and write by hand. Fill in what applies and skip the rest. Write in your own voice, as if talking to the person who will read it. Keep it somewhere your executor can reach, and tell them where it is.

## What this letter is, and is not

A letter of instruction is a practical guide for your family. It is not a will. In most places it is not legally binding, and it cannot transfer property by itself.

Do not use it to give away assets differently from your will. If the letter and the will disagree, the will usually controls, and the mismatch can cause fights or delays. Use the letter for information, preferences and messages. Rules vary by state, so ask an attorney licensed in yours if you are unsure.

Do not put passwords, PINs or full account numbers in this letter. Say where they are stored instead.

For the short outline version, see the [Letter of Instruction checklist](/checklists/letter-of-instruction-outline).

## Keeping the letter useful

- Tell your executor and one backup person where it is
- Do not store it only in a safe deposit box that may be sealed after your death
- Review it every year, and after any move, marriage, divorce, birth or death in the family
- Keep a dated copy of each version and note which one is current

### Common mistakes

- Contradicting the will on who gets what
- Naming a beneficiary in the letter who was not named on the account form
- Listing passwords in plain text
- Leaving the letter somewhere no one will look

## When to talk to an attorney

- Your letter lists wishes that differ from your will
- You want pet care or a keepsake list to be legally enforceable
- Your family has conflict, a blended household or a business to pass on
- You own property in more than one state
- You are not sure whether an account follows your will or a beneficiary form
