**How to use this checklist.** Print it and work through it a few weeks before the procedure if you can. Most surgeries go as planned. This list is about making recovery simpler and making sure someone you trust can step in if plans change. Rules for forms differ by state, so ask the hospital or an attorney licensed in your state if something is unclear.

## 1. Health care decisions

If you are asleep or too unwell to decide, hospital staff look for someone legally able to decide for you. Without a named person, state law usually sets an order of relatives, which may not match who you would choose.

- [ ] I have named a **healthcare agent** (sometimes called a health care proxy or health care power of attorney).
- [ ] I have named a **backup agent** in case the first is unavailable.
- [ ] My agent has agreed to serve and knows what matters to me.
- [ ] I have a signed **advance directive or living will** that states my wishes about life support, ventilators and feeding tubes.
- [ ] I have told my surgeon and anesthesiologist what my directive says, and asked how the hospital handles it during surgery.
- [ ] I have asked whether the hospital suspends any "do not resuscitate" wishes during the operation and discussed how I feel about that.

Many hospitals offer forms and a staff member who can witness or notarize. Ask during pre-admission. See [healthcare directives and living wills](/guides/healthcare-directives-and-living-wills) and the comparison of a [living will and a healthcare power of attorney](/compare/living-will-vs-healthcare-power-of-attorney).

Conversation starters for your agent:

- **What I would want if recovery takes much longer than expected:** ______________________
- **What I would not want:** ______________________
- **Faith, cultural or personal preferences the team should know:** ______________________

## 2. Privacy release (HIPAA)

Federal privacy rules limit what a provider can tell your family. A signed release lets chosen people receive information.

- [ ] I have signed a **HIPAA authorization** naming the people who may talk to my doctors.
- [ ] The hospital has my form on file, and I know where my copy is.
- [ ] I have included my healthcare agent, backup agent and anyone who will handle insurance questions.

## 3. Financial power of attorney

A healthcare agent cannot usually pay your rent or talk to your bank. A **financial power of attorney** can.

- [ ] I have a signed **durable financial power of attorney** naming someone I trust to handle money if I cannot.
- [ ] I have read it, or an attorney has explained it, so I know which powers I have granted.
- [ ] My agent knows the document exists, and where to find it.
- [ ] I know whether my bank requires its own form. Some do.

Many people choose a power of attorney that takes effect right away and trust the agent to use it only when needed. Others choose one that takes effect only after a doctor certifies incapacity. Both are options. Rules vary by state. See [powers of attorney](/guides/powers-of-attorney).

## 4. Bills and money while you recover

- [ ] Rent, mortgage, utilities and insurance premiums are scheduled to **autopay** or paid ahead.
- [ ] I have listed credit cards, loans and subscriptions with due dates.
- [ ] Someone knows how to check my mail and open important letters.
- [ ] I have asked my employer about **sick leave, short-term disability or FMLA** paperwork and filed it.
- [ ] I have asked the hospital's billing office for an **estimate** and about financial assistance if costs are a worry.
- [ ] I know my deductible and the out-of-pocket limit on my health plan.
- [ ] I have checked whether my surgeon, anesthesiologist and facility are all in my network.

Money list for the helper:

- **Bills due while I am away:** ______________________
- **Who can pay them, and from which account:** ______________________
- **Employer contact for leave paperwork:** ______________________

## 5. Keys, passwords and access

- [ ] Someone has a spare **house key** and the alarm code.
- [ ] My **phone passcode** is available to my agent or in a sealed envelope, if I am comfortable with that.
- [ ] I use a **password manager**, and a trusted person knows how to open it in an emergency.
- [ ] Two-factor codes will not lock out the person helping me. If my phone is the second factor, I have a plan.
- [ ] I have written down where important papers live: insurance cards, will, deeds, account lists.

Do not write passwords on the same page as account numbers. Keep this list in a safe place and not on a form handed to hospital staff. For a fuller approach, see the [digital assets inventory](/checklists/digital-assets-inventory).

## 6. Pets, kids and people who depend on you

- [ ] Someone will feed, walk and care for **pets**, and has the vet's number and any medications.
- [ ] I have arranged **child care** for the days I am in the hospital and the days after, including school pickups.
- [ ] Anyone caring for my children has a signed **medical consent form** for them if I am not available (many states have a short form).
- [ ] I have a backup for each of the above.
- [ ] I have arranged help for any adult who depends on me, such as an elderly parent.
- [ ] Meals, rides home and a first week of household help are lined up.

## 7. Wills and beneficiaries

You do not need to think the worst. Still, a hospital stay is a natural time to check the basics.

- [ ] My **will** is current and I know where the original is kept.
- [ ] My **beneficiary designations** on retirement accounts and life insurance are up to date. See [beneficiary designations](/guides/beneficiary-designations).
- [ ] If I have minor children, my will names a guardian.
- [ ] My family knows where my documents are.

## 8. What to bring to the hospital

- [ ] Photo ID and insurance cards.
- [ ] A current **medication list** with doses.
- [ ] A copy of my advance directive and healthcare power of attorney.
- [ ] A short list of **emergency contacts**, with phone numbers.
- [ ] A list of my allergies and past surgical problems, such as reactions to anesthesia.
- [ ] Reading glasses, hearing aids, dentures and chargers, labeled with my name.
- [ ] A notepad to jot down questions and discharge instructions.

Leave valuables, large amounts of cash and original estate documents at home. Bring copies. Give a second set to your agent and keep one at home where someone can find it.

## 9. Right before you go

- [ ] My agent and backup agent have a copy of everything above.
- [ ] I have told one trusted person where this checklist is.
- [ ] I have asked the pre-op team any questions I still have about what happens if complications arise.
- [ ] I have a plan for who updates family and friends after the procedure.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You do not have a healthcare directive or power of attorney, and surgery is within a few weeks.
- You are unsure whether your documents will be accepted in the state where you are having surgery.
- You and your family disagree about who should make decisions.
- You have minor children, a dependent adult or a business that needs coverage.
- Your surgery is high risk, or you have a serious diagnosis and want your whole plan reviewed.
