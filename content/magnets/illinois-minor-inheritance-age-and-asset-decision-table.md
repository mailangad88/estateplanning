**How to use this planner.** List what each child could inherit, pick a tool from the table, and fill in the age schedule. In Illinois, a child under 18 cannot manage a large inheritance alone, so someone has to hold it. If you do not choose that someone, a court may. Other states set their own ages and rules. This is general education, not legal advice.

## What happens if you do nothing

A minor cannot sign contracts or manage property. If a will or form leaves money straight to a minor, one of three things usually happens:

1. A court appoints a guardian of the estate who manages the money under court rules, files reports and may need to post a bond
2. A custodian holds it under the Illinois Uniform Transfers to Minors Act, 760 ILCS 20/, if one was named
3. A parent holds it informally, which works only for small amounts

Small sums may be paid in a simpler way, with a limit that the statute sets. <!-- verify: Illinois threshold for paying a minor's money without a guardian of the estate -->
Court supervision costs money and the child often receives everything at 18.

## Decision table

| Asset or goal | Tool that often fits | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A modest gift, such as a car or savings | UTMA custodial account | Simple, low cost, no court |
| A larger inheritance | Children's trust | You choose the age, rules and trustee |
| Education savings | 529 plan | Tax advantaged, with an owner you choose |
| A house | Trust, or a sale and then a trust | A minor cannot hold title and manage it |
| Retirement account | Name the trust, or a custodian, as beneficiary | A minor needs an adult to manage the account |
| Life insurance | Trust as beneficiary | Avoids a court guardian for the money |
| Gifts for several young children | Family pool trust | Money is shared by need until the youngest reaches an age you set |
| Grandchildren | A trust, UTMA gift or 529 | You choose control and timing |

## UTMA: the age question

Under the Illinois UTMA the custodian generally must hand over the property when the minor reaches 21, in most cases. <!-- verify: Illinois UTMA age of transfer, 760 ILCS 20/ -->
Other states use 18, 21 or a range up to 25. A UTMA account is the child's property. Once the age arrives, the child can use it for anything.

| Feature | UTMA | Children's trust |
|---|---|---|
| Who sets the age | The statute | You |
| Can money stop at a milestone | No | Yes |
| Can you stagger it | No | Yes |
| Trustee oversight | Custodian only | Trustee with written rules |
| Cost to set up | Low | Higher |

## Choosing the age

There is no right age. Many families pick later ages than 18. Think about maturity, other help, the size and the reason for the money.

| Question | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| How large is the gift? | A small gift at 21 may be fine, a large one may call for more time |
| Would the child need it for school or a home? | Allow for needs at a younger age |
| Does the child have a disability? | Use a special needs trust, since outright money can end benefits |
| Is there a history of addiction or poor money habits? | A discretionary trust helps |

## A house or a retirement account

- **House:** A minor can own a house, but a guardian or trustee must run it. The court may have to approve a sale. A trust lets a trustee rent it, sell it or let a guardian live in it.
- **Retirement account:** A minor named as beneficiary can lead to a guardian or custodian taking over. Federal rules on payout periods apply to inherited accounts, and the rules differ for a minor child of the owner. <!-- verify: current federal payout rules for a minor child beneficiary -->
  Ask whether a trust should be the beneficiary and who the trustee is.

## Family pool trust

A family pool trust holds money together for young children and pays for each child's needs, such as school and health, until the youngest reaches an age you pick. Then it splits. It suits families where a child may need more than the rest.

## Grandchildren and 529 plans

You can leave money to a grandchild in a will or a trust, give during life or fund a 529 plan. Gifts to a grandchild can reach generation skipping tax rules for larger amounts, which an attorney can check.

A 529 plan is an account for education costs. The account owner controls it, not the child. In an estate plan:

- Name a successor owner so the account does not stall at your death
- Treat contributions as gifts, with annual exclusion rules. See [gift tax and annual exclusion guide](/free/gift-tax-and-annual-exclusion-guide)
- Know that a 529 plan does not replace a trust for other uses

See also the [grandchildren education gift planner](/free/grandchildren-education-gift-planner).

## Wording in a will

To leave money to a minor in a will, many people write a testamentary trust, which starts at death. Illustrative wording: "I give $50,000 to my niece, in trust, to be held by my sister as trustee until my niece reaches age 25." A living trust avoids probate and may be simpler. Ask which your attorney recommends.

## Guardian oversight

Money handled by a court guardian is watched through reports. A guardian who misuses funds can be ordered to repay and removed. To add control:

- Name a trustee who is not the guardian
- Ask for annual statements to a named relative
- Require the trustee to pay the guardian for expenses with receipts
- Give a successor the power to remove the trustee

## When to talk to an attorney

- The inheritance is large or includes a house
- A child has a disability or special needs
- You want different ages for different children
- A retirement account or insurance policy is involved
- You are leaving assets to grandchildren
