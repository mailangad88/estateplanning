## How to use this checklist

Many trust problems show up only after the person who made it has died. A trust can look fine and still fail because it was signed wrong, never funded or sold by someone who did not understand the family. Go through each section and mark what you can confirm. This is general education, not legal advice. Rules vary by state.

## Does Illinois require witnesses or a notary?

Illinois has a Trust Code (760 ILCS 3). A revocable trust generally needs a written document, a settlor with capacity who intends to create a trust, a trustee, identifiable beneficiaries and trust property. <!-- verify: Illinois Trust Code requirements for creating a trust, 760 ILCS 3/401 and 3/402 --> Illinois does not require a trust to be witnessed or notarized to exist, though many are signed before a notary so the trustee can prove it later.

A notary is required for another step: a deed moving a house into the trust must be signed and notarized to be recorded with the county recorder. See the [house in trust deed worksheet](/free/house-in-trust-deed-worksheet).

| Question | Short answer in Illinois |
|---|---|
| Must a trust be witnessed? | Not by statute for a trust, but a will paired with it needs two witnesses |
| Must it be notarized? | Not to exist, but recommended; a deed needs notary |
| Must it be filed with a court? | No. A private trust is not filed or registered |
| Is it public record? | The trust itself is private, a recorded deed is public |
| Does it need its own EIN? | Generally not while it is revocable and the grantor is alive. After death, yes |

A pour-over will that goes with a trust is a separate document, and it does have to meet Illinois will signing rules. See [what makes a will valid](/guides/what-makes-a-will-valid).

## Signing checklist

- [ ] The document names the trust, the date and the person creating it
- [ ] The creator signed and dated it
- [ ] A trustee and a successor trustee are named
- [ ] Beneficiaries are named clearly
- [ ] The notary stamp is complete, if used
- [ ] All pages are present and numbered
- [ ] Amendments, if any, are signed and attached
- [ ] A certification of trust exists for banks
- [ ] Assets are retitled. A signed trust with an empty funding schedule does little. See the [trust funding checklist](/free/trust-funding-checklist)

## Can someone with dementia create a trust?

A trust maker must have capacity when signing. Capacity can fluctuate, and a diagnosis is not by itself proof of incapacity. If you are concerned, a doctor's letter on the day of signing and an attorney's file notes can matter later. See the [contest guide](/free/contest-a-beneficiary-change-guide) for the same issues in another setting.

## Online and do-it-yourself trusts

A trust made online or from a template can be valid. The risk is not the platform, it is the details.

| Risk | What goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Wrong state language | Terms that do not fit Illinois law |
| No funding help | House and accounts never retitled |
| Missing backup plans | Nobody named when a trustee cannot serve |
| Tax and special-needs issues | A beneficiary on benefits loses them |
| Poor signing | A lost or unsigned page |

DIY can work for a simple family with few assets. Blended families, large estates, out-of-state property, disabled beneficiaries and business owners often need advice.

## Seminars, mills and scams

A "trust mill" sells standard trusts at a seminar or by phone, often at a high price, then does little follow-up. Not every seminar is a scam. Look at how they sell.

Red flags:

- [ ] Pressure to sign today or "this price ends tonight"
- [ ] A promise that a trust will end all taxes or cure all legal problems
- [ ] No lawyer licensed in Illinois reviews your documents
- [ ] The seller is also an insurance or annuity salesperson who benefits from your choices
- [ ] A package price that includes products you did not ask for
- [ ] No help with deeds and retitling
- [ ] Vague answers about cost, who drafts the documents and who answers questions later
- [ ] Reluctance to put the agreement in writing

Ask the Illinois Attorney Registration and Disciplinary Commission whether the person is licensed. <!-- verify: ARDC lawyer search availability -->

## How trust challenges work

A trust can be challenged on grounds such as lack of capacity, undue influence, fraud, improper signing or forgery. A trust is often harder to contest than a will because it is a private lifetime arrangement and a trustee may start administering without court oversight. But it can be contested.

In Illinois, the Trust Code sets a limit for challenging a revocable trust after the settlor's death. In general it is the earlier of a set period after death or a shorter period after the trustee sends proper notice. <!-- verify: 760 ILCS 3/604 deadlines --> Do not wait. Trustees who send notices are starting a clock.

## Quick decision table

| If this, then that |
|---|
| Your trust was drafted by an attorney and funded | Keep a certification of trust, review every few years |
| Your trust came from a kit | Have an Illinois attorney read it |
| You bought one at a seminar | Ask who drafted it, and read the contract and refund terms |
| You got a notice as a beneficiary | Note the date, and ask an attorney about the deadline |

## When to talk to an attorney

- You signed a trust package and doubt it fits Illinois law
- A trust holds a house, a business or a large retirement account
- A beneficiary has special needs
- You received a trustee notice about a trust
- You think someone pressured the trust maker
