**How to use this checklist.** Set aside 30 minutes. Do the big three first (Apple, Google, Facebook), then your password manager, then everything else. Menus change, so if a step does not match your screen, search the company's help page for "legacy contact" or "inactive account."

## Why the settings matter

Without a legacy setting, families often hit a wall. Companies protect privacy, and terms of service may limit what anyone else can see. A legacy contact is a direct instruction to the company, made by you while you can.

## Apple Legacy Contact

Apple's tool lets a person you choose request access to data such as photos, messages, notes, files and device backups after your death. Per Apple, it does not include purchased media or iCloud Keychain.

- [ ] Two-factor authentication is on, on a device running a recent iOS, iPadOS or macOS version.
- [ ] I went to Settings, my name, Sign-In and Security, Legacy Contact, and added someone aged 13 or older.
- [ ] I shared the access key by message or printed a copy.
- [ ] My contact knows they will also need a death certificate.
- [ ] I stored a printed key with my important papers.

## Google Inactive Account Manager

This tool lets you pick up to 10 trusted contacts who are notified, and optionally given a download link to chosen data, after your account has been inactive for a period you set. If you set nothing, Google's policy is that it may delete accounts that are inactive across Google for at least two years.

- [ ] I opened Inactive Account Manager in my Google Account settings.
- [ ] I chose the time period.
- [ ] I picked trusted contacts and the data they may download (Drive, Mail, Photos, YouTube).
- [ ] I decided whether to delete the account after sharing.
- [ ] I wrote a short message for my contacts.

## Facebook and Instagram

Facebook lets you choose a legacy contact who can manage a memorialized account, or ask to have the account deleted when you die. Their access does not include reading your private messages. Instagram offers memorialization, and families can ask for removal.

- [ ] I chose between memorialize and delete in my Facebook settings.
- [ ] I named a legacy contact if memorializing.
- [ ] I downloaded a copy of photos and posts I want my family to keep.

## Password managers and other services

Many password managers offer emergency access or a trusted contact feature. See the [Password Manager Handoff Guide](/free/password-manager-handoff-guide).

- [ ] My password manager's emergency access is set up and tested.
- [ ] My email, which resets almost everything, has a contact or recovery plan.
- [ ] My phone's lock code is known to a trusted person in a safe way.

## RUFADAA in plain language

Most states have adopted a version of the Revised Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act. Illinois did, at 755 ILCS 70. Rules vary by state, but the typical structure looks like this:

1. If you use an online tool such as a legacy contact setting, that choice generally controls first.
2. If not, directions in your will, trust or power of attorney usually come next.
3. If neither exists, the company's terms of service apply.

Two more points. First, an executor often gets a "catalogue" of who you wrote to, but the content of emails and messages generally requires your clear consent in a document. Second, a fiduciary must usually show proof of authority, such as a certified death certificate and court letters.

That means your will or trust should include language about digital assets, and your legacy settings should not contradict it. If you named a legacy contact who is not your executor, tell your executor so they are not surprised.

## Sample language to discuss with your attorney

"My executor may access, manage, copy and close my digital accounts and may obtain the content of my electronic communications, to the fullest extent state law allows."

## Common mistakes

- Setting a legacy contact for Apple, then forgetting Google.
- Naming someone who has since died, moved away or is no longer in your life.
- Letting a spouse's name stay as the only emergency contact on a phone they cannot unlock.
- Using a work email as the main account. Employers may close it.

## When to talk to an attorney

- Your will or trust has no digital asset language.
- You run a business or hold valuable domains and online income.
- You want an account's content to go to a specific person.
- You are the executor and a company refuses access.
- Your state's law is different from the one described here.
