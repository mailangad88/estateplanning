## How to use this template

A letter of intent is not a legal document. It is a record, in your words, of who your child is and how to care for them. A new guardian, trustee or caregiver can read it and understand a life that took you years to learn. Write it in pieces, over several weeks. Use plain sentences. Date it, and update it at least once a year.

Because it is not a contract, it does not bind anyone. Courts and trustees often look to it for guidance, and families find it helpful. Share a copy with your attorney so it fits with your trust and guardian nominations.

## Part 1: The basics

- **Full name and nickname:** ______________________
- **Date of birth:** ______________________
- **Diagnoses:** ______________________
- **Lives with:** ______________________
- **Who knows them well:** ______________________

Sample opening: "This letter is about my daughter, Maya. I wrote it so that anyone caring for her can understand her quickly and treat her with the respect she deserves."

## Part 2: A typical day

Describe the day from waking to bedtime. Small details matter most.

- **Wake-up time and routine:** ______________________
- **Breakfast, foods, allergies and textures:** ______________________
- **Dressing and bathing, help needed:** ______________________
- **Daytime program, school or job:** ______________________
- **Transportation:** ______________________
- **Bedtime routine and sleep habits:** ______________________

Sample: "He needs five minutes of warning before any change. He eats the same breakfast. If it is not available, offer cereal without milk."

## Part 3: Medical care

- **Doctors, therapists and phone numbers:** ______________________
- **Hospital preferred:** ______________________
- **Medications, doses and times:** ______________________
- **Pharmacy:** ______________________
- **Insurance and Medicaid numbers (store the cards separately):** ______________________
- **Seizure, choking or other emergencies and what to do:** ______________________
- **How they show pain:** ______________________

- [ ] Attach a one-page medication list
- [ ] Attach copies of recent evaluations
- [ ] Note who holds a health care power of attorney or guardianship

## Part 4: Communication and behavior

Tell the reader how to understand and calm your child.

- **How they communicate (words, signs, device, gestures):** ______________________
- **Signs of being upset or overwhelmed:** ______________________
- **What helps:** ______________________
- **What makes it worse:** ______________________
- **Safety concerns (wandering, water, traffic):** ______________________
- **Approaches that have not worked:** ______________________

Sample: "When she hums loudly, she is anxious. Lower your voice, turn off the television and offer her headphones."

## Part 5: What they love

This section often matters most to a new caregiver.

- **Favorite foods and drinks:** ______________________
- **Music, shows, books, games:** ______________________
- **Favorite places and outings:** ______________________
- **Comforts (blanket, toy, scent):** ______________________
- **Holidays and traditions that matter:** ______________________
- **People they love and want to see:** ______________________
- **Faith or cultural practices:** ______________________

## Part 6: Services, money and benefits

- **Benefits received (SSI, SSDI, Medicaid, waiver):** ______________________
- **Case manager or support coordinator:** ______________________
- **Representative payee:** ______________________
- **Trust name, trustee and attorney:** ______________________
- **ABLE account provider:** ______________________
- **Where to find documents:** ______________________

Do not include account passwords or numbers. Say where the documents are kept.

## Part 7: Home and independence

- **Living situation now:** ______________________
- **Where I hope they live in the future:** ______________________
- **Skills they are learning:** ______________________
- **Things they can do alone, with help, or not yet:** ______________________
- **Pets or belongings that must stay with them:** ______________________

## Part 8: Your hopes and values

- **What I want most for their life:** ______________________
- **Friendships and family contact I want preserved:** ______________________
- **How to include them in decisions:** ______________________
- **What I hope the next caregiver will remember:** ______________________

Sample: "Please ask her what she wants, even if the answer takes time. She understands more than she says."

## Part 9: Who gets a copy

- [ ] Guardian or caregiver named in your will
- [ ] Trustee of a special needs trust
- [ ] Siblings or other trusted relatives
- [ ] Your attorney
- [ ] Case manager or program staff, if you are comfortable
- [ ] A sealed copy with your will and emergency papers

## Keeping it current

- [ ] Review each January and after any medical or program change
- [ ] Add a new date at the top each time
- [ ] Remove old versions so no one acts on outdated advice
- [ ] Add photos and a short video of daily routines if it helps

## When to talk to an attorney

- You are choosing a guardian, trustee or caregiver and want the letter to support that choice
- You want it to fit with a special needs trust
- Siblings or relatives disagree about future care
- Your child may need guardianship at 18
- Benefits or services are changing and you are unsure about the effect
