## How to use this guide

Public employees often hold most of their wealth in benefits, not in bank accounts. A pension, a 403(b) or 457 account and group life insurance all pass by forms, not by your will. If the forms are out of date, your will cannot fix them. This guide helps you sort the benefits and ask the right questions. Rules differ by retirement system and by state. Illinois systems such as the Teachers' Retirement System (TRS), the Illinois Municipal Retirement Fund (IMRF) and the State Universities Retirement System (SURS) are used as examples, so check your own system's current rules and forms.

## Why benefits override your will

Money that has a named beneficiary or a survivor option goes to that person directly. A will never gets a vote. Our comparison of a [beneficiary designation versus a will](/compare/beneficiary-designation-vs-will) explains the idea. For a public employee, that means a few forms control a large share of what your family receives.

## The benefits to inventory

| Benefit | Who decides where it goes | Where to find the form |
|---|---|---|
| Defined benefit pension (for example TRS, IMRF, SURS) | Your pension system's rules, plus any survivor option you choose at retirement | Your system's member portal or forms page |
| Refund of contributions if you die before retirement | Beneficiary on file with the system | Same |
| 403(b) or 457(b) plan | The beneficiary on file with the plan provider | Provider website or HR |
| Group life insurance through your employer | Beneficiary on file with the insurer | HR or insurer portal |
| Supplemental life or accidental death coverage | Beneficiary on file | HR |
| Final paychecks, unused leave and similar items | Employer policy, sometimes state law | HR |
| Individual IRA, savings and home | Your own titles and designations | Your own records |

Pension systems have different rules for who is a "survivor" and what is paid. Some pay a survivor benefit to a spouse. Some pay a lump sum to a named beneficiary. Some have no payment after a death before retirement beyond a refund of what you paid in. Do not rely on a coworker's memory. Ask the system, in writing.

## Pension survivor options at retirement

Many systems ask you to choose, once, how your pension continues after you die. Often the choice is permanent. Common patterns include:

- **Single life:** The highest monthly payment while you live, with little or nothing for survivors.
- **Joint and survivor:** A lower monthly payment now, with a percentage continuing to a spouse or named person after your death.
- **Period-certain or refund features:** Payments to a beneficiary if you die soon after retiring.

Check what your own plan calls these and whether your spouse must consent. Federal law requires consent for some private plans, but public plans follow their own state rules. If you are married, ask whether your spouse must sign.

Questions to ask your system:

- [ ] What survivor benefit exists if I die while working?
- [ ] What happens if I die after leaving public service but before drawing a pension?
- [ ] Which survivor options will I be offered at retirement, and what does each cost per month?
- [ ] Can I change my choice after retiring, or after a divorce, marriage or death?
- [ ] Who counts as a spouse, child or dependent for survivor purposes?
- [ ] Does the beneficiary on file apply to every benefit, or are there separate forms?
- [ ] Is there a retiree health insurance benefit that continues for a survivor?
- [ ] How does the system treat a trust as a beneficiary, if at all?

Ask for the answers and the form numbers in writing, and keep them with your plan.

## 403(b) and 457(b) accounts

Teachers often have a 403(b). Other public employees may have a 457(b). Both pass by beneficiary designation. After a death, a spouse may usually roll the account into their own retirement account. Other beneficiaries usually have shorter payout deadlines. Rules for inherited accounts changed in recent years, so see our guide on [inherited retirement accounts](/free/inherited-retirement-accounts-guide).

- [ ] Primary beneficiary named on every account
- [ ] Contingent beneficiary named
- [ ] Beneficiary names match current legal names
- [ ] Old accounts from past jobs located and updated

## Group life insurance

Group life is easy to forget. It is often a multiple of salary, and it may be your family's largest immediate cash. Check the beneficiary form every time your life changes.

Be careful with coverage that ends at retirement or when you leave your job. Ask HR if you can convert or port the coverage, and by what deadline.

## Naming minor children or a trust

Naming a child directly as beneficiary leaves a court to manage the money until the child comes of age. Many parents name a trust, or a trustee under their will, instead. Ask your plan whether it accepts a trust as a beneficiary, and what documentation it wants. Systems differ, so an attorney should read the plan rules.

## Calendar of forms to check

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| First day of work | Name beneficiaries for everything |
| Marriage, divorce, birth, adoption, death in the family | Update every form |
| Every year at open enrollment | Review group life and supplemental coverage |
| Five years before retirement | Ask for a benefit estimate and a list of survivor options |
| At retirement | Choose a survivor option with your spouse, an attorney and an advisor |
| After retirement | Confirm beneficiary for any death benefit |

## The rest of your plan

Public employees still need the usual documents: a will, a financial power of attorney, a health care power of attorney and, for parents, a guardian nomination. A beneficiary form cannot name who raises your children. Our [beneficiary designation audit](/checklists/beneficiary-designation-audit) lets you check every form in one sitting.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You are close to retirement and choosing a survivor option
- You are divorced and unsure whether your former spouse has rights in your pension
- A trust or minor child is a beneficiary
- You have a blended family and want a partner and children both protected
- You moved between public systems, or hold a pension from another state
- You are not sure the forms on file match your plan
