**How to use this checklist.** Take it slowly. You do not have to finish it in a week. Do a little each day. Ask someone you trust to help. This is general education, not legal or medical advice. Rules vary by state.

One gentle point: some paperwork can only be signed while a person is able to understand it. That is why many people start with the first few items. Start where you can.

## Step 1: Take a breath

- [ ] Eat something.
- [ ] Rest.
- [ ] Pick one person to help you.
- [ ] Write down the name of that person here.

**My helper:** ______________________

## Step 2: Three key documents

These let someone act for you if you cannot.

- [ ] Financial power of attorney
- [ ] Healthcare power of attorney
- [ ] Living will (advance directive)

**Who I trust to handle money:** ______________________

**Who I trust to speak for my health:** ______________________

**A backup for each:** ______________________

Tip: ask each person before you name them.

## Step 3: Medical privacy

- [ ] Sign a HIPAA release.
- [ ] List who may see my records.
- [ ] Ask the doctor's office to file it.

**People allowed to talk to my doctors:** ______________________

## Step 4: Check my will or trust

- [ ] Find my current will or trust.
- [ ] Read it again.
- [ ] Ask: is my executor still right?
- [ ] Ask: is my trustee still right?
- [ ] Ask: are my guardians for children still right?
- [ ] Ask: does it still say what I want?
- [ ] Call an attorney if anything has changed.

**Where my will or trust is kept:** ______________________

If there is no will, an attorney can often help quickly. Timelines vary.

## Step 5: Beneficiary check

Some accounts pass by form, not by will.

- [ ] Life insurance
- [ ] Retirement accounts
- [ ] Bank accounts with payable-on-death
- [ ] Annuities
- [ ] Workplace benefits

For each, check:

- [ ] Is the name correct?
- [ ] Is there a backup name?
- [ ] Does it still match my wishes?

**Notes:** ______________________

## Step 6: Who to tell

You choose when and how.

- [ ] Close family
- [ ] My healthcare agent
- [ ] My financial agent
- [ ] My employer, if I want to
- [ ] My faith community, if I want to
- [ ] An attorney

**Who I will tell first:** ______________________

**Who I will ask to tell others:** ______________________

- [ ] Read my health insurance plan.
- [ ] Ask what is covered and what is not.
- [ ] Ask my employer about leave.
- [ ] Ask about disability coverage.
- [ ] Check life insurance.
- [ ] Check long-term care insurance, if I have it.

## Step 8: Where things are

- [ ] List of bank accounts
- [ ] List of bills and when due
- [ ] Passwords or a safe way to find them
- [ ] Keys and safe locations
- [ ] Insurance cards
- [ ] Pet care plan, if any

## Step 9: Medical orders

If a doctor suggests it:

- [ ] Ask about a POLST or MOLST form.
- [ ] Ask what it would do.
- [ ] Ask who signs it.

Not everyone needs one. The name varies by state.

## Step 10: Share copies

- [ ] Give copies to my agents.
- [ ] Give copies to my doctor.
- [ ] Tell family where originals are.

## For another day

These matter, but they can wait.

- [ ] Funeral or burial wishes
- [ ] A letter to family
- [ ] Digital accounts
- [ ] A review of my whole plan

## Questions to ask at the first appointments

- [ ] What is the likely course of this illness?
- [ ] Will it affect how well I can think or decide?
- [ ] What should I plan for in the next few months?
- [ ] Can a social worker or patient navigator help with paperwork?
- [ ] Is there a palliative care team, to help with comfort and support?

Write the answers here:

**Notes:** ______________________

Bring a friend to take notes. It is hard to remember everything.

## A note on capacity

Capacity means being able to understand a document and its effects when you sign it. Many people with a serious diagnosis have it for a long time. Some conditions can change it. Signing while able keeps the choice in your hands. If you are unsure, an attorney can talk with you about it. There is no shame in starting now.

## When to talk to an attorney

- I am not sure I can sign documents on my own.
- Someone questions whether I have capacity.
- My family is complicated.
- I own a business or real estate.
- I may need long-term care and want to protect savings.
- I have a child with special needs.
