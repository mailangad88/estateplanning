## How to use this worksheet

This worksheet is a first screen. It does not recommend a vehicle. Charitable tools carry strict tax rules, and the right choice depends on your assets, your family and your goals. Complete Part 1, compare in Part 2 and score in Part 3.

## Part 2: The three vehicles at a glance

| Feature | Donor-advised fund (DAF) | Private foundation | Charitable remainder trust (CRT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | An account at a public charity | A separate charity you create | An irrevocable trust paying income, then charity |
| Setup | Often quick and online | Legal formation and IRS application | Attorney drafts a trust |
| Ongoing work | Minimal | Annual return, board, records | Annual trust return, trustee |
| Control | You recommend grants, sponsor has final say | Board has full control | Trustee manages, you choose the charity |
| Family involvement | Successor advisers | Family can serve as directors, with limits | Income beneficiaries named |
| Payout rules | Set by the sponsor | Annual minimum distributions required | Required payments to the income beneficiary |
| Income to you | None | None | Yes, for life or a term |
| Cost | Fees charged by the sponsor | Legal, accounting and filing costs | Legal and trustee costs |
| Privacy | Grants can often be anonymous | Annual returns are public | Less public |

The IRS treats these differently. Deduction limits are generally higher for gifts to public charities, which includes DAF sponsors, than for gifts to most private foundations. Private foundations are subject to rules about self-dealing and excise taxes. CRTs must meet minimum payout and remainder tests. Ask your adviser for current figures.

## Part 4: Reality check

- [ ] Am I willing to hold board meetings and file returns every year? If not, a foundation may not fit.
- [ ] Do I need income from the gift? A CRT is the one built for that.
- [ ] Is my gift large enough to justify setup and annual costs?
- [ ] Will my heirs be disappointed that the asset leaves the family?
- [ ] Have I looked at simpler options, like a gift in my will or a beneficiary designation?
- [ ] Could a donor-advised fund solve this with less work?

## Part 5: Coordinating with the rest of your plan

A charitable vehicle is one piece. Check how it fits with:

- Your will or trust, which should name any DAF successor.
- Retirement accounts, which can go directly to charity at death.
- A business or real estate you hope to give. Valuation and appraisal rules apply to noncash gifts.
- Family members who expected the asset.

Cross-reference the [charitable bequest language guide](/free/charitable-bequest-language-guide) for wording and the [charitable giving planner](/free/charitable-giving-planner) for yearly giving.

## When to talk to an attorney

- You are considering a gift of real estate, a business or restricted stock.
- You want income from the gift.
- You are thinking of creating a foundation or a trust.
- Your estate may be subject to estate tax.
- You want family to run the giving after you.
