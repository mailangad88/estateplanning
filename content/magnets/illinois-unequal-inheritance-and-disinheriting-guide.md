## How to use this guide

In Illinois you are generally free to leave your property to anyone you choose, with a few limits. The limits protect a spouse and a child who arrives after the will. They do not protect adult children, stepchildren or in-laws. This guide shows who has automatic rights, how to carry out an unequal plan and what tends to start fights. It is general education. Other states differ, and some protect children.

## Who has automatic rights in Illinois

| Person | Automatic right? | Source and notes |
|---|---|---|
| Surviving spouse | Yes, a right to renounce the will and take a share | 755 ILCS 5/2-8 <!-- verify: renunciation share and deadline --> |
| Child born or adopted after the will | Often yes, unless provided for or clearly omitted | 755 ILCS 5/4-10 <!-- verify: statute terms --> |
| Adult child | No forced share | A will can leave a child nothing |
| Minor child | No forced share of the estate, but support claims may exist | Ask an attorney |
| Stepchild | No, unless adopted or named | Not an heir by default |
| Ex-spouse | No, divorce ends rights under the will | 755 ILCS 5/4-7 <!-- verify: effect of divorce on will --> |
| Grandchild, niece, nephew | No, unless named, or taking for a deceased parent under intestacy | |

Without a will, Illinois intestacy law decides. See /free/illinois-intestacy-chart.

## Disinheriting an adult child

You can. The safe way is to name the child and say that you intend to leave them nothing, or a specific amount. Silence can invite a claim that you forgot them. Keep the reason in a private letter, not in the will, so it does not insult anyone in a public document.

- [ ] Name the child in the will or trust
- [ ] State that the omission is intentional
- [ ] Review beneficiary forms for the child's name
- [ ] Consider a smaller gift with a no-contest clause
- [ ] Consider whether the child's own children should receive something
- [ ] Keep a letter explaining why

A child who is disinherited may contest, so see /free/illinois-blended-family-contest-proofing-kit for ways to lower the risk.

## Leaving one child more

Parents leave more to a child for many reasons: a caregiver, a disability, a business, an earlier loan. Fair does not always mean equal.

| Reason | How to handle it |
|---|---|
| Child gave years of care | Pay them under a written care agreement now, or leave a stated share. See /free/personal-care-agreement-guide |
| Child has a disability | Use a special needs trust. See /free/special-needs-trust-funding-worksheet |
| Child already got a loan or help | Say whether it is a gift or counted against the share |
| Child works in the family business | Plan a buy-out. See /free/business-succession-scorecard |
| Child is financially struggling | Use a trust, not a smaller share |

The more specific the reason, the less likely a sibling will challenge it.

## Stepchildren

You do not have to leave anything to stepchildren, and they have no default right if you die without a will. If you want them to inherit, name them. Treating stepchildren and biological children fairly is a choice. Some people give equal shares. Some give stepchildren a smaller gift, or a gift after the surviving spouse dies. You can leave property to your spouse's children by naming them, but if you leave everything to your spouse and the spouse dies, the spouse decides.

| Goal | Tool |
|---|---|
| Equal shares to all children and stepchildren | Name each by name, not "my children" |
| Stepchildren inherit only after your spouse | A trust with the spouse as life beneficiary |
| Smaller gift to stepchildren | A fixed amount or percentage |
| Stepchildren left nothing | State it, or just do not name them, and ask whether silence is enough |
| Stepchildren's children | Name them, or use a class gift to "descendants" |

If your will says "my children," it may not include stepchildren. A gift to "my descendants" may not either. Define the term. See /free/stepchildren-inheritance-guide.

## Your ex and your children

In Illinois, divorce generally revokes provisions in your will for a former spouse. <!-- verify: 755 ILCS 5/4-7(b) --> Your children are not affected. Beneficiary forms are different: an old form that names an ex may be treated differently by the plan or insurer, and federal rules can override state law for some retirement plans. Update forms. See /free/divorce-estate-plan-update-kit.

## Grandchildren

You can leave a grandchild money directly, but a minor cannot control it. Choices:

| Method | How it works | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Outright gift | Minor receives at 18, or a court controls it | Control passes at 18 |
| Illinois UTMA custodian | Custodian manages until 21 <!-- verify: Illinois UTMA age --> | Gift is final, and the custodian has duties |
| Trust | Trustee follows your rules | Needs a document |
| 529 college plan | Funds go for education | Tax and financial aid rules apply |
| Gift to the parent | Parent decides | Might be diverted to their own debts |

If a grandchild has special needs, do not leave money outright. See /free/special-needs-trust-funding-worksheet. See /guides/leaving-money-to-minors and /free/grandchildren-education-gift-planner.

## Nieces, nephews and others

Yes. Name them, or include a clause such as "to my nieces and nephews who survive me, in equal shares." Say what happens if one dies before you. Without a backup, Illinois law may pass their share to their children or to the others, depending on the wording.

## Dividing personal property fairly

Family fights begin over small things. Use a process.

1. Each child lists the five items they care about most
2. Compare lists and look for overlap
3. Draw numbers or take turns picking
4. Give appraised items equal value in the totals
5. Put the result in a memorandum. See /free/personal-property-memorandum-template

## When to talk to an attorney

- You want to leave an adult child nothing
- A child with a disability is involved
- Stepchildren, a spouse and a trust are all in the plan
- You plan large gifts before death
- You worry a claim will follow
