## How to use these letters

You are doing something hard, and these letters are meant to make one piece of it easier. Banks each have their own process, so call first, then send the letter that fits. Rules vary by state and by bank. Keep a copy of everything you send, and send by a method that gives you a receipt.

Do not use the account to pay bills or withdraw money before you have the authority to do so. Ask the bank what it needs. Usually that means a certified death certificate and, later, court papers or a trust certification showing who may act.

## Before you write

- [ ] Order several certified death certificates. Banks often keep the copy you send.
- [ ] Find the most recent statement for each account, or the account number.
- [ ] Note whether the account was in one name, joint, payable on death or held in a trust.
- [ ] Find your own photo ID. You will need it.
- [ ] Find any letters of office from the court, if an executor has been appointed.

## Call script

Say it simply. "I am calling to report the death of [name]. I am the [relationship / executor]. What do you need from me, and where do I send it?"

Write down the answers.

- **Date and time of call:** ______________________
- **Representative name and ID number:** ______________________
- **Department or direct line:** ______________________
- **Documents they asked for:** ______________________
- **Mailing address or secure upload:** ______________________
- **Reference or case number:** ______________________

## Letter 1: Notice of death

Use this to put the death on record and ask for next steps. Do not include your loved one's full Social Security number. Use the last four digits.

> [Your name]
> [Your address, phone, email]
> [Date]
>
> [Bank name]
> [Department or branch address]
>
> Re: Notice of death of [full name], account(s) ending in [last four digits]
>
> To whom it may concern:
>
> I am writing to notify you that [full name] died on [date]. I am the [executor / surviving spouse / child / other] of [his / her / their] estate. A certified copy of the death certificate is enclosed.
>
> Please place a note on all accounts in the decedent's name to stop automatic withdrawals that are not authorized, and tell me what documents you require to proceed. Please also send me a written list of the accounts, safe deposit boxes and loans held in the decedent's name.
>
> You can reach me at [phone or email].
>
> Sincerely,
> [Signature and printed name]

Ask the bank to explain what happens to direct deposits, checks that have not cleared and automatic payments. Some banks freeze an account right away, which can cause late fees on bills that were set to pay automatically.

## Letter 2: Request for date-of-death balances

The executor and the tax preparer will usually need the balance on the day of death, plus interest earned through that date. This is separate from the current balance.

> Re: Request for date-of-death balances, [full name], died [date]
>
> Please provide a written statement showing, for each account in the decedent's name:
>
> 1. The account type and the last four digits of the number
> 2. The balance on [date of death]
> 3. Interest earned from January 1 of the year of death through the date of death
> 4. All named owners and beneficiaries on the account
> 5. Any holds, liens or loans tied to the account
>
> I have enclosed [letters of office / a certification of trust / my identification] showing my authority to request this information.

Keep the reply with the estate records. Do not assume the bank will volunteer interest or beneficiary details.

## Letter 3: Request to close or transfer accounts

Send this only after you have the authority to act. That could be court-issued letters of office, a successor trustee certification or, for a joint or payable on death account, the survivor's own paperwork.

> Re: Request to close and transfer, [full name], account ending in [last four]
>
> Enclosed are [letters of office issued by (court) on (date) / certification of trust / death certificate and my ID]. Please:
>
> - [ ] Close the account and issue a cashier's check payable to "Estate of [full name]"
> - [ ] Transfer the funds to the estate account at [bank] ending in [last four]
> - [ ] Retitle the account to [new owner] as the surviving joint owner or payable on death beneficiary
>
> Please confirm in writing when this is complete and send a final statement.

Often the executor first opens an estate account with the estate's own tax ID number, then moves money into it. That keeps estate money apart from personal money.

## Which path fits

| How the account was held | Usually needs | Often goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Single owner, no beneficiary | Letters of office | The estate |
| Joint with survivor | Death certificate, ID | The surviving owner |
| Payable on death | Death certificate, beneficiary ID | The named beneficiary |
| Held in a trust | Certification of trust, death certificate | The successor trustee |

Check the account record rather than guessing. A joint account may not always pass the way a family expects, and rules vary by state.

## When to talk to an attorney

- The account has no beneficiary and the estate has not been opened
- Family members disagree about who should receive the money
- The bank refuses documents you believe are sufficient
- A joint account, safe deposit box or loan complicates things
- You are not sure whether your authority covers an account
