South Dakota gets a lot of attention from wealth planners because of its trust laws, but for most residents the practical questions are simpler: Is my will valid, how does probate work at the county courthouse, and will my family owe any tax? The answer to the last one is no, at the state level.

This page covers what is different in South Dakota. For the basics, see our national guide to [wills](/learn/wills).

## Wills in South Dakota

A South Dakota will should be in writing, signed by you, and signed by two witnesses who watched you sign. Notarization is not required, but a [self-proving affidavit](/glossary/self-proving-affidavit) signed before a notary speeds probate because the court can accept the will without calling the witnesses.

South Dakota follows the Uniform Probate Code, which recognizes a [holographic will](/glossary/holographic-will): one where the signature and the material portions are in your own handwriting, even with no witnesses. That is a safety net, not a plan. Family members may disagree about whose handwriting it is, and the court may need evidence. Our article on [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills) explains why a witnessed will is still safer.

South Dakota is a common law property state. A married person cannot easily disinherit a spouse, because the Uniform Probate Code gives the spouse an elective share of the augmented estate, which counts certain transfers made during life. The percentage and exact rules are worth confirming with a South Dakota attorney.

If someone dies without a will, South Dakota law decides. The spouse's share turns on whether all of the children are also the spouse's, so blended families should be especially careful. Read [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will) for how intestacy works nationally.

## Probate in South Dakota

Probate runs through the circuit court in the county where the decedent lived, under Title 29A. Because it follows the Uniform Probate Code, South Dakota allows informal probate, where the clerk of courts handles the application and appoints the personal representative without a hearing. Formal probate, with a judge and notice to heirs, is used when someone challenges the will or an issue needs to be resolved.

The personal representative inventories the assets, gives notice to creditors by publication and mail, pays debts and taxes, and distributes the rest. A representative named in the will can often act with limited court supervision after being appointed. Our guide to [what probate is](/learn/probate/what-is-probate) walks through the steps.

For smaller estates, South Dakota offers an affidavit procedure to collect personal property and a summary administration that shortens the process. The dollar limits change, so confirm the current figures. See [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit).

Because South Dakota has a low population and few probate judges, rural counties move quickly in routine cases. What slows things down is land: South Dakota families often own farm or ranch land, and settling title, leases and shared ownership can add months.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in South Dakota

South Dakota is famous for its trust industry. State law allows dynasty trusts that can last indefinitely, private family trust companies, directed trusts, and strong creditor protection for trusts. These features appeal to large families and to people who live elsewhere but hold a trust in South Dakota. For an ordinary family, a [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) is the more common tool, used to avoid probate and to plan for incapacity.

Beneficiary designations, payable-on-death accounts and joint ownership also pass assets outside probate. Our article on [how to avoid probate](/learn/probate/how-to-avoid-probate) lists the main options. Whether South Dakota allows a transfer-on-death deed for land is worth confirming with an attorney; see [transfer-on-death deeds](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed) for how they work in states that do.

For farm families, a trust or a limited liability company can hold land while keeping control with the older generation. See our article on [passing a family business to children](/learn/business-owners/passing-a-family-business-to-children).

## Taxes

South Dakota has no estate tax and no inheritance tax, and it repealed its inheritance tax years ago. The federal estate tax exclusion is $15 million per person in 2026. Anyone with property in a state that does tax estates, such as [Minnesota](/estate-planning/minnesota), may face that state's tax on that property. Our guide to [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes) compares the states.

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in South Dakota

A [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) lets a trusted agent manage your money. South Dakota law governs how it is signed and what it can do; having it notarized is standard. Confirm whether your document needs special language for gifts or real estate.

For health decisions, South Dakota recognizes a living will declaration and a durable power of attorney for health care. See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) and [living will](/learn/healthcare-directives/living-will) for what to include.
