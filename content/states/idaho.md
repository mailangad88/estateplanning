Idaho's estate planning begins with community property. Combined with Uniform Probate Code procedures, it makes Idaho estates straightforward when the paperwork is organized. This guide covers what is specific to Idaho.

## Wills in Idaho

An Idaho will is valid when an adult signs it in front of two witnesses; no notary is required. A self-proving affidavit signed before a notary lets the court admit the will without witness testimony. Idaho also recognizes a will written and signed in the testator's own handwriting. Read [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills) for the risks and [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will) for the steps.

Because Idaho is a [community property](/glossary/community-property) state, each spouse can leave only their half of community property and any separate property through a will. The surviving spouse already owns the other half. Couples who moved to Idaho from a common law state may have quasi-community property, which is treated similarly for some purposes; an attorney can sort out what is what. Records of separate property, such as an inheritance kept in its own account, matter.

With no will, the spouse takes the community property, and shares the separate property with children when any of them is from another relationship. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will).

## Probate in Idaho

Probate is handled by the magistrate division of the district court in the county where the person lived, under Idaho's version of the Uniform Probate Code. Informal, unsupervised probate is available when no one objects. The personal representative publishes notice to creditors, and creditors have a limited time to file claims. The process is described in what is probate and how long probate takes.

Idaho's small estate affidavit lets a successor collect personal property worth $100,000 or less, after liens, 30 days after the death, without court filing. The holder of the asset, such as a bank, accepts the affidavit. Real estate is excluded, but a surviving spouse may use a summary route for real property in some cases. Confirm the details with an Idaho attorney. See [small estate affidavit](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit) for the general procedure and the [probate pillar](/learn/probate) for formal administration.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Idaho

A [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) works well in Idaho, which has adopted the Uniform Trust Code. Many Idahoans own land or recreational property in more than one state, so a funded trust can avoid ancillary probate ([how to fund a trust](/learn/trusts/how-to-fund-a-trust)).

Married couples can hold assets as community property with right of survivorship, which passes automatically to the survivor. Because it remains community property, both halves can receive a full step-up in basis at the first death. Confirm with an attorney how to title assets for this treatment. Beneficiary designations and payable-on-death accounts also avoid probate; see how to avoid probate. Whether Idaho has a transfer-on-death deed is something to confirm with an Idaho attorney.

Idaho families with farms, ranches or mountain property should coordinate community property with any business entities and land held in a trust. A spouse who inherits community property gets a full step-up in basis, which can be valuable for appreciated land, but only if the title records and the paper trail show the property was community. Keep a short file with purchase documents, deeds and notes on which assets are separate.

If you are widowed, update your own plan promptly: the survivor of a community property couple often ends up with the whole estate and no one else to correct mistakes, so naming a new agent, executor and beneficiaries soon after a death keeps the plan working.

## Taxes

Idaho has no estate tax and no inheritance tax. Only the federal estate tax can apply, and it begins above $15,000,000 per person in 2026. See [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes).

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Idaho

An Idaho [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) is signed before a notary and should say clearly that it survives incapacity. For health care, Idaho's Medical Consent and Natural Death Act supports a living will and a durable power of attorney for health care, and the Idaho Secretary of State runs a registry where you can file these documents so providers can find them. Idaho also uses POLST forms. See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney), living will and DNR and POLST.
