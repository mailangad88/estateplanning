Oklahoma is a flexible state for the form of a will: it accepts handwritten wills, offers two ways to make a will self-proved, and now allows electronic wills. At the same time, it enforces specific steps, such as signing at the end and declaring the document to the witnesses, that trip up people using generic templates.

## Wills in Oklahoma

A witnessed Oklahoma will must be signed by you at the end. You must declare to the two witnesses that the document is your will, and each witness signs at the end at your request and in your presence. A notary is not required. A self-proving step is available either through a notarized acknowledgment with witness affidavits or through a declaration signed under penalty of perjury, which does not need a notary. Compare that with the general rules in [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will).

Oklahoma also recognizes a [holographic will](/glossary/holographic-will) that is entirely written, dated and signed in your own handwriting. The word entirely matters: typed text or a printed form with handwritten blanks can defeat it. See [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills). Since November 1, 2024, Oklahoma has also allowed electronic wills, signed electronically and witnessed by two people physically or electronically present.

If you die without a will, your spouse shares with your children. With one child, each takes half; with several, the spouse takes an equal share but not less than one-third. How property is characterized, jointly acquired versus separate, can change the result, so ask an attorney about your situation. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will). A surviving spouse can also elect against a will to claim a share of marital property.

## Probate in Oklahoma

Probate takes place in district court under Title 58. The usual case involves a petition to admit the will, appointment of an executor, notice to creditors by publication, and a creditor claim period. Oklahoma has a specific timeline for presenting claims after the notice is published, which governs how long an estate stays open. See [what is probate](/learn/probate/what-is-probate) and [how long probate takes](/learn/probate/how-long-does-probate-take).

Oklahoma has two streamlined tracks. A successor can use an affidavit for personal property, currently reported at $50,000, ten days or more after death. For estates that include real estate, summary administration is a short court case, and the threshold was reported to rise to $300,000 in 2026. The two limits differ, so confirm both before relying on them. See [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit).

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Oklahoma

Oklahoma authorizes transfer-on-death deeds. The deed is signed and recorded before death, names a beneficiary, and lets the owner keep the property and change plans while alive. Our guide to [transfer-on-death deeds](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed) covers common problems. A [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) is more useful for owners of oil and gas minerals, farmland or several properties, because it keeps management in one place and avoids multiple county filings. Beneficiary designations pass retirement accounts and insurance outside probate; see [how to avoid probate](/learn/probate/how-to-avoid-probate).

## Taxes

Oklahoma has no estate tax and no inheritance tax. Only the federal estate tax applies, to estates above the federal exemption. See [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes). Heirs of inherited retirement accounts can still owe income tax.

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Oklahoma

Oklahoma's Advance Directive Act provides a statutory form that combines a living will, a health care proxy and instructions about treatment, and it requires two witnesses who are at least 18 and not related to you or entitled to your estate. A [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) should be notarized, especially to deal with land. See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) and [living wills](/learn/healthcare-directives/living-will). Confirm the current form and witness rules with an Oklahoma attorney.
