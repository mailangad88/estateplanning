Washington is one of the few states that combines community property with a state estate tax set far below the federal threshold. A couple with a house in Seattle, a retirement account and life insurance may have a taxable estate without feeling wealthy.

For national background, see our [estate tax guide](/learn/estate-tax) and [probate guide](/learn/probate).

## Wills in Washington

A Washington will must be in writing, signed by you, and witnessed by two competent people. Notarization is not required. The witnesses can sign a declaration under penalty of perjury that makes the will self-proving, so the court can admit it without calling them. See [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will).

Washington does not generally recognize a handwritten will that lacks witnesses. See [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills).

The central concept is [community property](/glossary/community-property). Property earned or acquired during marriage belongs equally to both spouses, and a will can only dispose of your half plus your separate property. Separate property includes assets owned before marriage and inheritances or gifts. Washington has no elective share; the surviving spouse is protected by community property, the homestead and awards for support.

With no will, the surviving spouse takes the decedent's community property and, when there are children, one-half of the decedent's separate property, with the children taking the rest. Registered domestic partners are treated like spouses. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will).

## Probate in Washington

Probate is filed in the Superior Court of the county where the person lived. Washington lets the court grant **nonintervention powers** to the personal representative, often at the first hearing if the heirs agree. With these powers, the representative sells property, pays debts and distributes assets without asking the judge each time, then closes the estate with a declaration of completion. This makes Washington probate fast for routine estates.

The representative must give notice to creditors, who have a limited period to file claims, and must file a notice of appointment. Our guide to [what probate is](/learn/probate/what-is-probate) covers the stages.

Small estates can use an affidavit to collect personal property after a waiting period. The limit has been around $100,000, but confirm the current number and what property counts. See [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit).

The estate tax return is the main timeline driver in larger estates. If the estate is above the exemption, the Washington return is due nine months after death, and a tax lien can block selling real estate until it is resolved.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Washington

Washington offers several tools. A **community property agreement** passes everything to the surviving spouse at the first death without probate. It is simple, but it can be a poor fit for blended families because the survivor takes full control. See [protecting children from a first marriage](/learn/blended-families/protecting-children-from-first-marriage).

A **transfer-on-death deed** lets you name a beneficiary for real estate under RCW chapter 64.80, with no probate. The deed must be recorded before death. See [transfer-on-death deeds](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed).

A [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) is useful for larger estates and for estate tax planning, particularly a plan that divides assets into trusts at the first death. Beneficiary designations and joint accounts also pass assets outside probate.

## Taxes

Washington has its own estate tax with a $3 million exemption and graduated rates. Recent legislation changed the rate schedule for deaths on or after July 1, 2026 and effectively froze the exemption, so the numbers depend on the date of death; confirm with an attorney. Washington has no inheritance tax and no state income tax on wages.

Washington does not allow portability of the state exemption between spouses, so a married couple's plan matters. A plan that uses both spouses' exemptions can reduce the tax. Community property receives a full [step-up in basis](/learn/estate-tax/step-up-in-basis) on both halves at the first death. See [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes).

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Washington

Washington has adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act. A [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) must be signed before a notary or two witnesses. Specific powers, such as gifts, need express language.

For health care, Washington has a Health Care Directive, a living will, and a durable power of attorney for health care. The directive must be signed before two witnesses or a notary. Washington also has a Death with Dignity Act, and a POLST form is used for serious illness. See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) and [DNR and POLST](/learn/healthcare-directives/dnr-and-polst).

Neighbors with different rules include [Oregon](/estate-planning/oregon) and [Idaho](/estate-planning/idaho).
