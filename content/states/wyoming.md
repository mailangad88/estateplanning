Wyoming is known for low taxes and trust-friendly laws, but residents often have more ordinary needs: ranch or family land, mineral interests and a plan that works when the next generation lives in another state. The state offers several tools that make settling an estate simpler than in many places.

For national background, see our [probate guide](/learn/probate) and [trusts guide](/learn/trusts).

## Wills in Wyoming

A Wyoming will must be in writing, signed by you, and witnessed by two competent people. Notarization is not required, but adding a [self-proving affidavit](/glossary/self-proving-affidavit) before a notary lets the court accept the will without calling witnesses. See [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will).

Wyoming recognizes a [holographic will](/glossary/holographic-will) that is entirely in your handwriting and signed. Two witnesses must prove the handwriting, which can be difficult when the writer has died and no one else saw the document. See [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills).

Wyoming is a common law property state. A surviving spouse who is left out of a will can claim a statutory elective share of the estate, so a spouse cannot easily be disinherited. Confirm the percentage with an attorney.

With no will, a spouse and children generally share, with the spouse taking one-half and the children dividing the rest. For ranch families, that can mean children inherit shares of land along with a surviving parent, a common source of later disputes. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will).

## Probate in Wyoming

Probate takes place in the district court of the county where the person lived, under Title 2 of the Wyoming Statutes. The personal representative files a petition and the will, is appointed, publishes notice to creditors and gives notice to heirs. Creditors have a limited period to file claims. After debts and taxes are paid, the representative distributes the property and closes the estate. See [what probate is](/learn/probate/what-is-probate).

Wyoming allows an affidavit procedure for small estates. Heirs can collect personal property from banks and other holders by giving an affidavit after a waiting period, for estates under about $200,000. Confirm the current limit with an attorney. See [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit).

Wyoming also has summary probate procedures for estates that qualify. Family land and mineral interests are the main sources of delay: properties can be spread across counties, old deeds may contain errors, and mineral and royalty interests are separate property that must be retitled.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Wyoming

Wyoming allows **transfer-on-death deeds** for real estate. You sign and record a deed naming a beneficiary, keep full control during life, and the property passes at your death without probate. See [transfer-on-death deeds](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed).

Beneficiary designations, payable-on-death accounts and joint ownership also pass assets outside probate. A [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) helps if you own land in several states, because it avoids [ancillary probate](/learn/probate/ancillary-probate).

The state is also known for its trust statutes. Wyoming allows self-settled asset protection trusts, directed trusts, private family trust companies and long-duration trusts. These work best for larger estates and for people with sophisticated planning goals. See [irrevocable trusts](/learn/trusts/irrevocable-trusts) and [spendthrift trusts](/learn/trusts/spendthrift-trusts). Most ordinary families do not need them, and any asset protection trust requires careful attention to the rules for transfers.

For ranch and business owners, see [passing a family business to children](/learn/business-owners/passing-a-family-business-to-children) and [LLC ownership at death](/learn/business-owners/llc-ownership-at-death).

## Taxes

Wyoming has no estate tax, no inheritance tax and no state income tax. The federal exclusion is $15 million per person in 2026, which most families do not reach. Heirs of retirement accounts may owe federal income tax when they withdraw. See [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes).

If you own land in another state, check whether that state taxes estates, because its rules can apply to that property.

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Wyoming

A [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) in Wyoming is governed by statute and should be signed and notarized. Real estate and gifting powers are worth stating clearly.

For health care, Wyoming's Healthcare Decisions Act provides forms for a Living Will and a Health Care Power of Attorney, signed before two witnesses. Wyoming also has a POLST form for serious illness, and an out-of-hospital DNR. See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) and [DNR and POLST](/learn/healthcare-directives/dnr-and-polst).
