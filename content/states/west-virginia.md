West Virginia probate looks different from most states. A county commission, not a judge, oversees many estates, and the process is often handled in the clerk's office. Land is also a big part of family wealth here, including family farms and mineral rights that can complicate who inherits what.

For national background, see our [probate guide](/learn/probate).

## Wills in West Virginia

A West Virginia will must be in writing, signed by you, and witnessed by two competent people who sign in your presence. Notarization is not needed, but a [self-proving affidavit](/glossary/self-proving-affidavit) signed before a notary lets the clerk accept the will without the witnesses. See [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will).

West Virginia recognizes a [holographic will](/glossary/holographic-will) when the entire document is in your handwriting and signed. Two disinterested witnesses must prove the handwriting. A family member cannot be that witness if they inherit. That requirement is why a witnessed will is better; see [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills).

West Virginia is a common law property state with an augmented estate elective share. A surviving spouse can choose a share that rises with the length of the marriage, and the calculation includes some transfers made during life. Confirm the percentages with an attorney.

If there is no will, the spouse and children share under a statutory formula. A blended family can have surprising results, and so a will with clear instructions often matters more than people expect. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will).

## Probate in West Virginia

In West Virginia, the county commission, acting through the county clerk, receives wills and appoints personal representatives. A fiduciary supervisor, who works for the commission, oversees estate administration. Hearings in circuit court are held only when there is a dispute.

The process usually begins with a petition at the clerk's office, where the will is admitted and the executor qualifies, often by posting a bond. The estate files an inventory within a set period and later files settlements, which the fiduciary supervisor reviews. The clerk's office publishes notice to creditors. That supervision is the biggest practical difference from other states: the supervisor reviews filings and can require corrections. Our guide to [what probate is](/learn/probate/what-is-probate) covers the common stages.

West Virginia has simplified procedures for small estates. The qualifying limit has been around $100,000, but confirm the current number with a West Virginia attorney. See [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit).

Common delays include real estate with unclear title, heirs who live out of state, and mineral rights with old leases. Our article on [how long probate takes](/learn/probate/how-long-does-probate-take) explains the usual causes.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in West Virginia

Beneficiary designations, payable-on-death accounts and joint ownership with survivorship pass assets without probate. Whether West Virginia allows a transfer-on-death deed is something to confirm with an attorney. See [transfer-on-death deeds](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed) for how they work where they exist.

Land is the main reason to consider a [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust). Family farms and mineral interests are often held in several counties, and titles may be uncertain. A trust avoids separate proceedings and keeps the plan private. See [leaving your home to children](/learn/property-and-assets/leaving-your-home-to-children) and [joint tenancy risks](/learn/property-and-assets/joint-tenancy-risks), because adding a child to the deed can create tax and creditor problems.

If you own land across the border in [Ohio](/estate-planning/ohio) or [Pennsylvania](/estate-planning/pennsylvania), a trust helps avoid [ancillary probate](/learn/probate/ancillary-probate). Pennsylvania also has an inheritance tax.

## Taxes

West Virginia repealed its estate tax and has no inheritance tax. The federal exclusion is $15 million per person in 2026, so most families owe nothing. Heirs of retirement accounts may owe income tax. See [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes).

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in West Virginia

A [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) in West Virginia is governed by state statute and should be signed and notarized. Banks often prefer documents that spell out real estate and gifting powers.

For health care, West Virginia's Medical Power of Attorney Act lets you name a representative, and its Living Will Act lets you state your wishes. Both documents are signed before two witnesses or a notary. West Virginia also developed a POST form, Physician Orders for Scope of Treatment, for people with serious illness. See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) and [DNR and POLST](/learn/healthcare-directives/dnr-and-polst).
