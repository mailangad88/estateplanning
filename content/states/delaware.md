Delaware is small, but it plays a big role in trust law. For residents, the practical points are simple: no estate or inheritance tax, a two-witness will, and a probate system run by each county's Register of Wills. This guide covers what is specific to Delaware.

## Wills in Delaware

A Delaware will must be written, signed by an adult testator and signed by two witnesses. A notary is not required. A self-proving affidavit, signed before a notary at the time of signing, lets the Register of Wills accept the will without finding the witnesses later. Delaware has no special rule for informal handwritten wills that clearly applies, so a [handwritten or DIY will](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills) should be witnessed anyway, and a lawyer should confirm the point. The signing process is described in [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will).

A surviving spouse can claim an elective share of one-third of the elective estate, which limits how fully a will can disinherit a spouse. Delaware divides an intestate estate differently for real estate and personal property when children survive, so see [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will) and confirm the numbers with a Delaware attorney.

## Probate in Delaware

Delaware probate starts at the Register of Wills in the county where the person lived: Kent, New Castle or Sussex. The Register accepts the will, appoints the executor and issues letters testamentary. The executor must publish notice to creditors, file an inventory and account for the estate, and pay debts and Delaware's required fees. The Court of Chancery handles contested matters such as will disputes and removal of an executor. See what is probate and how long probate takes.

Delaware has simplified procedures for small estates, handled through the Register of Wills and by affidavit for the collection of limited amounts of personal property. The dollar limits have been updated recently, so check the current figures. Real estate is generally not covered. Our [small estate affidavit](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit) article explains how affidavits work in general.

Delaware residents who also own property in another state should plan for ancillary probate, which a funded trust can avoid.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Delaware

Delaware has adopted the Uniform Trust Code and has one of the most trust-friendly legal systems in the country. Features like asset protection trusts, directed trusts and dynasty trusts that can last for generations attract families from other states, but they are specialized tools. See irrevocable trusts and does a trust avoid taxes for the tradeoffs.

For most Delaware families, a [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) plus beneficiary designations is enough ([how to fund a trust](/learn/trusts/how-to-fund-a-trust)). Joint ownership and payable-on-death accounts also bypass probate; see how to avoid probate. Whether Delaware offers a transfer-on-death deed is a point to confirm with a Delaware attorney.

Delaware's small size means the process is often quick and personal: the Register of Wills offices are accessible and staff can explain forms, though they cannot give legal advice. Executors should still keep careful records of every payment, because the Register reviews the accounting. If a family business is a Delaware entity, check its operating agreement, since it may control what happens to an owner's interest at death regardless of the will.

## Taxes

Delaware repealed its estate tax and has no inheritance tax, so only the federal estate tax can apply. As of 2026 the federal exclusion is $15,000,000 per person. See [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes) and federal estate tax exemption. Delaware's lack of tax is a large part of why wealthy families from other states choose Delaware trusts, but a trust's tax treatment can still depend on where the beneficiaries live.

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Delaware

Delaware has adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act, so a [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) is generally durable unless it says otherwise. Confirm the current signing and notarization requirements with an attorney. For health care, Delaware's advance health care directive lets you name an agent and state treatment wishes in one document, and Delaware uses POLST forms for patients who are seriously ill. See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney), [living will](/learn/healthcare-directives/living-will) and DNR and POLST.
