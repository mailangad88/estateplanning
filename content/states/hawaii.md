Hawaii's estate planning issues come from the cost of land and the way land is owned. A state estate tax with a low threshold, leasehold property and Land Court registration all appear alongside ordinary will and probate rules. This guide covers what is specific to Hawaii.

## Wills in Hawaii

A Hawaii will is valid when an adult signs it in front of two witnesses. No notary is required. A self-proving affidavit signed before a notary lets the court admit the will without finding the witnesses. Hawaii also accepts a will written and signed entirely in the testator's handwriting, though proof of handwriting can be disputed; see [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills). Steps for formal signing are in [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will).

A surviving spouse has an elective share of the augmented estate that grows with the length of the marriage. With no will, a spouse takes the whole estate when every child is also the spouse's, and a fixed amount plus a share when some children are from another relationship. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will).

## Probate in Hawaii

Hawaii adopted the Uniform Probate Code, and probate happens in the Circuit Court. Both informal and formal proceedings are available. Informal probate suits uncontested cases, and formal probate is for disputes or a binding ruling. Creditors get a limited window after notice is published; see how long probate takes.

Small estates have two shortcuts. A successor can collect personal property by affidavit when the estate does not exceed $100,000, after a waiting period. A clerk-administered track is available for small estates and can include real property of limited value. Check the current limits and what counts toward them. See [small estate affidavit](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit) for the general procedure and the [probate pillar](/learn/probate) for full administration.

Hawaii property raises special questions. In a **leasehold** condominium or house, you own the building but lease the land, so the remaining lease term affects what heirs receive. **Land Court** property is registered under a separate system, and transfers at death require Land Court procedures. And many residents own ohana or family homes with several relatives on title. Ask an attorney early about the type of ownership; see leaving your home to children and inheriting a house.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Hawaii

A [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) is common in Hawaii, partly because it avoids a second probate when a family also owns property on the mainland (ancillary probate). Hawaii has adopted the Uniform Trust Code. A trust must be funded; see [how to fund a trust](/learn/trusts/how-to-fund-a-trust). Married couples can hold property as tenants by the entirety, which passes automatically to the survivor ([tenancy by the entirety](/glossary/tenancy-by-the-entirety)).

Families who want to keep a family home in the family sometimes use a trust or an LLC with clear rules for who uses and pays for it. Beneficiary designations and payable-on-death accounts also avoid probate; see how to avoid probate. Whether Hawaii offers a transfer-on-death deed is a point to confirm with a Hawaii attorney.

Isolation shapes Hawaii planning. Heirs frequently live on the mainland or abroad, and shipping original documents and arranging court hearings across time zones takes effort. Name an executor or successor trustee who can act locally or who has reliable local help, and keep digital copies of key papers. For leasehold property, record the lease expiration date and landlord contact in your plan so heirs can see how much time remains.

## Taxes

Hawaii imposes an estate tax with an exemption of roughly $5.5 million, far below the federal $15,000,000 for 2026, and graduated rates above it. Given Hawaii real estate values, a couple with a house, retirement accounts and life insurance can exceed the exemption. Hawaii has no inheritance tax. Planning tools such as bypass trusts for married couples may help; confirm the current numbers with a Hawaii attorney. See [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes) and portability for married couples, keeping in mind that federal portability does not carry over to the Hawaii exemption.

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Hawaii

Hawaii has adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act, and a [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) is generally signed before a notary. For health care, Hawaii's Uniform Health-Care Decisions Act lets you name an agent and give instructions in an advance health-care directive, and Hawaii uses a POLST form (called MOLST). See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney), living will and DNR and POLST.
