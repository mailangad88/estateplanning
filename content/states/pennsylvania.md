Pennsylvania is one of a small group of states with an inheritance tax, and it has two features that confuse out-of-state families: the Register of Wills, a county official who handles probate paperwork, and a rule that a will needs to be signed only at its end, not initialed on every page or witnessed at signing.

## Wills in Pennsylvania

A Pennsylvania will must be in writing and signed by you at the end. If you sign it yourself, no witnesses are required at signing, and no notary is needed. In practice, nearly all wills are witnessed, because two people must otherwise prove the will at probate. A self-proving affidavit, signed by you and the witnesses before a notary at signing, skips that step. See [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will).

The strict part is where the signature goes. A signature at the end matters, and text added after the signature can be disregarded. That is the trap for handwritten additions to a typed will. A handwritten or typed document signed at the end can qualify as a will, which makes Pennsylvania flexible and also prone to disputes over informal documents. Read [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills). Electronic wills are not currently authorized.

With no will, a spouse takes the first $30,000 plus half the balance when all the children are also the spouse's, or one-half if some are not. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will). A surviving spouse can also elect against a will and claim one-third of the net estate, including certain assets passing outside the will.

## Probate in Pennsylvania

You start at the Register of Wills in the county of residence. The Register admits the will, grants letters and receives the inheritance tax return. The Orphans' Court Division hears disputes about validity, accounts and removal. The executor must advertise the grant of letters, notify beneficiaries within 90 days, and generally wait before distributing. See [what is probate](/learn/probate/what-is-probate).

Pennsylvania's inheritance tax drives much of the timeline. The tax return is due nine months after death, and a 5 percent discount applies if the tax is paid within three months, so executors often file early. See [what an executor does](/learn/after-a-death/executor-duties) and [how long probate takes](/learn/probate/how-long-does-probate-take).

For small estates, a petition under 20 Pa.C.S. 3102 lets the court distribute an estate of $50,000 or less, not counting real estate and certain direct payments, without a full administration. Real estate cannot pass this way. See [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit). Confirm the current limit before relying on it.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania does not authorize transfer-on-death deeds. Homeowners use other tools. Married couples commonly hold title as tenants by the entirety, so the survivor takes the home automatically. A [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) under the Pennsylvania Uniform Trust Act is the usual vehicle for avoiding probate and for managing property if you become incapacitated. Retirement accounts and life insurance pass by beneficiary designation. Be careful about adding a child to a deed: it can cause gift, capital gains and Medicaid problems. See [how to avoid probate](/learn/probate/how-to-avoid-probate).

## Taxes

Pennsylvania has no estate tax but has an inheritance tax paid by beneficiaries from the estate. A surviving spouse pays 0 percent. Children, grandchildren and parents pay 4.5 percent. Siblings pay 12 percent, and all other heirs, including nieces, nephews and friends, pay 15 percent. Charities are exempt. A transfer from a child age 21 or younger to a parent is taxed at 0 percent. Life insurance proceeds are generally not taxed, but most other assets are, including accounts that pass outside probate such as jointly held property in many cases. The tax applies at death even if the estate is far below the federal exemption of $15 million.

Read [do you pay taxes on an inheritance](/learn/after-a-death/inheritance-taxes-for-heirs) and [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes). Confirm current rates and exemptions with a Pennsylvania attorney.

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Pennsylvania

After the 2014 reform known as Act 95, a Pennsylvania power of attorney must be signed by the principal, acknowledged before a notary and witnessed by two people who are not the agent. The agent signs an acknowledgment, and the form includes a notice explaining the agent's powers. Banks look at these details; see [when a bank refuses a power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/bank-refuses-power-of-attorney). For health care, Pennsylvania uses a health care power of attorney and a living will under its advance directive act, each witnessed by two adults. See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) and [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney). Confirm current forms with a Pennsylvania attorney.
