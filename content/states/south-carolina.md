South Carolina has a modern, Uniform Probate Code-based system, and that works in your favor: informal probate is common, personal representatives can act with limited court supervision, and there is no state estate or inheritance tax. The details still matter, especially the spouse's one-third election and the way real estate moves at death.

For the national picture, start with our [probate guide](/learn/probate) and the overview of [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will).

## Wills in South Carolina

A South Carolina will must be in writing, signed by you, and signed by at least two people who watched you sign or heard you acknowledge the signature. Notarization is not required for validity. Adding a [self-proving affidavit](/glossary/self-proving-affidavit), signed before a notary, lets the probate judge admit the will without testimony from the witnesses.

South Carolina does not give effect to a will that is only in your handwriting, unless it also meets the witnessing rules. If you wrote a will on your own, have it re-executed with two witnesses.

The most distinctive rule is the elective share. South Carolina is a common law property state, but a married person cannot fully cut out a spouse. The surviving spouse may elect one-third of the probate estate instead of taking under the will. The key phrase is probate estate: assets that pass by beneficiary designation or joint ownership are generally not counted, so the election mainly matters for property titled in the deceased spouse's sole name.

Without a will, the spouse takes one-half when children survive, and the children share the rest. If there are no children, the spouse takes the entire estate. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will) for how heirs are ranked beyond that.

## Probate in South Carolina

Probate is handled by the Probate Court in the county where the person lived, with an elected probate judge. Title 62 of the South Carolina Code governs.

Informal probate is the usual route. The applicant files the will and an application, the court appoints a personal representative, and the representative sends notice to creditors and publishes notice once a week for three weeks. Creditors have a limited window to file claims, and the estate generally cannot be closed until that window ends. Formal proceedings with a hearing are used when there is a dispute or a question about the will.

For estates under a modest dollar limit, a small estate affidavit lets heirs collect property without opening an estate. The limit has been around $25,000, but confirm the current number with a South Carolina attorney. Our article on [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit) explains how affidavits generally work.

Timeline drivers in South Carolina include the creditor claim period, real estate that must be sold, and disputes that move a case from informal to formal. Our guide to [how long probate takes](/learn/probate/how-long-does-probate-take) covers the usual slow points.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in South Carolina

Beneficiary designations on life insurance and retirement accounts, payable-on-death bank accounts, and jointly owned property with survivorship all pass outside probate. Our guide on [how to avoid probate](/learn/probate/how-to-avoid-probate) shows how they fit together.

For real estate, South Carolina has not clearly authorized transfer-on-death deeds, so confirm with an attorney before relying on one. The dependable alternative is a [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) that holds title to the house. South Carolina has a Trust Code that governs how trustees must act.

Coastal families sometimes own a second home in North Carolina or Georgia. A trust spares heirs from opening a separate proceeding there. See [ancillary probate](/learn/probate/ancillary-probate).

## Taxes

South Carolina has no estate tax and no inheritance tax. Only the federal estate tax applies, with a $15 million exclusion per person in 2026. Heirs may still owe income tax on inherited retirement accounts. Our article on [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes) explains how states compare.

If you own property in a state that does tax estates, that state can tax the property even though you live in South Carolina.

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in South Carolina

A [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) is governed by the South Carolina Probate Code. Banks in the state tend to prefer documents that are signed before a notary and that spell out gifting and real estate powers.

For healthcare, South Carolina offers a Health Care Power of Attorney, signed before two witnesses, and a Declaration of a Desire for a Natural Death, which is its living will. If neither exists, the Adult Health Care Consent Act names a priority list of family members who can decide. See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) for how to choose your agent.

Neighbors [North Carolina](/estate-planning/north-carolina) and [Georgia](/estate-planning/georgia) use different forms, so redo your documents if you move.
