California's estate planning landscape is shaped by community property, probate fees tied to gross estate value, and property tax rules. Together they push many families toward trusts, a point this guide explains alongside the state's other tools.

## Wills in California

A California will must be signed by an adult testator in front of two witnesses who are present at the same time. The witnesses must understand they are signing a will. There is no notary requirement, and California has no formal self-proving affidavit for ordinary wills. Instead, the will is usually proved by the witnesses' declaration under penalty of perjury, which is why an attestation clause matters. If a witness is also a beneficiary, the law presumes the gift was obtained by undue influence, so pick disinterested witnesses. Steps are in [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will).

California honors handwritten wills if the signature and material provisions are handwritten. See [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills). There is no elective share because spouses already own half of the [community property](/glossary/community-property), but an omitted spouse or child may have a claim if a will was written before the marriage or birth.

Without a will, the surviving spouse takes all community property. Separate property is divided between the spouse and children: half to the spouse if there is one child, one third if there are two or more. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will).

## Probate in California

California probate happens in the Superior Court. The executor petitions to admit the will, receives letters, and must inventory assets, which a court-appointed probate referee appraises. Executors can often use the Independent Administration of Estates Act to take many actions without court approval. Typical drivers of the timeline are the four-month creditor period after letters issue, tax filings and appraisals; see how long probate takes.

The distinctive feature is cost. The attorney and the executor each receive a statutory fee based on the gross value of the estate, not the equity. A paid-off house and a house with a large mortgage generate the same fees. Extraordinary services can add more. See probate costs for the full picture.

California offers shortcuts. For deaths on or after April 1, 2025, an heir can collect personal property with an affidavit when the estate is $208,850 or less, after a 40-day wait; the figure is adjusted every three years. There is also a simplified court petition for a primary residence valued up to $750,000. Our [small estate affidavit](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit) article explains the common structure, and the [probate pillar](/learn/probate) covers formal administration.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in California

Because of the fee structure, the [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) is the classic California tool, backed by a pour-over will that catches forgotten assets. A trust only works if funded; see how to fund a trust.

California also offers a revocable transfer on death deed. It lets you name a beneficiary for real estate with no probate, and you can change your mind at any time. The deed must be notarized and recorded within 60 days of signing. See [transfer-on-death deeds](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed) for general tradeoffs.

Property taxes affect planning. Under Proposition 19, a child who inherits a parent's home keeps the lower assessed value only if the child moves in as a primary residence, up to a limit; other transfers are reassessed. Discuss that with a California attorney before deciding who gets the family home; see leaving your home to children.

California families should also ask about retirement accounts and the family home together. Because beneficiary-designated assets such as an IRA skip probate but a house does not unless it is in a trust or has a transfer on death deed, the probate estate is often just the house and a few accounts. That makes a quick check of how each asset is titled the single most useful hour of planning, and it is where most California estates are won or lost.

## Taxes

California has no estate tax and no inheritance tax. The federal exclusion is $15,000,000 per person in 2026. Because community property gets a full step-up in basis at the first spouse's death, capital gains planning is a bigger practical issue than estate tax for many families. See [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes).

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in California

California has a statutory form for a [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney), which must be signed before a notary or two witnesses. The state's Advance Health Care Directive combines a health care agent appointment with end-of-life wishes and is signed before two witnesses or a notary. Related: [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) and living will. California also uses POLST forms for seriously ill patients; see DNR and POLST.
