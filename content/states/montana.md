Montana is one of the states that adopted the Uniform Probate Code, which gives it a more streamlined, less court-driven system than many states. The rules are friendly to ordinary families, and a few features, such as handwritten wills and a harmless-error rule, make the system forgiving. That does not make a do-it-yourself plan risk free.

## Wills in Montana

A Montana will must be in writing, signed by you, and signed by at least two people who witnessed you sign it or heard you acknowledge the signature. The witnesses can sign afterward, within a reasonable time. No notary is needed. Our national guide to [making a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will) walks through the choices that apply everywhere; what is distinctive here is how much Montana forgives.

Montana accepts a [holographic will](/glossary/holographic-will), meaning one where your signature and the important provisions are in your own handwriting. Montana also lets a court treat a defective document as a will if there is clear and convincing evidence that you meant it to be one. That rule covers a writing, not a video or audio recording. Treat it as a safety net. It means a family may have to prove intent in court, which costs money and invites arguments. See [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills) for why a properly witnessed will is still the better path.

If you die without a will, your spouse inherits everything when all your children are also the spouse's. If any child is from another relationship, the spouse receives half and your children share the other half, which is a common surprise in [blended families](/learn/blended-families). Our guide to [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will) explains the order of heirs.

A surviving spouse also has protection against being disinherited. Montana gives a spouse an elective share of the augmented estate, a number that increases the longer the marriage lasted, plus homestead and family allowances.

## Probate in Montana

Probate is handled by the district court in the county where the person lived, under Title 72 of the Montana Code Annotated. Because Montana uses the UPC, the process has an informal track, where a personal representative is appointed with limited court involvement, and a formal track for disputes. Most family estates stay informal. See [what probate is](/learn/probate/what-is-probate) for the general sequence.

Montana's small estate procedure is a sworn affidavit that a successor presents to whoever holds the decedent's personal property, after a 30-day waiting period. It covers personal property, such as bank accounts and vehicles, not land. The dollar limit was recently raised and reported at $100,000; confirm the current figure before you rely on it. Our guide to [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit) explains when this shortcut is enough.

Real estate is where families get stuck. Owning land in Montana and living elsewhere can trigger [ancillary probate](/learn/probate/ancillary-probate), which is common for out-of-state owners of ranch and recreational property.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Montana

Montana authorizes transfer-on-death deeds. You sign and record a deed naming who receives the property at your death, and you keep full control while alive. It must be recorded before you die. Details such as who may be named and what happens to a property with a mortgage are worth reviewing with an attorney, and our article on [transfer-on-death deeds](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed) covers the pros and cons.

A [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) is the other main tool, especially for owners of several properties, a business, or land in more than one state. Beneficiary designations on retirement accounts and life insurance, and payable-on-death bank accounts, also bypass probate. Our guide on [how to avoid probate](/learn/probate/how-to-avoid-probate) compares these tools.

## Taxes

Montana has no estate tax and no inheritance tax. Only the federal estate tax can apply, and only to estates above the federal exemption. See our overview of [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes).

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Montana

Montana lets you name an agent for finances under a [durable power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) and an agent for medical decisions in a health care directive. Montana's statutes include optional forms, and a Montana-licensed attorney can confirm which current form to use and how it must be signed and witnessed. A [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) matters most when a hospital needs someone with legal authority to speak for you. Confirm with an attorney whether lenders and title companies in your county accept a particular form.
