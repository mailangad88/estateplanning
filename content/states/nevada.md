Nevada draws retirees and second-home owners from all over the country, and many of them arrive with plans written in another state. Nevada is a [community property](/glossary/community-property) state, which changes who owns what in a marriage, and it has some of the most modern will rules in the country.

## Wills in Nevada

A Nevada will must be signed by you and two competent witnesses who sign in your presence. No notary is required. Witnesses can later sign a statement under penalty of perjury, making the will self-proved, so the court can accept it without calling them. The practical advice in our guide to [making a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will) applies, and it helps to choose witnesses who are not beneficiaries.

Nevada also recognizes a handwritten will if you write the material provisions, date it and sign it yourself, no witnesses needed. The date is mandatory, which catches people who assume a handwritten note works without one. See [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills).

Nevada was the first state to recognize electronic wills. An electronic will must carry your electronic signature and meet one of the authentication routes in the statute, such as an electronic notary or two electronic witnesses. Remote execution is possible, but because errors void the document, have an attorney supervise.

If you die without a will, community property and separate property are treated differently. Your spouse takes your half of the community property. Of your separate property, the spouse takes half if you leave one child and one-third if you leave two or more. Our guide to [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will) explains the general order of heirs.

## Probate in Nevada

Probate is a district court matter under Nevada Revised Statutes Title 12. A formal administration involves a petition, notice to heirs and creditors, an inventory and a period for creditor claims. Many estates can use a simpler track, and [how long probate takes](/learn/probate/how-long-does-probate-take) depends on those creditor deadlines and on any real estate sale.

Nevada has two shortcuts for small estates. A personal-property affidavit requires a 40-day wait and notice to anyone with an equal or better claim, and it cannot be used if the estate includes an interest in real property. A court set-aside petition can include real property and can be filed after 30 days. Both limits were raised by the Legislature in 2025 and are currently reported at up to $150,000, with a lower limit for the affidavit when the claimant is not the surviving spouse. Confirm the current numbers before relying on them. See [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit) for how these compare with full probate.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Nevada

Nevada offers three useful non-probate tools. First, a deed upon death, Nevada's name for a transfer-on-death deed, lets you name who receives real estate and keep control while you are alive. Our guide to [transfer-on-death deeds](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed) covers the tradeoffs. Second, spouses can hold community property with right of survivorship, so the survivor takes the asset automatically; the form of title should be spelled out on the deed. Third, a [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) remains the main tool for couples with several properties or heirs from earlier marriages.

Joint tenancy is common between parents and adult children, but it carries risks that are separate from spouses holding community property. See [joint tenancy risks](/learn/property-and-assets/joint-tenancy-risks). For couples who move to Nevada from a common-law state, a lawyer should check how existing assets will be characterized.

## Taxes

Nevada has no state estate tax and no inheritance tax. Only the federal estate tax can apply, to estates above the federal exemption. Community property can also matter for income tax, because both halves of community property can receive a stepped-up basis at the first spouse's death. Read [step-up in basis](/learn/estate-tax/step-up-in-basis) and our overview of [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes).

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Nevada

Nevada's statutes contain a power of attorney chapter and statutory forms for health care decisions and living wills. A [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) must be signed before a notary, and a [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) requires witnesses or a notary. Nevada also has a POLST program for people with serious illness; see [DNR and POLST](/learn/healthcare-directives/dnr-and-polst). Confirm the current statutory forms with a Nevada attorney.
