Vermont stands apart for estate planning mainly because of its state estate tax, which kicks in far below the federal threshold. Add a strong tradition of owning land and camps across generations, and planning ahead has real value.

For national guidance, see our [wills guide](/learn/wills) and [estate tax guide](/learn/estate-tax).

## Wills in Vermont

A Vermont will must be in writing, signed by you, and witnessed by two or more credible people (14 V.S.A. 5). Vermont required three witnesses until 2006, which is why older Vermont wills often have three. If you moved to Vermont with a will from elsewhere, have an attorney review it. See [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will).

Notarization is not required. A [self-proving affidavit](/glossary/self-proving-affidavit) signed before a notary can speed probate. Vermont does not accept a handwritten will without witnesses, so [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills) are risky here.

Vermont is a common law property state. A surviving spouse who is unhappy with the will can generally elect against it and take a statutory share, so a spouse cannot be completely cut out. Confirm the share with an attorney.

If there is no will, the estate passes under Vermont's intestacy statute, and the spouse's share depends on whether the children are all the spouse's. Confirm the rules with an attorney. For how intestacy works nationally, see [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will).

## Probate in Vermont

Probate in Vermont is handled by the Probate Division of the Superior Court. The state is divided into probate districts, and the register in the district where the person lived handles filings. Title 14 of the Vermont Statutes governs.

The executor files the will and a petition, is appointed by the court, and then must inventory assets, give notice to creditors and heirs, and settle debts before distributing property. Vermont probate is generally managed through paperwork and a hearing for formal matters. Our guide to [what probate is](/learn/probate/what-is-probate) describes the stages.

Vermont offers simplified procedures for small estates. Dollar limits are low and can change, so confirm the current figure. See [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit).

Two features commonly extend Vermont estates. First, a taxable estate must file a Vermont estate tax return and receive tax clearance before the court will close the estate. Second, land and camps held with relatives, sometimes with old deeds, can need title work. Land titles that cross town and county lines add steps. See [how long probate takes](/learn/probate/how-long-does-probate-take).

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Vermont

Beneficiary designations, joint accounts with survivorship rights and payable-on-death accounts pass outside probate. A [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) lets you transfer a house or land without probate and keeps your plan private. Vermont has adopted a Uniform Trust Code.

For real estate, confirm whether a transfer-on-death deed is available in Vermont before relying on one; see [transfer-on-death deeds](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed). A trust is the more dependable way to hold a house.

Many Vermonters own second homes in New Hampshire, New York or Massachusetts. A trust can avoid probate in each state. See [out-of-state property](/learn/property-and-assets/out-of-state-property) and [ancillary probate](/learn/probate/ancillary-probate).

For farms, a plan that combines a trust, a limited liability company and conservation or use-value tax programs is common. See [passing a family business to children](/learn/business-owners/passing-a-family-business-to-children).

## Taxes

Vermont has an estate tax. For deaths in 2025 and later, the threshold is $5 million with a flat 16 percent rate on the excess; confirm the current figure because it can be changed by legislation. Vermont has no inheritance tax. The federal exclusion is $15 million per person in 2026, so Vermont is the lower threshold for large estates.

Married couples should plan the first death carefully so that the state exemption is not wasted. See [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes) and [portability for married couples](/learn/estate-tax/portability-for-married-couples).

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Vermont

A [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) is governed by Vermont law and should be signed and notarized. Confirm which powers, such as gifting or real estate, require specific language.

For health care, Vermont has a statutory advance directive form that lets you name an agent and describe your wishes. It must be signed before two witnesses or a notary, and Vermont maintains an advance directive registry that lets providers find it. See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) and [living will](/learn/healthcare-directives/living-will).

Neighbors include [New Hampshire](/estate-planning/new-hampshire) and [New York](/estate-planning/new-york).
