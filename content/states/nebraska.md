Nebraska looks like a straightforward Uniform Probate Code state, and in most respects it is. The surprise is the inheritance tax, which is rare nationally and can change who receives how much of a bequest to a nephew, cousin or friend. Plan for that first, then for the rest.

## Wills in Nebraska

A Nebraska will needs your signature and two witnesses who saw you sign or heard you acknowledge the document as your will. Notarization is not required, and a notary cannot be one of the witnesses. If you add a self-proving affidavit, signed before an officer who can administer oaths, the court can accept the will without calling the witnesses. Our national guide to [making a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will) covers capacity and common signing errors.

Nebraska recognizes handwritten wills but with a stricter rule than some states: the signature, the material provisions and an indication of the date must all be in your own handwriting. Fill-in-the-blank forms are the usual trap, because printed text can undercut a will that is meant to be entirely handwritten. Read [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills) before relying on one. Nebraska does not allow electronic wills, so a will is a signed paper document.

Without a will, a surviving spouse takes the first $100,000 plus half of the remainder when all the children are also the spouse's. If some children are from another relationship, the spouse's first amount is smaller and the children take more. In a blended family, that can leave a spouse with less than the couple expected. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will).

## Probate in Nebraska

Probate happens in county court, and Nebraska's version of the Uniform Probate Code offers informal and formal paths, so many estates proceed with limited court involvement. [Probate timing and cost](/learn/probate/how-long-does-probate-take) depend mostly on creditor deadlines, real estate sales and, in Nebraska, the inheritance tax filing.

Nebraska has two small estate affidavits. One covers personal property and goes straight to whoever holds the asset. The other covers real property: all successors sign it and it is recorded with the register of deeds in the county where the land sits. Both have a 30-day waiting period and, as currently reported, a $100,000 limit that was raised in recent years. Confirm current limits, and see [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit) for how they compare with a full case.

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Nebraska

Nebraska authorizes a transfer-on-death deed for real estate. The key practical rule is timing: record it within 30 days of signing and before you die. Our guide to [transfer-on-death deeds](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed) explains how they compare with a trust. A [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) suits owners of farmland or ranchland who want one plan for several parcels and a smooth handoff of management.

## Taxes

Nebraska has no state estate tax but does have a county inheritance tax. A surviving spouse and charities are exempt. Closer relatives, such as children and siblings, pay a low rate above an exemption, while more distant relatives and unrelated heirs pay higher rates with smaller exemptions. Because the rates and thresholds have been adjusted over the years, confirm the current schedule with an attorney or your county court. Beneficiaries should read [do you pay taxes on an inheritance](/learn/after-a-death/inheritance-taxes-for-heirs), and our overview of [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes) explains how these taxes differ from the federal estate tax.

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Nebraska

Nebraska has a statute for health care powers of attorney and a separate form of living will under its Rights of the Terminally Ill Act, and it adopted a version of the Uniform Power of Attorney Act for financial agents. A [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) should be notarized, and a [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) typically needs either two witnesses or a notary. Confirm witness rules with a Nebraska attorney, because signing defects are the usual reason a bank or hospital refuses a form.
