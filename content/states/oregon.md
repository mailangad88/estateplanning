Oregon is the state where moderately wealthy families most often get a surprise. A family home in a rising market, a retirement account and a life insurance policy can add up to more than $1 million, which is Oregon's estate tax threshold, even though the federal exemption is $15 million in 2026.

## Wills in Oregon

An Oregon will must be signed by you in front of two witnesses. Each witness must see you sign, hear you acknowledge your signature, or see you direct someone to sign for you, and then must sign before your death. A notary is not required. Oregon does not have the usual self-proving affidavit, but a witness who signs an affidavit at the same time as the will is treated as having signed the will itself. See [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will) for the general rules.

Handwriting does not replace witnesses in Oregon, because [holographic wills](/glossary/holographic-will) are not recognized. A court may excuse a defect in execution if there is clear and convincing evidence you intended the document to be your will, but that means litigation. Electronic wills are not allowed; the statute states that a writing does not include an electronic record. Read [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills).

If you die without a will, a surviving spouse takes one-half of the estate when children survive, and the children share the other half. See [dying without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will). Oregon also gives a spouse an elective share on a sliding scale that grows with the length of the marriage, up to one-third of the augmented estate.

## Probate in Oregon

Probate takes place in the circuit court of the county where the person lived. Oregon requires the personal representative to notify heirs and devisees and to publish notice to creditors, and creditors have four months from first publication to present claims. Oregon probate often takes six months to a year. See [what is probate](/learn/probate/what-is-probate).

Oregon renamed its small estate affidavit the simple estate affidavit. It is available 30 days after death when personal property is no more than $75,000 and real property and manufactured homes total no more than $200,000. That makes it useful even when a house is involved, which is not true in many states. It is filed with the circuit court. Confirm the current limits before relying on them. See [small estate procedures](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit).

## Trusts and avoiding probate in Oregon

Oregon adopted the Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act, so a recorded transfer-on-death deed names a beneficiary for real estate while you keep control. The deed is recorded before death. Our guide to [transfer-on-death deeds](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed) covers mortgages and creditor questions. A [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust) governed by the Oregon Uniform Trust Code is a common choice for avoiding probate for larger estates, and a trust can also be written to use both spouses' exemptions. A trust by itself does not avoid Oregon estate tax; see [does a trust avoid taxes](/learn/trusts/does-a-trust-avoid-taxes).

## Taxes

Oregon has an estate tax with a $1,000,000 exemption, and rates from 10 percent up to 16 percent on the amount above it. There is no inheritance tax. Oregon does not recognize portability, so a surviving spouse cannot inherit the first spouse's unused Oregon exemption. Couples whose combined estate is above $1 million often use a credit shelter structure in their will or trust. Lifetime gifts made close to death can matter. The federal exemption is $15 million per person in 2026; see [federal estate tax exemption](/learn/estate-tax/federal-estate-tax-exemption) and [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes). Confirm the current exemption and rates with an Oregon attorney.

## Powers of attorney and healthcare directives in Oregon

Oregon has a statutory advance directive form under ORS chapter 127, which names a health care representative and states treatment wishes. It must be signed in front of two witnesses or a notary, and the health care representative cannot be a witness. Oregon also has a POLST registry for people with serious illness; see [DNR and POLST](/learn/healthcare-directives/dnr-and-polst). A [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) should be notarized, especially for real estate. See [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney). Confirm the current forms with an Oregon attorney.
