# Template: scenario ("what happens if ...")

Target: one thing that goes wrong when someone delays or skips a step, phrased the way people search
("What happens if my ex is still my beneficiary?"). Length 1,200 to 1,800 words. Cluster: `what-if`.

The reader is usually already in the situation, or just realized they might be. Be calm and specific. Never
use fear as a sales tactic: no countdowns, no "you could lose everything", no invented odds or dollar
losses. Describe what the law and institutions actually do, then what can still be fixed.

- **Title:** the question itself, starting "What happens if ...".
- **answer:** what happens by default in one or two sentences, then the single biggest factor that changes it
  (state law, account type, whether the person can still sign).
- **Sections:**
  1. `## What happens by default` - who decides, who gets what, which court or institution is involved.
  2. `## A realistic example` - a short story with invented first names that follows the default outcome
     step by step, with the time and cost drivers named (not invented figures).
  3. `## What can still be fixed` - numbered steps for (a) the person while they can still act, and
     (b) the family if it is now too late.
  4. `## How state law changes this` - name the kind of rule that varies; link state guides; name a state
     only when you are sure of its rule.
  5. `## How to prevent it` - the one to three documents or forms that would have avoided it, each linked.
  6. `## How we can help`
- **FAQs:** 4 to 6 follow-ups, never repeating one used on another page.
- Link the `what-if` pillar, the topic cluster the scenario belongs to, and at least one tool or checklist.
