Your father died two months ago. The gas bill still comes in his name, the mortgage autopay ran from an account the bank just froze, and your sister has been sleeping in her old bedroom since the funeral. Nobody has decided whether to sell, and someone has to keep the place standing.

This page, part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois), covers the stretch from the death to the day the house is sold or deeded: who may make decisions, who pays the bills, and how to handle a sibling who lives there. The sale itself is in [selling a house in probate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/selling-a-house-in-probate-in-illinois). If you want a lawyer to sort out who holds authority over your parent's house, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-taking-care-of-a-deceased-parents-house-in-illinois&topic=probate).

## The short version

- Find out how the house passed before anyone makes promises. The deed or trust decides who is in charge.
- Protect the house first: lock it, insure it, keep the heat on. Big decisions can wait.
- Keep paying the mortgage and the property tax, even if a family member has to front the money for a while.
- Log every dollar anyone spends on the house, and tell the other heirs the same week.
- If a sibling lives there, put who pays what, and for how long, in writing.

## Who is in charge of the house after a parent dies?

It depends on how your parent held title, not on who is oldest or lives closest. Our pages on [what to do when a parent dies with the house in their name](/learn/illinois/parent-died-house-in-their-name) and [inheriting a house in Illinois](/learn/illinois/inheriting-a-house-in-illinois) explain how title passes. This table shows who holds the keys and the checkbook in the months that follow.

| How the house passed | Who is in charge | When they can act | Whose money pays the bills |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent's name alone, with a will | The named executor | Narrow steps before letters, full authority once letters of office issue | The estate account, once it is opened |
| Parent's name alone, no will | An administrator the court appoints | Only after appointment | The estate account; until then, family advances |
| Living trust | The successor trustee | After accepting the role, with no court | Trust funds |
| Recorded transfer on death instrument | The beneficiary | After recording a notice of death affidavit (755 ILCS 27/75) | The beneficiary |
| Joint tenancy or tenancy by the entirety | The surviving owner | Right away | The surviving owner |

Two Illinois details matter. A deed to two people creates a tenancy in common unless it says joint tenancy (765 ILCS 1005/1), so a parent's half may still be in the estate. See [joint tenancy and tenancy by the entirety in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-joint-tenancy-and-tenancy-by-the-entirety). The small estate affidavit does not reach real estate (755 ILCS 5/25-1), so a house in one name usually needs a probate case even when everything else is small.

Before letters issue, a named executor's power is limited to things like burial, paying necessary funeral charges and preserving the estate (755 ILCS 5/6-14). Locking up and insuring the house fits that. Signing a lease or a listing does not. A TODI beneficiary is in a different spot: the house is theirs once the death occurs, but it can still be reached by the estate's creditors (755 ILCS 27/85). The beneficiary's paperwork is on [the notice of death affidavit for an Illinois TODI](/learn/illinois/notice-of-death-affidavit-for-an-illinois-todi). A trustee's own calendar is in [an Illinois successor trustee's first 90 days](/learn/illinois/illinois-successor-trustee-first-90-days).

## What should happen in the first 30 and 90 days?

The first month is about holding the house steady. The next two are about putting it on a footing someone can manage until it is sold or deeded.

**Days 1 to 30**

- [ ] Put the deed, the latest tax bill, the mortgage statement and the insurance declarations page in one folder, and check the recorded deed with the county to confirm which row of the table applies.
- [ ] Count the keys. Change the locks if you cannot account for all of them, and tell every heir who holds a key now.
- [ ] Call the insurer, report the death and ask what the policy requires if the house sits empty.
- [ ] Keep the heat on at a safe level, and keep electricity and water running unless the insurer says otherwise.
- [ ] Move cash, jewelry, firearms, medication and papers to a safe place, and list what you took.
- [ ] Make sure any original will goes to the circuit clerk, which must happen within 30 days after death (755 ILCS 5/6-1).
- [ ] Start a payment log on day one.

**Days 31 to 90**

- [ ] Open the probate case if one is needed, so a representative can get letters. See [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois).
- [ ] Once letters issue, open an estate account and move the house bills to it. Our page on [opening an estate bank account in Illinois](/learn/illinois/opening-an-estate-bank-account-in-illinois) lists what the bank asks for.
- [ ] Repay any family member who advanced bills, from the estate account, and note it in the log.
- [ ] Call the mortgage servicer with the death certificate and ask for its successor or estate contact.
- [ ] Check the tax bill's exemption lines and ask the county assessor what changes now.
- [ ] If someone lives in the house, sign a short occupancy agreement.
- [ ] Set a target date for the family to decide: sell, buy out or keep.

Vacancy, repairs and squatters are covered in [a vacant inherited house](/learn/property-and-assets/vacant-inherited-house-insurance-repairs-squatters).

## Who pays the mortgage, property taxes and utilities?

The estate or trust pays, when it has money, and a relative often covers the gap until it does. Expenses of preserving the house are administration costs, and the Probate Act ranks funeral and administration costs at the top of the claim classes (755 ILCS 5/18-10), as explained in [how creditor claims work in an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/how-creditor-claims-work-in-an-illinois-estate).

**The mortgage.** The loan does not end at death and payments should continue. Federal law generally keeps a lender from calling the loan due just because a close relative inherits, but missed payments still lead to foreclosure. An autopay from your parent's own account may stop when the bank freezes it, so check the next statement. The options for the loan are in [what happens to the mortgage when the homeowner dies](/learn/property-and-assets/mortgage-when-the-homeowner-dies).

**Property tax.** The county keeps billing, and unpaid taxes can lead to a tax sale. Your parent's exemptions may not carry over. In Cook County, the homeowner exemption goes to an owner who lives in the home, so an heir who rents the house out would not get it. A surviving spouse's exemptions are covered in [Illinois property tax exemptions after a spouse dies](/learn/illinois/illinois-property-tax-exemptions-after-a-spouse-dies). If your parent used the state's senior tax deferral program, the deferred amount is a lien that comes due at death; see [the Illinois senior citizens real estate tax deferral program](/learn/illinois/illinois-senior-citizens-property-tax-deferral-program-and-what-heirs-repay).

**Utilities.** Keep them on, and expect each company to ask for the death certificate before it moves the account. The [household bills and autopay map](/free/household-bills-and-autopay-map) helps you find every bill that was running.

The creditor homestead exemption is a separate thing. It shields $15,000 of equity for a surviving spouse or minor children (735 ILCS 5/12-902) and does nothing to pay a bill. See [the Illinois homestead exemption after a death](/learn/illinois/illinois-homestead-exemption-after-a-death).

## Does the sibling who pays the bills get paid back?

Usually yes, if the payment helped preserve the house and is documented. The executor repays it from the estate account before the heirs divide what is left. Trouble comes from missing receipts, payments nobody heard about until the end, and improvements nobody agreed to. Treat an advance as a loan to the estate, not a gift. The fuller rules on reimbursement and on costs after co-owners take title are in [who pays for an inherited house](/learn/property-and-assets/who-pays-for-an-inherited-house-upkeep-taxes-and-reimbursing-the-sibling-who-fronts-it).

If the executor refuses to repay a documented advance, the family member can make a written claim against the estate, and Illinois bars unfiled claims two years after death at the outside (755 ILCS 5/18-12). Do not wait that long.

## What if a sibling is living in the house?

During the estate, a sibling who is not an owner lives there with the permission of whoever is in charge. The executor or trustee owes duties to every heir, so the arrangement has to be fair to the others. That usually means the occupant pays the utilities and upkeep, and sometimes a share of the taxes or a modest rent into the estate account. Tell the insurer someone is living there.

If the house will be sold, the executor can set a move-out date. If the sibling will not leave, the executor cannot lock them out or put their things outside, and must use a lawful process, usually written notice and then a court case. Once heirs take title as co-owners, each can use the whole house, and the remedies change to a buyout or partition, covered in [siblings disagree about an inherited house in Illinois](/learn/illinois/siblings-disagree-inherited-house). The [Illinois staying in the house guide](/free/illinois-staying-in-the-house-after-a-death-guide) is a handout you can share with the family.

### Example: a Rockford bungalow and three siblings

This is an invented example. Denise is the executor of her mother's will, which splits everything equally among Denise, her brother Marcus and her sister Lena. Marcus moved in to help their mother and is still there. Before letters, Denise pays the March mortgage and the insurance premium herself, about $2,300, emails both siblings the receipts, and logs it. After letters issue in May, she opens the estate account and repays herself.

The three sign a one-page agreement. Marcus pays the utilities and lawn care, puts a fixed monthly amount toward the property tax into the estate account, and agrees to move out within 30 days of an accepted offer. When the house sells in the fall, every payment is already logged, and the accounting causes no fight.

## What records should the person in charge keep?

Keep enough that a sibling who was never there could follow every dollar:

1. A payment log with date, payee, amount, who paid and from which account.
2. Receipts and invoices for every repair, with before-and-after photos.
3. A key log: who has a key and since when.
4. Dated photos of every room, taken soon after the death.
5. Copies of letters and emails to the insurer, servicer and assessor, and any signed occupancy agreement.

In a supervised estate, the inventory is filed with the court, generally within 60 days after letters (755 ILCS 5/14-1); see [the Illinois probate inventory](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-inventory). Careful records also protect the executor, as [executor personal liability in Illinois](/learn/illinois/executor-personal-liability-in-illinois) explains, and the [executor workload tool](/tools/executor-workload) estimates how much work the role takes.
