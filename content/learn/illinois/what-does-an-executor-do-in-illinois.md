  - q: "What is the very first job of an Illinois executor?"
    a: "Getting the original will to the circuit clerk of the county where the person lived, because Illinois law requires it to be filed right after the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1). You also have 30 days after learning you were named to start the case or decline (755 ILCS 5/6-3). The funeral can be handled in the same days."
  - q: "How many months does an Illinois executor usually stay on the job?"
    a: "For a simple estate, often a little over six months, because creditors get at least six months after the first published notice and the closing report adds a 42-day wait. A house to sell, a will contest or an estate tax return can stretch it past a year. No statute sets a fixed finish date for an independent estate."
  - q: "Is an Illinois executor required to use a lawyer?"
    a: "The Probate Act does not say every executor must hire one, but the Cook County clerk tells filers they need an attorney to open a probate estate there. Many executors hire one for the court filings and handle the house, bills and records themselves. Reasonable legal fees are paid from the estate."
  - q: "Can an Illinois executor sell the house if one heir objects?"
    a: "An independent executor can generally sell without a court order (755 ILCS 5/28-8). The exception is a house the will leaves to a named person, which needs that person's written consent unless the estate needs the money. An objecting heir can ask the court to supervise the estate, which brings the sale under court approval."
  - q: "Can beneficiaries in Illinois get money before the estate closes?"
    a: "Sometimes. The court can allow an early distribution when the estate clearly has enough to cover every claim, and the person receiving it posts a bond for double the value of the share (755 ILCS 5/24-4). Without that step, an executor who pays out early takes a personal risk if a valid claim shows up later."
  - q: "What should an Illinois executor never do with estate money?"
    a: "Never put it in a personal account, never pay yourself before the work is done and approved, and never pay a small creditor while a higher-priority bill is unpaid in an estate that may run short. Each of those can make you personally responsible for the loss. Keep every dollar in an estate account under the estate's own tax ID."

Someone you love has died, the will names you as executor, and people keep asking what happens next. You need a clear list of the job, in the order it happens, so you can see the whole road before you start.

This page, part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois), is that list. Each step gets a short explanation and a link to the page on this site that covers it in full, so you can go deep only where you need to. ## What is an Illinois executor, in plain terms?

An [executor](/glossary/executor) is the person a will names to settle the estate, and in Illinois the job is official only after the circuit court issues [letters of office](/glossary/letters-of-office). From then on you are a fiduciary, which means you act for the heirs and creditors, not for yourself. The [fiduciary duty](/glossary/fiduciary-duty) is the thread through every step below: keep estate money separate, keep records, treat beneficiaries as the will directs, and be able to explain every payment.

The court's side of the case, what the judge and clerk do, is laid out in the [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process). This page is the executor's side: your jobs, in sequence. If you are not yet sure you want the role, read [can you refuse to be executor in Illinois](/learn/illinois/can-you-refuse-to-be-executor-in-illinois) and [who can be an executor in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-can-be-an-executor-in-illinois) first.

## What can you do before the court appoints you?

Very little, by design. Until letters issue, a named executor may carry out a gift of the body, arrange the burial, pay necessary funeral charges and preserve the estate (755 ILCS 5/6-14). Selling, spending and moving money are not on that list.

1. **Handle the funeral and keep receipts.** Funeral and burial costs sit in the first payment class, so whoever pays can be repaid from the estate later. See [paying for a funeral from an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/paying-for-a-funeral-from-an-illinois-estate).
2. **Order certified death certificates.** You will need one for each bank, insurer and agency. [Illinois death certificates](/learn/illinois/illinois-death-certificates-who-can-order-county-clerk-or-idph) explains who can order them and from where.
3. **Get the original will to the circuit clerk.** Illinois requires the holder to file it right after the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1), and a copy will not do. [Filing the original will](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) covers the mechanics.
4. **Secure the house, car and valuables.** Lock up, keep insurance and utilities running, and collect the mail. [Taking care of a deceased parent's house](/learn/illinois/taking-care-of-a-deceased-parents-house-in-illinois) has the practical checklist.
5. **Decide within 30 days.** A named executor has 30 days after learning of the nomination to start probate or decline (755 ILCS 5/6-3). Before you file, check whether probate is needed at all; a [small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) may be enough for personal property alone.

## How do you get the authority to act?

You petition the court, attend a short hearing, take an oath and receive letters. Step by step:

6. **File the petition for letters.** It lists the heirs and legatees, estimates the property and asks for independent administration. What goes in it is in [the Illinois petition for letters of office](/learn/illinois/illinois-petition-for-letters-of-office), and the filing day is in [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois).
7. **Arrange a bond if the court requires one.** A will can excuse it, but the court may still ask for security, especially from an executor who lives out of state (755 ILCS 5/6-13). [Letters and the surety bond](/learn/illinois/illinois-letters-of-administration-and-the-surety-bond) explains sizing, and [serving from another state](/learn/illinois/non-resident-executor-in-illinois) covers the out-of-state angle.
8. **Pick up certified letters.** Order extra copies, since banks want recently certified ones.

Most Illinois estates are independent; [independent vs. supervised administration](/learn/illinois/independent-vs-supervised-administration-in-illinois) explains what changes if yours is not.

## What are your jobs in the first two months after letters?

Notices, money and a list, running side by side.

9. **Mail notice to heirs and legatees within 14 days** after the order admitting the will (755 ILCS 5/6-10). The packet, the rights it describes and the proof to keep are on the [notice to heirs and legatees](/learn/illinois/notice-to-heirs-and-legatees-in-illinois-probate) page.
10. **Get an estate EIN and open the estate account.** Every dollar in and out should run through it. [Opening an estate bank account in Illinois](/learn/illinois/opening-an-estate-bank-account-in-illinois) lists what the bank asks for.
11. **Publish the claims notice and mail known creditors.** The notice runs once a week for three weeks, and the bar date is the later of six months from first publication or three months from mailing (755 ILCS 5/18-3). [How creditor claims work in an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/how-creditor-claims-work-in-an-illinois-estate) walks through allowing and disallowing claims.
12. **Build the inventory.** List every probate asset at its date-of-death value. A supervised executor files it with the court, generally within 60 days after letters (755 ILCS 5/14-1); an independent executor keeps it for heirs who ask. See [the Illinois probate inventory](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-inventory), plus [finding unclaimed property](/learn/illinois/finding-unclaimed-property-for-an-illinois-estate) and the [Illinois digital assets law](/learn/illinois/illinois-digital-assets-law-for-executors) for assets that hide.

## What do you do while the claims period runs?

You collect, protect, pay and file.

13. **Pay valid claims in the legal order.** Funeral and administration costs come first, and the classes are set by 755 ILCS 5/18-10. A surviving spouse or minor child may also be owed a spouse's or child's award; a spouse's award is at least $20,000 (755 ILCS 5/15-1), as the [spouse rights page](/learn/illinois/illinois-spouse-rights-at-death) explains. Paying out of order can expose you personally; see [executor personal liability in Illinois](/learn/illinois/executor-personal-liability-in-illinois).
14. **Sell or transfer property as needed.** An independent executor can sell real estate without a court order (755 ILCS 5/28-8), with limits for a house the will gives to a named person. [Selling a house in probate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/selling-a-house-in-probate-in-illinois) has the details and the supervised route.
15. **File the tax returns.** That means the person's last [Illinois income tax return](/learn/illinois/final-illinois-income-tax-return-for-someone-who-died), an estate income tax return such as the [Form IL-1041](/learn/illinois/illinois-form-il-1041-estate-and-trust-income-tax-return) if the estate has income, and an Illinois estate tax return, due nine months after death, if the estate exceeds $4 million. The [Illinois estate tax](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax) page has the threshold rules.

## When do beneficiaries get paid?

Usually after two clocks have run out: the creditor bar date and the six-month window to contest the will, which starts when the will is admitted (755 ILCS 5/8-1). Paying out before both pass is possible but risky. The court can allow an early distribution when assets clearly cover every claim, with a bond from the person receiving it (755 ILCS 5/24-4).

16. **Distribute and collect receipts.** Pay specific gifts first, then the rest, and ask each beneficiary to sign a receipt when they get their check. Signed receipts are what make the closing short.

## How do you close the estate and get discharged?

17. **File the closing report and wait 42 days.** An independent executor files a verified report, mails copies within 14 days to anyone who has not signed a receipt or waiver, and asks for discharge once 42 days pass with no objection (755 ILCS 5/28-11). A supervised executor files accounts with the court instead (755 ILCS 5/24-1). [Closing an Illinois probate estate](/learn/illinois/closing-an-illinois-probate-estate-final-account-and-discharge) covers both, and [reopening a closed estate](/learn/illinois/reopening-a-closed-illinois-estate) covers the asset that turns up afterward.

## How long does an Illinois executor have for each step?

Some steps have a statutory deadline; others, like a house sale, take as long as they take. These are the dates you can calendar on day one.

| Job | Deadline or wait | Source |
|---|---|---|
| File the original will | Immediately after death | 755 ILCS 5/6-1 |
| Start probate or decline | 30 days after learning you were named | 755 ILCS 5/6-3 |
| Mail notice to heirs and legatees | 14 days after the order admitting the will | 755 ILCS 5/6-10 |
| Inventory (supervised estate) | Generally 60 days after letters | 755 ILCS 5/14-1 |
| Creditor bar date | Later of 6 months from first publication or 3 months from mailing | 755 ILCS 5/18-3 |
| Will contest window | 6 months after admission | 755 ILCS 5/8-1 |
| Illinois estate tax return, if required | 9 months after death | [Illinois estate tax](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax) |
| Wait before discharge (independent) | 42 days after the closing report is filed | 755 ILCS 5/28-11 |

The full calendar view, including what usually slows things down, is on [how long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois) and [what slows down Illinois probate](/learn/illinois/what-slows-down-illinois-probate). The free [Illinois executor duties checklist](/free/illinois-executor-duties-checklist) puts these dates on a printable page with boxes to tick.

## Does an Illinois executor have to hire a lawyer?

Not by statute in every case, but in practice most do for the court filings. The Cook County clerk tells filers they need an attorney to open a probate estate there. Many executors split the work: the attorney handles the petition, notices and closing report, and the executor handles the house, bills and records. Reasonable fees come out of the estate. [Handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) shows when going alone is realistic, and [hiring help and staying protected](/learn/after-a-death/executor-hiring-help-and-staying-protected) covers accountants and appraisers. You may also be paid for your own time; see [how much an executor gets paid in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-much-does-an-executor-get-paid-in-illinois).

## What does the job list look like in a real case?

Example. Teresa, 52, is named executor in her father Leon's will. Leon owned a house in his own name, a checking account and a small brokerage account, and left everything equally to Teresa and her brother Omar.

- Week one: Teresa pays the funeral home from her own card and keeps the receipt. She orders ten death certificates and takes the original will to the circuit clerk.
- Week three: she files the petition, asking for independent administration. The court admits the will on February 3 and issues letters.
- February 10: she mails notice to Omar and files proof of mailing. She gets an EIN and opens an estate account.
- February 20: the claims notice first runs in the newspaper. She mails a copy to the hospital and two card companies she found in Leon's mail. The bar date in the notice is August 21.
- March: she finishes the inventory, emails it to Omar and repays herself for the funeral.
- April to July: she pays the hospital, disallows one card balance that Leon had already paid off, lists and sells the house, and files Leon's final income tax return.
- August: the contest window closes on August 3 and the bar date passes. In early September she pays Omar his half and he signs a receipt.
- September 30: she files the closing report. Because Omar has already signed, the 42 days run quietly, and the court discharges her in mid-November.

Nothing in Teresa's case needed a judge after letters issued. A dated log and a receipt for every payment kept it smooth.
