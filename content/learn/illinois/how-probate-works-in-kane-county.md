When a parent or spouse who lived in St. Charles, Geneva, Aurora, Elgin or Batavia dies, the first week is usually a blur of phone calls. Sooner or later someone asks where the will goes and which courthouse to drive to. Kane County is the 16th Judicial Circuit, and it spreads its work across several buildings, so the answer is not obvious.

This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). It follows a Fox Valley family through the first steps: sorting out whether a case is needed, finding the right building, handing over the original will, filing, getting help and tracking the case online. If you want to talk through your own situation, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-kane-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## What should a Kane County family do first?

Start by finding the will and learning what the person owned. Do not begin with the courthouse. Look through the home, the desk, a safe deposit box and the files of any attorney who drafted a will (see [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois)). Then list the accounts, the house and any beneficiary forms.

That list answers the big question, which is whether a court case is needed at all. Accounts with a named beneficiary and property held jointly pass outside the court. A modest estate with no real estate may qualify for the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit). A house titled only in the deceased person's name usually means probate. The [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter) can help you sort the list.

Order certified death certificates early; see [who can order Illinois death certificates](/learn/illinois/illinois-death-certificates-who-can-order-county-clerk-or-idph).

## Does the estate belong in Kane County?

It does if the person lived here at death. Illinois ties probate to the county of residence (755 ILCS 5/5-1, discussed in [which county handles probate when property is in more than one county](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties)). Aurora and Elgin both reach into other counties, so look at the county of the home address, not the mailing city. A cabin or rental in another Illinois county does not require a second case.

## Which Kane County building handles probate?

The Illinois Courts directory lists the Kane County Courthouse, 100 S. Third Street, Geneva, as hearing most civil matters, including probate. The directory gives the courthouse phone as (630) 208-5145 and hours of 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and lists court administration in Room 360.

Other 16th Circuit buildings can confuse a family, so here is how the court's address page and directory lay them out:

| Location | Address | Why a probate family might go there |
|---|---|---|
| Kane County Courthouse | 100 S. Third Street, Geneva | Listed by the state directory as hearing probate; court administration in Room 360 |
| Kane County Judicial Center | 37W777 Route 38, St. Charles | Second-floor Law Library and Self Help Legal Center |
| Kane County Branch Court | 530 S. Randall Road, St. Charles | A branch court; the directory says branch courts handle mainly traffic and ordinance cases |
| Aurora Branch Court | 1200 East Indian Trail Road, Aurora | Branch court; the clerk lists a satellite window here |
| Elgin Branch Court | 150 Dexter Court, Elgin | Branch court |

The pages we reviewed do not name a floor or room for a probate courtroom, so your hearing notice is the place to find it.

## Where is the Circuit Clerk, and how is the original will filed?

The Clerk of the Circuit Court lists its main office at 540 South Randall Road, St. Charles, phone 630-232-3413, open Monday to Friday from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The clerk also lists satellite windows at the Aurora Branch Court, the Judicial Center and the Courthouse, each with a midday break, and says the Aurora window closes at 3 p.m. Because hours differ, call before you drive.

The clerk's e-filing FAQ is direct about the will: wills are exempt from e-filing. You file the original at a Circuit Clerk's office and get a certified copy. You then include that certified copy with the petition you file through eFileIL. The statute behind the filing duty is 755 ILCS 5/6-1, which asks the person holding the will to file it right after the death; our page on [filing the original will with the circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) covers it.

Practical steps:

1. Keep the original flat and unstapled, and make a color copy first.
2. Bring a death certificate if you have one.
3. Ask the clerk for a certified copy of the will.

Filing the will does not by itself start the case. A petition has to ask the court to admit it.

## How do filings work for the rest of the case?

After the will is filed, everything goes through eFileIL, the statewide system. The clerk's e-filing page says attorneys must register their ARDC number with the clerk, that envelopes are generally processed within three business days in the order received, and that supporting documents should sit behind the petition as one PDF. It also says to contact Scheduling at 630-232-3413 before filing if you need a court date.

The clerk posts probate forms online. The Kane County form for a petition for probate of will and letters testamentary is numbered P1-PR-001a. It asks for the date and place of death, the date of the will, a rough value of the estate, a list of heirs and legatees, the nominated executor, and whether any heir is a minor or disabled person. [How to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) explains the order of events, and [the petition for letters of office](/learn/illinois/illinois-petition-for-letters-of-office) walks through each line.

Here is the rest of the road, with the state rules the Probate Act sets (the citations appear on our [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) page):

| Stage | What happens | Illinois rule |
|---|---|---|
| Executor decides | Serve or decline | 30 days after learning of the nomination (755 ILCS 5/6-3) |
| Order and letters | Judge admits the will and the clerk issues letters | Independent administration requested under 755 ILCS 5/28-2 |
| Notice to heirs | Mailed notice | Within 14 days after the order (755 ILCS 5/6-10) |
| Creditor claims | Newspaper notice and mailing | Later of 6 months from first publication or 3 months from mailing (755 ILCS 5/18-3) |
| Will contest window | Heirs may challenge the will | 6 months after admission (755 ILCS 5/8-1) |
| Closing | Closing report, then discharge | 42 days after the report in an independent estate (755 ILCS 5/28-11) |

## Do I need a lawyer in Kane County?

The pages we reviewed from the clerk and the court do not say a lawyer is required, and Illinois does not bar someone from representing themselves in a simple estate. Still, the work is technical. Our page on [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) says when families get stuck, and [do I need a lawyer for a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/do-i-need-a-lawyer-for-a-will-in-illinois) is a related read.

## What free or low-cost help does the Kane County court point to?

The 16th Circuit's website links three starting points. The Kane County Law Library and Self Help Legal Center is on the second floor of the Judicial Center, 37W777 Route 38, St. Charles. Its posted hours are Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and Wednesday from 8:30 a.m. to 7 p.m. The library's phone is 630-406-7126. The center says staff can help with Zoom court, e-filing, research, forms and general procedural questions.

The court's site also links Illinois Legal Aid Online and the state's approved standardized forms at illinoiscourts.gov. For the court's own fees, see [the Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver).

If you file as a self-represented person, the clerk says e-filing fees are paid by card or electronic check, and that someone with no computer or internet can ask the clerk for a Certification for Exemption From E-Filing when filing in person.

## Can I attend a Kane County hearing by Zoom?

Sometimes. The 16th Circuit's site points to a Zoom Court streaming directory at kanecourt.org, where you pick the courtroom and join the hearing. The court's partner site also offers hearing reminders and a Find My Court Date search. That search looks only 30 days ahead, and the site says schedules change often. Your notice is the source of truth.

## How do I look up a Kane County probate case?

Use the Circuit Clerk's Online Court Records portal, reached from the clerk's eServices page. It offers a Smart Search by name or case number. For a name, enter the last name, then the first. For a case number, start with the four-digit year, then the case type, then the remaining digits. The clerk notes that the system is party-based and that a name spelled differently from the one in the system will not appear.

Try the name as it would appear on a petition, including middle names or a spelling variant. If you are an heir and are unsure whether an estate was ever opened, see [how to find out if you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois). The clerk's phone line, 630-232-3413, is the fallback for a case you cannot find.

## Example: Priya and her mother's house in Batavia

This is an invented example. Priya, 49, lives in Chicago. Her mother, Lakshmi, lived in Batavia and died in October. Lakshmi's will names Priya as executor and leaves the house and a bank account equally to Priya and her sister Anjali. The house is in Lakshmi's name alone.

Priya first searches for a Batavia courthouse and finds nothing useful. A call to the clerk's line tells her the original will is filed at a clerk's office, not e-filed, so she drives to the St. Charles office with the will unstapled and a color copy at home. She asks for a certified copy and keeps the receipt.

She books an attorney who e-files the petition through eFileIL, attaches the certified copy and asks for independent administration. The judge admits the will at a hearing that Anjali attends by Zoom from Arizona. The clerk issues letters of office. The attorney mails notice to Anjali within the 14 days and publishes the creditor notice.

Priya searches the portal using Lakshmi's last name first and finds nothing, because the case lists the full name differently. A second try with the spelling from the petition works. The house sells in month five. After the claim and contest periods pass, the attorney files the closing report, and Priya is discharged roughly nine months after the death. Priya used the [Kane County probate guide](/free/kane-county-probate-guide) to track each deadline.
