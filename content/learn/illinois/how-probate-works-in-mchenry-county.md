If a parent or spouse lived in Woodstock, Crystal Lake, McHenry, Algonquin or Huntley, the paperwork after the death points to one place: the McHenry County courthouse in Woodstock. McHenry County is the 22nd Judicial Circuit, and the Circuit Clerk keeps the probate files.

This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). It covers where to go, how an estate opens, how the original will is filed, remote hearings, self-help, case lookup and what a family farm changes. For your own facts, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-mchenry-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Does this estate belong in McHenry County?

It does if the person lived here when they died. Illinois ties probate to the county of residence (755 ILCS 5/5-1; see [which county handles probate when property is in more than one county](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties)). The clerk's probate page also says the person must have been a McHenry County resident.

A farm or lake cabin in another county does not move the case. If the person lived in Lake or Kane County but owned land here, the case usually belongs in the county of residence, and our page on [how probate works in Cook County](/learn/illinois/how-probate-works-in-cook-county) shows how another large county handles it.

## Where is the probate counter at the Woodstock courthouse?

The Michael J. Sullivan Judicial Center is at 2200 North Seminary Avenue, Woodstock, IL 60098. The county lists its main phone as 815-334-4000. The Circuit Clerk's pages give the same address and list Rooms 136 and 353 for the clerk.

The county's pages use both "Government Center" and "Judicial Center" for the campus, which confuses visitors. The Illinois Courts directory lists the building as the 22nd Circuit's McHenry County Courthouse, with hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and free parking lots on the east and west sides.

| What you need | Where the official pages place it |
|---|---|
| Will filing and probate forms | Room 136, first floor, civil customer service windows |
| Public case terminals | Rooms 136 and 353 |
| Civil and probate courtroom | Courtroom 357 |
| Legal Self-Help Center | Room 346 |
| Law Library | Room 344 |

The county's probate page and the clerk's probate page do not agree on one room for forms, and the Illinois Courts directory and the clerk list different self-help hours. Phone before you drive, and ask which window serves you that day.

## How does an estate open, and how is the original will filed?

Start with the will. The clerk's page says anyone holding a will must file it promptly, within 30 days after the date of death. It cannot be filed before death, and there is no filing fee. The Circuit Clerk keeps the original. You can buy certified copies after filing. Our page on [filing the original will with the circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) explains the statute, 755 ILCS 5/6-1.

Wills are the exception to e-filing. The clerk says wills are not accepted electronically and must be filed at the civil customer service windows in Room 136. Bring the original flat and unstapled, and keep a copy for yourself.

The petition can come next, or at the same time. The clerk says the petition and original will can be filed together, and the clerk keeps both. The petition asks the court to admit the will and appoint the executor. For the full sequence, see [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois).

The clerk posts probate forms in groups: probate with a will, probate without a will, closing an estate and not probating the estate. The with-will set includes a petition for probate of will and letters testamentary, an order admitting the will, and an oath and bond form without surety.

## Must everything else be filed electronically?

Mostly, yes. The clerk's page says all probate filings must be done electronically through its E-File Documents service, unless there is good cause not to. The will is the exception described above.

A person who cannot e-file can ask for an exemption. The clerk's page points to Illinois Legal Aid Online for the certification for exemption from e-filing. The Illinois Courts directory also lists an e-filing kiosk at the clerk's office, on the third and first floors.

Whoever signs the petition should read it first. The [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) page lists what the court expects before it issues letters of office.

## Can a McHenry County probate hearing be attended remotely?

Sometimes. The clerk's remote appearance page says judges of the 22nd Circuit may hold proceedings in a virtual courtroom on Zoom or another platform. It lists Courtroom 357, labeled civil and probate, with remote appearances for some matters under Local Court Rule 22.

The page gives a check-in routine:

1. Find your courtroom, or use the court's remote check-in page.
2. Search by case number, or by last name, then first name.
3. Join by the Zoom app on a phone or computer, or by phone.
4. Wait in the virtual waiting room until the court admits you.
5. Stay muted until your case is called.

The page also asks you to dress as you would in person and choose a quiet place. It gives no separate instructions for probate, so your hearing notice and the attorney handling the case control which format applies. Our page on [handling a probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) covers what a self-represented executor faces.

## Where can a family get help with forms?

The Legal Self-Help Center is in Room 346 of the Judicial Center. Its page lists 815-334-0317 and says it is a project of the 22nd Circuit with Prairie State Legal Aid. It offers procedural information, computer access and links to court forms.

Staff cannot give legal advice, and the page says self-represented people must follow the same rules as attorneys. The Law Library, Room 344, also offers online forms.

For a small estate, the clerk's page says estates with no real estate or debts or claims, and worth $150,000 or less, may avoid probate court, and it lists a small estate affidavit. See our guide to the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) before relying on the form.

## How do I look up a McHenry County case?

Use the county's online case information site, linked from the clerk's pages. The clerk's Zoom check-in search takes a case number or a name entered as last name, then first name. A misspelled name can hide a real case.

For documents, public viewing terminals in Rooms 136 and 353 give access to the clerk's court database and document imaging database. The Self-Represented Litigant Online Access Portal is another route the clerk lists. We could not confirm from the official pages what each tool shows for probate files, so ask the clerk.

## What changes when the estate includes a family farm?

Rural McHenry County has many acreage parcels, and they raise a problem a house usually does not: several children inheriting one piece of land. This section states Illinois law generally.

Land titled only in the parent's name is normally an estate asset. The executor controls it during the case, and the children do not own it yet. Land held in a land trust, a living trust or a transfer on death arrangement may avoid court, depending on the documents. Our page on [removing a deceased owner from a deed](/learn/illinois/how-to-remove-a-deceased-owner-from-a-deed-in-illinois) explains which deeds work that way.

Once the estate distributes the land, children commonly hold it together. Each owns a share of the whole. One child cannot sell or lease the entire parcel alone, so disagreements about farming, renting or selling become title problems. The [siblings disagree about an inherited house](/learn/illinois/siblings-disagree-inherited-house) ladder, from talking to buyout to partition, applies to acreage too.

A farm lease generally continues after the owner dies, so the estate or heirs become the landlord. See [Illinois farm estate tax and cash rent leases](/learn/illinois/illinois-farm-estate-tax-and-cash-rent-leases) for the lease and the state estate tax threshold.

## Example: the Whitaker parcel near Huntley

This is an invented example. Dolores Whitaker, a widow, owned 40 acres near Huntley in her own name. Her daughter Anna farmed it under a handshake rent arrangement. Her sons Paul and Jonah lived in Chicago and Denver. Dolores left a will dividing everything equally.

Paul found the original will in a desk the day after the funeral. He took it to Room 136 in Woodstock that week, well inside the 30 days, and asked for certified copies. He did not try to e-file it, since wills go to the windows.

The family hired an attorney, who e-filed the petition. Anna worried that the court case would end her farming. It did not, because the executor could keep the arrangement in place and formalize it in writing. The harder question came after distribution: three siblings now owned thirds of one parcel.

They agreed Anna would buy out her brothers over time, with the land valued before closing. Without agreement, any co-owner could have asked a court for partition, which is slower and costlier than a negotiated buyout. The example shows why the title plan matters as much as the filing.
