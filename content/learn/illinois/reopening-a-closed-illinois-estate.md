The estate closed last spring, and the executor has the discharge order. Then a letter arrives. An insurance refund, a bank account nobody knew about, or a deed to a lot in another county. The estate is closed, yet the asset is real. What now?

This page covers the Illinois choices. It is a companion to our general piece on [reopening a closed probate estate](/learn/probate/can-you-reopen-a-closed-probate-estate), and it picks up where [closing an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/closing-an-illinois-probate-estate-final-account-and-discharge) ends. For more Illinois pages, see the [Illinois guide](/learn/illinois).

## Why does the executor need new authority?

Letters of office end when the court discharges the executor. After that, the former executor cannot sign for the estate, and a bank or title company will not release property to them as executor. The asset still belongs to the estate and goes to the people entitled to it under the will or by intestacy. The task is to find a lawful way to collect it.

## Which route fits which asset?

| New asset | Likely route | Court needed? |
|---|---|---|
| Illinois unclaimed property such as an old bank balance | Claim through the Illinois Treasurer's I-Cash site | Often no |
| Uncashed check or small refund | Ask the issuer what it needs; may accept an affidavit | Often no |
| Personal property within the statutory limit | Small estate affidavit (755 ILCS 5/25-1) | No, if the conditions are met |
| Large bank or brokerage account | Petition for a representative and new letters | Yes |
| Real estate in the decedent's name | Petition to reopen or for new letters, then deed | Yes |
| Lawsuit or insurance claim | New representative with limited letters | Yes |

## When can you skip reopening?

Start by asking whether the holder will accept something simpler. Illinois unclaimed property can often be claimed directly. Our guide on [finding unclaimed property for an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/finding-unclaimed-property-for-an-illinois-estate) shows what proof the Treasurer wants. For other small personal property, the [small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) can work if no letters are outstanding and no petition is pending, and if the holder accepts it. The person who signs must pay valid claims first. Test the numbers with the [small estate checker](/tools/small-estate-checker).

Some holders reject an affidavit for property that came to light after a discharge. If that happens, move to the court route rather than arguing at the counter.

## What are the steps to reopen or get a new representative?

1. Get paperwork from the holder that shows what the asset is, whose name it is in and how much it is worth.
2. Collect the original case number, the discharge order and the final report.
3. Ask the clerk what filing is correct: a petition to reopen, for supplemental administration or for new letters. Practice differs by county.
4. List who is entitled to the asset under the will or intestacy. If there was no will, the intestacy statute decides.
5. File the petition describing the asset, the original case and who should serve.
6. Give notice to the people affected, as the court directs.
7. Receive new letters, often limited to the new asset.
8. Collect the asset, pay any costs and divide it.
9. File a short report so the court can close the matter again.

## What about real estate that turns up?

Land is the tricky case. A deed in the decedent's name that was missed needs a representative or a proof of heirship before it can be sold or transferred. Our article on [proving heirship in Illinois](/learn/illinois/proving-heirship-in-illinois) explains the heirship route. For a sale, a title company will want letters, so the court route is common.

## Who gets the new asset, and what about shares already paid?

The new asset is divided as the will or the intestacy statute directs. Heirs who already received their shares generally keep them. If the will gave a fraction of the residue, each person gets that fraction of the new asset. A specific gift that has already been paid is not affected. If the asset is small, the cost of reopening may be larger than the asset, and heirs can agree in writing to a practical split.

## What about creditors?

Creditor claims are barred two years after death regardless of notice (755 ILCS 5/18-12), and the claim period for the original estate has run out. A late-found asset does not by itself revive stale claims, though a tax claim or a creditor who was not given required notice may have different rights. Do not pay out freely if a bill arrives. Talk to an attorney.

## What if there are tax consequences?

A refund or income that arrives after closing can mean a late estate return. Check the estate's income tax filing status. See [final tax returns](/learn/after-a-death/final-tax-returns) and the IL-1041 guide for estates.

## Example: two finds in one family

Marguerite, 71, is the former executor of her sister Odile's estate, closed in March. In September she finds two things. A $2,150 insurance refund check is made out to Odile's estate. She sends the insurer the discharge order and a small estate affidavit, and it pays. The second is a deed to a $48,000 vacant lot in Kankakee County that Odile inherited years ago and never recorded. A title company will not close without letters, so Marguerite's lawyer files a petition, the court issues limited letters, and the lot is sold. The estate pays about $1,600 in court and legal costs. See the [Kankakee County page](/estate-planning/illinois/counties/kankakee) for local details.

## How do you keep the cost in line with the asset?

Before you file anything, weigh the asset against the cost. A $400 refund is not worth a court petition, and heirs can often agree in writing on who receives it and have one person collect it if the payer will accept that. A $48,000 lot easily justifies a filing. In the middle, ask the holder what it will accept, ask the clerk what the fee would be and ask an attorney for a rough estimate before you commit. Put the decision and the reason in writing and send it to the heirs, so nobody wonders later why a small item was handled informally.

## What should you keep from the closed estate?

Keep the final report, the discharge order, the receipts signed by heirs and the case number for at least several years. They tell a bank, the Treasurer or a title company that the estate was properly closed and who the heirs are, which speeds up any later claim. If the original executor has died or moved away, the heirs can ask the clerk for copies from the court file. A tidy closing file turns a surprise asset into a short errand instead of a long search.
