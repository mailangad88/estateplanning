A parent died, and you are named in the trust as the one in charge. There is no judge, no court date and no one telling you what to do next. That freedom is the hard part, because the law still expects you to act carefully from the first day.

This page puts the first 90 days into a calendar. For the long view of the job, read [trust administration](/learn/after-a-death/trust-administration). For the notice itself, read what an [Illinois trustee must tell beneficiaries](/learn/illinois/illinois-trustee-duty-to-inform-beneficiaries). Our [Illinois guide](/learn/illinois) links the other pages.

## What deadlines apply in the first 90 days?

Only two are firm and both are short. A will must be filed with the circuit clerk within 30 days after death (755 ILCS 5/6-1), and the trustee must send the notice to qualified beneficiaries within 90 days after the trust becomes irrevocable or the trustee accepts (760 ILCS 3/813.1). Everything else is a good-practice target.

| Item | Source | Deadline type | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| File any original will with the clerk | 755 ILCS 5/6-1 | Statute | Within 30 days of death |
| Written notice to qualified beneficiaries | 760 ILCS 3/813.1 | Statute | Within 90 days |
| Order death certificates | Practice | Soft | Week 1 |
| Get an EIN and open a trust account | IRS and bank practice | Soft | Weeks 2 to 3 |
| 65-day election for the first year | Internal Revenue Code | Tax | 65 days after year-end |
| Illinois estate tax return, if over $4 million | Illinois estate tax law | Tax | Nine months after death |

Do not treat the soft targets as optional. Banks, insurers and tax offices all work faster when you arrive with a tax ID and a certification of trust.

## What should happen in days 1 to 7?

Secure what exists and gather proof. Tasks for the first week:

1. Read the trust, any amendments and the will. Note who the successor trustee, the beneficiaries and any co-trustee are.
2. Order certified death certificates. Our page on [how many you need](/learn/after-a-death/how-many-death-certificates-do-i-need) gives a sensible count.
3. Lock the house, collect mail and call the homeowners insurer, because many policies limit coverage when a home sits empty.
4. Find original documents, including the will, deeds and insurance policies.
5. Confirm in writing that you accept the role and the date you did so.

## What should happen in days 8 to 30?

Build the paperwork the trustee needs to deal with banks. Request an EIN from the IRS, because the trust becomes its own taxpayer after death. Our page on the [trust EIN and the 65-day election](/learn/after-a-death/trust-ein-and-65-day-election) explains the choices. Open an account in the trust's name and route every trust receipt and payment through it.

Sign a [certification of trust](/learn/trusts/what-is-a-certification-of-trust) so you do not have to show the whole document. The Trust Code protects a person who relies on one without actual knowledge it is wrong (760 ILCS 3/1013). If the decedent left a will, file the original with the circuit clerk in the right county before day 30, even if the will only pours into the trust. Our page on [the Illinois executor's role](/learn/illinois/who-can-be-an-executor-in-illinois) explains who handles probate if it is needed.

## What should happen in days 31 to 60?

Take inventory. List every asset with its value on the date of death, because heirs who sell later will use that value as their tax basis. Check each asset's title to see whether it is in the trust, in the decedent's name or passes by beneficiary form. Our article on [what happens when a trust was never funded](/learn/illinois/unfunded-living-trust-in-illinois) shows the options for gaps.

Gather the debts you can find: funeral bills, credit cards, taxes and any Medicaid claim. Keep a reserve in the trust account until you know what is owed. Our pages on [credit card debt after death](/learn/illinois/credit-card-debt-after-death-in-illinois) and [Illinois Medicaid estate recovery](/learn/illinois/illinois-medicaid-estate-recovery) cover the two debts families find most confusing. Check whether the estate could owe [Illinois estate tax](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax), which starts above $4 million.

## What should happen in days 61 to 90?

Send the notice and settle the plan for the first year. The written notice must tell each qualified beneficiary that the trust exists, that the beneficiary may request a copy of the instrument and whether they may receive accountings (760 ILCS 3/813.1). Send it by a method you can prove, such as certified mail, and keep a copy. Choose a tax year and decide whether to make the 65-day election on distributions. Learn the IL-1041 requirements on our [estate and trust income tax page](/learn/illinois/illinois-form-il-1041-estate-and-trust-income-tax-return). Talk with an attorney before your first distribution.

## What should a trustee not do in the first 90 days?

Do not distribute, sell or take pay before the inventory. Specific mistakes to avoid:

- Paying one beneficiary early, which can leave nothing for a later tax bill.
- Mixing trust money with your own accounts.
- Selling a house before you know what the trust says about it.
- Ignoring a sibling's written request for the trust copy.

A trustee who gets these wrong can be personally liable. Our article on [when a trustee can be personally liable](/learn/after-a-death/can-a-trustee-be-personally-liable) gives examples.

## Example: Dana's first three months

Dana, 52, becomes trustee of her father's trust on March 3. She orders eight death certificates, files his pour-over will with the [DuPage County](/estate-planning/illinois/counties/dupage) clerk on March 20, gets an EIN on March 24 and opens a trust account on March 28. On April 25 she finishes an inventory showing $410,000 in the trust and $52,000 owed. She mails the 813.1 notice to her two brothers on May 15, day 73, and keeps the green cards. She makes no distribution until the bills are paid in July.

## When should the trustee call an attorney?

Call early if any of these apply: the trust is large, there is a business or farm, beneficiaries disagree, a beneficiary has a disability or receives public benefits, or the trust is silent on a key point. A short meeting in week two costs less than undoing a wrong distribution. A trustee can also hire help and charge reasonable costs to the trust. Our article on [whether you need an attorney to settle a trust](/learn/after-a-death/do-you-need-an-attorney-to-settle-a-trust) lists the common triggers, and the page on [trustee pay](/learn/after-a-death/trustee-compensation) explains what a trustee may charge.
