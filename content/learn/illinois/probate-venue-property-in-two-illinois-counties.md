Many Illinois families own more than a house. There is the farm two counties away, the lake cabin, the rental flat in the city and the family home downstate. When the owner dies, the first thing the executor asks is whether that means two court cases. Usually it does not.

This page explains how Illinois chooses the county, what to do with land in other counties and how a non-resident decedent changes the answer. For the opening steps, see [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois). The broader Illinois picture is in our [Illinois guide](/learn/illinois).

## Which county gets the case?

The proper court is set by the venue section of the Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/5-1). The rule of thumb is simple for an Illinois resident. The estate is opened in the circuit court of the county where the person lived at death. That is called residence or domicile. It is not the county of the executor, the county of the house they were selling or the county of the hospital.

For a non-resident who left property in Illinois, the case goes to a county where that property is located. If property sits in several Illinois counties, the statute gives a way to pick among them, so ask the clerk before you file. Our statewide article on [which county to file probate in](/learn/probate/which-county-do-you-file-probate-in) compares the rules with New Jersey.

| Situation | Where probate is filed | Second Illinois case? |
|---|---|---|
| Lived in Lake County, owned land in Kane County | Lake County | No |
| Lived in an Illinois nursing home in Will County, home was in Cook County | Usually the county of residence; confirm with the clerk | No |
| Lived in Texas, owned a cabin in Jo Daviess County | A county where the Illinois property sits | Texas may need its own case |
| Lived in Illinois, owned land in Wisconsin | Illinois county of residence | Wisconsin proceeding for the land |

## How is residence decided?

Residence means where the person actually made their home and meant to stay. Look at where they paid property tax, voted, held a driver's license and received medical care. A short stay in a nursing home, a hospital or a relative's house does not automatically change it. If a person had two homes, the one they treated as their main home counts, and the death certificate address is a helpful clue though not final.

## What do you do with land in another Illinois county?

You use the letters from the home county. The steps look like this.

1. Open the case in the county of residence and receive certified letters.
2. Ask the clerk for several certified copies, and refresh them if they get old.
3. For a sale, give the title company the letters and the order granting independent administration. See [selling a house in probate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/selling-a-house-in-probate-in-illinois).
4. Record the deed in the county where the land sits. The recorder's office in that county takes the filing. See, for example, the [Kane County page](/estate-planning/illinois/counties/kane) for a county recorder's address.
5. If the land passes to heirs rather than being sold, record the proof of the heirs' title in that county as the title company directs.
6. Report the property on the inventory. See [the Illinois probate inventory](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-inventory).

## What if the property is in another state?

An Illinois judge has no power over land in Wisconsin or Florida. The executor uses Illinois letters, and the other state may require its own proceeding, usually called ancillary probate. Our guide to [ancillary probate](/learn/probate/ancillary-probate) explains the idea, and the Illinois-specific article on [Illinois residents who own property in another state](/learn/illinois/illinois-resident-owning-property-in-another-state) covers how to plan around it.

## What if the person lived outside Illinois?

Then the Illinois case is secondary in many families' minds, but it is still needed for Illinois real estate. File where the property is. The home state of the decedent normally opens the main estate, and the Illinois proceeding is limited. Because the rules cross state lines, ask an attorney licensed in both places to coordinate the filings and the notices.

## What can go wrong?

Bernard, 72, lived in Joliet and owned a farm in Livingston County worth $540,000. His daughter Carol thought she had to open a case in Livingston because that was where the land was. She nearly did, then called the Will County clerk and learned that his estate belonged in Will County. She filed there, received letters, and brought certified copies to the Livingston County recorder when she sold the farm. One case, one filing fee, one set of notices. Filing the second case would have cost the fee again and caused a conflict.

The reverse mistake also happens. An executor who lives in Chicago files in Cook County because it is convenient, even though the parent lived in Sangamon County. The clerk may reject the filing or the court may transfer it, and the notices that went out could need to be sent again. See the [Sangamon County page](/estate-planning/illinois/counties/sangamon) for the county's verified details.

## How do you choose the county in practice?

1. Find the address on the death certificate.
2. Confirm it against tax bills and a driver's license.
3. Call the clerk of that county and ask whether the case belongs there.
4. If the person lived out of state, list the Illinois properties and counties.
5. Ask an attorney if the facts are mixed.

The [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter) can help you list the property before you call.

## What do title companies and banks ask for?

Out-of-county land is where the paperwork shows. A title company will usually want a certified copy of the letters, often dated within the last 60 to 90 days, a copy of the will or the order admitting it, and proof that independent administration was granted. A bank in another county will want the same letters and an EIN. Order four to six certified copies at the start and refresh them if the sale drags on. If the land passes to heirs without a sale, ask the title company what it needs before you spend money on a deed.

Keep one list of every parcel with its county, parcel number and tax bill. That list becomes part of the inventory and prevents a forgotten parcel from reopening the estate later.
