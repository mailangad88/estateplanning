Your father died in March. In April of the next year, an IL-1040 is due, and he is not around to sign it. The Illinois Department of Revenue treats the final return like any other, with a few extra lines to show that the filer has died and who is filing for him.

This page covers the Illinois side: who files, what goes on the return, how a refund reaches the right person, and the traps. The federal side, including Form 1041 and Form 706, is on our general page on [final tax returns after a death](/learn/after-a-death/final-tax-returns). The broader Illinois process is in our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). Confirm current forms and thresholds on tax.illinois.gov before you file.

## Do you have to file a final Illinois income tax return for someone who died?

You do if the person would have had to file had they lived. The final return covers the year of death, January 1 through the date of death. Income earned before death goes on it: wages, interest, a pension payment received that year, dividends and capital gains. Income that comes in after death belongs to the estate or to the heir who receives it, and it is reported separately.

The Illinois return is built on the federal one. You normally prepare the federal Form 1040 first, because Illinois starts from the federal figures and then adds and subtracts items. Do both together. Doing the state one first leads to mistakes.

## Who signs and files the Illinois return?

The person who handles the estate. That is the executor if a will names one, or the administrator the court appoints. If no one has been appointed, a surviving spouse can generally file jointly for the year. Where no spouse or court appointee exists, an heir or relative who takes responsibility may file, and the state may ask for more proof when money is due back.

| Situation | Who files |
|---|---|
| Married at death, spouse survives | The spouse, usually a joint return |
| Executor named in will, letters issued | The executor |
| Estate opened without a will | The administrator |
| No court case, small estate | The person who signs the small estate affidavit, with care |

The right title on the return matters. The instructions for the current-year IL-1040 explain how to show that the taxpayer has died and who is signing. Sign in the capacity you actually hold, never in the dead person's name alone. If you are still waiting for court papers, our page on [letters of administration in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-letters-of-administration-and-the-surety-bond) says how long that can take.

## How do you claim an Illinois refund owed to a deceased taxpayer?

File the return as usual. If a refund is due and you are not the surviving spouse on a joint return, expect to attach proof that you may receive it. That may be certified letters of office, or a form the state publishes for a person claiming a refund due a deceased taxpayer. The federal counterpart is Form 1310, as the IRS describes it. Check the Illinois Department of Revenue instructions for the matching state form and for what to attach.

Here is an example with invented numbers. Marguerite, 71, died on June 3. Her employer withheld Illinois income tax on her paycheck through that date, and her estimated tax was low. Once her son Paul, as executor, files the final IL-1040 and the federal return, the state calculates a refund of $412. Paul attaches a copy of his letters of office. The refund check is made to the estate, not to Paul personally, and he deposits it into the estate account. Paul does not keep it. It is divided under the will.

The estate account matters here. If there is no account yet, open one with the estate's EIN. See our page on [IRS paperwork for an executor](/learn/after-a-death/irs-paperwork-for-an-executor-form-56-estate-ein-tax-transcripts-and-form-4810).

## What does the estate owe after the person dies?

Tax on the final year is a debt of the estate. If the executor distributes the estate first and finds a bill later, the executor can be personally responsible. So treat the return as part of the estate's debts, along with other creditors. Our guide to [how creditor claims work in an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/how-creditor-claims-work-in-an-illinois-estate) covers that order, and the page on [dealing with debts in probate](/learn/probate/dealing-with-debts-in-probate) gives the broader picture.

The estate itself may also have to file. Income that arrives after death, such as interest on an estate bank account or rent from a house held during probate, can create an Illinois fiduciary income tax return in addition to the federal one. The IRS filing threshold for the federal estate return, as our site states, is gross income of $600 or more in a tax year. Illinois has its own rules, so check them with a tax preparer.

Illinois also has its own estate tax, separate from income tax. If the estate is large, read our page on the [Illinois estate tax](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax). Most families are below it, and it has nothing to do with the final income tax return.

## What if the person who died owed back taxes or skipped years?

Start by pulling transcripts. Our page on IRS paperwork for an executor, linked above, explains how to request them with Form 56 in place. The IRS record shows which federal years were filed and what was reported to it by employers and banks. Illinois can then be matched against the same years.

File the oldest missing year first, and be honest about the figures. Penalties and interest keep running on the unpaid amount, and the estate bears them, which means the heirs do. If the estate cannot cover everything, the order of payment among creditors becomes the issue, and the page on [what happens when an estate owes more than it has](/learn/probate/what-happens-when-an-estate-owes-more-than-it-has) is the place to read next. Do not pay a family member or a credit card first and leave the tax unpaid.

One more practical point. If the final return shows a balance, pay it from the estate account, never from your own pocket without a record. Keep the canceled check with the file. The closing report in a probate case lists what the estate paid, so a clean paper trail makes [closing the estate](/learn/illinois/closing-an-illinois-probate-estate-final-account-and-discharge) much easier.

## What should you gather before you file?

1. A certified death certificate. See our guide to [Illinois death certificates](/learn/illinois/illinois-death-certificates-who-can-order-county-clerk-or-idph).
2. The last two or three years of returns, so you can match items.
3. W-2s, 1099s and brokerage statements for the year of death. Some arrive late, so ask for them early.
4. Statements for medical bills, which can be deducted on the final return in some cases.
5. Your letters of office, or the small estate affidavit.
6. The estate's EIN if one has been issued.

A word of advice from the people who do this work. Do not file in a rush to get it off your desk. A wrong return is slower to fix than a late one. If the person who died had a brokerage account, wait for the corrected 1099 forms, which can come in February or March.
