If your mother or father lived in DeKalb County, Illinois, in Sycamore, DeKalb, Sandwich, Genoa or one of the farm towns between them, the estate is handled through one courthouse and one Circuit Clerk. The Illinois probate law is the same statewide. What changes is the building, the phone line and the filing habits. Georgia also has a DeKalb County, so check that the page you are reading is about Illinois.

This page, part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois), covers where the DeKalb County court sits, how an estate opens and the original will is filed, how remote hearings and case lookup work, and what the court lists for people without lawyers. It also takes up a question common in this part of the state: what an executor does when a parent's farmland was rented to a neighbor. If you want to talk about your own family, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-dekalb-county-illinois&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the DeKalb County probate court?

The DeKalb County Courthouse is at 133 W. State Street, Sycamore, IL 60178. The Illinois courts directory lists it in the 23rd Judicial Circuit, with a courthouse phone of 815-895-7160 and hours of 8:30 to 4:30. The Circuit Clerk's page says civil, probate, traffic, criminal and juvenile cases are all filed through the clerk's office.

The official pages gave no room number for probate, so call before you drive over. The clerk's contact page lists 815-895-7131 for civil, divorce, small claims, probate and child support. The directory also says free parking lots sit within two blocks of the courthouse and that a law library is across the street inside the Sycamore Public Library.

The county's footer lists county government at 200 North Main Street, Sycamore. That is not the courthouse address.

Venue follows the county where the person lived at death (755 ILCS 5/5-1, covered in [which county handles probate when property is in more than one county](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties)). A DeKalb County resident with a lake cabin in another county still has the main case here.

## Does the estate need a court case at all?

Many do not need one. Accounts with a named beneficiary, jointly owned property and a funded trust pass outside court. A modest estate with no real estate may fit the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit). Land or a house in the parent's name alone usually means probate, and [handling an Illinois estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) explains where the line falls.

## How does an estate open in DeKalb County, and where does the original will go?

A person holding the original will files it with the circuit clerk right after the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1, from the page on [filing the original will](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk)). Then a petition asks the judge to admit the will and appoint the executor. Here is the sequence, with what the DeKalb pages confirmed and what they left open.

| Stage | What happens | DeKalb County detail |
|---|---|---|
| Will filed | Original goes to the clerk of the county of residence | Clerk is at 133 W. State Street. The clerk's pages did not describe will intake, so ask how the office receives it |
| Petition | Asks the court to admit the will and appoint the executor | The clerk says all civil documents must be filed electronically |
| Hearing | Judge admits the will and appoints the representative | Zoom rooms are listed by judge; confirm the format for your date |
| Letters | Clerk issues letters of office | Ask the clerk how to get certified copies |
| Notices | Heirs get mailed notice within 14 days of the order (755 ILCS 5/6-10) | Done by the executor or attorney |
| Claims | Later of six months from first publication or three months from mailing (755 ILCS 5/18-3) | Newspaper and mailed notice |
| Closing | Report, then a 42-day wait in independent estates (755 ILCS 5/28-11) | Filed with the same clerk |

The statute citations come from the pages on [the Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) and [how to open a probate estate](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois). [How long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois) has the full calendar.

Filing the will does not open the case. It preserves the document. The petition is what starts the estate, and the [petition for letters of office](/learn/illinois/illinois-petition-for-letters-of-office) explains what goes in it. If you are still looking for the paper, start with [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois).

## How does e-filing work at the DeKalb County courthouse?

The Circuit Clerk's filing page states that all civil documents must be filed electronically under an Illinois Supreme Court order, and it links to an e-filing portal, an e-filing FAQ and resources for filers without lawyers.

The Illinois courts directory adds that the courthouse has e-filing kiosks, and that the Self-Help Legal Center also provides them. It is unclear from the directory where the kiosks sit, so ask at the front. The clerk links a civil filing fee schedule as a PDF, so check it or call for amounts.

The pages did not say how probate petitions are treated compared with other civil filings. Ask the clerk that question directly.

## What does the court offer people who file without a lawyer?

The Self-Help Legal Center is inside the courthouse, open weekdays from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., closed 12:00 to 1:00 p.m., with a phone listed on the clerk's filing page at 815-899-4540. Its page says staff help people choose the correct forms, answer basic form questions, explain procedures and guide them through e-filing. A JusticeCorps Fellow staffs it and does not give legal advice.

The directory says the center is on the second floor, while the center's own page says the first floor. Since the two disagree, ask the front desk. The center's page does not mention probate, so call before relying on it for an estate.

The page on [the Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver) covers how to ask to have court costs waived, and [Illinois probate costs](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-costs) covers the rest of the budget.

## Can I attend a DeKalb County hearing by Zoom?

Possibly. The clerk's courtroom live-stream page lists a Zoom meeting room for each judge, with the courtroom number beside it. One courtroom's entry says you may appear in person or by Zoom unless a court order says otherwise. The page does not mention probate, so do not assume your judge follows that line.

Recording the live stream is not allowed without court permission, and participants should stay muted until their case is called. The page links to statewide guidance on joining Zoom from a phone or computer.

Your hearing notice and your attorney are the safest guide to the format.

## How do I look up a DeKalb County probate case online?

Use the Online Records page in the Circuit Clerk's section of dekalbcounty.org. It loads a search form. The page did not describe the case types covered or say whether results are the official record.

Treat what you find as a pointer. If a name does not appear, call 815-895-7131 and ask. If you are an heir wondering whether you are named, [how to find out if you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois) covers the next steps.

## What does an executor do when the parent's farmland is rented to a neighbor?

The lease generally keeps going. A cash-rent lease is a contract that binds the landowner's estate and heirs, so the tenant keeps the right to farm under its terms, and the rent belongs to the estate until the land is distributed. The page on [Illinois farm estate tax and cash-rent leases](/learn/illinois/illinois-farm-estate-tax-and-cash-rent-leases) explains the details.

Before saying anything to the tenant, the executor reads the lease. These are the points that matter:

- When does it end, and how is it renewed or ended? Many Illinois cash-rent leases run year to year.
- Is there a death clause, a right of first refusal or an option to buy?
- Is the landlord named as the parent personally, a trust or an LLC?
- Is the rent paid in one payment or two, and is any past due?
- Who pays property taxes, drainage district assessments and insurance?
- Is there a written lease at all, or only a handshake?

Once the court issues letters, the executor collects the rent into the estate account. If a trust holds the farm, the successor trustee does that. The rent is estate income, reported on the estate's income tax return, which the page on [Illinois Form IL-1041](/learn/illinois/illinois-form-il-1041-estate-and-trust-income-tax-return) covers.

Crops are a separate question. A tenant who planted under a valid lease usually owns the growing crop. Under a crop-share lease, the landowner's share is estate property. Put the harvest arrangement in writing before the combine rolls.

Illinois taxes estates over $4 million, and farmland can cross that line alone, as [Illinois estate tax](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax) explains. For planning ahead, read [farm succession planning](/learn/business-owners/farm-succession-planning) and the [farm succession checklist](/free/illinois-farm-succession-checklist).

## Example: Ben, his mother's 160 acres and a neighbor's lease

This is an invented example. Ben, 49, lives in Sycamore. His mother, Lorraine, lived outside Genoa and died in October. Her will names Ben executor and leaves everything equally to Ben and his sister Carol. Lorraine owned 160 acres in her own name, rented for cash to a neighbor, Dennis, who also farms nearby.

Ben finds the original will in a kitchen drawer and copies it. He calls the Circuit Clerk at 815-895-7131 to ask how the office wants the original delivered, and files it. He also asks how a probate petition is e-filed, since the clerk's page says civil documents are filed electronically.

Dennis calls Ben, worried about spring. Ben reads the lease first. It is a one-page year-to-year lease with rent due in two payments, and the second is still owed. He tells Dennis only that he will review it with the attorney and answer in writing.

Once the judge appoints Ben and the clerk issues letters, he collects the second payment into the estate account. Ben signs a short written lease for the next season with Dennis, and the estate's income tax return reports the rent. The claims period and the will contest window close with nothing filed.

## What mistakes do DeKalb County families make?

- Assuming the will is filed because a relative has a copy. Only the original counts.
- Driving to the county government building instead of the courthouse.
- Telling a tenant "keep farming" or "you are done" before reading the lease.
- Treating an empty online search as proof that no estate exists.
