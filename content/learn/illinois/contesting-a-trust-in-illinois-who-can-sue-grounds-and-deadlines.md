When a parent's living trust leaves the house to one child, the others often ask the same thing: can we challenge it? In Illinois you can, but the clock is shorter than most people expect, and it depends on a notice the trustee may or may not have sent. The answer also differs from a will contest in ways that matter.

This page covers the Illinois Trust Code rules on who can sue, the grounds, the deadlines and what happens to money already distributed. It is general education, not legal advice. If you are working through a death, our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois) has the wider picture, and the national article on [whether a living trust can be contested](/learn/trusts/can-a-living-trust-be-contested) gives the general view.

## What is the deadline to contest a trust in Illinois?

For a trust that was revocable when the settlor died, a contest must begin within the earlier of two dates (760 ILCS 3/604):

1. **Two years** after the settlor's death.
2. **Six months** after the trustee sends you a copy of the trust instrument and a notice.

That notice must state that the trust exists, give the trustee's name and address, and tell you the six-month period for filing. A trustee who skips it, or sends a letter without the trust copy, leaves you with the two-year outer limit. A notice that does include everything starts a much shorter clock, so read any trustee letter the day it arrives and write down the date.

The statute adds a twist when the trust receives a legacy under the settlor's will and that will is admitted to probate. Then the deadline to contest the will under the Probate Act applies. That period is six months from admission of the will, as described in our article on the [Illinois will contest deadline](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-contest-six-month-deadline). This is common, because many Illinois plans use a pour-over will to feed leftovers into the trust.

Example: Roberta dies on January 10. Her trustee, her son Neil, mails the trust copy and the required notice to her daughter Joan on March 3. Joan has until about September 3 to start a proceeding, which is earlier than the January two-year mark. If Neil never sent the notice, Joan would be measured against the two-year limit instead.

Do not wait for the last week. Courts generally want pleadings that are specific, and gathering medical records takes time.

## Who can contest a trust?

Someone with a real stake in the outcome. In practice that means people who would be better off if the trust or an amendment failed: an heir who would inherit if there were no valid plan, or a beneficiary of an earlier version of the trust or will. A friend or a stepchild with no claim under either usually has nothing to gain, and the court is unlikely to hear them. Whether you qualify depends on your facts, so an attorney should look at what you would receive if the trust fell.

Standing also depends on timing. While the settlor is alive and can revoke, the settlor controls beneficiaries' rights and the trustee owes duties to the settlor alone (760 ILCS 3/603). A child worried about a parent's new trust cannot sue to stop it. Only after the settlor dies, or if the settlor loses capacity, do others gain a voice. If the settlor lacks capacity, current beneficiaries can challenge trustee actions, but only as those actions affect them.

## What are the grounds for contesting a trust?

The Trust Code names several. Capacity is first. A trust exists only if the settlor had capacity and intended to create it (760 ILCS 3/402). Section 406 then covers the pressure grounds: if fraud, duress, mistake or undue influence procured the creation, amendment or restatement of a trust, the affected part is void. The same applies to a revocation obtained that way.

| Ground | What a challenger tries to show |
|---|---|
| Lack of capacity | The settlor could not understand the plan when signing |
| Undue influence | Someone overpowered the settlor's own judgment, often a caregiver or a child who arranged the lawyer |
| Fraud | The settlor was deceived about what was signed |
| Duress | The settlor signed because of a threat |
| Mistake | The document did not say what the settlor meant |
| Failed creation | A basic requirement of section 402 was missing, such as intent or a trustee with duties |

Evidence beats suspicion. The records that matter are doctor notes from the weeks around signing, who drove the settlor to the lawyer, who was in the room, and whether the lawyer met the settlor alone. See our page on [undue influence](/glossary/undue-influence) for the basic idea.

A mistake claim has a separate route if the question is not validity but wording. The Trust Code lets a court reform even unambiguous terms to match the settlor's intent when clear and convincing evidence shows both the intent and a mistake of fact or law (760 ILCS 3/415).

## How is a trust contest different from a will contest?

The forum, the clock and the notice rules all differ.

| | Will contest | Trust contest |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | 755 ILCS 5/8-1 | 760 ILCS 3/604 |
| Clock | 6 months after the will is admitted to probate | Earlier of 2 years after death or 6 months after trustee notice |
| What starts it | Admission of the will | Death, or the trustee's notice with the trust copy |
| Court notice | Interested persons get notice in the probate case | The trustee decides whether to send the notice |
| Public record | Will and probate file | Usually private unless a case is filed |

One practical effect: a will goes through probate and becomes a public file, so challengers learn of it quickly. A trust can be administered quietly, which is why the trustee's duty to give notice matters. Our article on the [Illinois trustee duty to inform beneficiaries](/learn/illinois/illinois-trustee-duty-to-inform-beneficiaries) explains that separate 90-day notice. A trust contest and a will contest are often filed together when the same person is attacking both.

## What happens to the money while a contest is pending?

A trustee may distribute trust property nine months after the settlor's death and is not liable for doing so unless two things apply. One is that the trustee knows a contest is pending. The other is that a potential contestant told the trustee of a possible contest and then filed within 60 days of that notice (760 ILCS 3/604). So an early letter to the trustee can freeze the distribution. A letter without a timely filing does not.

If the trust is later held invalid, a beneficiary who received property must return it with income and appreciation from receipt. That can make a beneficiary reluctant to spend an early distribution.

## Should you contest, or ask questions first?

Start with the documents. Ask the trustee in writing for the trust and all amendments, the accountings and the date of the last signing. That often settles the matter. A caregiver child named sole beneficiary of a house in an amendment signed two weeks before death looks different from a sibling disappointed by a plan signed ten years earlier.

If there is a no-contest clause, read it with an attorney before you act. Whether and how such a clause is enforced in Illinois is not something this page covers.

Do not sit on a clock for months. If the facts suggest incapacity or pressure, an attorney can tell you which deadline applies from the notices you have.
