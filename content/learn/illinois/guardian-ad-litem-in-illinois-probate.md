Most families never meet a guardian ad litem until a letter arrives saying one has been named in the estate. The name sounds alarming, and the person behind it is not an adversary. They are a court-appointed reviewer who looks out for someone who cannot speak up in the case.

This page explains what the role is, when a judge appoints one in an Illinois probate case, what the guardian ad litem does step by step, and how the cost works. For the general picture of Illinois courts, see our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

## What does "guardian ad litem" mean?

The Latin phrase means "guardian for the suit." The person is a representative for one lawsuit or proceeding, not for the person's life. In Illinois probate the appointee is usually an attorney. The judge appoints them by order, and they report back to the judge. That makes the guardian ad litem different from the long-term protector in [Illinois guardianship](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship), who manages a person's affairs over time.

## When does a judge appoint one in a probate case?

A judge may appoint a guardian ad litem when an interested person cannot protect their own interest and no one else can do it neutrally. Common triggers include:

- A minor is an heir or legatee, and the plan or a settlement affects that child.
- An adult heir has a disability and has no guardian.
- An heir is unknown, missing or unborn, for example a contingent beneficiary not yet born.
- The parent or guardian who would normally speak for the child has a conflict, such as being the executor who benefits.
- The estate wants court approval of a family settlement, and someone affected cannot sign.

The Probate Act also lets the court require supervised administration when a minor or a person with a disability has an interest that is not adequately represented (755 ILCS 5/28-2). Our article on [independent versus supervised administration](/learn/illinois/independent-vs-supervised-administration-in-illinois) explains how that works.

## What does a guardian ad litem do?

Here is the usual sequence once the judge signs the order.

1. The guardian ad litem reads the petition, the will, the inventory and any proposed agreement.
2. They speak with the executor, the lawyer and, where possible, the person they represent or that person's caregiver.
3. They review whether the share is fair, whether a settlement is reasonable and whether the plan protects the person's interest.
4. They may ask for more information, such as bank records or an appraisal.
5. They write a report with a recommendation: approve, change or object.
6. The report goes to the court and the parties, and the judge holds a hearing if needed.
7. The judge rules and decides the guardian ad litem's fee.
8. The appointment ends when the issue is resolved.

## What is different from other roles?

| Role | Who appoints | Who they serve | How long it lasts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guardian ad litem | The judge, for one case | The court, about one person's interest | Until the proceeding ends |
| Guardian of the estate of a minor | The court | The minor, managing property | Until age 18 or longer if disabled |
| Guardian of the person | The court | The ward, personal decisions | Long term |
| Executor | The will and the court | The estate and all beneficiaries | Until the estate closes |
| Attorney for a party | The party | That party | As hired |

The guardian ad litem is not the person's lawyer in the usual sense. They give the judge an independent view. Some courts also appoint a separate lawyer for a person with a disability. Practices vary by county, so ask the clerk or a local attorney what to expect.

## Who pays?

The court sets a reasonable fee, and the usual practice is payment from the estate or from the share belonging to the person represented. That is one reason to ask early about the expected cost. Fees add to the totals described in [Illinois probate costs](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-costs). An executor who knows a minor heir is coming can reduce the need for an appointment by planning ahead. A trust for a young beneficiary, or a custodian under the Illinois transfers to minors act, often removes the question. See [when a minor inherits money in Illinois](/learn/illinois/when-a-minor-inherits-money-in-illinois) and [UTMA accounts](/learn/illinois/illinois-utma-accounts-and-gifts-to-minors).

## Example: Nia, 16, and the settled house

Hector, 66, dies and leaves his house to his three children, one of whom, Nia, is 16. Her mother is the executor and also wants to buy out the other heirs. The court appoints a guardian ad litem. The guardian ad litem reviews an appraisal of $330,000, asks that the buyout price reflect it and recommends that Nia's share go into a blocked account until she is 18. The judge adopts the plan, and the guardian ad litem's $1,800 fee comes from the estate.

## How should an executor prepare?

Keep the file clean. Give the guardian ad litem the will, the inventory, the proposed distribution plan and the reasons for it. Answer questions plainly and quickly. The faster the report is filed, the faster the case moves, and the [executor workload tool](/tools/executor-workload) shows how much time the rest of the work will take. If the person represented is the one who has a disability and benefits matter, read our guide to [special needs planning in Illinois](/learn/illinois/special-needs-planning-in-illinois-for-a-disabled-child) because a direct inheritance can affect benefits.

## Can the appointment be avoided?

Sometimes, with planning. A will that leaves a young beneficiary's share to a trust, with a named trustee, gives the court less to worry about. A custodian account for a small gift to a minor does the same. For an heir with a disability, a special needs trust protects benefits and gives the court a ready structure. When an estate has to deal with an unknown heir, a thorough search and proper publication show the court that the executor did the work first. None of these steps removes the judge's power to appoint a guardian ad litem, but they shorten the review and lower the fee.

If you receive a notice of appointment, do not treat it as an accusation. Reply promptly, give the guardian ad litem what they ask for and keep your own lawyer informed. Cooperation is the fastest way to a report.
