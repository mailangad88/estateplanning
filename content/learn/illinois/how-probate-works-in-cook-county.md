If someone you love lived in Chicago or anywhere else in Cook County, their estate will be handled in one building downtown, under one clerk's rules. The state law is the same as in the other 101 counties. What is different here is the courthouse, the filing system and a clerk who tells families up front to bring a lawyer.

This page, part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois), walks through a Cook County case from the first phone call to the last order. It covers where the court is, how an estate is opened, whether you need a lawyer, how long it takes and how to follow the case online. If you would rather talk it through for your own family, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-cook-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the Cook County probate court?

It is in the Richard J. Daley Center, 50 West Washington Street, Chicago. According to the Circuit Court of Cook County, the Probate Division sits on the 18th floor, where the judges hear decedent estates, adult guardianships and minors' estates. The clerk's probate office is on the 12th floor, in Room 1202. The clerk lists the office phone as 312-603-6441, open Monday to Friday from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

The suburbs do not have their own probate court. The clerk's district courthouses in Skokie, Rolling Meadows, Maywood, Bridgeview and Markham handle other kinds of cases, so a family in Evanston or Orland Park still ends up at the Daley Center.

The case belongs in Cook County when the person lived here at death, because Illinois sets venue by the county of residence (755 ILCS 5/5-1, as explained in [which county handles probate when property is in more than one county](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties)). Where the executor lives does not matter.

## Does the estate need to go to court at all?

Ask this before anything else. Assets with a named beneficiary, joint owners or a funded trust pass without the court. If the person left only personal property worth $150,000 or less and no real estate, the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) may replace a case entirely, as our page on [handling an Illinois estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) explains. A house in the person's name alone usually means probate. For a Cook County house sale, [heirship affidavit vs probate in Cook County](/learn/illinois/heirship-affidavit-vs-probate) covers what title companies tend to ask for.

## How do I open an estate in Cook County?

You file the original will with the clerk, then an attorney files a petition through eFile Illinois, and a judge enters an order appointing the representative. The clerk's Probate Division page describes the same path with or without a will. Here is the whole case in order.

| Step | Where it happens in Cook County | Who usually does it | Illinois rule behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. File the original will | Room 1202 at the Daley Center, by mail to the Probate Division, or any of the clerk's five offices | Whoever holds the will, or their attorney | File immediately after the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1) |
| 2. Decide who will serve | Outside court | The named executor | Act or decline within 30 days of learning of the nomination (755 ILCS 5/6-3) |
| 3. File the petition | eFile Illinois | The attorney | Ask for independent administration (755 ILCS 5/28-2) |
| 4. Hearing and order | A Probate Division courtroom on the 18th floor, or by Zoom where allowed | Judge, attorney, representative | Order admits the will or appoints an administrator |
| 5. Letters issue | Clerk's office | Clerk | Letters of Office with a will, Letters of Administration without one |
| 6. Notice to heirs and legatees | Mailed | Representative or attorney | Within 14 days after the order (755 ILCS 5/6-10) |
| 7. Creditor notice | Newspaper publication and mailing | Representative or attorney | Claim date is the later of 6 months from first publication or 3 months from mailing (755 ILCS 5/18-3) |
| 8. Gather, pay and distribute | Estate bank account | Representative | Pay valid claims before heirs |
| 9. Close the estate | Closing report filed with the court | Attorney | 42-day wait after the report in an independent estate (755 ILCS 5/28-11) |
| 10. Change the deed, if there is a house | Cook County Recorder of Deeds, 118 N. Clark St. | Representative or buyer's title company | The clerk describes taking the signed Order of Release and the letters there |

The statute citations come from our pages on [the Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process), [opening a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) and [filing the original will](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk), which go deeper on each step.

Two Cook County details trip people up at step 1. The clerk says a will cannot be filed without a date of death and asks for a copy of the death certificate, so order certified copies early (see [who can order Illinois death certificates](/learn/illinois/illinois-death-certificates-who-can-order-county-clerk-or-idph)). And filing the will does not open the estate. The will just sits in the clerk's custody until a petition asks the court to act.

At step 3, the petition lists the heirs, the legatees and a rough value of the property. Our page on [the petition for letters of office](/learn/illinois/illinois-petition-for-letters-of-office) explains each part. Where there is no will, the court usually requires a bond, which [letters of administration and the surety bond](/learn/illinois/illinois-letters-of-administration-and-the-surety-bond) covers.

The clerk posts Cook County court forms on its site, and the Illinois Supreme Court publishes statewide approved forms. Your attorney will normally prepare them.

## Do I need a lawyer for Cook County probate?

To open the estate, yes, according to the clerk. The Probate Division page says that to open an estate in Cook County, with or without a will, you need to hire an attorney, and that the attorney completes the court forms and files them electronically. Filing the will itself is something you can do on your own.

That does not mean the lawyer does everything. Many families split the work: the attorney handles the petition, hearings, notices and closing report, and the executor handles the house, the bills and the records. [What does an executor do in Illinois](/learn/illinois/what-does-an-executor-do-in-illinois) lays out the family side of the job.

If money is tight, there are places to start. The Circuit Court lists Chicago Volunteer Legal Services, which offers pro bono representation to low-income people referred by probate judges and screened by the program. The clerk links a guide to free and low-cost legal services and runs a Self-Represented Litigant Center in the Daley Center. For the court's own costs, [the Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver) explains how to ask to have them waived, and [Illinois probate costs](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-costs) covers the rest of the budget.

## How long does Cook County probate take?

Plan on a little over six months for a simple estate, and a year or more if there is a house to sell, a dispute or a missing heir. Cook County does not set its own clock. The Illinois waiting periods do, and they apply here as anywhere else.

The creditor claim period is the floor: the later of six months from the first newspaper notice or three months from mailing. Heirs have six months after the will is admitted to contest it (755 ILCS 5/8-1). Both run at the same time, so they overlap rather than add up. After the closing report, an independent executor waits 42 days before discharge. [How long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois) has the full calendar.

## Can I check a probate case online?

Yes. The Clerk of the Circuit Court offers an online case search at casesearch.cookcountyclerkofcourt.org, and its Probate Division page also points to a CCC Portal case lookup. The clerk describes the online docket as brief summaries of court documents and court events, meant to show the general status of active and historical cases.

Keep three limits in mind. The clerk says the electronic docket is not the official record of the court. Updates can take a few days to appear. And for filings made in the last day or two, the clerk suggests the public access terminals in the courthouses or checking again later. If you are an heir wondering whether an estate was opened, [how to find out if you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois) covers the next steps.

## What are hearings like at the Daley Center?

Most are short and procedural. The Circuit Court says in-person operations have resumed and that Zoom appearances remain permitted under certain circumstances, under Amended General Administrative Order 2023-P-01. Do not assume either way; your notice and your attorney will tell you.

If the family is fighting, the Probate Division has a mediation calendar, Calendar 14, open to people in pending Cook County probate cases. Disputes over a slow executor are covered in [what heirs can do about a slow Illinois executor](/learn/illinois/what-heirs-can-do-about-a-slow-illinois-executor).

## Example: Gloria and her father's house in Oak Park

This is an invented example. Gloria, 56, lives in Naperville. Her father, Eduardo, died at home in Oak Park, which is in Cook County. His will names Gloria as executor and leaves everything to her and her brother Tomás. Eduardo owned the house in his name alone and had a checking account with no beneficiary.

Gloria first assumes she will file at the Maywood courthouse, since it handles Oak Park cases. A call to Room 1202 sets her straight: the estate is heard at the Daley Center. She mails the original will to the Probate Division with a copy of the death certificate and keeps a copy for herself.

She hires an attorney, who files the petition through eFile and asks for independent administration. At a short hearing, the judge admits the will and appoints Gloria. The clerk issues Letters of Office. Within the 14 days, the attorney mails notice to Tomás, and the creditor notice runs in the newspaper.

Gloria opens an estate account, pays the final utility bills and lists the house. She checks the online case search now and then to see each filing on the docket. The house sells in month five. The claims period and the contest window close in month seven with nothing filed. The attorney files the closing report, the 42 days pass, and the court discharges her about nine months after her father's death. A worksheet like the one in the [free Cook County probate guide](/free/cook-county-probate-guide) kept her dates in one place.
