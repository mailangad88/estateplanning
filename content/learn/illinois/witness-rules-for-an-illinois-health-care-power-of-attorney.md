Most signing mistakes with health care documents are about the witness. A daughter signs as a witness for her father, or a nurse at the hospital signs because she is standing there, and later someone asks whether the form is valid. A short check beforehand avoids that.

Our overview, the [Illinois health care power of attorney](/learn/illinois/illinois-healthcare-power-of-attorney), covers what the document does. This page goes deeper on the witness. Our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois) links the other forms. This is general education, and the statute's exact list can change, so confirm it on ilga.gov.

## How many witnesses does Illinois require?

One. The statutory form requires your signature and the signature of one witness who is 18 or older, and it does not require a notary (755 ILCS 45/4-10). That differs from the Illinois property form, which needs a witness and a notary. Our article on [filling out the property form](/learn/illinois/filling-out-the-illinois-statutory-short-form-poa-for-property) covers that difference.

You can add a second witness or a notary for extra comfort. A hospital in another state may prefer it. But the statute's minimum is one qualified witness.

## Who cannot be the witness?

Section 4-5.1 sets out the people who cannot witness. As summarized on this site and in the statute, the list includes:

- Your agent and any successor agent.
- Your attending physician.
- Certain other providers who treat you, such as an advanced practice registered nurse, physician assistant, dentist, podiatric physician, optometrist or psychologist.
- The owner or operator of a health care facility where you are a patient or resident.
- Relatives of you or of the agent, and relatives of the excluded providers, to the extent the statute lists them.

The agent also has limits. Your attending physician and other treating providers cannot serve as your agent (755 ILCS 45/4-5). A licensed professional who is not caring for you can serve.

| Person | Can witness? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Your named agent | No | Specifically excluded |
| Your successor agent | No | Specifically excluded |
| Your attending physician | No | Treating provider |
| Your nurse practitioner or psychologist | No | Listed provider type |
| Owner or operator of your care home | No | Facility owner or operator excluded |
| Your adult child (not the agent) | Risky | Relatives may be excluded; avoid |
| Law office staff member, 18 or older | Usually yes | No role in your care |
| Neighbor, friend or coworker, 18 or older | Usually yes | No stake in the document |
| A person under 18 | No | Must be 18 or older |

The word "usually" matters. If your facts are unusual, ask an attorney before you sign.

## How do you choose and use a witness, step by step?

1. Name your agent and successor first, so you know whom to exclude.
2. List adults in your life who do not provide your medical care and are not relatives of your agent.
3. Choose one, and ask them in advance. They should be willing to sign and, if needed, confirm what happened.
4. Check the current list in 755 ILCS 45/4-5.1.
5. Sign the form in the witness's presence, and have the witness sign.
6. Write the witness's printed name and address next to the signature.
7. Give copies to your agent, your doctors and any hospital where you go, and keep the original where your family can find it.

Our article on [where to keep your advance directive](/learn/healthcare-directives/where-to-keep-your-advance-directive) has more on storage.

## What if you are signing in a hospital or care home?

This is where mistakes happen. A hospital employee may offer to witness, but treating providers are excluded, and the owner or operator of the facility is excluded as well. An employee who is not a listed provider, and who does not own or run the facility, may fit, but the rules are technical. Safer is to bring an outside adult, or to ask the hospital's social worker whether the hospital has a policy and a neutral volunteer.

A patient in a care home who is signing near the end of life should not wait. Call a family friend or a staff member from a law office to come witness. See [what the Health Care Surrogate Act does when there is no valid document](/learn/illinois/illinois-health-care-surrogate-act-who-decides).

## What if the wrong person witnessed?

Sign a new form with a proper witness while you have capacity. Do not rely on an argument that the error was small. A hospital's legal department may refuse to follow a document it doubts, which can lead to the surrogate list rather than your chosen agent. Tell your agent and your doctors that the new form replaces the old one. The ways to cancel the old form are in [how to change or revoke an advance directive](/learn/healthcare-directives/how-to-change-or-revoke-an-advance-directive).

## Example: Philip fixes his form

Philip, 70, signed a health care power of attorney in a hospital room. His daughter Carla, who was named agent, signed as the witness. A month later, the hospital's records office noticed. Philip's attorney explained that Carla could not witness for her own appointment. Philip, still clear in thought, signed a new form the next day with a paralegal from the law office as the witness. The hospital scanned the new form. Philip also used the [plan finder](/plan-finder) to review his other documents.

## Do the witness rules differ from the property form?

Yes, and mixing them up causes errors. The two forms sit in different articles of the same Act.

| Feature | Health care form | Property form |
|---|---|---|
| Witnesses | One adult | One |
| Notary | Not required | Required |
| Agent may witness | No | No |
| Providers and facility owners excluded | Yes, in 4-5.1 | Yes, in the property form's list |
| Can be signed on the same day | Yes | Yes |

Many people sign both forms at one sitting. That is fine, as long as each form has its own signatures and the witness meets each list. A neutral adult who qualifies for the strictest list is a safe choice for both. Our article on the [Illinois property power of attorney](/learn/illinois/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property) describes the property rules. Keep the witness's contact information in case the hospital or a court has a question later.
