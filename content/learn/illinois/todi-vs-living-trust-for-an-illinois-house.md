You own a house in Illinois and want your children to get it without probate. Two tools come up again and again: the transfer on death instrument and the living trust. People often hear that one is "better," when in fact they solve different problems.

This page puts them side by side with Illinois details. For a national view, see our article on [whether a TOD deed is better than a trust](/learn/probate/is-a-tod-deed-better-than-a-trust). For each tool in depth, see the [Illinois transfer on death instrument](/learn/illinois/illinois-transfer-on-death-instrument) and [Illinois living trusts](/learn/illinois/illinois-living-trusts). The full set is in our [Illinois guide](/learn/illinois). This is general education, not legal advice.

## What does each tool actually do?

A TODI, under 755 ILCS 27, names who receives the real estate at your death. You keep full ownership during life. It must be signed, witnessed by two credible witnesses, notarized and recorded before you die.

A revocable living trust is an agreement under the Illinois Trust Code (760 ILCS 3). You deed the house to yourself as trustee. You manage it, you can change the terms, and at your death the successor trustee follows your instructions. The same agreement can say what happens if you cannot manage things yourself.

## How do they compare?

| Question | TODI | Revocable living trust |
|---|---|---|
| Avoids probate for the house | Yes, if recorded in time | Yes, if the house is deeded in |
| Cost to set up | Lower: one document plus recording | Higher: trust, deed, related papers |
| Manages the house if you are incapacitated | No | Yes, through the successor trustee |
| Holds the house for a minor | No, the minor owns it | Yes, until an age you choose |
| Stages (for example half at 30, half at 35) | No | Yes |
| Can cover several assets | Real estate only | Many kinds of assets |
| Private or public | Recorded and public | Trust terms are not recorded |
| Creditors, spouse rights | Still apply (755 ILCS 27/85, 27/66) | Still apply in many ways |
| Easy to change | Record a new instrument | Amend the trust |

Neither tool pays off a mortgage or changes who owes the lender.

## Which one fits which family?

Choose by the problem you are solving.

1. **One house, one or two adult children, no special concerns.** A TODI is often enough. See [naming TODI beneficiaries](/learn/illinois/todi-beneficiaries-and-contingent-beneficiaries-in-illinois).
2. **You worry about your own incapacity.** A trust, plus a [power of attorney for property](/learn/illinois/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property), covers it. A TODI does not.
3. **A beneficiary is a minor or receives benefits.** Use a trust, since both groups need management. See [special needs planning](/learn/illinois/special-needs-planning-in-illinois-for-a-disabled-child).
4. **Second marriage or stepchildren.** A trust can give a spouse the right to live in the house and then pass it to your children. See [blended family inheritance](/learn/illinois/blended-family-inheritance).
5. **Several properties or accounts to coordinate.** A trust is easier to keep organized.
6. **Out-of-state real estate.** A trust can avoid a second probate there. See the [Illinois resident owning property in another state](/learn/illinois/illinois-resident-owning-property-in-another-state).

## A worked example

Rosa, 70, owns a paid-off house worth $320,000 and has two adult children, Mia and Dev, who get along. She wants it split equally and does not want probate. A TODI costs her little: a lawyer prepares it, two witnesses and a notary sign, and she pays the county recording fee. She names both children for half each and gives each a backup. If Rosa later develops dementia, her agent under the power of attorney can still manage her bills and sell the house if care requires it.

Change one fact. Suppose Dev is 19 and has a disability that makes him eligible for SSI. A direct gift of half a house could affect his benefits. Rosa chooses a trust, with the house deeded to herself as trustee and a special needs trust for Dev's share. The cost is higher, but the plan fits.

## What are the traps with each?

For a TODI: not recording before death, a beneficiary who dies first with no backup, and forgetting that the instrument cannot be changed by a will. For a trust: signing the trust but never deeding the house in. Our article on [unfunded trusts](/learn/trusts/how-to-fund-a-trust) explains how the house then ends up in probate anyway. Both: ignoring the mortgage lender, which may have rules about transfers into a trust.

## What about taxes?

Illinois has its own estate tax with a $4 million exclusion. See our Illinois estate tax article. A house passing by either tool is counted in the estate for tax. The date-of-death value generally resets the income tax basis. Neither tool changes that.

## Does a TODI ever work with a trust?

Yes. A TODI can name the trustee of your trust as the beneficiary. The house then passes into the trust at death and follows its terms, without the cost of deeding it in now. This can be helpful for a rental or a second property. Have a lawyer check that the names match.

## What does the cost difference look like in practice?

Costs vary by lawyer and county, so use these as questions to ask rather than prices to expect. For a TODI, ask about the drafting fee, the witnesses and notary, and the county recording fee. For a trust, ask what is included: the trust agreement, the deed into the trust, a pour-over will, powers of attorney, and help moving accounts. Our article on estate planning cost explains how packages are priced.

Think about cost over time, not only today. A TODI that is quick and cheap now can cost more later if it fails, because a missed recording or a beneficiary who died first sends the house to probate. A trust costs more to set up and a little more to maintain, but it can avoid a court-appointed guardian of the estate if you become incapacitated, and it can remove the need for probate on every asset you deed or assign into it. Put a rough number on each outcome for your family, then decide whether the extra protection is worth it.
