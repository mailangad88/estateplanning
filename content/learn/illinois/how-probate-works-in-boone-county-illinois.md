If your father or aunt lived in Belvidere, Poplar Grove, Capron or Caledonia, the estate belongs in Boone County, Illinois. That is a small county in the 17th Judicial Circuit, sharing a circuit with Winnebago County to the west. Searches for "Boone County probate" also turn up Boone counties in other states, so confirm Illinois first.

This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). It covers the Belvidere courthouse, filing the will, opening an estate, remote hearings, self-help and case lookup. Its main question is what happens when the estate is a house or farmland plus a few accounts. If you want to talk through your own family, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-boone-county-illinois&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the Boone County courthouse for probate?

The Boone County Courthouse is at 601 North Main Street, Belvidere, IL 61008. The Illinois Courts directory lists it in the 17th Circuit with hours of 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and free parking. The Circuit Clerk's office is on the first floor, and an e-filing kiosk sits outside it.

The clerk's website posted a notice that the front entrance was closed and that a temporary entrance was open on Webster Street at the back. Notices like that change, so check the clerk's home page before you go. The Circuit Clerk's phone number is 815-544-0371.

| Place | Where | Why a probate family might go |
|---|---|---|
| Boone County Courthouse | 601 North Main Street, Belvidere | Courtrooms and the Circuit Clerk |
| Circuit Clerk's office | First floor of the courthouse | Filings, records, certified copies |
| Self-Help Center and law library | Inside the courthouse | Computers, printing, general information |
| Winnebago County Courthouse | 400 West State Street, Rockford | Not for a Boone County estate |

## What should the family gather before going to the courthouse?

Start with the will and a rough list of what the person owned. Look in the home, a safe deposit box and the files of any attorney who drafted a will, as described in [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois).

Then sort the list into things that pass outside the court and things that do not. A beneficiary form, a joint account or a recorded transfer on death instrument moves property without probate. A house or land in the dead person's name alone usually points to probate. The [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter) lets you place each item.

Illinois ties probate to the county where the person lived at death. A Belvidere resident with a lake cabin elsewhere still has one case, as [which county handles probate when property is in more than one county](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties) explains.

## How is the original will filed in Boone County?

Illinois law says whoever holds the original will must file it with the circuit clerk right after the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1). The guide to [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) covers that duty and the 30-day clocks in section 6-3. A photocopy cannot take the place of the original.

The Boone County clerk's site does not describe a separate will counter, a will receipt or a room number. So the practical step is a phone call to 815-544-0371 before you drive over. Ask where the original goes, whether a certified copy can be bought the same visit and what the fee is.

Copy the will first, carry the original flat, bring the dates of birth and death, and ask for a receipt. Filing the will does not open the estate. That is a separate step, described next.

## How does an estate case open in Boone County?

The 17th Circuit's self-help page says you can file in person at the clerk's office or online, and that online filing needs an account. The Circuit Clerk's e-filing page says electronic filing is mandatory for all civil cases in Boone County, and has been since January 1, 2018. Estates are civil matters, so expect to e-file rather than hand paper across a counter.

The clerk lists two e-filing options, Odyssey and i2File, and says one eFileIL account works with every certified provider. Two scanning stations sit in the courthouse lobby. The clerk warns that construction may disrupt them.

Opening a case usually requires a filing fee, and a fee waiver may be available if you cannot afford it. The clerk posts a civil fee schedule, which this page does not reproduce. The [Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver) explains how waivers work.

After the court admits the will and issues letters of office, the state rules set the pace. [How to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) and the [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) cover notices, the claims period and closing. Heirs get written notice within 14 days after the order admitting the will, and have six months to contest it, as the process page explains.

## Can a small estate affidavit move a house or farmland in Boone County?

No. This is the question that surprises Boone County families most, because the county has many small homes and rural parcels held by one person for decades.

The [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) lets a person collect personal property without opening probate. The page says the personal estate limit is currently $150,000, not counting registered motor vehicles. It also says real estate cannot pass this way, and that the person who signs must pay valid debts first.

So the affidavit can reach the checking account, the car and the savings bond. It cannot reach the house or the quarter section down the road. Land in the dead person's name alone generally needs a court-appointed representative before anyone can sell, refinance or retitle it, as [when a parent dies and the house is still in their name](/learn/illinois/parent-died-house-in-their-name) describes.

Here is how the common assets sort out.

| Asset | Small estate affidavit? | Usual route |
|---|---|---|
| Checking or savings, no beneficiary | Yes, if within the limit | Affidavit at the bank |
| Registered car | Counted separately | Secretary of State retitling |
| House in the dead person's name alone | No | Probate, then deed from the representative |
| House with a recorded transfer on death instrument | Not needed | Beneficiary follows the instrument |
| Farmland in the dead person's name alone | No | Probate |
| Account with a named beneficiary | Not needed | Claim directly with the bank |

A recorded [transfer on death instrument](/learn/illinois/illinois-transfer-on-death-instrument) is the exception that lets a house skip probate. It only works if recorded in the county where the land sits before the owner dies, as [recording an Illinois transfer on death instrument](/learn/illinois/recording-an-illinois-todi) explains.

## Can I attend a Boone County hearing remotely?

Often, yes. The 17th Circuit's virtual court page says judges may hold hearings by Zoom or another platform. You can join with a Meeting ID the court provides, through the Virtual Courtroom Directory, or by phone if you cannot log on. The page lists dial-in numbers and says to enter the Meeting ID, then press the pound key.

The page also lists etiquette. You wait in a virtual waiting room until your case is called, stay muted unless speaking and use your title and legal name as your screen name. Attorneys must appear by video. Recording or rebroadcasting a hearing is prohibited under General Order 1.09.

If you are outside the United States, the page warns that Zoom is generally unavailable to servers abroad and says to contact the court early.

Whether your own hearing is remote is up to the court. Read the notice you receive and ask the attorney handling the case.

## What self-help does Boone County list?

The 17th Circuit lists a Boone County Self-Help Center inside the courthouse law library. Navigators are listed Monday through Friday, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., at 815-547-8083. They can help you use the center's computers and printing. They cannot give legal advice or tell you which forms to complete.

The page says the center's forms are approved for use in Boone and Winnebago Counties and cost nothing to use. The clerk's self-help page links Prairie State Legal Services and Illinois Legal Aid Online, and says the clerk's office may not give legal advice.

For a family weighing a lawyer against going alone, [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) explains where people get stuck.

## How do I look up a Boone County case?

The Circuit Clerk's Court Records page says all cases since August 1993 may be searched on the Judici website. Cases before that are reviewed at the courthouse, where indexes, files and microfilm are available.

Clerk staff will run a search for you if you send a written request. It must include the years, the names, the search fee and a self-addressed stamped envelope. The page lists a per-name, per-year search fee and asks you to allow 4 to 6 weeks. Copies require prepaid fees under a separate table.

If you are an heir and are not sure an estate was ever opened, see [how to find out if you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois).

## Example: Gerald's house in Belvidere

This is an invented example. Gerald, 79, a widower, lived in Belvidere and died in October. His daughter Maya lives in Phoenix. His son Tom lives in Poplar Grove. Gerald left a checking account of about $55,000, a registered pickup and a small house in his name alone. His will names Maya as executor.

Tom takes the original will to the Circuit Clerk in Belvidere after a phone call about where it goes. He keeps a receipt and sends Maya a copy.

Maya's attorney explains the split. The bank will honor a small estate affidavit for the checking account, because it is well under the limit and no petition is pending. The pickup is retitled through the state. The house is another matter, because Gerald never recorded a transfer on death instrument. Selling it needs a case, so the attorney e-files a petition to admit the will.

Maya joins the hearing from Phoenix by Zoom. The house sells after the court issues letters of office, and Maya pays valid debts and the claims period runs before she distributes anything. Had Gerald recorded a transfer on death instrument in Boone County years earlier, the house might have gone straight to Tom and Maya.
