When someone dies in Morris, Coal City, Minooka or Gardner, a probate case runs through the courthouse in the county seat. If you are searching for how grundy county illinois probate works, this page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois) and keeps the focus on Grundy County. A short call with an Illinois estate planning attorney can also help, and you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-grundy-county-illinois&topic=probate) when you are ready.

The page covers where to go, how an estate opens, what to do with the original will, how hearings are held, what self-help exists and how to look up a case. It then looks at one question many married families face: what happens to the survivor's bank accounts. This is general education from official court pages read in November 2026. It is not legal advice, and offices change their rules, so check with the clerk before you travel.

## Where is the Grundy County courthouse and the Circuit Clerk?

The courthouse is at 111 East Washington Street in Morris, Illinois 60450. The Illinois Courts directory places it in the 13th Judicial Circuit and lists weekday hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The clerk's office is in Room 30, and mail goes to P.O. Box 707, Morris.

The clerk's contact page lists a general number, 815-941-3256, and separate lines by department. Probate sits with the civil side, under a line labeled Civil / Probate at 815-941-3207. The same circuit covers Bureau and LaSalle counties, and [LaSalle County probate](/learn/illinois/how-probate-works-in-lasalle-county) shows how a neighboring court describes its offices.

## How does a Grundy County estate open?

An estate opens when someone files a petition with the Circuit Clerk in the county where the person lived at death. Someone named in the will asks the court to admit it and issue letters of office. If there is no will, a relative asks to be named administrator. The steps are laid out in [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) and the order of events in [the Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process).

Some estates never need the court. Illinois lets a small estate affidavit cover personal property up to $150,000 as of 2026, not counting registered vehicles, and it cannot move real estate. The page on the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) explains the limits, and [using the affidavit at a bank](/learn/illinois/using-an-illinois-small-estate-affidavit-at-a-bank) shows what a teller asks for.

## What should you do with the original will?

Whoever holds the original must file it with the clerk right after the death under 755 ILCS 5/6-1. A copy cannot be admitted in its place. The page on [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) explains that duty and the 30-day clocks in section 6-3.

A sensible routine looks like this:

1. Find the signed original, and keep it flat with no staples removed or notes added.
2. Photograph each page for the family.
3. Call the Civil / Probate line and ask how the clerk wants it delivered.
4. Get a dated receipt and write down any case number.
5. Tell the named executor where the original now sits.

If you cannot find the will, start with [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois). Filing a will preserves it. Authority over accounts and property comes later, from the court's order and the letters of office.

## Can you attend a Grundy County hearing by Zoom?

Do not assume so. The 13th Circuit's Zoom page explains how to join from the court location page for Bureau, Grundy or LaSalle. But the Grundy County Circuit Clerk's own site says that as of March 24, 2025, virtual court has been suspended and all parties are to appear in person until further notice.

The two pages point in different directions, and neither one describes probate hearings specifically. The clerk's notice is the more specific for this county, so plan to attend in person unless the clerk or your attorney confirms otherwise. A relative out of state should ask before booking anything. For timing, see [how long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois).

## Does Grundy County offer self-help and e-filing?

Yes, in part. The Illinois Courts directory lists a Self-Help Center and an e-filing kiosk in the Circuit Clerk's office. The clerk's e-filing page describes filing documents electronically instead of on paper and lists reasons a person can be excused, such as no home internet or a disability. It also says staff cannot give legal advice or file for you.

That page links Illinois Legal Aid Online, Illinois Court Help and an information sheet for filers without a lawyer. It does not mention probate. If you are weighing a do-it-yourself filing, read [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) and [do you need a lawyer for a will](/learn/illinois/do-i-need-a-lawyer-for-a-will-in-illinois) first.

## How do you look up a Grundy County case?

Both the clerk's site and the court directory point to Judici for case information. The clerk's menu also lists a court docket, a copy request form and local rules. None of those pages says what appears for a probate file or how quickly it shows up.

If the search turns up nothing, call 815-941-3207 and ask. For how estate files become public, see [is probate public](/learn/probate/is-probate-public) and [how to look up a probate case](/learn/probate/how-to-look-up-a-probate-case).

## What happens to a surviving spouse's bank accounts?

Each account follows the paper at the bank, not the will. Married couples often hold several accounts, and each one can end differently. Sorting them first tells a spouse how much of the money needs the court at all. The page on [payable-on-death and joint bank accounts in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-payable-on-death-and-joint-bank-accounts) explains the rules in depth, and [what happens to a deceased person's bank account](/learn/probate/what-happens-to-a-deceased-persons-bank-account) covers the bank's side.

<!-- visual: diagram name=BeneficiaryBeatsWill -->

| How the account is titled | What happens at the first death | Court involved? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Joint with the spouse | A bank may pay the survivor (765 ILCS 1005/2) | Usually no |
| Payable-on-death to the spouse | The spouse collects with a death certificate and the bank's form (205 ILCS 625/4) | No |
| Payable-on-death to someone else | That person collects, even if the will says otherwise | No |
| Sole name, no beneficiary | Frozen until an executor or administrator has authority | Yes, or the small estate affidavit |

Two details matter for a widow or widower. First, a payable-on-death account naming a child, not the spouse, pays the child. The spouse gets nothing from it, and a renunciation reaches only what passes under the will. The page on [a surviving spouse's rights in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-spouse-rights-at-death) notes that no statute was found on how the one-third or one-half is measured against such accounts, so ask an attorney.

Second, if there is no will, the spouse takes one-half of the probate estate when there are children and everything when there are none (755 ILCS 5/2-1). The page on [who inherits without a will](/learn/illinois/who-inherits-without-a-will-in-illinois) shows this with numbers. For the first weeks, [what to do after a spouse dies](/learn/illinois/spouse-died-in-illinois-frozen-accounts-bills-and-the-house) covers frozen accounts and bills, and the [surviving spouse checklist](/free/surviving-spouse-checklist) gives a printable list.

### Example: the Peterson family of Morris

This is an invented example. Walter Peterson, 77, lived in Morris and died in March. His wife Ruth was on a joint checking account with him. His savings account named their son Greg as payable-on-death. A certificate of deposit was in Walter's name alone. His will, signed years earlier, left the certificate to his two children from his first marriage.

Ruth kept using the joint checking account and paid the funeral bill from it, saving the receipt. Greg took the savings account with a death certificate and the bank's form. That left the certificate. An attorney explained that the will controlled it, so Walter's daughter Dana, named executor, filed the original will with the Grundy County clerk.

Ruth had been left nothing under the will. She asked the attorney to compare the gift with her right to renounce and to claim the spouse's award, and she wrote the 7-month date on a calendar. She did not sign anything until that comparison was done. The attorney checked the whole estate before choosing between a petition and a small estate affidavit.

## What mistakes should a Grundy County family avoid?

- Driving to Morris with only a photocopy of the will.
- Planning to join a hearing by Zoom without asking the clerk.
- Moving money out of a sole-name account before anyone has authority.
- Assuming a spouse is first in line for a payable-on-death account naming a child.
- Missing the 7-month window to renounce.
- Treating an empty online search as proof that nothing was filed.
