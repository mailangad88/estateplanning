If your mother or husband lived in Springfield, Chatham, Rochester or Sherman, the estate belongs in Sangamon County, Illinois. People often search "probate Springfield IL" and land on pages about other states or counties, so confirm you have the right one before you drive downtown.

This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). It covers where the courthouse and the Circuit Clerk are, how a will is filed and an estate opens, remote hearings, the self-help the court lists and case lookup. It ends with an example of a retired state worker whose pension never touches the court. For help with your own family, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-sangamon-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the Sangamon County courthouse for probate?

The Illinois Courts directory lists the Sangamon County Courthouse, called the Sangamon County Complex, at 200 South 9th Street, Springfield, IL 62701. It is in the 7th Judicial Circuit. The directory gives (217) 753-6674 and hours of 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

Courtrooms are on the fifth, sixth and seventh floors, and video monitors in the building direct visitors to the right one. The directory does not say which courtroom hears estates, so use the notice you receive. It also lists an online public access terminal at the law library on the sixth floor.

| Place | Where | Why a probate family might go |
|---|---|---|
| Circuit Clerk | Room 405, fourth floor | Filing, e-filing kiosk, self-help workstations |
| Courtrooms | Fifth, sixth and seventh floors | Hearings, directed by monitors |
| Law library terminal | Sixth floor | Online public access to legal research |
| Public eating area | Basement | Vending machines, if the day runs long |

The same directory says mobile devices are allowed but must be off in the courtroom. The page lists no parking or transit details, so check the county's site before you go.

## What should the family gather before going downtown?

Start with the will and a list of what the person owned. Look in the home, a safe deposit box and any attorney's file, as described in [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois). The [find the will and assets kit](/free/illinois-find-the-will-and-assets-kit) gives a search order and a log.

Then sort the list. Accounts with a named beneficiary and jointly held property usually pass outside the court. A house in the deceased person's name alone usually points to probate. The [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter) shows which pile each item falls in.

## How is the original will filed in Springfield?

Illinois law, covered in [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk), makes whoever holds the original will file it with the circuit clerk of the proper county right after the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1). A photocopy cannot take its place.

The Sangamon County pages reviewed for this guide do not spell out the will-filing counter, room or paperwork. The Circuit Clerk's office is in Room 405, so call 217-753-6674 before you travel and ask what to bring and whether a receipt is issued. Hours are Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

A few habits help in any county:

1. Copy the will before you leave home.
2. Carry the original flat, without staples.
3. Bring the dates of birth and death in writing.
4. Ask for a receipt, and keep it with the estate papers.
5. Do not hand the only original to anyone without one.

Filing the will does not open the estate. It preserves the document, and the case starts separately.

## How does an estate case open and move forward in Sangamon County?

An attorney or the family files a petition, and the court appoints a representative. The steps are described in [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) and the [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process). Notices, the claims period and closing follow.

In Sangamon County the practical question is how you file. The clerk's home page says the Illinois Supreme Court mandates electronic filing. It advises allowing 30 to 45 minutes at the front counter, and links an eFile sign-on, a Guide and File tool for self-represented people, Tyler eFile help and a filing fee schedule.

The clerk's page also links forms for exemption from e-filing for good cause, which matters for someone without internet at home. Its FAQ says you may file without an attorney but recommends one, and clerk employees may not give legal advice of any kind.

## Can I attend a Sangamon County hearing remotely?

Possibly, but the Sangamon County pages reviewed do not say when. The Circuit Clerk's site links a Zoom download and a "Scheduled Live Stream Court" section. The 7th Circuit's page lists Zoom instructions for self-represented litigants, but that file could not be opened for this guide.

So treat remote attendance as something to confirm, not assume. Your hearing notice, the attorney handling the case or the judge's staff can say whether a hearing is in person or on Zoom. The clerk's number is 217-753-6674.

The county's court page also lists local rules of practice and administrative orders, which govern how remote proceedings work. An heir who lives far away can ask the attorney whether joining by video is allowed. An out-of-state executor may also want [non-resident executor rules in Illinois](/learn/illinois/non-resident-executor-in-illinois).

## What self-help does the Sangamon County court list?

The Circuit Clerk's FAQ describes self-help workstations at the Room 405 front counter, with legal self-help software that gives step-by-step instructions. The courthouse directory adds that the clerk's office has an e-filing kiosk and a self-help center.

Staff can answer questions but cannot give legal advice. Most forms are online, and the FAQ says multipart and other forms are available at the clerk's office. It lists civil and family, criminal, order of protection, traffic and Spanish-language form categories, and none is labelled for estates. The 7th Circuit also lists a Legal Aid Self Help Center and a law library on its pages.

For attorney referrals the clerk points to the Sangamon County Bar Association, and for other help to Illinois Legal Aid Online. For court costs, see [the Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver).

## How do I look up a Sangamon County case?

Use the Circuit Clerk's Records Lookup. It searches by name, case number or filing date, and says registration is not required. You can view a court schedule by picking a date, and narrow it by attorney, case type, courtroom or judge. The home page also lists a Daily Court Docket.

Searches are very specific, so use fewer letters if you are unsure of a spelling. The page says searching "Do" can find "Doe, John." Adoption, juvenile, mental health, sealed and similar cases are confidential, and the lookup may be down for maintenance.

The FAQ says unsealed court records can be viewed by anyone, but copies are not available online. For the general method, see [how to look up a probate case](/learn/probate/how-to-look-up-a-probate-case). If you are an heir unsure an estate exists, read [how to find out if you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois).

## Example: Gerald, a retired state worker in Springfield

This is an invented example. Gerald, 71, spent thirty years in Illinois public service and lived in Chatham. He dies in April. His will leaves everything to his daughter Alyssa. His retirement system beneficiary form, signed years ago, names his son Marcus.

The [pension survivor benefits page](/learn/illinois/illinois-pension-survivor-benefits-imrf-trs-surs-sers) explains that a public pension's death benefit goes to the person named on the plan's form, not the will. A will cannot redirect it. Marcus contacts the fund, which asks for the death certificate and its own claim forms. No court is involved for that money.

<!-- visual: whatif id=beneficiary-form-overrides-will -->

Gerald also owned a house in his name alone. Alyssa, named executor, files his original will with the clerk and gets an attorney to open the estate. The pension page notes that if no form is on file, a benefit often falls back to the estate, which is why Marcus's form mattered.

Alyssa still pays valid estate debts before dividing the house proceeds. She also tells Marcus to hold any pension deposit that arrives after the death, since the page says those payments usually must be returned. The estate is smaller than expected, and the house is the only reason for court. See [probate versus non-probate assets](/learn/probate/probate-vs-non-probate-assets) for the sorting idea.

## When might a Sangamon County estate avoid court?

Sometimes there is no case to open. The [small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) lets a person collect personal property without probate. The page gives a personal estate limit of $150,000, not counting registered motor vehicles, and says real estate cannot pass this way.

The person who signs must pay valid debts first and can be personally responsible if they get it wrong. If the estate is just above the limit, read [an Illinois estate just over the small estate limit](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-just-over-the-small-estate-limit). For the bank side, see [using a small estate affidavit at a bank](/learn/illinois/using-an-illinois-small-estate-affidavit-at-a-bank).
