Families in Marion, Herrin, Carterville and Johnston City who lose a parent or spouse often face two jobs at once: finding the right court office, and answering the mail from lenders and hospitals. Williamson County sits in the 1st Judicial Circuit, in the southern part of the state. This page belongs to the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). Other states also have a Williamson County, so check that a form or number is Illinois.

It covers the courthouse and clerk, opening an estate, filing the will, remote hearings, self-help and case lookup. Then it takes a different turn and follows what happens to the deceased person's debts. If you would like to talk about your own situation, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-williamson-county-illinois&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

This is general education from official pages read in November 2026, not legal advice. Hours, extensions and orders change, so confirm with the clerk before you act.

## Where is the Williamson County Courthouse and the Circuit Clerk?

The courthouse is at 200 West Jefferson Street in Marion, Illinois 62959. The Illinois Courts directory lists it as a circuit court in the 1st Circuit, 5th District, at Suite 260, with the phone number (618) 997-1301.

The Circuit Clerk's page shows the same main number and the hours Monday to Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., closed on listed holidays. It says the traffic division is on the first floor, entered from the north, and that the other divisions are on the second floor, entered from the south.

| Item | What the official page says | Why it matters for an estate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Street address | 200 West Jefferson Street, Marion | Where the original will is taken |
| Hours | 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., Monday to Friday | Arrive well before the afternoon close |
| Main phone | (618) 997-1301 | Start here and ask for probate |
| Entrance | South entrance for non-traffic divisions | Skip the traffic door on the north side |
| Circuit | 1st Judicial Circuit | Circuit orders apply, not another circuit's |

The clerk's page lists probate and guardianship as a case type with its own extension. The extension printed in the text and the one in the page's link did not match when it was read, so ask the main desk to connect you rather than trusting either number.

The 1st Circuit has nine counties, including Jackson to the west. The [Jackson County probate page](/learn/illinois/how-probate-works-in-jackson-county-illinois) is useful if the person owned land there too, but each county has its own clerk and its own courthouse.

## How do you open an estate in Williamson County?

Someone files a petition with the clerk in the county where the person lived. With a will, the executor named in it asks the court to admit the will and give the executor authority. With no will, a relative asks to be appointed administrator. The step-by-step version is in [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois), and the larger picture is in [the Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process).

The clerk's Williamson page does not describe probate filing, e-filing or any estate forms. It does link the statewide standardized forms. The Illinois Courts directory links e-filing, so ask the clerk whether your first papers should go in electronically. If the home is in another county as well, [probate venue when property is in two Illinois counties](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties) explains which court opens the case.

## What should you do with the will?

Bring the original to the clerk. Under 755 ILCS 5/6-1, a person who holds a deceased person's will must file it with the clerk immediately after the death, and a photocopy cannot be admitted in the original's place. [Filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) covers the duty and the 30-day deadlines in section 6-3.

A practical order for the day:

1. Handle the will gently: no staples, no notes in the margin.
2. Photograph or scan each page for the family.
3. Phone (618) 997-1301 and ask how the clerk takes a will in person or by mail.
4. Leave with a dated receipt.
5. Tell the named executor where the original now sits.

If you cannot find a will, read [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois). Whether an estate needs a court case at all depends on what the person owned, and the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) page shows when a short form can do the job.

## Can you attend a Williamson County hearing remotely?

Maybe, but the official pages do not give a how-to. The 1st Circuit's standing orders page lists a Williamson County order titled "Second Amended Standing Order for Remote Hearings in Probate, Chancery, Divorce, and Family Cases," entered September 4, 2020. Only the title and date appear on that page. Newer standing orders there cover hearings in individual courtrooms.

The Illinois Courts site says you may be able to join by phone or video and tells you to contact the local circuit clerk. It also says a remote appearance is an official court proceeding, so test your audio, stay muted until it is your turn, and say your full name when you speak.

For a sibling who lives out of state, ask the clerk or the attorney on the estate as soon as a hearing date is set. The decision about how the hearing runs belongs to the court.

## What self-help and case lookup does Williamson County offer?

The clerk's page says plainly that the office is not allowed to advise anyone on legal matters. For help beyond that, the 1st Circuit and Illinois Legal Aid Online list four Legal Self-Help Centers, all in public libraries:

- Marion Carnegie Library, 206 South Market Street, (618) 993-5935
- Crab Orchard Public Library, 20012 Crab Orchard Road, Marion, (618) 982-2141
- Anne West Lindsey District Library, 600 North Division Street, Carterville, (618) 985-3298
- Johnston City Public Library, 506 Washington Avenue, (618) 983-6359

Illinois Legal Aid Online says the centers give legal information, instructions, help with some documents and forms, and referrals, but not legal advice. Its description does not mention probate or wills, so call the library before driving there for estate paperwork. For the work a do-it-yourself executor takes on, see [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer).

For lookup, the clerk's site has a Case Info link to a Judici search. If a name does not show, phone the clerk, because new filings and restricted records may not appear. [How to look up a probate case](/learn/probate/how-to-look-up-a-probate-case) and [is probate public](/learn/probate/is-probate-public) explain what an outsider can expect to see.

## Who pays the bills after a death in Williamson County?

The estate does, not the children. Everything the person owned in their own name becomes the estate, and the executor or administrator uses it to pay valid claims before heirs receive anything. The page on [whether you inherit your parents' debt in Illinois](/learn/illinois/do-you-inherit-your-parents-debt-in-illinois) goes through the debt types one by one.

The mechanics matter to a Williamson County family because the court sets the calendar. The representative publishes a notice once a week for three weeks in a newspaper in the county where the estate is open, and mails notice to creditors who are known. The deadline for claims is at least six months after the first publication or three months after mailing, whichever is later (755 ILCS 5/18-3, as described on [how creditor claims work in an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/how-creditor-claims-work-in-an-illinois-estate)).

When money is short, Illinois pays claims in classes under 755 ILCS 5/18-10. Funeral, burial and administration costs rank at the top, and the final year of medical bills ranks fourth. Most credit card and personal loan balances share what remains. Unpaid claims that could have been barred end two years after the death (755 ILCS 5/18-12).

Here is how common bills sort out.

| Bill that arrives | Who answers for it | When a child could owe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card in the parent's name only | The estate | Never, unless the child shared the account |
| Hospital bill from the last year | The estate, ahead of cards | If the child signed a personal promise |
| Mortgage on the family home | The house secures it | If the child co-signed |
| Medicaid estate recovery claim | The probate estate only | Not personally, according to HFS |

For the executor's side, [credit card debt after death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/credit-card-debt-after-death-in-illinois) has the full payment order, and [executor personal liability in Illinois](/learn/illinois/executor-personal-liability-in-illinois) explains why paying in the wrong order is the mistake to avoid. A Medicaid claim is covered on [Illinois Medicaid estate recovery](/learn/illinois/illinois-medicaid-estate-recovery).

### Example: the Hargrove family in Herrin

This is an invented example. Gene Hargrove, 81, a widower, died in Herrin and left a will naming his daughter Leah as executor. He owned a house with a mortgage and an account in his own name. Mail soon brought a card statement, a hospital bill and a letter from a collector. His son Wade had co-signed a small car loan for Gene years ago.

| Person or bill | Who is responsible | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gene's card and hospital bill | The estate | Debts of the decedent |
| Leah as executor | Not personally | She acts for the estate and pays by class |
| The mortgage | Secured by the house | The family can keep paying, refinance or sell |
| The car loan | Wade, in his own right | He co-signed the loan |

Leah took the original will to the clerk in Marion, then had a petition filed so the court would give her authority. She told the collector in writing that the estate would handle the claim and did not pay it from her own pocket. After the court's notice period ran, she paid the claims in order and split what remained with Wade. Only the car loan stayed with a child, because Wade had signed for it.

The estate's house question is covered in [what to do when the house is still in a parent's name](/learn/illinois/parent-died-house-in-their-name).
