If your mother or uncle lived in Edwardsville, Alton, Granite City, Collinsville or Highland, the estate belongs in Madison County, Illinois. Many states have a Madison County, so this page is only about the Illinois one, which is the 3rd Judicial Circuit. It is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

It covers where the courthouse and the Circuit Clerk's probate filings are, how an estate opens and the original will is filed, remote appearances, self-help and case lookup. It also looks at a Metro East question: a house here, with accounts or heirs in Missouri. To talk through your own family, [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-madison-county-illinois&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the Madison County courthouse and Circuit Clerk?

The Madison County Courthouse is at 155 North Main Street, Edwardsville, IL 62025. The Illinois Courts directory lists it as the Third Judicial Circuit courthouse, open 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The Circuit Clerk's page lists phone (618) 296-6200 and the same weekday hours.

The county's civil courts page says the Civil Division handles the probate division along with small claims, eviction and other dockets. It says paperwork goes to the Circuit Clerk's Office, Suite 120. The clerk's own page also lists probate resources: a small estate affidavit, an estate claim form, guardian forms and a status report for decedent's estates.

| Place | Where | What an estate family uses it for |
|---|---|---|
| Circuit Clerk's Office | Suite 120, 155 N. Main St. | Civil and probate paperwork, in-person e-filing help |
| Records Division | Room 101 | Public computers for case lookup |
| Legal Self-Help Center | Law Library, same address | Forms and legal resources |
| Satellite clerk office | Alton | Public terminals for records |

The pages did not list parking or security rules, so ask the clerk before your first visit.

## How does an estate open and the original will get filed?

Illinois law requires whoever holds the original will to file it with the circuit clerk of the proper county after the death. The statewide rule and the executor's 30-day clock are covered in [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk).

The Madison County court pages do not explain how a will is physically filed. They list no room, hours or identification rules for it, and the clerk's FAQ does not address wills. The clerk's phone line is the place to ask before you carry the original downtown.

The case itself is e-filed. The clerk's e-filing page says civil cases go through a certified service provider, and the clerk does not accept emailed documents. Typical steps look like this:

1. Find the original will and a copy of the death certificate.
2. Ask the clerk how the original should be delivered.
3. Register with a certified e-file provider.
4. Prepare each document as a separate, readable PDF.
5. Pay the filing fee, because an unpaid fee is one reason filings get rejected.
6. Watch your email for acceptance or a rejection reason.

The clerk warns that most rejected filings come from defects in the pleading or a technical problem. For in-person help with e-filing, the page points civil filers to Room 120, though it says the clerk cannot help with individual filings.

After that, the usual Illinois steps follow. [How to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) and the [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) cover petition, notice, claims and closing.

## Do self-represented people have to e-file in Madison County?

Yes. The clerk's page says that since June 1, 2022, self-represented litigants can no longer file paper pleadings and are required to e-file, under Administrative Order AO 22-M-11. Other exemptions need a certification form filed with the clerk.

That matters for a family member who plans to handle the estate alone. The page on [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) explains what the work involves. Court costs may be reduced for some people, as described in [the Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver).

If the estate is small, ask whether an [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) is enough. The clerk's probate page links that form, and the [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter) helps you see which items would need court.

## Can I attend a Madison County probate hearing by Zoom?

Sometimes. The circuit clerk's Court Schedule for Zoom Hearings page lists judges who may hear matters by Zoom, tied to case types. It lists Courtroom 327 for "Probate, Guardianships, Foreclosure, Tax and All Non-Asbestos Matters."

The page gives these setup steps for each courtroom's meeting:

1. Join through the courtroom's link, with a Zoom account or a new one.
2. Enter the Meeting ID and password listed for that judge.
3. Choose your video and audio options.
4. Wait in the virtual waiting room until the court admits you.
5. Use your legal name as your display name.
6. Stay muted until your case is called.

The page warns that recording or sharing the feeds is strictly prohibited under Illinois Supreme Court Rule 68(A)(8), with contempt penalties. It does not explain who may appear remotely or how to ask. Ask the courtroom or your attorney, and join early.

## What self-help does the Madison County court list?

The Illinois Courts directory lists a Legal Self Help Center in the Law Library at the courthouse. It gives weekday hours of 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and phones (618) 296-4472 and (618) 296-5930.

The county's site links court forms, legal resources and community resources from the Legal Self-Help Center. It also links Illinois Legal Aid from the Attorney's Corner. The clerk's FAQ says the office cannot give legal advice, so staff will not tell you what to put in a petition.

The clerk's FAQ also lists the Odyssey filer support line, (800) 297-5377, for e-filing technical problems. That line helps with the system, not with the estate.

## How do I look up a Madison County case?

Use the Court Records Search on the Circuit Clerk's site. The clerk's FAQ says you can search by name or case number, and that case numbers follow a year, type and number pattern. After you open a case, the Case Overview shows the next and last court dates.

If you prefer a screen at the courthouse, public terminals are at the service counters and the public access area, plus the Alton satellite office. For a general walkthrough, read [how to look up a probate case](/learn/probate/how-to-look-up-a-probate-case). If you are not sure you were named in a will, see [how to find out if you are named in a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois).

## What if the house is in Illinois but the accounts or heirs are in Missouri?

This is common in the Metro East. People live in Madison County but bank, work or have children in the St. Louis area. This section stays with Illinois law only, and the general rules below are not about any other state's procedure.

An Illinois house owned in the deceased person's name alone is handled through the Illinois court where the person lived, as the page on [which Illinois county handles probate](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties) explains. Heirs who live elsewhere do not move the case. A named executor who lives out of state can still serve, as the page on a [non-resident executor in Illinois](/learn/illinois/non-resident-executor-in-illinois) explains.

If the person also owned real estate in another state, a second case may be needed there. The page on [ancillary probate](/learn/probate/ancillary-probate) explains why, and [an Illinois resident owning property in another state](/learn/illinois/illinois-resident-owning-property-in-another-state) covers planning ahead.

## Example: the Okafor family in Collinsville

This is an invented example. Daniel, 74, lived in Collinsville and died in October. His will names his daughter Grace, who lives across the river, as executor. The house is in his name alone, and he had a bank account in a St. Louis suburb.

Grace learns she has 30 days to start the case or decline. She asks the clerk how to deliver the original will, then registers with an e-file provider. She keeps a copy of the death certificate and each PDF separate to avoid a rejection.

Her attorney explains the court may require a bond for a non-resident executor, and that the Illinois house is handled in Madison County. Grace asks about a Zoom hearing for her brother, who lives in Oregon. She checks the court schedule and the courtroom before the hearing date.
