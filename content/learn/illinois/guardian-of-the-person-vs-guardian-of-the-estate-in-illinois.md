When a court appoints a guardian for an adult, the order usually names one or two jobs. One job is about the person's daily life and health. The other is about money and property. Families often assume that "guardian" means complete control, then find that the bank or the hospital wants a different set of papers.

This page explains how Illinois divides the work under Article XIa of the Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/11a). For the full process, see our [Illinois guardianship overview](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship). For the minor child version of this question, read [guardian of the person versus conservator of a minor's estate](/learn/guardianship/guardian-of-the-person-vs-conservator-of-the-estate).

## What does a guardian of the person do in Illinois?

A guardian of the person makes decisions about the person's care and living situation. The statute says the guardian must provide for the person's support, care, comfort, health, education and maintenance, and must help the person develop maximum self-reliance (755 ILCS 5/11a-17). In practice that means:

- Choosing and approving where the person lives, within the powers the court gave.
- Consenting to medical treatment and to services.
- Making sure the person has food, clothing and safe housing.
- Visiting, staying informed and respecting the person's preferences.

The guardian of the person must consider the person's wishes. Some actions need court approval, such as placing the person in a mental health facility, which has its own rules. Our article on [Illinois health care decisions without a power of attorney](/learn/illinois/illinois-health-care-surrogate-act-who-decides) explains what a surrogate can do before guardianship.

## What does a guardian of the estate do?

A guardian of the estate manages the person's property. The statute directs the guardian to manage the estate frugally and use income and principal for the person's support, care and comfort (755 ILCS 5/11a-18). Typical tasks are:

- Collecting income such as Social Security, pensions and rent.
- Paying bills, taxes and care costs.
- Protecting and investing assets as the court allows.
- Filing an inventory soon after appointment and later accounts with the court.

The guardian of the estate does not own the money. It stays the person's, and the guardian answers to the judge. The paperwork is covered in [annual reports and accountings for an Illinois guardian](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardian-reports-and-accountings). Many guardians of the estate must also get a bond, explained in [guardian bonds in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardian-bond).

## How do the two roles compare?

| | Guardian of the person | Guardian of the estate |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Health, housing, daily care | Money, property, bills |
| Statute | 755 ILCS 5/11a-17 | 755 ILCS 5/11a-18 |
| Reports to court | Reports on the person's condition | Inventory and accounts |
| Bond | Usually not needed | Often required |
| Fees | Court approves reasonable pay | Court approves reasonable pay |
| Can sell a house | No | With court authority |

## Can the court appoint just one guardian?

Yes. The court can appoint a guardian of the person only, of the estate only, or both, and the order should match the person's actual limits (755 ILCS 5/11a-3). A person who lives happily at home but cannot manage investments may need only an estate guardian. A person with a small income and strong money habits who cannot give medical consent may need only a person guardian.

Splitting the jobs between two people can reduce conflict, but it adds coordination. The guardian of the person may need funds that only the estate guardian can release. If siblings dislike each other, a split can make this worse.

## How does the court choose between the roles, step by step?

1. The petition asks for a person guardian, an estate guardian or both.
2. The physician's report describes what the person can and cannot decide.
3. The guardian ad litem reports on the person's needs.
4. The judge finds which areas need help and which do not.
5. The written order names the guardian or guardians and lists their powers.
6. The clerk issues letters of office once any bond is filed.

## Example: Sam and his mother

Sam, 49, files for his mother Ruth, 82. She has $210,000 in savings and a house. She can still cook and decide how she wants to live, but she has been giving money to callers. The judge appoints Sam guardian of the estate only, with a bond, and leaves Ruth's personal choices alone. If her health declines, Sam can ask the court to add the person role. Our article on [limited and full guardianship](/learn/illinois/limited-vs-plenary-guardianship-in-illinois) explains the next step.

If you can still plan, [powers of attorney](/learn/illinois/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property) let someone act in both areas without a case. The [plan finder](/plan-finder) helps you find the right documents.

## What do banks, doctors and agencies ask for?

Each outside party wants proof that matches the role. A hospital asks for letters of the person guardian. A bank asks for letters of the estate guardian and often a certified copy no more than a few months old. Social Security may name a representative payee without a court order, so a guardian of the estate does not automatically receive benefit checks. See [what counts as income and resources for SSI](/learn/special-needs/what-counts-as-income-and-resources-for-ssi) for how benefits and savings interact.

Keep a folder with certified copies of the order and letters. Order several copies, since many institutions keep one. Also tell the guardian ad litem and the family who holds which role, so requests go to the right person.

## What if the person already has a power of attorney?

A valid power of attorney can limit the need for a court estate guardian, because the agent already manages money. A court can still appoint a guardian, and the guardian's authority can affect the agent's. Disputes over this are covered in [power of attorney versus guardianship](/learn/power-of-attorney/power-of-attorney-vs-guardianship). If the agent is doing a good job, tell the court so it can leave the money role out.
