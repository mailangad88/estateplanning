If your father lived in Kankakee, Bourbonnais, Bradley or Manteno, his estate is most likely a Kankakee County matter. The county sits in the 21st Judicial Circuit, and probate cases run through the courthouse on Court Street. This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

It covers where the clerk's probate desk is, how an estate opens, what to do with the original will, remote hearings, self-help and case lookup. It also covers a situation many Kankakee families face: a parent who died in a nursing home or veterans home with Medicaid involved. If you want to talk through your own estate, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-kankakee-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the Kankakee County courthouse and the Circuit Clerk?

The Illinois Courts directory lists the Kankakee County Courthouse at 450 East Court Street, Kankakee, IL 60901. Its hours are 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The directory shows two court phone numbers but does not say which office each reaches.

The Circuit Clerk's own page gives the same street address and says Room 209 handles probate along with civil, criminal and family filings. Its number is 815-936-5700. Traffic and child support help is in Room 108, so probate families skip that office.

| Place | Where | Use for a probate family |
|---|---|---|
| Circuit Clerk, main office | Room 209, 450 E. Court Street | Probate filings and questions |
| E-filing kiosk | Inside the Clerk's office | Sending documents electronically |
| Self-help center | Second floor, outside the clerk's office | Forms and printing for people without a lawyer |
| Law library | Kankakee Public Library | Legal reference |

The directory also lists parking lots south of the courthouse on Merchant Street, and it gives a Court Disability Coordinator contact for accessibility needs. It lists no probate-specific information, so call the clerk with specifics.

## What should the family gather before filing?

Start with the will, if there is one. The page on [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois) explains where wills turn up.

Then make a rough list of what the person owned and how each item is titled. Accounts with a named beneficiary and jointly held property usually pass outside court. A house in the deceased person's name alone usually points to probate. The [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter) helps you sort them.

If your parent also owned land in another county, read about [which county handles probate when property is in two counties](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties).

## How does an estate open, and what happens to the original will?

Illinois law says whoever holds a will must file the original with the circuit clerk. The page on [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) covers that duty, the 30-day clocks and why a copy is not enough.

Kankakee County's pages do not describe a will drop-off procedure. The Documents and Forms page lists only the petition form. So call 815-936-5700 before bringing the paper will, and ask what the clerk wants.

The county lists six probate forms on its Documents and Forms page: Estate Claim, Petition for Probate of Will and for Letters Testamentary, Petition for Letters of Administration, Small Estate Affidavit, and two guardianship petitions. The petition you use depends on whether there is a will. [How to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) walks through that choice.

The 21st Circuit's local rules add two probate items. A handwritten will needs a typed transcription filed with an affidavit that it is true and correct to the affiant's knowledge. A will in another language needs a typed English translation with a translator's certification.

Filing is electronic. The local rules say all documents must be e-filed through a Supreme Court-approved system unless exempt. The clerk's page points to the Illinois Courts e-file portal. Documents that do not meet format requirements may be rejected, so check each scan before sending.

## Can I attend a Kankakee County hearing remotely?

Sometimes. The county's Zoom instructions say civil cases may be heard by Zoom, and they do not mention probate by name. So ask rather than assume.

The 21st Circuit's administrative order on remote appearances says a person may ask by oral motion, written motion or phone request to the judge's clerk, at least two days ahead. The local rules say the presiding judge must approve remote appearances. Pre-trials, trials and evidentiary hearings are expected in person unless the court approves otherwise.

If approved, the county's page says to contact the clerk for the meeting number, then join at Zoom.us/join or through the Zoom app. A phone-only option is 312-626-6799, followed by the meeting ID and #. You may wait in the waiting room until the judge admits you.

Everyone must enter a first and last name. The order says remote appearances must be by video and audio unless the judge decides otherwise. Missing a hearing, remote or in person, may lead to a default. The directory links remote proceeding information on the court's website at courts.k3county.net.

## What self-help does the county list?

The state directory places the self-help center on the second floor, just outside the Circuit Clerk's office. The county's Online Help and Documents pages link to Illinois approved statewide forms, Illinois Legal Aid and the Kankakee County Bar Association.

The clerk's page also says forms are available through ilcourthelp.gov. Those links do not describe probate help in particular. ## How do I look up a Kankakee County case?

The county runs an Online Portal at portal.k3county.net, and the Documents and Forms page links to it. The county's pages do not say what the portal covers or which probate records it shows. That is a reason to call the clerk at 815-936-5700 if a search returns nothing.

An account on the county website lets you save form progress and manage notification subscriptions, according to the Documents and Forms page. It does not say whether that applies to a probate case.

If you are unsure whether anyone ever opened an estate for a relative, see [how to find out if you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois).

## What if a parent died in a nursing home or veterans home on Medicaid?

This is the angle most county pages skip. Your mother spent her last years in a nursing home, with Illinois Medicaid paying much of it. After her death, you may get a letter from the Department of Healthcare and Family Services (HFS) saying it intends to file a claim.

The claim is against the estate, not against you. The page on [Illinois Medicaid estate recovery](/learn/illinois/illinois-medicaid-estate-recovery) explains that HFS describes the estate as property subject to probate, such as a house, a car or cash. Heirs are not expected to use their own funds. For deaths on or after July 1, 2022, the first $25,000 of estate value is protected.

Recovery is deferred while a spouse or a child under 21 survives, or a child is blind or disabled. Funeral costs, legal costs and a home mortgage are paid before the Medicaid claim. A hardship waiver exists, but the heir has to ask and prove it.

If the claim comes through probate, it goes through the case at the clerk's office. So the family usually needs an administrator or executor, and that person handles the claim. The page on [how creditor claims work in an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/how-creditor-claims-work-in-an-illinois-estate) covers the usual order.

A veterans home changes the paperwork, not the basic idea. An existing page notes that for a VA or state veterans home, who pays depends on the home's rules, so ask the admissions office. Whether Medicaid was involved is a fact to confirm early, from billing statements.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Was Medicaid paying long-term care? | HFS may send a claim notice |
| Is the house in the parent's name alone? | It is likely part of the probate estate |
| Does a spouse or protected child survive? | Recovery is deferred |
| Did any asset have a named beneficiary? | It generally passes outside probate |

For how the nursing home was paid before Medicaid began, read [how to pay for a nursing home before Medicaid](/learn/illinois/paying-for-a-nursing-home-before-medicaid-in-illinois). If the house passed by a transfer on death instrument, [creditors and Medicaid after a TODI](/learn/illinois/todi-creditors-and-medicaid-in-illinois) explains why that is not an automatic shield. The [estate recovery guide](/free/illinois-medicaid-estate-recovery-guide) gives a scenario table and steps.

## Example: the Martin family in Bourbonnais

This is an invented example. Helen, 84, a widow, died in a Kankakee nursing home after three years on Medicaid. She had no will. Her house in Bourbonnais is in her name alone. Her two children, Greg and Tanya, are her only heirs, and neither is disabled.

A letter arrives from HFS about a possible claim. Greg meets with an Illinois estate planning attorney. They agree that the house is likely in the probate estate, so someone must be appointed administrator before any sale. Greg agrees to serve, and the petition is e-filed, with a call to the clerk's office about timing first.

The attorney explains that funeral costs, legal costs and a small mortgage come before the Medicaid claim. The first $25,000 of the estate is protected, because Helen died after July 1, 2022. Tanya receives income-based benefits, so the family asks HFS about a hardship waiver and gathers proof.

Greg cannot attend the first hearing, because of work, so his attorney asks the judge's clerk about Zoom well ahead. Whatever happens with the claim, the plan avoids the common mistake of listing the house before anyone has authority to sell it.
