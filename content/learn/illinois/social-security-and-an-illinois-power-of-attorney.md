You sit at the Social Security office with an Illinois power of attorney that a lawyer prepared, signed, witnessed and notarized. The clerk says it is not enough. This surprises many families, and it is not a sign that your document is defective.

Our general piece, [how a power of attorney works with Social Security, Medicare and the IRS](/learn/power-of-attorney/does-a-power-of-attorney-work-with-social-security-medicare-and-the-irs), covers all three agencies. This page focuses on the Social Security side for Illinois families and gives the steps. The [Illinois guide](/learn/illinois) links the rest of the planning documents.

## Why does Social Security not accept a power of attorney?

SSA runs its own federal system for people who cannot manage their benefits. Under federal rules in 20 CFR part 404, subpart U, the agency appoints a representative payee and supervises that person. A state power of attorney is a private document, and SSA does not treat it as proof that you control the benefits. The Illinois form can still be useful. It just does its work elsewhere, such as at the bank that receives the deposit.

The Illinois property form does include a government benefits power for dealing with agencies, as described in [our article on the Illinois statutory form](/learn/illinois/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property). A particular agency may still insist on its own forms.

## What are your options, in plain terms?

| Option | What it lets you do | Who decides | Good fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Representative payee (SSA-11 request) | Receive and manage the benefits for the person | SSA | Person cannot manage money |
| Appointed representative (Form SSA-1696) | Help with a claim, appeal or paperwork | The beneficiary signs; SSA accepts | Person can decide but needs help |
| Illinois power of attorney | Manage the bank account the benefit goes into | The principal, at signing | Money already in the person's account |
| Guardianship of the estate | Court-supervised control, may support a payee request | Illinois court | No power of attorney and no capacity |

## How do you become a representative payee, step by step?

1. Gather the beneficiary's Social Security number and your own ID.
2. Contact SSA by phone or at a local field office to say that the person cannot manage benefits and you want to be payee.
3. Complete the payee request, such as Form SSA-11, with your relationship and your finances.
4. Provide medical evidence if SSA asks, such as a doctor's statement about the person's ability to manage money.
5. Attend the interview, in person or by phone. SSA may also notify the beneficiary and ask for their view.
6. Wait for SSA's written decision. Until then, benefits continue to the beneficiary's account.
7. If you are chosen, open an account titled to show the funds belong to the beneficiary, such as "Name, beneficiary, by your name, payee."
8. Use the benefits for current needs such as housing, food and care, and save the rest.
9. Keep receipts and complete SSA's periodic accounting report.

## What does an appointed representative do instead?

Form SSA-1696 appoints a representative, often an attorney or advocate, to deal with SSA for the beneficiary on a claim or appeal. It does not give the representative control of payments. Choose it when the beneficiary can make decisions but needs help with paperwork. A person who cannot decide is better served by a payee.

## How do the Illinois documents fit together?

The pieces do different jobs. SSA's payee controls how the benefit is spent. The Illinois property power controls the person's own bank accounts, investments and real estate. A person with both a payee and an agent should keep the accounts apart. Benefits that a payee holds are not the person's general funds to be mixed with other money, so the agent should not move them without checking with the payee.

For example, Beatriz, 83, has dementia. Her Illinois power of attorney names her daughter Elena. Elena asks SSA to change the direct deposit, and SSA declines. Elena then applies to be payee using Form SSA-11, submits a letter from Beatriz's physician, and is approved six weeks later. The $2,100 monthly benefit goes into a payee account, and Elena uses her power of attorney only for Beatriz's pension account and savings. Elena keeps one ledger for each.

## What if there is no power of attorney and no payee?

Then SSA may appoint a payee on its own review, and your family may also consider guardianship. Read about [Illinois guardianship](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship) and [what to do when a parent has dementia and no power of attorney](/learn/illinois/dementia-no-power-of-attorney). A payee can often be set up faster than a guardianship.

## What if someone misuses the benefits?

Payees must use the money for the beneficiary's needs. Concerns about misuse can be reported to SSA, and elder financial exploitation can also be reported in Illinois, as described in [how to report elder financial abuse in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-report-elder-financial-abuse-in-illinois). Keep records either way. The [plan finder](/plan-finder) can help you see what else is missing.

## What happens to other income, such as an Illinois pension?

Social Security is only one stream. A state or local pension, such as one from a public retirement system, is paid under its own rules. Many pension systems accept a power of attorney or require their own form, and some name a conservator or guardian only. Our article on [Illinois pension survivor benefits](/learn/illinois/illinois-pension-survivor-benefits-imrf-trs-surs-sers) shows how those systems deal with families. Private pensions and annuities follow the plan's rules.

The same pattern applies to Medicare. Medicare accepts a signed authorization to speak with a named person, but it does not grant control of the person's coverage. Read the [general article on Social Security, Medicare and the IRS](/learn/power-of-attorney/does-a-power-of-attorney-work-with-social-security-medicare-and-the-irs) for the IRS side, where Form 2848 is the usual tool. Keep a list of each agency, the form it wants and the date you filed.

## How long does it take, and what should you do meanwhile?

SSA does not publish a fixed time, and local offices vary, so plan for several weeks. While you wait, the benefit keeps going to the beneficiary's own account, and the Illinois agent can pay bills from that account if the document allows it. Keep every notice from SSA, write down the date of each call, and ask for the name of the person you spoke with. If the beneficiary is in a nursing home or hospital, ask the social worker to document that the person cannot manage money, since SSA may ask for that evidence.
