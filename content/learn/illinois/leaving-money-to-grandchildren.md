Your grandchild is going to college in a few years, or will someday, and you want to help without making it harder for the family to get financial aid. The honest answer is that the way you give matters more than how much. Money in the grandchild's own name counts most. Money you keep control of, or money in a trust, usually counts less.

This page covers the Illinois tools: Bright Start and Bright Directions 529 plans, Illinois UTMA custodial accounts and trusts. It is general education, not legal advice, and it is part of our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). If you want an Illinois estate planning attorney to look at your family, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-leaving-money-to-grandchildren&topic=trust).

## The short version

- Titling decides how aid forms see the money. Student's name counts most.
- A 529 you own keeps control with you and earns an Illinois deduction, within limits.
- A UTMA account in Illinois ends at 21. Whatever is left is the grandchild's.
- A trust can hold the money until you say, and can pay the school directly.
- Giving now and leaving it at death are different plans with different aid and tax effects.

## How do the main options compare for college aid?

Here is the general pattern. Aid rules change and schools differ, so treat this as a map and not a rule book. The federal form is the FAFSA, and some private colleges also use their own form.

| Option | Who owns it | General effect on aid | Control | Illinois detail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 529 owned by a grandparent | The grandparent | Generally not counted as the student's or parents' asset on the FAFSA. Some private colleges ask differently. | Grandparent | Possible Illinois deduction on contributions |
| 529 owned by a parent | The parent | Counted as a parent asset | Parent | Same deduction rules |
| UTMA custodial account | The grandchild | Counted as the student's asset, which formulas weigh most | Custodian until 21 | Ends at 21 (760 ILCS 20/21) |
| Trust for the grandchild | The trust | Depends on the trust terms and on whether money is paid to or for the student | Trustee | Written to your wishes |
| Tuition paid directly to the school | No one | Usually not an asset. Check how the school treats it as help received. | You | Federal gift-tax rule for direct tuition |
| Outright inheritance at death | The grandchild | Student's asset from then on | None | Court supervision or age 21 if a minor |

For anything federal, read the current page at studentaid.gov or ask the school's aid office. Attorneys do not set aid awards, and a college aid officer is the right person to answer how your grandchild's school will treat the money.

## How do Bright Start and Bright Directions fit in?

They are the Illinois 529 plans. Bright Start is the direct-sold program you open yourself, and Bright Directions is the advisor-guided program (brightstart.com). For a grandparent, the useful features are control and the state deduction.

Illinois lets a taxpayer filing an individual return deduct up to $10,000 of contributions per tax year, or up to $20,000 on a joint return. The limit is shared across Bright Start, Bright Directions and College Illinois, and a withdrawal that is not for qualified expenses means adding the deduction back (brightstart.com). Check with a tax preparer whether the deduction belongs to you or to the grandchild's parents, since it is claimed on a return.

A 529 contribution is a gift for federal purposes, so the $19,000 per recipient annual exclusion in 2026 is the number to compare against. Our page on the [gift tax annual exclusion](/learn/estate-tax/gift-tax-annual-exclusion) explains it. The practical points for a grandparent:

1. Name a successor owner on the account form. If you die with no successor, the account can end up in your estate.
2. Do not use the 529 as the only way you provide for a grandchild. It is for education.
3. Keep the beneficiary flexible. Many plans let the owner switch to another family member, so check the program terms.

## What does an Illinois UTMA account do to aid?

It makes the money the grandchild's. An Illinois custodian must hand over property given by gift or by a will or trust at 21 (760 ILCS 20/21), and until then the property still belongs to the child. That is why aid formulas treat it as the student's asset. The full rules are in our page on [Illinois UTMA accounts](/learn/illinois/illinois-utma-accounts-and-gifts-to-minors).

A UTMA account is cheap and simple, and it is a fine home for a modest gift. For a large one, many grandparents move to a 529 or a trust. A twenty-one-year-old who has just finished college and receives a custodial account has no one to stop a bad decision.

## How can a trust help with college aid?

A trust holds the money in the trust's name, with a trustee who follows your instructions. A well-drafted trust can allow the trustee to pay tuition and housing bills directly, to wait until a later age for large amounts, and to keep an inheritance away from a grandchild's creditors or an unreliable spouse. Our guides on [setting up a trust for your grandchildren](/learn/trusts/how-to-set-up-a-trust-for-your-grandchildren) and [what happens when a minor inherits in Illinois](/learn/illinois/when-a-minor-inherits-money-in-illinois) show the choices.

How aid forms treat a trust turns on its terms and on what the trustee actually pays. Money paid out to or for the student can show up as income or as a resource on some forms. That is why a trust drafted for aid purposes should be read alongside the schools' rules. A trust is also the right tool when the grandchild has a disability and benefits are at stake. See [grandparents leaving money to a grandchild with special needs](/learn/special-needs/grandparents-leaving-money-to-a-grandchild-with-special-needs).

## Does it matter when the money arrives?

Yes, and this is the point families miss. Aid forms look at the family's finances, and a large amount landing in the student's name shortly before or during college can reduce eligibility for years.

Consider Gail and Raymond in Naperville. They have three grandchildren, ages 4, 9 and 14, and about $150,000 they want to leave them. Their first idea was an equal share to each grandchild in their will, paid outright. The attorney points out that the 14-year-old could be in college when the estate is settled, so her share would arrive in her own name at the worst moment for aid. The two younger ones, if still minors, would get a custodian or a court-supervised account and then the money at 21 or 18, with no one to say wait. So they change it. They put money each year into Bright Start accounts they own, with a successor owner named, and leave the rest in a trust with their daughter as trustee. The trust pays education costs first and releases the remainder at ages 25 and 30. This is an example with invented numbers, not advice.

## What about my own long-term care planning?

Gifts leave your name. Illinois Medicaid reviews transfers in the 60 months before an application for long-term care coverage, and a gift to a grandchild or to a 529 account can be counted as a transfer. If you are healthy and the amounts are small, that is rarely a problem. If nursing home care is a real possibility within five years, talk to an attorney first. Our page on [Illinois Medicaid nursing home eligibility and planning](/learn/illinois/illinois-medicaid-nursing-home-eligibility-and-planning) explains the rule, and the [Medicaid look-back date tool](/tools/medicaid-lookback-date) shows your window.

## What should grandparents do first?

1. List each grandchild, their age and what you want the money for.
2. Decide how much to give now and how much to leave at death.
3. Open a Bright Start or Bright Directions account for education money, and name a successor owner.
4. Use a trust for amounts large enough that 21 feels too early, and name a trustee who is not afraid to say no.
5. Check beneficiary forms. An IRA left to a grandchild has its own rules, covered in [leaving an IRA or 401(k) to children or grandchildren](/learn/beneficiary-designations/leaving-an-ira-or-401k-to-children-or-grandchildren).
6. Ask about generation-skipping tax only if your estate is large. The [generation-skipping transfer tax](/learn/estate-tax/generation-skipping-transfer-tax) page explains the threshold.
7. Use the free [grandchildren education gift planner](/free/grandchildren-education-gift-planner) and the [Illinois minor inheritance age and asset decision table](/free/illinois-minor-inheritance-age-and-asset-decision-table) to work through each child.
