Families often hear a single number for probate costs, but real costs come in separate pieces. Some are set by the clerk, some by the newspaper, some by the court and some by agreement. Seeing them one at a time makes the bill easier to predict and easier to question.

This page covers costs only. For the steps, see [Illinois probate, step by step](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process). For timing, see [how long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois). Our [Illinois guide](/learn/illinois) links the whole set.

## What are the cost items in Illinois probate?

| Cost item | Who sets it | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Court filing fee | The clerk of the circuit court | Varies by county; Cook County lists $479 for independent administration as of October 2025 |
| Newspaper publication | The newspaper | Creditor notice runs once a week for three weeks |
| Surety bond premium | The bonding company | Only if a bond is required; a will can ask that surety be excused |
| Attorney fees | Agreement, within a reasonableness standard | No statutory percentage schedule |
| Executor compensation | The executor, subject to being reasonable | Optional; many family executors waive it |
| Certified copies, appraisals, other fees | The provider | Small, but they add up |

The sections below take them one at a time. Do not treat any of these as a quote. Confirm the current amount with your county clerk.

## How much is the court filing fee?

It depends on the county. The Cook County Clerk of the Circuit Court publishes a probate filing fee schedule that lists $479 as the filing fee for an independent administration and for a probate of a decedent's estate, effective October 1, 2025. That fee does not rise with the size of the estate in the schedule we reviewed. Other counties publish their own schedules, and fees change. Our [table of Illinois probate filing fees by county](/estate-planning/illinois-probate-fees) lists what ten county clerks charge, with a link to each schedule. Check your clerk's website for the current figure. Extra court fees can come up if there are petitions, contested matters or additional certified copies.

## What does newspaper publication cost?

In a formal estate, the executor must publish a claims notice once a week for three weeks and mail notice to known creditors (755 ILCS 5/18-3). The newspaper charges for the ad, and prices differ by paper and by length of the notice. We do not list an average here. Ask the newspaper for a quote when you open the estate. If you also must publish notice to unknown heirs, that is a separate expense.

## Is a bond required, and what does it cost?

A bond is insurance for the estate. If the executor mishandles funds, the bond company pays and then looks to the executor. Courts may require a bond and a bonding company, called a surety, can charge a premium based on the size of the estate and the executor's credit. Many wills ask that the executor serve without surety. Whether the court agrees can depend on the facts, such as who the heirs are and whether any are minors. We could not confirm the exact bond rules on a primary source for this page, so ask the clerk about your case. Our attorneys can explain this in your county.

## How are attorney fees set in Illinois?

Illinois does not set attorney fees as a percentage of the estate in the way some states do. Fees are generally based on what is reasonable for the work, and heirs and the court can ask questions if they seem too high. Lawyers use several models:

- Hourly: you pay for time spent, which is common when an estate is complicated or contested.
- Flat fee: a fixed price for a defined scope, which is more common for simple estates.
- A hybrid: a flat fee for the standard steps and hourly rates for extras.

Whatever the model, ask for a written agreement that lists what is included. Ask what happens if a dispute starts. Our guide to [whether you need a probate lawyer](/learn/probate/do-i-need-a-probate-lawyer) can help you decide how much help to hire.

## Can the executor be paid?

Yes. An executor may receive reasonable compensation for their services. The money is taxable income to the executor. Many executors who are also heirs waive the fee, since they would pay income tax on it and could receive the same money as an inheritance. If a person other than a family member serves, expect them to charge. If heirs disagree with the amount, they can raise it with the court. For related issues see [what an executor may take from the estate](/learn/probate/can-an-executor-take-money-from-the-estate).

## What other costs come up?

- Certified death certificates and certified copies of letters of office.
- Appraisals for real estate, jewelry or a business.
- Estate tax preparation and the decedent's final income tax return. See [final tax returns](/learn/after-a-death/final-tax-returns).
- Upkeep on a house while it is for sale, such as utilities, insurance and lawn care.
- Accounting fees, if the estate is large.

Illinois also has its own estate tax for large estates. That is a tax, not a probate cost, and our [Illinois estate tax article](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax) covers it.

## Does the size of the estate change the cost?

Mostly through the work, not through a formula. A bigger estate usually means more accounts, more property to value and more creditors to notice, so there are more hours to bill. Real estate adds appraisals and sale costs. A business interest or property in another state can add a second court case; see [ancillary probate](/learn/probate/ancillary-probate). A tiny estate with one bank account costs far less to settle, and may not need a court case at all.

Contested estates cost the most. A will contest or a fight over accounting can use up months of attorney time, and those fees come out of the estate unless a court orders otherwise.

## What questions should you ask before you hire an attorney?

- What is included in the fee, and what is billed extra?
- Is the fee flat, hourly or a mix, and how often will I get a bill?
- Who will do the work, and what are their rates?
- How are court costs, publication and copies billed?
- What happens to the fee if the estate turns out to be contested?
- Can I receive an itemized statement for the closing report?

Heirs have a right to see how the money was spent, and an executor who can show clear records is in a stronger position.

## How can you lower probate costs?

1. Keep the estate simple by naming beneficiaries on accounts. See [beneficiary designations](/learn/beneficiary-designations).
2. Check whether the estate qualifies for the [small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit), which avoids opening an estate.
3. Choose independent administration so there are fewer court hearings.
4. Get a written fee agreement and ask for an itemized bill.
5. Plan ahead with a [living trust](/learn/illinois/illinois-living-trusts) or a [transfer on death instrument](/learn/illinois/illinois-transfer-on-death-instrument) for real estate.

You can also try our [probate cost estimator](/tools/probate-cost-estimator) for a rough, non-binding estimate.

## Example: Marcus and a simple estate

Marcus, 52, serves as executor for his father's estate in Cook County. His father owned a small house and a checking account in his own name. Marcus pays the clerk's filing fee, pays the newspaper for the creditor notice and hires an attorney under a written flat-fee agreement for the standard steps. Marcus waives executor pay, since he is also an heir. All of these costs come out of the estate account, and Marcus lists each one in his closing report.
