Tanya, 38, lives in Naperville with her son Eli, 6. His father lives in another state and sees Eli twice a year. Tanya has a $450,000 term life policy through work and about $60,000 in a 401(k). She has never signed a will. If something happened to her tomorrow, who would raise Eli, and who would manage the money?

This page walks a single parent through the pieces of a plan: the guardian, the money, the other parent and the emergency papers. It covers Illinois rules and a clear order of steps. See the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois) for the wider view.

## What happens to my child if I die and I am the only parent in the home?

It depends on the other parent. If the other parent is alive, has legal rights and is fit, they generally keep or take custody. Our article on whether [the surviving parent gets custody](/learn/guardianship/does-the-surviving-parent-get-custody-if-you-die) explains the usual rule. A guardian named in your will matters when the other parent has died, cannot be found, has lost their rights or is unfit.

If you name a guardian and the other parent is alive, the court weighs your choice but does not ignore the other parent's rights. If you have a custody order, a parenting plan or a child support order, bring it to your attorney so the plan fits it. Read our page on [naming a guardian for your children in Illinois](/learn/illinois/naming-a-guardian-for-your-children) for the choice itself.

## Which documents does a single parent need?

Use this table as a checklist.

| Document | What it does | Why a single parent needs it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Will with guardian clause | Names who raises your child and who serves as executor | Gives the court your choice if the other parent cannot serve |
| Trust or testamentary trust for the child | Holds money until ages you choose | A child cannot manage a large inheritance |
| Short-term guardian authorization | Lets a trusted person act for up to 365 days | Covers hospital stays and travel |
| Power of attorney for property | Lets someone pay your bills if you are ill | No second parent is available to step in |
| Health care power of attorney | Lets someone make medical choices for you | Hospital needs an agent |
| Beneficiary forms updated | Sends insurance and retirement money to the trust | Forms beat your will |

## How do you set up a plan step by step?

1. Choose a guardian and a backup. Ask both. Pick a home where your child will be stable and loved. Our [guardian picker tool](/tools/guardian-picker) can help you compare options.
2. Choose a trustee for the money. It can be the guardian or a different person. Some parents split the roles so each checks the other.
3. Count what your child would need. Use the [life insurance needs tool](/tools/life-insurance-needs) and the [guardian fund calculator](/tools/guardian-fund-calculator).
4. Sign a will that names the guardian, the executor and a trust for the child.
5. Change the beneficiary on your life insurance and retirement accounts to the trust. Never name a minor child directly.
6. Sign a short-term guardian paper for emergencies, with two adult witnesses (755 ILCS 5/11-5.4).
7. Write a letter of instruction about school, doctors, routines and who should stay in contact.
8. Review everything after a move, remarriage, birth or change in custody.

## How should money reach your child?

Through a trust or a custodian. Without a plan, a court may appoint a person to manage a minor's money, and the child usually receives it outright at 18 or 21. An Illinois UTMA account ends at age 21 (760 ILCS 20/21) and costs little to set up, but a large sum in the hands of a 21-year-old may not be what you want. A trust lets you set ages, such as one-third at 25, one-third at 30 and the rest at 35, and let the trustee pay for school and health care first. See our page on [UTMA accounts and gifts to minors in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-utma-accounts-and-gifts-to-minors) and the guide to [leaving money to minors](/learn/guardianship/leaving-money-to-minors).

For Tanya, a $450,000 policy plus $60,000 in a 401(k) is $510,000. If Eli would live with a guardian until 18, a trust can pay the guardian a monthly amount, cover tuition and hold the balance for his future.

## What about the other parent's role in the money?

Think carefully. If the other parent is the guardian, they may also be the natural choice to manage the funds, but many single parents prefer a separate trustee. If the other parent is not involved, naming them could put the money in their control through the child. Your attorney can explain how a trust protects the money while the other parent raises the child. Child support may continue from the other parent, and the child may also qualify for Social Security survivor benefits if you have worked long enough, as described at ssa.gov. Neither replaces a plan.

## What if you are never married to the other parent, or the other parent is not in the picture?

Legal parentage matters. If paternity was never established, the other parent may have no legal rights to custody, or may not be able to inherit from your child. If a parentage order or acknowledgment exists, it gives that parent standing. Bring any court order, parenting plan or support order to your attorney, because it affects who the court would look to first. If the other parent has no involvement, say so in your planning notes and name a strong backup guardian.

If your child has a disability, a different set of tools applies. A special needs trust protects benefits, as explained in [special needs planning in Illinois](/learn/illinois/special-needs-planning-in-illinois-for-a-disabled-child). If grandparents help raise your child, see the guardianship page on [naming a grandparent as guardian](/learn/guardianship/can-grandparents-be-named-guardian).

## What can go wrong?

- The guardian lives far away and the child changes schools. Talk to them first.
- Insurance names an ex-spouse, or the child, by mistake.
- The will is signed but the beneficiary forms still name an old person.
- No backup guardian is named.
- The short-term papers were never signed, so a school will not accept a relative.
