If your mother or father lived in Bloomington, Normal, LeRoy or Heyworth, the estate is most likely a McLean County matter, and many people search for "probate Bloomington IL" first. McLean County sits in the 11th Judicial Circuit, and its probate cases run through the Law and Justice Center downtown. This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

It covers the building, the Circuit Clerk, e-filing, the original will, remote hearings, self-help and case lookup. It also looks at a common family problem: siblings who inherit equal shares with no will and must decide what to do with the parent's house. For your own situation, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-mclean-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the McLean County Law and Justice Center?

The Illinois Courts directory lists the Law and Justice Center at 104 W. Front Street, Bloomington, IL 61701. Its mailing address is PO Box 2400, Bloomington, IL 61701. The main phone is (309) 888-5254, and hours are 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

The Circuit Clerk's Civil Division handles probate, which the clerk lists as case type "Probate (PR)". It is on the 4th floor, Room 404. The probate line is (309) 888-5350, and the general Civil Division line is (309) 888-5340.

The county's own address, 115 E Washington Street, is a separate building. Probate families go to Front Street.

| Place | Where | What a probate family uses it for |
|---|---|---|
| Circuit Clerk, Civil and Probate | 104 W. Front Street, 4th floor, Room 404 | Probate case files, filing questions |
| E-filing kiosk | 4th floor, Circuit Clerk's office | Sending documents electronically |
| Law Library and self-help | 6th floor, same building | Terminals, scanners, limited e-filing help |

The directory also shows a Court Disability Coordinator at (309) 888-5266 for accessibility needs. It did not list parking, so ask the court before you go.

## What should the family gather first?

Start with the will, if there is one, and a rough list of what the person owned. [Finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois) explains where wills turn up.

Then sort the list. Accounts with a named beneficiary and jointly held property usually pass outside court. A house in the deceased person's name alone usually points to probate. The [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter) shows which pile each item lands in.

## How does an estate open, and what happens to the original will?

Illinois law requires whoever holds a will after a death to deliver it to the circuit clerk. The page on [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) covers that duty and its timing.

McLean County's pages do not describe will drop-off. The e-filing page does not address wills, and the Civil and Probate page does not either. So call the probate line at (309) 888-5350 before you bring the paper will, and ask what the clerk wants.

The case itself is then filed electronically. The clerk's e-filing page says all civil cases filed in McLean County have been required to be filed electronically since January 1, 2018. It says the clerk no longer accepts pleadings, petitions or related documents in person or by mail.

The page refers readers to Court Rule 3 for e-filing exceptions, and it links to eFileIL and a rejection standards document. Rejections come from things like the wrong case type or an illegible scan, so check each file before sending.

After that, the steps are the usual ones. [How to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) and the [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) walk through the petition, notice, claims and closing.

## Can I attend a McLean County hearing remotely?

Often, yes. The Circuit Clerk's Remote Hearings page explains how to join by video and by phone, and it lists Zoom courtrooms by judge. One judge's room is listed for law, chancery, foreclosure and probate matters.

To join by video, download the Zoom app, find your judge, and click the Zoom link. Then wait in the waiting room until the judge admits you.

The page is firm about names. You must reset your screen name to match your name on the court schedule, and you may be denied entry if you cannot be identified.

If you cannot use video, dial one of the Zoom phone numbers on the page. Then enter the meeting ID and password from your Notice of Remote Hearing. The page lists +1 312 626 6799 as a Chicago-area number.

Recording a proceeding on any device is strictly prohibited, with a narrow exception for approved media. The county refers to Circuit Rule 4 of the 11th Judicial Circuit court rules for remote hearings.

The three-business-day written permission rule on that page is for felony courtrooms. The page does not give a separate request deadline for probate, so ask the clerk or your attorney. It also lists no direct phone for remote hearing questions.

## What self-help does the county list?

The county's help page for people without a lawyer lists the Law Library on the 6th floor of the Law and Justice Center. It says the library is open for self-help Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., with public terminals and scanners. Volunteer help hours vary, so call 309-888-4801 first.

The same page says limited e-filing assistance is available at the Law and Justice Center for people without an attorney. The Circuit Clerk's office on the 4th floor also has terminals and scanners.

Illinois Court Help takes calls or texts at 833-411-1121. The page lists initial hours of 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., Monday through Friday. Court-approved forms are on the county's Forms and Resources page, with a fee waiver form and instructions for civil cases.

The page does not describe probate help specifically, so ask what the library has before you go. For doing it yourself, read [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer). For court costs, see [the Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver).

## How do I look up a McLean County case?

Use the Circuit Clerk's Court Records Search. For civil cases, which include probate on the clerk's division list, you can search by name, business name or case number.

Coverage is limited. Open civil cases are available from 2008 forward, and closed civil cases for ten years from today's date. The page says the data is not pulled from a real-time system, so recent events may not appear yet. It also says the site is not the official court record.

If a name and case number return "no information available," the page says to submit a written inquiry to the Circuit Clerk. The page does not describe probate searching in particular, so a missing estate file is a reason to call (309) 888-5350.

For much older records, the clerk's genealogy page links to a probate record index for 1834 to 1934 and a will record index for 1838 to 1940. If you are not sure whether an estate was ever opened for a relative, see [how to find out if you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois).

## What if siblings inherit a Bloomington-Normal house with no will?

Here is the angle most county pages skip. A parent dies without a will, owning a house alone, and three children are the only heirs. Under 755 ILCS 5/2-1, as the page on [who inherits without a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-inherits-without-a-will-in-illinois) explains, children with no surviving spouse share equally.

Equal shares do not mean the house is theirs the next day. As the page on a [parent who died with the house in their name](/learn/illinois/parent-died-house-in-their-name) explains, no child can sign a listing, contract or deed until a court appoints an administrator. That is a probate case, filed with the clerk in Room 404.

Once title reaches the children, they hold shares of the whole house. Then the choice is the one covered in [siblings who disagree about an inherited house](/learn/illinois/siblings-disagree-inherited-house): talk, buyout, mediation or, last, a court-ordered partition.

| Choice | What it means for equal-share siblings | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Keep together | All three own it and share costs | Taxes, insurance and repairs need a written split |
| Buyout | One sibling pays the others for their shares | Use an appraisal, not an argument |
| Sell | The estate or owners list it and divide proceeds | All must agree on price and timing |
| Partition | A court orders a sale if they cannot agree | Slowest and most costly path |


Two habits help. First, agree early on who pays the taxes and insurance while the estate is open. The page on [who pays for an inherited house](/learn/property-and-assets/who-pays-for-an-inherited-house-upkeep-taxes-and-reimbursing-the-sibling-who-fronts-it) covers credit for the one who fronts the money. Second, read about [buying out a sibling](/learn/property-and-assets/buying-out-a-sibling-or-co-owner-of-an-inherited-house-or-forcing-a-partition) before anyone names a price.

If the house stays in shared title for years, the situation gets harder with each death. The page on [heirs property](/learn/property-and-assets/heirs-property) explains why.

## Example: the Okafor siblings in Normal

This is an invented example. Ruth, 79, a widow, died in Normal with no will. Her house is in her name alone. Her three children, Denise, Paul and Mark, are her only heirs, so each is entitled to one third of the estate.

Denise, who lives in Bloomington, wants to keep the house. Paul wants to sell. Mark lives in another state and cannot travel. Denise agrees to serve as administrator, and the family's attorney files the petition electronically.

Mark joins the first hearing by Zoom, after changing his screen name to match the court schedule. Before anything is listed, the siblings sign a one-page agreement. Denise pays the taxes and insurance, and she has a set period to buy out her brothers at an appraised value. If she misses the date, the house is listed.

The agreement did not need a judge. It also avoided the partition case that could have followed if Paul and Denise had simply argued.
