If your mother lived in Champaign, Urbana, Rantoul or Mahomet, her estate belongs to Champaign County, Illinois, which is the Sixth Judicial Circuit. The courthouse is in Urbana, not in the city of Champaign, and that surprises many families who go looking for it.

This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). It covers where the courthouse and the Circuit Clerk are, how an estate opens and the original will is filed, remote appearances, self-help and case lookup. It ends with an example of an adult child handling an estate from another state. ## Where is the Champaign County courthouse for probate?

The Champaign County Courthouse is at 101 East Main Street, Urbana, IL 61801. The Illinois Courts directory lists the phone as (217) 384-3707 and the hours as 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The Circuit Clerk's website gives the same street address.

The Circuit Clerk's office is inside the courthouse. Its website lists a phone of (217) 384-3725 and Monday through Friday hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The pages I read do not say which courtroom hears estates, so the notice you receive is the place to find that.

| Place | Where | Why a probate family might go |
|---|---|---|
| Champaign County Courthouse | 101 East Main Street, Urbana | Courtrooms for hearings |
| Circuit Clerk's office | Same building | Filing the will, case files, copies |
| File Viewing Room | Inside the clerk's office | Paper files, digital files and old microfilm |
| Self-help center | First floor, off the main lobby | Forms help and general guidance |

## What should the family sort out before going to Urbana?

Start with the will and a list of what the person owned. Look in the home, a safe deposit box and the files of any attorney who drafted a will, as described in [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois).

Then sort the list. Accounts with a named beneficiary and jointly held property pass outside the court. A house in the deceased person's name alone usually points to probate. Illinois ties probate to the county where the person lived at death, and [which county handles probate when property is in more than one county](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties) covers the cabin-in-another-county problem.

## How is the original will filed in Champaign County?

The original will goes to the Circuit Clerk of the county where the person lived. The Illinois duty to file, and how it works, is explained in [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk). Read that page for the deadline rather than relying on a family rumor.

The Champaign County clerk's pages do not name a room or a counter for will filing, so call the clerk or ask at the office before you travel. The clerk's site says the office is proud of its collection of wills and probate files, which go back to the late 1800s.

When a petition to admit a will is filed, the Probate Court Guide says to attach a copy of the will as an exhibit with its file number. Champaign County uses a year, a case type and a number, with an example in the guide of 2025-WI-102.

A few habits help:

1. Make a copy of the original before you leave home.
2. Carry the original flat, without staples.
3. Write down the date of death and the person's full name.
4. Keep any receipt or file number the clerk gives you.

## How does an estate case open and move forward?

The clerk's Probate Court Guide, current as of May 2025, says a new estate generally needs three orders. One appoints the representative, one finds who the heirs are, and one approves the oath and bond. Petitions follow 755 ILCS 5/6-2 when there is a will naming an executor, and 755 ILCS 5/9-4 when there is no will or no available executor.

The petition must list every heir and legatee with an address, and it must name minors or disabled persons. Each petition for letters also needs an affidavit of heirship that states the exact relationship to the person who died. The guide says not to file an affidavit claiming there were no heirs.

An executor named in an admitted will generally does not need a hearing to receive letters. An administrator usually needs written waivers from every person with an equal or greater right to serve, or a hearing with notice. Any hearing needs 14 days of notice, unless all interested persons waive it.

On bonds, the guide says a personal surety bond is set at twice the personal estate and a corporate surety bond at 1.5 times, citing 755 ILCS 5/12-5. It says surety can be waived only by the person who made the will. For the steps in general, see [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) and the [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process).

## Does Champaign County require e-filing?

For civil cases, yes in practice. The clerk's page says the Illinois Supreme Court announced mandatory e-filing of civil cases in circuit courts by January 1, 2018. Both attorneys and self-represented people may e-file, using any certified e-file service provider through eFileIL.

A self-represented filer who cannot file electronically can use the clerk's good-cause form to ask the judge to allow paper filing. The Illinois Courts directory adds that the clerk keeps a computer in the file viewing room for e-filing by attorneys and the public.

The clerk's Commonly Used Case Forms page links the Champaign County Probate Court Guide and a fillable small estate affidavit. It also has a guardianship guide, which is a separate kind of case from probate.

## When does a small estate avoid the Champaign County court?

Sometimes there is no case to file. Picture an estate that is just a car and a bank account. The Illinois small estate affidavit lets a person collect personal property without opening probate, if no letters of office are outstanding and no petition is pending.

The [small estate affidavit page](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) gives the current personal estate limit and the rules for signing. Real estate cannot pass this way, so a house in one name alone usually brings the family back to the courthouse. The clerk's forms page lists the Champaign County version of the affidavit.

## Can I attend a Champaign County hearing remotely?

Often it is possible, but the answer depends on the case and the judge. The Sixth Circuit's local rule on remote appearance says civil participants may attend remotely without advance approval, while evidentiary hearings, settlement conferences and trials need the presiding judge's approval.

Champaign County's administrative order 2023-1 encourages judges to allow remote appearance in all proceedings when practicable. It requires in-person appearance for some case types unless a judge approves remote attendance beforehand. The closest match to estates is guardianship cases filed as GR or P, and the order does not name probate.

The Probate Court Guide adds that a notice allowing remote appearance must include the information needed to appear in person or remotely. For current instructions, the court's order lists the Court Administrator at (217) 384-1204 and the judge's clerk by courtroom email. Orders can change, so check before relying on this.

## What self-help does the Champaign County court offer?

The Illinois Courts directory says the legal self-help center is on the first floor of the courthouse, off the main lobby. A retired attorney navigator is listed on certain weekdays, and a JusticeCorps fellow or member staffs the room on most Tuesdays and Thursdays. Appointments are encouraged.

The Probate Court Guide is blunt about the limit: the court cannot advise litigants on how to proceed, and an attorney is required for any complex or contested proceeding. Read [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) before you decide how much to do yourself. For court costs, see [the Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver).

## How do I look up a Champaign County case?

Use the clerk's Public Court Case Record Search, linked from its Public Court Cases page. The clerk says most case files are public, with exceptions such as adoptions, sealed cases and juvenile matters. Even in public files, some documents are restricted, such as Social Security numbers and medical records.

You can also visit the File Viewing Room in the clerk's office during regular courthouse hours. It offers paper files, digital files and microfilm of the oldest cases. The clerk's record search and copy request pages cover certified copies.

If you are an heir and are not sure an estate was ever opened, see [how to find out if you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois). ## Example: Dana handles her mother's Mahomet estate from Phoenix

This is an invented example. Dana, 46, lives in Phoenix. Her mother, Elaine, lived in Mahomet and died in October. Elaine's will names Dana as executor. She owned a house in her name alone, an account of about $60,000 and a car. Dana's brother, Paul, lives in Urbana.

Dana hears that executors can serve from another state, as the page on [serving as an executor when you live in another state](/learn/illinois/non-resident-executor-in-illinois) explains. She hires an Illinois attorney before she leaves home. The attorney tells her the court will want an Illinois agent for service of process, and that bond may be required.

Paul finds the original will in a desk and takes it to Urbana. Dana signs the sworn documents before a notary in Arizona, and the attorney e-files the petition. Because Dana is the named executor in an admitted will, she may not need a hearing to receive letters. If one is set, the notice must say how to join remotely.

Dana makes one trip to meet the attorney and look at the house. Paul handles the mail and the locks. She tracks her costs and time, as described in [how to be an executor when you live out of state](/learn/after-a-death/how-to-be-an-executor-when-you-live-out-of-state), and uses the [executor workload tool](/tools/executor-workload) to plan her hours.

## What should a family do first?

Work through the same short list, whether you live across town or across the country:

1. Find the original will and any codicils.
2. Order certified death certificates.
3. Sort assets into probate and non-probate piles.
4. Decide whether a small estate affidavit fits.
5. Read the clerk's Probate Court Guide before filing.
6. Talk to an Illinois attorney about the agent for service and the bond.
