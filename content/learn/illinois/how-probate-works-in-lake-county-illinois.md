If your mother or father lived in Lake County, Illinois, the estate will be handled by the 19th Judicial Circuit in Waukegan. That is true for a condo in Libertyville, a lakefront home in Lake Forest or an apartment in Mundelein. The state law is the same in every county, but the courthouse, the clerk and the filing habits are local.

This page, part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois), covers what the courts and clerk publish: where to go, how the will is filed, how an estate opens, what Zoom rules apply, where to find self-help and how to look up a case. If you want to talk through your own family's situation, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-lake-county-illinois&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the Lake County probate court?

It is in the Main Courthouse at 18 N. County St., Waukegan, IL 60085. The 19th Circuit's Probate Court page lists that address and gives 847-377-3260 for the Probate Division in the Circuit Clerk's Office. The courthouse main number is 847-377-3600, and the Illinois courts directory lists hours of 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. 

The directory also says the Circuit Clerk's Office is on the first floor and that clerk staff help people at e-filing kiosks inside the building. The clerk's website lists other branches in Mundelein, Park City and Round Lake. Treat those as general clerk locations, not as places to open an estate.

The case belongs here because of where the person lived. The clerk's probate page says the deceased must have been a Lake County resident, and our page on [which county handles probate](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties) explains the venue rule. An executor's home address does not change the county.

## How does a Lake County estate open, step by step?

The will goes to the clerk first, then an attorney files the petition, and a judge appoints the representative. The table below follows that order using the clerk's pages and the court's standing order. The deeper Illinois steps are in [the Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process).

| Step | What happens in Lake County | Source of the rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1. File the original will | Delivered to the Circuit Clerk, which keeps it on file | Clerk's Wills & Probate page |
| 2. Hire an attorney | The clerk says a petition for probate must be filed by an attorney | Clerk's Wills & Probate page |
| 3. File the petition | Petition for Probate and for Letters, e-filed through a certified provider | Clerk's e-filing page |
| 4. Get a court date | The clerk says the petition is filed before a date is assigned; estate dates are Mondays, Tuesdays and Thursdays at 9:00 a.m. | Clerk's page and standing order |
| 5. Send papers to the judge | Cover letter, file-stamped copies, will copy and proposed orders arrive ahead of the date | Probate standing order |
| 6. Hearing | The judge declares heirship, sets any bond and appoints the representative | Standing order |
| 7. Notice and creditors | Heirs are told in writing and a newspaper notice runs; creditors have six months | 19th Circuit decedent's estate page |
| 8. Close the estate | Final report, receipt and waiver, and an order of discharge | Clerk's forms list |

The standing order is detailed. It asks for a cover letter saying whether the estate is testate or intestate, the exact relief requested and, if a bond waiver is requested, the reason. It also lists three proposed orders: one declaring heirship, one for the oath of office and bond, and one appointing the representative.


## How and when do I file the original will in Lake County?

File it within 30 days of the death. The 19th Circuit's decedent's estate page says anyone holding a will must file it with the Circuit Clerk of the county where the person lived within 30 days after learning of the death. The clerk's page says the original must be submitted and that the clerk keeps it.

Two more clerk points matter. A will cannot be filed before the person has died. And the petition and the original will can be filed together. That is the common path when an attorney is already hired.

If you hold a will and no one has started a case, filing it still protects you. The court's page warns that hiding a will for 30 days or more, or altering or destroying it, can lead to criminal prosecution for theft. Our page on [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) covers the statute and what to do if the will is lost. Order certified copies of the death certificate early; [who can order Illinois death certificates](/learn/illinois/illinois-death-certificates-who-can-order-county-clerk-or-idph) explains how.

## Do I need a lawyer to open a Lake County estate?

Yes, to file the petition. The Circuit Clerk's Wills & Probate page states that petitions for probate must be filed by an attorney and that a self-represented person must hire one before opening a petition. The clerk's forms list is available whether or not there is a will, but that does not remove the attorney requirement.

Families still keep a lot of the work. The executor gathers records, protects the house and keeps the books, as [what does an executor do in Illinois](/learn/illinois/what-does-an-executor-do-in-illinois) describes. Many estates also avoid court entirely, which [the Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) and [handling an Illinois estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) explain.

## Can I appear by Zoom in a Lake County probate case?

Sometimes, but not by default for hearings. The standing order effective January 12, 2026 says hearings, pre-trial conferences, settlement conferences and trials are in person, and the court may allow Zoom only on a proper motion and good cause shown. Other matters may proceed in person or by Zoom.

The order also sets some habits. Participants must show their full name on Zoom, and attorneys and self-represented parties must show the case number too. Attorneys tell the clerk on entering whether the matter is contested or uncontested. Uncontested estate openings and closings are called first.

The order warns that even an agreed order can require an appearance or extra documents. If you live far away, ask your attorney early whether a motion is worth filing. 

## What self-help or legal aid does Lake County list?

The Probate Court page points to the Center for Self-Representation, a Probate Guide Handbook (PDF) and the court forms. The Center sits in the William D. Block Memorial Law Library at the courthouse, and its brochure gives 847-377-2800 and weekday hours of 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. It offers forms, procedure guidance and public computers.

Know the limits. The brochure says staff are not licensed attorneys, cannot give legal advice, cannot draft or review your documents and cannot go to court with you. The probate page itself says probate law is complex and suggests consulting an attorney. It does not list a legal aid provider, so we do not name one here.

For costs, the standing order says self-represented litigants can request fee waivers for expenses such as publication and birth certificates without a court appearance, by email or in person at the Clerk's Office. Our pages on [the Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver) and [Illinois probate costs](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-costs) cover the wider budget.

## How do I look up a Lake County probate case?

Use the Circuit Clerk's Online Portal, or ask the clerk. The portal page says users can access basic case information, download documents, pay fees and submit proposed orders. Attorneys and public users register on the portal, and parties of record submit a case access request that may take one to two business days.

Do not count on it being up. The clerk's site and the 19th Circuit's pages both showed alerts that the portal was temporarily offline when we checked.

For a general question, the clerk's Waukegan phone is 847-377-3380 and the Probate Division number is 847-377-3260. ## What if the family has a Wisconsin cabin, or heirs across the state line?

Lake County sits on the Wisconsin border, so this comes up here more than in most counties. Two separate questions are easy to confuse. One is where the case is heard. The other is where property is located.

If the person lived in Lake County, Illinois, the Illinois estate is opened in Waukegan. Land in another state is usually governed by that state's courts, which can mean a second case called [ancillary probate](/learn/probate/ancillary-probate). Our page on [an Illinois resident owning property in another state](/learn/illinois/illinois-resident-owning-property-in-another-state) explains the options, including a trust, which can avoid the second case. We do not describe Wisconsin procedure, which a Wisconsin attorney would confirm.

If the heir or executor is the one who lives in Wisconsin, the picture is simpler. Illinois lets a qualified U.S. resident serve as executor, though the court may require a bond. See [serving as executor of an Illinois estate when you live in another state](/learn/illinois/non-resident-executor-in-illinois) for the task list, and [being an executor from out of state](/learn/after-a-death/how-to-be-an-executor-when-you-live-out-of-state) for the practical side.

## Example: Priya and her father's Lake Forest house

This is an invented example. Priya, 48, lives in Kenosha, Wisconsin. Her father, Anand, lived in Lake Forest and died in October. His will names Priya as executor and splits everything with her sister Leena, who lives in Mundelein. Anand owned the house alone, and he also owned a small lake cottage in Wisconsin.

Leena holds the original will. Within a week she brings it to the Circuit Clerk in Waukegan and keeps a copy. They hire an Illinois attorney, who e-files the petition and asks the clerk for a Monday date. The attorney sends the court the cover letter, file-stamped copies and three proposed orders ahead of the hearing.

The attorney files a motion so Priya can appear by Zoom. The judge may or may not allow it, so Priya also plans the drive. The court appoints her, and she signs bond papers if the judge requires them.

The Lake Forest house goes through the Illinois estate. The Wisconsin cottage needs its own paperwork under Wisconsin law, which a Wisconsin attorney handles. Priya tracks both on a worksheet like the one in the [Lake County probate guide](/free/lake-county-illinois-probate-guide).

For neighboring counties, see [Cook County](/learn/illinois/how-probate-works-in-cook-county), [Kane County](/learn/illinois/how-probate-works-in-kane-county) and [DuPage County](/learn/illinois/how-probate-works-in-dupage-county). A rough sense of delay is on [how long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois).
