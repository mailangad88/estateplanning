Families tend to ask about cost after the shock of a diagnosis. The honest answer is a range, because the biggest factor is whether anyone fights. This page breaks the costs into parts so you can ask each professional for a number.

We do not quote exact court fees, because they change and differ by county. We explain what each item is, who normally pays and where to look. For the process itself, read our [overview of Illinois guardianship](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship). A death later creates a different bill, covered in [what probate costs in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-costs).

## What are the costs of starting the case?

The costs of starting an Illinois case fall into five groups:

- **Filing fee.** The circuit clerk charges a fee to open the case. The amount depends on the county, so ask the clerk.
- **Physician's report.** The petition needs a report from a licensed physician. Some doctors charge for the exam and the written report, and insurance may not cover it.
- **Service and notice.** The person must be served, and relatives must receive notice. Mailing and sheriff or process-server costs are small but real.
- **Guardian ad litem.** The court appoints one to investigate (755 ILCS 5/11a-10). They are paid for their time.
- **Attorney fees.** The petitioner usually has a lawyer. The person may have one too, either hired or appointed.

The court can order reasonable fees paid from the person's estate. Our article on [the rights of the person](/learn/illinois/rights-of-the-person-in-an-illinois-guardianship-case) explains why a lawyer and guardian ad litem are part of the case.

## What does it cost after the guardian is appointed?

The case does not end at the order. A guardian of the person reports to the court on the person's condition. A guardian of the estate files an inventory, accounts and sometimes more. A bond may be required, with an annual premium. The guardian may ask the court to approve fees for their own time, and an attorney may be needed for each accounting or major step such as a house sale.

Our articles on [guardian reports and accountings](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardian-reports-and-accountings) and [guardian bonds](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardian-bond) describe each requirement. Plan for these as annual costs, not one-time costs.

## Who pays for an Illinois guardianship?

The person under guardianship usually pays, through their own estate, when the court approves it. That is why costs matter: a $60,000 savings account can be reduced by fees. A family member who files may agree to pay and seek reimbursement from the estate. If the person has no money, the court may reduce or waive some costs. If no family can serve, a public or professional guardian may be appointed. See [professional and public guardians when no family can serve](/learn/guardianship/professional-and-public-guardians-when-no-family-can-serve).

## How does an agreed case compare with a contested one?

| | Agreed case | Contested case |
|---|---|---|
| Hearing | Short, often one date | Long, may need several dates |
| Lawyers | Petitioner's lawyer, possibly appointed counsel | Both sides, plus guardian ad litem |
| Jury | Not requested | May be requested |
| Medical evidence | One physician's report | Competing medical opinions |
| Total cost | Lower | Often many times higher |
| Time | Weeks to a few months | Months or longer |

## Is the cost worth it?

Sometimes the answer is yes. If a person is being exploited, refusing needed care or sitting in a hospital with no decision-maker, a court order can protect their money and health and the fees are small by comparison. The point of listing costs is not to discourage a needed case. It is to help you choose the narrowest order, choose the right guardian and avoid paying twice.

## How can families keep costs down?

1. Ask whether a power of attorney can still be signed, since that avoids the case entirely.
2. Ask for limited powers only, which means fewer ongoing tasks.
3. Gather medical records before meeting the lawyer.
4. Agree on who serves before filing, so there is no contest.
5. Ask for a written fee estimate and a billing schedule.
6. Ask the clerk about fee waivers and local forms.
7. Keep careful records so each accounting is short.

## Example: Linda and her mother

Linda, 55, plans to file for guardianship of her mother, Joan, 83, who has about $75,000 in savings and a small pension. Everyone agrees. Linda collects the doctor's report, hires a lawyer, pays the clerk's filing fee, and the court names a guardian ad litem. These are the same line items listed above. The court orders the guardian ad litem's fee and attorney fees paid from Joan's savings. Linda files a first inventory and then a yearly report, which the lawyer prepares for a smaller fee.

If Joan had signed a power of attorney two years earlier, none of these court costs would have arisen. See [the Illinois power of attorney for property](/learn/illinois/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property) and the [alternatives to guardianship](/learn/special-needs/guardianship-alternatives-for-adult-children).

## What costs are easy to miss?

Some costs do not appear on the first estimate:

- Certified copies of the order and letters, needed by each bank and agency.
- Recording fees if a real estate sale or lien release is part of the case.
- Time off work for hearings and filings, which falls on the family.
- The cost of a second physician report if the first is too old by the hearing.
- Court-ordered mediation, if the family disagrees.

Add a cushion to your plan for these items. A family that budgets for a year of reporting is less likely to feel surprised.

## How do these costs compare with planning ahead?

Signing a property power of attorney, a health care power of attorney and a will is a one-time, predictable cost, usually much lower than a contested court case and its yearly reports. It cannot be done after capacity is gone, which is why timing matters. See our [Illinois estate planning cost](/learn/illinois/estate-planning-cost) article and [the cost of long-term care](/learn/elder-care/how-much-does-a-nursing-home-cost-per-month), which shows what the person's money may also need to cover. A guardianship does not pay for care. It only lets someone arrange it.
