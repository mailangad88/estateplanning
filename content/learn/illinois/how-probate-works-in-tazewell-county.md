When a parent or spouse dies in Pekin, East Peoria, Morton or Washington, the estate is a Tazewell County, Illinois matter, not a Virginia one. Tazewell County sits in the 10th Judicial Circuit with Peoria, Marshall, Putnam and Stark counties. This page belongs to the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

It covers where the courthouse and clerk are, how an estate opens, what to do with the original will, remote appearances, self-help and case lookup. It also covers a worry that comes up often: only a copy of the will can be found. If you want to talk to an Illinois estate planning attorney, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-tazewell-county&topic=probate).

This is general education from official county and court pages read in November 2026, not legal advice. Phone numbers, rooms and rules change, so confirm them with the clerk before you travel.

## Where is the Tazewell County Courthouse and the Circuit Clerk?

The Tazewell County Courthouse is at 342 Court Street, Pekin, IL 61554. The Circuit Clerk's page gives that same street address, hours of 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday, and a main phone of 309-477-2214. The county's courts page lists the administrative office in Room 102 at 309-477-2201.

The Illinois Courts directory lists the building as the Tazewell County Courthouse in the 10th Judicial Circuit and gives the same address. The county's pages do not give a room number for the clerk's counter, and they do not describe a probate division. Ask on the phone where to go once you are inside.

## How does an estate open, and what do you do with the original will?

An estate opens when someone files a petition with the Circuit Clerk in the county where the person lived. A named executor files to admit the will and ask for letters of office. If there is no will, a relative asks to be appointed administrator. The longer walk-through is in [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois).

The original will is a separate and earlier step. Under 755 ILCS 5/6-1, anyone holding the original must file it with the clerk immediately after the death. The page on [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) explains the duty and the 30-day clocks in section 6-3.

In practice:

1. Keep the original flat and unstapled, and do not write on it.
2. Photograph or scan every page for your own records.
3. Bring the original to the clerk in Pekin, or ask the clerk whether it can be delivered another way.
4. Ask for a receipt that shows the date and the case or will number.
5. Tell the named executor and the family where the original now is.

Filing the will with the clerk preserves the document. It does not by itself give anyone authority over a bank account or a house. That comes from the court's order and letters of office after the petition is heard.

## Does a small estate need the court at all?

Sometimes not. The clerk's forms page lists a Small Estate Affidavit, and the Illinois statute caps the personal property that route can cover at $150,000 as of 2026, not counting registered vehicles, per the page on the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit). It cannot move real estate.

The forms page has no probate, estate or will category, so do not expect a full set of probate forms there. A family with a house, or with more than the limit, usually needs a petition. See [independent versus supervised administration](/learn/illinois/independent-vs-supervised-administration-in-illinois) for how the court then oversees the executor.

## What if the original will cannot be found, or only a copy turns up?

Start with a careful search and a written log. The page on [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois) lists the usual places: the home, the drafting attorney, a safe deposit box, the named executor, relatives and the circuit clerk in the county where the person lived. For Tazewell families that last stop is a phone call to the Pekin clerk.

The Safe Deposit Box Opening Act (755 ILCS 15/1) lets an interested person watch a bank open a box to look for a will. The bank sends any will it finds to the circuit clerk, not to the family. You can use the [Illinois lost or missing will guide](/free/illinois-lost-or-missing-will-guide) and the [search kit for the will and assets](/free/illinois-find-the-will-and-assets-kit) to keep the search in order.

If only a copy exists, a copy is evidence, not the will. The page on [what to do if you only have a copy](/learn/illinois/lost-will) explains that the family must show the will was validly signed, was lost and not revoked, and what it said. The page on [the presumption of revocation](/learn/illinois/lost-original-will-in-illinois) explains why a will last kept by its maker is the hard case.

| Where the original was last kept | How hard is the case | What helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In the parent's own home | Harder, because the court may presume the parent destroyed it | Proof others had access, a fire or flood, the parent's statements |
| With the drafting attorney | Usually easier | The firm's receipt, vault record, file copy and witnesses |
| Filed with a clerk during life | Usually easier | The clerk's receipt and records |

Do not hand a lone copy to a relative to "sit on", and do not tear up anything. An unfiled original or a hidden will can carry criminal penalties under the same section 6-1. If the court does not accept the copy, an earlier will or the intestacy rules in 755 ILCS 5/2-1 decide, as described in [who inherits without a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-inherits-without-a-will-in-illinois).

## Can you attend a Tazewell County hearing remotely?

Sometimes. The county's courts page lists an administrative order titled CourtCall Remote Appearances in Tazewell County, Courtroom 101 and Courtroom 308. The clerk's forms menu also has a Remote Hearing Request link.

Neither page says how far ahead to ask, which hearings qualify or how a probate matter is treated. The judge decides whether a remote appearance is allowed in your case. Ask the clerk or the attorney handling the estate how your hearing will be run before you plan to join from another state.

## How do you e-file in Tazewell County?

The clerk's e-filing page sends filers to e-File Illinois and lists an Odyssey e-file support line, an E-Filing Waiver form, and guides on paying fees and adding a payment account. A separate guide for self-represented filers is linked from the county courts page.

The pages do not say whether probate petitions must be e-filed or when a waiver is granted. Treat that as a question for the clerk. Whatever the answer, the original will is the paper that cannot be replaced by a scan.

## What self-help does the Tazewell County court list?

Two resources appear on official pages. The Legal Self Help Center is in Room 103 at the courthouse. The county lists a navigator line at 309-478-5790 and an email address, lawlibrary@tazewell-il.gov.

The Self-Represented Litigant Help Desk is run by the 10th Circuit Pro Bono Committee of the Public Interest Law Initiative. Volunteer attorneys give brief advice, referrals and limited scope help for about 30 minutes per person. You must have no lawyer on the matter and household income within 200% of the federal poverty guidelines.

These offices can point you to forms and resources. They do not replace an attorney who reads your will and your family facts. The page on [handling an Illinois estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) describes what a do-it-yourself executor takes on.

## How do you look up a Tazewell County case?

The clerk's site links to an online court records portal, a hearings search and a mobile app. The county notes that Judici is no longer used. It also warns that, under the state's Remote Access Policy effective January 1, 2020, online access may show less than the courthouse does.

So a missing document online does not mean the case has none. Call the clerk at 309-477-2214 or visit 342 Court Street. For how estate files become part of the public record, see [is probate public](/learn/probate/is-probate-public) and [how to look up a probate case](/learn/probate/how-to-look-up-a-probate-case).

## Example: the Reyes family in Morton

This is an invented example. Dolores Reyes, 79, lived in Morton and died in October. Her son Mateo found a clean photocopy of her 2018 will in a folder, with two witness signatures and the name of a Peoria lawyer. The original was not in her desk.

Mateo searched the house, then called the bank about a safe deposit box, and the lawyer's office about its files. The lawyer's file held a signed copy with an attestation clause, and the lawyer's staff remembered the signing. Mateo logged each call with a date.

He then called the Pekin clerk to ask whether any will had been filed. None had. An attorney reviewed the log and the file copy, and the family learned that the court would need proof the will was lost and not revoked. Dolores had kept the original in a dresser that was emptied after a burst pipe. His sister Inez was told early, so no one felt kept in the dark. The case was now an attorney's matter, not a form at the self-help center.

## What mistakes should a Tazewell family avoid?

- Assuming a photocopy is enough and skipping the search.
- Waiting for the whole family to agree before the original reaches the clerk.
- Relying on the online portal to prove a document was or was not filed.
- Mixing up Tazewell County, Illinois with Tazewell County, Virginia when searching for forms or phone numbers.
- Distributing property before anyone has letters of office or a court order.
