If your mother or father lived in Ogle County, Illinois, in Oregon, Rochelle, Byron, Mount Morris, Polo or one of the farm towns around them, the court work runs through the Judicial Center in Oregon and its Circuit Clerk. The probate law is the same across the state. What changes is the building, the phone line and the habits of the local court.

This page, part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois), covers where the court sits, how an estate opens, how the will is filed, what the court says about remote hearings, and what help it lists for people without lawyers. It also takes up a situation common in small counties: the executor lives in another state. If you want to talk through your own family, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-ogle-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the Ogle County probate court?

The Ogle County Judicial Center is at 106 S. 5th Street, Oregon, IL 61061. The Illinois courts directory lists it in the 15th Judicial Circuit, with a courthouse phone of 815-732-3201 and hours of 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. It also lists free on-street parking and a southeast parking lot.

The Circuit Clerk's contact page gives the same building, Suite 300, with civil division phone 815-732-1130 and the same weekday hours. One other clerk page mentions Suite 200, so ask the front desk which floor you need.

Do not confuse the clerk with the County Clerk. The County Clerk, at 105 S. 5th Street, Suite 104, handles birth, death and marriage records. For certified copies, see [who can order Illinois death certificates](/learn/illinois/illinois-death-certificates-who-can-order-county-clerk-or-idph).

Venue follows where the person lived at death (755 ILCS 5/5-1, covered in [which county handles probate when property is in more than one county](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties)). The pages on Ogle County did not list a probate room, so call first.

## Does the estate need a court case at all?

Often it does not. A payable-on-death account or a jointly titled car goes straight to the survivor. If what remains is small and includes no land, the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) may be enough. Farmland or a house titled only to the parent is the usual reason a family ends up in court, and the guide to [handling an Illinois estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) shows how families decide.

## How does an estate open in Ogle County, and where does the original will go?

Whoever holds the original will files it with the circuit clerk of the proper county immediately after the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1, from the page on [filing the original will](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk)). A named executor then has 30 days after learning of the nomination to start probate or decline (755 ILCS 5/6-3). A photocopy cannot stand in for the original.

Filing the will preserves it. A petition is what starts the estate, as the page on the [petition for letters of office](/learn/illinois/illinois-petition-for-letters-of-office) explains.

| Stage | What happens | Ogle County detail |
|---|---|---|
| Will filed | The original is deposited where the person lived | Ogle's clerk sits in the Oregon Judicial Center; the site gives no will intake steps |
| Petition | Requests probate of the will and names the proposed executor | Civil filings in Illinois go through e-filing, per the clerk |
| Hearing | Judge admits the will and appoints the representative | Zoom links for court are posted by the clerk |
| Letters | Clerk issues letters of office | Ask how to get certified copies |
| Notices | Each heir is mailed a notice no later than 14 days after the order (755 ILCS 5/6-10) | The representative or their lawyer sends it |
| Claims | Creditors get six months from the first newspaper notice, or three months from a mailed one, whichever ends later (755 ILCS 5/18-3) | A local paper runs the notice |
| Closing | An independent estate files a final report and waits 42 days (755 ILCS 5/28-11) | The report goes to the Oregon clerk |

These dates are drawn from [the Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) and the steps in [how to open a probate estate](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois). For a month-by-month view, read [how long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois).

## Can I attend an Ogle County hearing by Zoom?

Possibly. The 15th Circuit's local rule on remote appearances says all courts in the circuit use Zoom, and that courtroom links are posted on the circuit website and on each county's website. The Ogle County clerk's site has a Zoom Links for Court page.

The rule ties remote appearance to Illinois Supreme Court Rule 45 and certain criminal statutes. For a person who is not covered, the usual route is a written motion, unless a judge's standing order or a case-specific exception allows it. The rule text did not mention probate, so do not assume your hearing is remote.

Participants must use their real names on Zoom. Anyone testifying remotely must appear by both video and audio. Ask your attorney or the clerk how your date is set.

## What help does the court list for people without a lawyer?

The Illinois courts directory says a Self-Help Center is on the third floor of the Judicial Center, with an e-filing kiosk and a law library. Illinois Legal Aid Online describes the Ogle County Legal Self-Help Center as offering legal information, forms, court procedure information and referrals to free and low-cost legal services, and states that it does not give legal advice. The phone listed there is 815-732-3201.

Neither page mentions probate. Call before you rely on the center for an estate.

The clerk's pages also list the e-filing portal, a forms page and a statement that clerk staff are not attorneys and cannot give legal advice. For costs, see [Illinois probate costs](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-costs) and the [Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver).

## How do I look up an Ogle County case online?

The clerk links a Judici search for Ogle County cases, along with a daily court docket. A Resource Room in the clerk's office lets the public view case information. None of those pages say which case types they cover, so call 815-732-1130 if an estate does not appear. If you suspect a parent left you something, [checking whether you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois) walks through the options.

## What if the executor lives in another state?

This is the situation where Ogle County's size matters. A parent stays on the home place in Oregon or Polo, and the child named executor moved to Texas or Oregon, the state, decades ago. The child can still serve.

Illinois asks that an executor be a U.S. resident, not an Illinois resident (755 ILCS 5/6-13, from [who can be an executor in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-can-be-an-executor-in-illinois)). The court may require a bond from a non-resident even if the will excuses one, per the page on the [non-resident executor](/learn/illinois/non-resident-executor-in-illinois). The general logistics are in [how to be an executor when you live out of state](/learn/after-a-death/how-to-be-an-executor-when-you-live-out-of-state).

What changes in a rural county is mostly the local help:

- Choose an Illinois attorney who can file in Oregon and speak to the clerk.
- Name one person near the property who can check the house and the mail.
- Ask the clerk early whether a bond is likely.
- Ask whether your hearing can be remote, and how to get a motion on file if needed.
- Order several death certificates by mail.
- Keep one shared folder and a travel log.

The page on [caring for a deceased parent's house](/learn/illinois/taking-care-of-a-deceased-parents-house-in-illinois) covers insurance, winter care and mail. An executor three states away cannot walk down the hall to ask a question, so write down every answer you get from the clerk.

## Example: Dana in Phoenix and her father's house in Oregon

This is an invented example. Dana, 52, lives in Phoenix. Her father, Walter, lived in Oregon, Illinois, and died in October. His will names Dana executor and leaves everything equally to Dana and her brother Paul, who lives in Rockford. Walter owned his house alone and had a checking account in his own name.

Paul has the original will and files it with the Circuit Clerk. He calls 815-732-1130 first to ask how the office wants it delivered. Dana hires an Illinois attorney who files the petition electronically. Her attorney asks about a bond and whether she can join the hearing by Zoom.

Dana signs the oath and other sworn papers before a notary in Phoenix. After the judge appoints her and the clerk issues letters, she opens an estate account and sets up forwarding for the mail. A neighbor checks the house each week for a small agreed fee. Dana makes one trip to meet the attorney and sort papers. The claims and will contest periods close with nothing filed.

## What mistakes do Ogle County families make?

- Driving to the County Clerk's office instead of the Judicial Center, or the reverse.
- Mailing a photocopy of the will when the clerk needs the original.
- Assuming no estate was opened just because Judici shows nothing.
- Waiting to hire local help until the house has been empty for months.
