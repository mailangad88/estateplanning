The last child has moved out. The will you signed when they were small names your sister as guardian, holds everything in trust until age 25, and lists your brother as executor. Now the kids are 24, 28 and 31, and one lives in Denver.

This page, part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois), is a review for empty nesters: what changes, a document-by-document table and a worked example. If you would rather go through your own papers with someone, you can [book a plan review](/book?from=learn-updating-your-estate-plan-when-kids-are-grown-in-illinois&topic=review) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## What actually changes when your children are adults?

Four things, each pointing at a different document. The general version, for any growing family, is in [updating your plan when your family grows](/learn/life-stages/updating-your-plan-when-your-family-grows).

1. **Who needs protecting.** The kids no longer need a guardian. You and your spouse are now more likely to need someone to act for you.
2. **Who can help.** Adult children can serve as executor, trustee or agent.
3. **What "fair" means.** Equal shares were the obvious default. Adult children may have very different incomes and needs.
4. **What threatens the money.** For an adult child the risks are a divorce, a creditor or a spending problem.

## Do you still need a guardian clause?

Usually it stops doing its main job, but do not just delete it. A guardian for a minor is only needed while a child is under 18, so once your youngest is an adult the nomination has nothing to act on. See [naming a guardian for your children in Illinois](/learn/illinois/naming-a-guardian-for-your-children) for how the nomination works.

Two situations keep some version of it alive:

- **An adult child with a disability.** A child who will need help after 18 is a separate planning job. Start with [guardianship alternatives for adult children](/learn/special-needs/guardianship-alternatives-for-adult-children) and [special needs planning in Illinois](/learn/illinois/special-needs-planning-in-illinois-for-a-disabled-child).
- **Minor grandchildren.** Under Illinois rules, a gift to a descendant who dies before you usually passes to that person's own descendants unless the will says otherwise (755 ILCS 5/4-11, discussed on [can you disinherit a child in Illinois](/learn/illinois/can-you-disinherit-a-child-in-illinois)). A minor grandchild cannot hold that money directly, so your plan should name a trustee to manage it.

## How should you review each document?

Start with the forms and powers of attorney, because they act without a court.

| Document | What it was built for | What to check now that the kids are grown | What the fix usually is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beneficiary forms (401(k), IRA, life insurance, POD accounts) | Often "my estate" or a minor's trust | Do they name the children directly, the right trust, or an ex-spouse? Are backups named? | New forms with each company; name a child's trust share if you use one |
| Power of attorney for property | Your spouse, then a sibling | Is an adult child a better successor agent? | New statutory short form, signed with one witness and a notary (755 ILCS 45/3-3) |
| Health care power of attorney | Your spouse, then a sibling | Which child lives close and can handle a hospital conversation? | New form, signed with one adult witness (755 ILCS 45/4-10) |
| Will: guardian clause | Who raises minor children | Is any child still under 18 or unable to manage alone? | Remove, keep or redirect toward grandchildren |
| Will: executor | A sibling or friend, because the kids were young | Should an adult child serve, and who is the backup? | Codicil or new will |
| Will or trust: payout ages | Money held until 25 or 30 | Have the children passed those ages already? | Rewrite shares as outright, lifetime trust or co-trustee trust |
| Trust: successor trustee | A sibling or bank | Can an adult child take over, alone or with help? | Trust amendment or restatement |
| House title or TODI | Passing to a spouse | Who gets it after both of you, and does one child want it? | New TODI or deed into trust |

The power of attorney rows often show the biggest gap, because the person you named at 40 may be your own age now. See [co-agents and successor agents in Illinois](/learn/illinois/co-agents-and-successor-agents-in-illinois) for how to line up a younger backup, and the [Illinois power of attorney for property](/learn/illinois/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property) page for the form itself.

## Should your adult child be your agent or executor?

Often yes, but pick by job, not by birth order. Illinois sets short legal tests for an executor in 755 ILCS 5/6-13: at least 18, a U.S. resident, not of unsound mind, not in prison and no felony conviction, with a narrow exception. A child who lives out of state can serve, though the court may require a bond, as explained on [who can be an executor in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-can-be-an-executor-in-illinois). The general tradeoffs are on [choosing an executor](/learn/wills/choosing-an-executor). Then ask:

- **Who lives nearest?** A health care agent may need to be at the hospital in an hour. The child in Denver can still be executor of an estate that is mostly accounts.
- **Who is organized and calm?** The job runs a year or more. A named executor has 30 days to act or decline after learning of the appointment, so ask first.
- **Will the siblings trust that child?** A child who is both executor and heir is common, and it works well when the family already gets along. [An executor who is also a beneficiary](/learn/probate/executor-who-is-also-a-beneficiary) covers the safeguards.

You can split the jobs: one child for health care, another for money, with the executor and successor trustee kept the same person.

## Equal or unequal shares among adult kids?

Either is allowed. Illinois gives adult children no forced share, so you can divide by need, by past help or by any reason you choose. See [whether to leave your children equal shares](/learn/life-stages/should-you-leave-your-children-equal-shares) walks through when fair is not equal, and the free [Illinois unequal inheritance and disinheriting guide](/free/illinois-unequal-inheritance-and-disinheriting-guide) has a worksheet.

Three Illinois points matter when shares differ:

1. **Say it is intentional.** A sentence saying the difference is deliberate heads off a "Mom forgot" argument.
2. **Decide about past gifts.** If you paid for one child's wedding or a down payment, Illinois counts that as an advance on the inheritance only in limited cases, usually with a clear writing (755 ILCS 5/2-5). See [advancements and lifetime gifts](/learn/illinois/advancements-lifetime-gifts-and-inheritance-in-illinois).
3. **Match the forms.** A will that splits 40/30/30 means little if the IRA form says one-third each. [Leaving unequal shares with beneficiary forms](/learn/beneficiary-designations/leaving-unequal-shares-with-beneficiary-forms) shows how percentages work on the forms.

## What if your adult child has a spouse or debts?

Then think about how the money arrives, not only how much. An outright inheritance can be spent, borrowed against or reached by a creditor.

**A child's marriage.** In an Illinois divorce, an inheritance is generally the receiving spouse's non-marital property. It can lose that status once it is mixed into a joint account or used to pay down a jointly owned home. See [whether an inheritance is marital property in Illinois](/learn/illinois/is-inheritance-marital-property-in-illinois). A trust that holds the share, with your child as beneficiary, keeps the money separate without anyone having to keep perfect records.

**A child's debts.** A trust with a [spendthrift clause](/glossary/spendthrift-clause) under 760 ILCS 3/502, and a trustee who has discretion over payments under 760 ILCS 3/504, keeps most of a beneficiary's creditors out. The limits are covered on [protecting assets from creditors with a trust in Illinois](/learn/illinois/can-you-protect-assets-from-creditors-with-a-trust-in-illinois). If the concern is addiction rather than debt, [how to leave money to a child with addiction in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-leave-money-to-a-child-with-addiction-in-illinois) goes further into trustee choice and terms.

A trust for a grown child can still let the child serve as co-trustee. The broader picture is in [keeping an inheritance safe from a child's divorce, addiction or bad spouse](/learn/trusts/keeping-an-inheritance-safe-from-a-childs-divorce-addiction-or-bad-spouse).

## When should you switch from a will to a trust?

Consider a trust when one of these is true for you now:

- You want someone to manage money if you become incapacitated.
- You want shares held in trust for an adult child.
- You want the plan kept out of the public court file.
- You own a house you want to pass without probate, and a TODI does not fit.

The side-by-side comparison is on [will vs. trust in Illinois](/learn/illinois/will-vs-trust-in-illinois), and [Illinois living trusts](/learn/illinois/illinois-living-trusts) covers how one is set up and funded.

If none of those apply, a fresh will with current beneficiary forms and a recorded [transfer on death instrument](/learn/illinois/illinois-transfer-on-death-instrument) for the house may be enough. A trust still needs a [pour-over will](/glossary/pour-over-will) to catch anything left outside it.

## A worked example: Denise and Tom

This is an invented example with round numbers. Denise, 61, and Tom, 63, live in Naperville. Their 2007 wills name Tom's sister as guardian, leave everything to a trust that pays each child at 25, and name Tom's brother as executor. Their children are Maya, 31, a nurse in Naperville; Eli, 28, in Denver; and Sam, 24, who has about $40,000 in credit card debt.

- **Guardian.** Everyone is over 18, so they drop the guardian clause. Because Maya is pregnant, they add a trust for any grandchild under 25 who inherits through a child who died first.
- **Powers of attorney.** Each names the other first and Maya second for health care and property. Eli is the third choice for property, since he can do most of it online.
- **Executor and trustee.** They sign a living trust with a pour-over will. Maya is executor and successor trustee, with Eli as backup.
- **Shares.** They keep equal thirds. Maya and Eli take theirs outright. Sam's third stays in a discretionary spendthrift trust with Maya as trustee, and Sam becomes co-trustee once his debt is paid off.
- **Forms.** The IRAs and Denise's life insurance had named "my estate." They update each form to name the surviving spouse first and then the trust, so Sam's share is protected there too.
- **Tax.** Their total is about $2.1 million, under the $4 million line where Illinois estate tax starts, with no portability between spouses, so they note it and recheck later. See [Illinois estate tax](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax).

They explained the jobs, and Sam's trust, to all three children over dinner.

## How do you make the changes?

One small change can be a codicil, signed like a will with two credible witnesses (755 ILCS 5/4-3). A review that changes several things usually calls for a new will or a trust. [How to change a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-change-a-will-in-illinois) shows which change needs which tool. The [beneficiary audit tool](/tools/beneficiary-audit) lists your forms.
