It has been eight months. Your mother's house is still sitting there, the executor, your brother, says "it's in process," and nobody has seen an accounting. You are not sure whether he is overwhelmed, hiding something or just avoiding a hard job.

Illinois gives heirs tools for this, but the court will not remove someone for being slow alone. This page sets out what the Probate Act lets you do, in the order that usually makes sense. For the full process these steps fit into, see our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

## Is slow the same as wrong?

No, and that distinction decides what you can ask for. Illinois probate has fixed waiting periods that nobody can skip. The creditor notice runs for at least six months after first publication or three months after mailing, whichever is later (755 ILCS 5/18-3). A house sale needs a buyer. Our page on [how long Illinois probate takes](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois) lays out the usual calendar.

So the first question is not "why is this taking so long" but "is anything that could be happening, not happening?" Has the will been filed? Has the executor got letters? Has the notice to creditors run? Are the bills paid? If the answer to most of these is yes, patience is probably right. If the answer is "I do not know," you need information, which is the first step below.

## What should you do first?

1. **Check the court file.** Ask the clerk of the circuit court whether a case is open, whether letters of office issued, and what has been filed. The case file shows whether an inventory or accounting has been requested. Our [county probate guides](/estate-planning/illinois) help you find the right clerk.
2. **Confirm you got your notice.** Within 14 days after the court admits or denies the will or appoints a representative, the representative or petitioner must mail the petition and order to each heir and legatee listed, and publish notice if an address is missing (755 ILCS 5/6-10). If you never received it, ask.
3. **Write a short, specific letter.** Ask for a copy of the will, the date letters issued, a list of assets and debts, what has been paid, and a date for the next step. Keep it calm. Send it by a method with a receipt.
4. **Give a deadline of about 30 days.** Executors respond to dates. Anger makes them defensive.
5. **See an attorney if the letter is ignored.** The next steps are petitions, and they cost money and can split a family.

## When can an Illinois court remove an executor?

Section 23-2 of the Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/23-2) says any interested person may petition for removal, or the court can act on its own motion. The grounds in subsection (a) include:

| Ground | In plain words |
| --- | --- |
| False pretenses | Letters were obtained by lying. |
| Disability or involuntary admission | The executor was adjudged unable to manage, or ordered into involuntary admission under the mental health code. |
| Felony conviction | The executor is convicted of a felony. |
| Waste or mismanagement | The executor wastes or mismanages estate property. |
| Danger to co-representative or surety | The executor's conduct endangers a co-representative or the bond company. |
| Failure to give bond | The executor was ordered to post bond or new bond and did not. |
| Failure to file | The executor was ordered to file an inventory or accounting and did not. |
| Hiding | The executor conceals themselves so notice or process cannot be served. |
| Incapable or unsuitable | The executor becomes incapable of or unsuitable for the job. |
| Other good cause | A catch-all. |

Subsection (b) adds that the court may remove a representative who becomes a non-resident of the United States.

Notice what is not on the list. Being slow, being rude or being someone you do not like is not a ground by itself. But several of these grounds describe slowness in disguise. A missed court order to file an inventory or accounting is on the list. So is "other good cause," which a judge applies to a real pattern of neglect. Our general article on [when an executor can be removed](/learn/after-a-death/can-an-executor-be-removed) discusses the question across states.

A related page, [can an executor take money from the estate](/learn/probate/can-an-executor-take-money-from-the-estate), covers the worst case, where the delay hides spending.

## Can you end independent administration instead?

Often this is the better tool. Under independent administration, the executor acts without asking the judge each time. Section 28-4 (755 ILCS 5/28-4) lets any interested person petition the clerk to terminate it, and the court must enter an order terminating the status, with two limits.

- If the will directs independent administration, the court must find good cause to require supervised administration.
- If the petitioner is a creditor or a legatee who does not take the residue, the court must find termination necessary to protect that petitioner's interest. The court can order other adequate action instead.

If the order is entered, the representative has to mail notice to known interested persons and file proof of mailing. From then on, the executor follows supervised rules. The order sets deadlines for filings such as an inventory or account. The executor can no longer use independent powers and is liable for damage caused by doing so. Anything the executor did before learning of the termination stays valid toward outsiders, other than beneficiaries and creditors who knew.

For the difference between the two styles, see our [Illinois probate process guide](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process).

## Example: Gloria and the stalled house

Gloria's father, Henry, died in Joliet. His will named her brother Curtis as executor and left everything equally to the two of them. A year later the house was still unsold, and Curtis would not answer her texts. Gloria checked with the clerk and found letters had issued but nothing had been filed since. She sent Curtis a letter asking for a list of assets and bills and a date for listing the house. When it went unanswered, her attorney filed a petition to terminate independent administration. The judge entered the order and set a date for an inventory and account. Curtis, now answering to the court, produced the paperwork in a month. Gloria never needed removal.

## What if the job is simply too big for the executor because the job is too big?

Sometimes the executor is not dishonest, just buried. A letter that offers help, such as taking a task like listing the house or collecting mail, may move things faster than a petition. An executor can also hire an attorney, and the cost comes from the estate. Our article on [executor duties](/learn/after-a-death/executor-duties) is a fair checklist to share, and it keeps the conversation about tasks rather than blame.

Keep the tone factual in anything you put in writing. Letters you send can end up as exhibits, and a judge reading a polite, dated request will think better of you than one reading a rant. If you are the executor and feel stuck, you are allowed to ask for help early. The Probate Act expects the work to be done, not done alone.
