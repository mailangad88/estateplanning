## Can someone in hospice sign a will in Illinois?

Yes. Hospice is a kind of care, not a legal status. Under the Probate Act, a person who is 18 or older and of sound mind and memory can make a will (755 ILCS 5/4-1), and the will must be in writing, signed, and attested in the signer's presence by two or more credible witnesses (755 ILCS 5/4-3). Those rules do not change because the signer is dying or taking morphine.

What courts look at is the moment of signing. Illinois cases describe capacity as knowing the people who would naturally inherit, understanding in a general way what you own, and being able to form a plan for it. A person can be very weak and still meet that test on a clear morning. Our page on [testamentary capacity in Illinois](/learn/illinois/testamentary-capacity-in-illinois) explains the test and how lucid intervals are treated.

There is one hard limit. If a court has already appointed a guardian for the person, a will signed afterward is presumed invalid, and whoever defends it must show capacity by clear and convincing evidence (755 ILCS 5/4-1).

## If time is short, what should be signed first?

Sign in the order the family will need things. A will matters after death. A power of attorney matters tomorrow, when someone has to talk to the hospice team or pay the mortgage.

1. **Health care power of attorney.** It names who speaks for the patient when they cannot. The statutory form needs one adult witness and no notary (755 ILCS 45/4-10), so it can be signed at the bedside in minutes. See the [Illinois health care power of attorney](/learn/illinois/illinois-healthcare-power-of-attorney) and the [witness rules for that form](/learn/illinois/witness-rules-for-an-illinois-health-care-power-of-attorney).
2. **Property power of attorney.** It lets an agent pay bills, talk to the bank and handle the house while the person is alive. It needs one witness who is not the agent, plus a notary (755 ILCS 45/3-3). See the [Illinois power of attorney for property](/learn/illinois/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property).
3. **A short will.** Executor, backup executor, and who gets what. Two neutral witnesses. Trusts and tax planning can usually wait or be skipped.
4. **Beneficiary forms.** Retirement accounts and life insurance follow their forms, not the will. Check them with the [beneficiary audit tool](/tools/beneficiary-audit) and read about [Illinois retirement account beneficiaries](/learn/illinois/retirement-account-beneficiaries-and-spousal-consent-in-illinois) and [payable on death accounts](/learn/illinois/illinois-payable-on-death-and-joint-bank-accounts).
5. **A transfer on death instrument for the house, if it fits.** It must be signed with two witnesses and a notary and recorded with the county before the owner dies, or it is void (755 ILCS 27). See the [Illinois transfer on death instrument](/learn/illinois/illinois-transfer-on-death-instrument).

The POLST runs alongside this list, not inside it. It is a medical order completed with a practitioner, and no witness is required (755 ILCS 40/65). The hospice team usually raises it. Our page on [Illinois POLST and DNR orders](/learn/illinois/illinois-polst-and-dnr-orders) explains how EMS and facilities treat it.

## What does each document need on signing day?

A bedside signing often covers several documents, so plan the witnesses and notary around the strictest one.

| Document | Witnesses | Notary | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Will | Two or more credible witnesses, ideally people who gain nothing | No | 755 ILCS 5/4-3, 5/4-6 |
| Health care power of attorney | One adult, not the agent or the attending physician | No | 755 ILCS 45/4-10, 4-5.1 |
| Property power of attorney | One, not the agent | Yes | 755 ILCS 45/3-3 |
| Living will | Two adults | No | Illinois Living Will Act |
| Transfer on death instrument | Two or more credible witnesses | Yes, and recorded before death | 755 ILCS 27 |
| POLST | None required | No | 755 ILCS 40/65 |

Under 755 ILCS 5/4-6, a gift to a witness or a witness's spouse is void unless the will has enough other witnesses. Adult children who inherit should not be the witnesses, even if they are the only people in the room. The [Illinois will requirements](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-requirements) page lists who is safe. The free [will signing day checklist](/free/will-signing-day-checklist) is worth printing.

## Can the will be signed in the hospital or a hospice facility?

Yes. Illinois does not say where a will must be signed. A hospital room, a hospice unit or a bed in the living room all work if the signer has capacity and the witnesses can see the signing.

Ask the nurse when the patient is usually most alert, and schedule then, with the room private for half an hour. Do not count on staff to witness; many facilities prefer that they not, so bring two neutral adults. Ask the attending physician for a short note that day about the patient's alertness. The [comfort care and hospice orders](/learn/healthcare-directives/palliative-care-hospice-and-comfort-care-orders) page explains what the medical team is doing in parallel.

## Can the witnesses join by video?

Yes, under the Electronic Wills and Remote Witnesses Act. Two or more witnesses can watch the signing on a live video call if these conditions are met (755 ILCS 6/15-10):

- The will names Illinois as its place of execution.
- Each witness is in the United States.
- Each witness confirms the signer's identity and sees the signing.
- Any separate signature pages are attached to the will within 10 business days.

Video helps when the attorney is in another county. It is less helpful when capacity may be questioned or the family is divided, because the attorney cannot see who else is in the room. Our pages on [signing a will remotely in Illinois](/learn/illinois/sign-will-or-trust-remotely-in-illinois) and the [Illinois Electronic Wills and Remote Witnesses Act](/learn/illinois/illinois-electronic-wills-and-remote-witnesses-act) set out the steps. For a hospice patient, a paper signing at the bedside is often simpler.

## What if the person cannot physically sign?

Illinois lets another person sign the will for the maker, in the maker's presence and at the maker's direction (755 ILCS 5/4-3). The two witnesses still watch. The maker should say out loud that this is their will and that they want the named person to sign for them.

Choose someone who does not inherit. For a video signing the rule is written down: the person who signs for the maker cannot be a witness, a beneficiary, or the spouse or child of a beneficiary (755 ILCS 6/15-10). Tell the attorney ahead of time so the will and the witness statement describe what actually happened.

## What if the person no longer has capacity?

Then nobody can sign a will for them. A power of attorney agent cannot make one, and family members cannot sign on the person's behalf. If the person dies without a will, the Probate Act decides who inherits (755 ILCS 5/2-1), as explained in [who inherits without a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-inherits-without-a-will-in-illinois).

Other parts of the plan still have answers:

- **Medical decisions.** If there is no health care agent, the Health Care Surrogate Act ranks who decides: a guardian, then the spouse, then adult children and others (755 ILCS 40/25). See [who decides under the Surrogate Act](/learn/illinois/illinois-health-care-surrogate-act-who-decides).
- **Money.** Without a property power of attorney, broad authority over accounts comes from a court through [Illinois guardianship](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship). A [temporary guardian](/learn/illinois/temporary-guardianship-in-illinois) can be appointed for an emergency for up to 60 days (755 ILCS 5/11a-4).
- **Good days and bad days.** Capacity can come and go. An attorney can assess the person on a clearer day, as described in [dementia and no power of attorney in Illinois](/learn/illinois/dementia-no-power-of-attorney). The [is it too late to sign checklist](/free/is-it-too-late-to-sign-checklist) helps the family sort out what they are seeing.

## A worked example: Dolores, home hospice near Joliet

Example only. Dolores, 81, is a widow on home hospice. She owns her house in her own name, has a checking account and an IRA that still names her late husband. Her two sons, Mark and Tony, live in Illinois. She has a 1994 will that names her late husband as executor and nothing else. Her nurse says she is clearest before lunch.

On Monday, Mark calls an Illinois estate planning attorney and sends a short list of names, the house address, the accounts and what Dolores has said she wants. The attorney speaks to Dolores alone by phone, and she confirms an equal split between her sons, with Tony as executor.

On Wednesday at 10 a.m., the attorney comes to the house with a notary. Two neighbors who inherit nothing serve as witnesses. Mark and Tony step out of the room. In this order, Dolores signs:

1. A health care power of attorney naming Tony, with Mark as successor, witnessed by one neighbor.
2. A property power of attorney naming Mark, witnessed by the other neighbor and notarized.
3. A new will, read aloud to her, which she explains in her own words before signing. Both neighbors sign the attestation clause.
4. The IRA company's beneficiary form naming both sons.

The attorney writes notes of what Dolores said, and her hospice physician adds a short note that afternoon. A transfer on death instrument is discussed but left aside because Dolores is tiring; it would have had to be recorded before her death to work.

Dolores dies three weeks later, and Tony files the will with the circuit clerk. Had Mark waited another week, Dolores might not have been able to follow the conversation, and the 1994 will would have been the only plan.

## How do you protect a hospice will from a challenge?

Build the record while you can. Illinois gives a challenger 6 months after the will is admitted to file a contest, and the challenger carries the burden of proof, as the page on the [Illinois will contest deadline](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-contest-six-month-deadline) explains. Signing-day evidence is what decides most of these cases.

- The attorney meets the person alone and asks open questions about family and property.
- Anyone who inherits leaves the room for the key conversation.
- Witnesses sign an attestation clause stating that the signer seemed of sound mind (755 ILCS 5/6-4).
- A physician writes a short note that day.
- If the will favors the caregiver child, a sentence in the will says why.

Pressure claims are separate; see [undue influence will challenges in Illinois](/learn/illinois/undue-influence-will-challenges-in-illinois) and [signing a will with illness or family pressure](/learn/wills/signing-a-will-with-dementia-illness-or-family-pressure). The [serious diagnosis first week checklist](/free/serious-diagnosis-first-week-checklist) gathers the rest.
