Ellen, 74, is a retired teacher in Peoria with no spouse and two grown children. Her estate is about $1.2 million, including a $300,000 IRA. She wants to leave part of it to the animal shelter and the school foundation she has supported for years. She asks, "Do I put this in my will, my trust or my IRA forms?"

The answer depends on the asset, the tax result and the wording. This page compares the main ways to leave a charitable gift in Illinois and explains how to check that the gift will land. For the basics across states, see [how to leave money to charity in your will](/learn/wills/how-to-leave-money-to-charity-in-your-will). The [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois) has the wider setting.

## What are the ways to leave a gift to charity?

There are four common types, and each fits a different goal. The table shows how they compare.

| Method | How it works | Good fit | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed sum in a will or trust | A dollar amount, such as $25,000 | A clear gift to one charity | Can look small as the estate grows |
| Percentage of the residue | A share of what is left after other gifts | Estates that may rise or fall | Debts and taxes reduce the residue |
| Specific item | A painting, land or shares | An asset the charity wants | The charity may decline an item it cannot use |
| Beneficiary form | Name the charity on an IRA, 401(k), life insurance or POD account | Retirement accounts | The form beats your will, so keep it current |

A gift can also be made as a [bequest](/glossary/bequest) of the [residuary estate](/glossary/residuary-estate) in a will, or by a trust provision that continues after your death.

## Which assets should go to charity first?

Often retirement accounts. A charity does not pay income tax on an IRA it receives, while a child who inherits it usually owes income tax on withdrawals over a 10-year period. Our page on the [inherited IRA 10-year rule](/learn/beneficiary-designations/inherited-ira-10-year-rule-yearly-withdrawals) explains the timing. Meanwhile your house and brokerage shares may pass to heirs with a step-up in basis, so they often owe little tax. Here are Ellen's numbers.

- Option A: Ellen leaves her $300,000 IRA to her children. If they owe income tax at 24 percent, about $72,000 goes to tax, leaving about $228,000.
- Option B: She leaves the IRA to the shelter and the foundation and leaves $300,000 of other assets to her children instead. The charities receive the full $300,000 and the children receive $300,000 with a step-up in basis.

The rate is only an example. The result will vary with each heir's income. Check with a tax adviser before you decide. See how Illinois treats the income in our page on [income tax on an inherited IRA](/learn/illinois/do-you-pay-illinois-income-tax-on-an-inherited-ira-or-401k).

## How do you write the gift step by step?

1. List the charities. Get each one's full legal name, tax ID (EIN) and address from the charity.
2. Check that each is registered. Use the Illinois Attorney General's charity search.
3. Decide the form: a sum, a percentage, an item or a beneficiary designation.
4. Decide if the gift has a purpose, such as a scholarship. Ask the charity if it can accept the terms.
5. Add a backup. Say what happens if the charity has closed, merged or refuses the gift.
6. Sign the will or trust amendment with the usual formalities, and update beneficiary forms with each institution.
7. Tell your executor and keep a copy of the charity's letter confirming its name.

## Does a charitable gift lower tax?

It can. A bequest to a qualified charity reduces the taxable estate, which matters because Illinois taxes estates over $4 million (see [the Illinois estate tax](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax)). The federal exclusion is $15 million per person in 2026, so most estates owe no federal tax. Use the [estate tax estimator](/tools/estate-tax-estimator) to see where you fall. A charitable gift does not change income tax in the year of death, except for the IRA effect above. Ask your tax adviser about the Illinois computation, because it follows its own rules.

## What if the charity changes before you die?

A renamed or merged charity usually still receives the gift through its successor. If it has closed, a court can sometimes redirect the gift to a similar cause when your documents show a general charitable purpose. In a trust, the Illinois Trust Code addresses charitable purposes that become impractical, and the Attorney General may take part in the case. Our page on [a charity that no longer exists](/learn/what-if/charity-named-in-will-no-longer-exists) explains the details. A backup clause avoids the court trip.

## Can your family contest a charitable gift?

Yes, on the same grounds as any gift, such as lack of capacity or undue influence, and within 6 months after the will is admitted (755 ILCS 5/8-1). See [the six-month deadline](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-contest-six-month-deadline). A calm explanation to your children, and a plan that leaves them well provided for, lowers the risk.

## What does sample wording look like?

Plain wording works. Here are three patterns, which an attorney should adapt to your facts.

- Fixed sum: "I give $25,000 to [full legal name], a charitable corporation, tax ID [EIN], located at [address]."
- Percentage: "I give ten percent of my residuary estate to [full legal name]. If it is not an organization described in section 170(c) of the Internal Revenue Code when I die, I give this share to [backup]."
- Purpose gift: "I give $50,000 to [full legal name] for its scholarship program. If the program has ended, the charity may use the gift for its general charitable purposes."

The second pattern includes a backup and a tax status test. The third lets the charity change course if the program ends, which lowers the chance of a court case. Leave out gifts with strict conditions, such as "only if the shelter stays no-kill," because the charity may decline them. If you want to guide how the money is used, ask the charity about a gift agreement.
