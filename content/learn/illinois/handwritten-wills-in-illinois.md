Dale, 71, sat at his kitchen table and wrote a will on a yellow legal pad. It said his house goes to his daughter and his savings to his son. He signed and dated it. Then he put it in a folder. Nobody else was in the room.

Many people believe handwriting makes a will official. In some states it does. In Illinois it does not. This page explains what a handwritten will needs here, what happens when it fails, and how to fix one while you can. For the full rules on valid wills, see our [Illinois will requirements](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-requirements) and the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

## Does Illinois accept a will written entirely by hand?

Only if it was also witnessed. The Probate Act asks for a writing, a signature, and attestation in the signer's presence by two or more credible witnesses (755 ILCS 5/4-3). The law does not carve out a separate category for wills in the maker's own handwriting. Some states do. Illinois treats Dale's legal pad the same way it treats a typed will from a law office.

So the real question is not "is it handwritten?" but "who watched you sign?" Pen and paper are fine. A missing witness is not.

## What is the difference between a handwritten will and a witnessed handwritten will?

The difference is the signing. Here is how common situations come out.

| Situation | Valid in Illinois? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Written by hand, signed, two neighbors watched and signed | Yes | Meets 755 ILCS 5/4-3 |
| Written by hand, signed alone, no witnesses | No | No attestation |
| Typed, signed by you, one witness only | No | Two witnesses are required |
| Written by hand, one witness is a child who inherits | Risky | A gift to a witness can be void (755 ILCS 5/4-6) |
| Written by hand in a state that allows it, you lived there | Often yes | Illinois may honor it under 755 ILCS 5/7-1 |
| Written by hand on a phone note or email | No | See our page on electronic wills |

Our article on [witnesses in Illinois and New Jersey](/learn/wills/who-can-witness-a-will-in-illinois-and-new-jersey) shows who is a safe choice.

## What happens if the handwritten will is not valid?

The court treats the person as having died without a will. Under 755 ILCS 5/2-1, if there is a spouse and descendants, the spouse takes one-half and the descendants share the other half. Without a spouse, the descendants take all. Our guide on [who inherits without a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-inherits-without-a-will-in-illinois) walks through the rest of the order.

Back to Dale. He dies with a spouse, Joan, and two adult children. His note is ignored. Joan receives one-half of his probate estate and the children share the other half. That is not what he wrote, and the house may now need to be shared or sold.

The note is not worthless. Family members can read it as a statement of his wishes and may agree to follow it. They may also use it in a settlement. But the court is not bound by it.

## How do you fix a handwritten will while you are alive?

Sign it again the right way. Use this order.

1. Decide whether to keep your handwritten words or have a clean copy prepared. Clear wording avoids disputes.
2. Choose two adult witnesses who receive nothing under the will and are not married to someone who does.
3. Gather everyone in one room with the final pages and a pen.
4. Tell the witnesses that this is your will, then sign it where they can see you.
5. Have each witness sign in your presence, and add an attestation statement so the will is easy to prove (755 ILCS 5/6-4).
6. Store the original where your executor can find it, and write down the location.

Once it is signed with witnesses, the old unwitnessed pages are only a draft. Mark them "replaced" or shred them so no one argues about which is real. Our article on [changing or revoking a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/changing-or-revoking-a-will-in-illinois) covers the safe way to do that.

## What if the handwritten will comes from another state?

Illinois may still honor it. A will signed outside Illinois can be admitted here if it was executed under the law of the place where it was signed or where the maker lived (755 ILCS 5/7-1). Some states recognize a handwritten will if the key terms and the signature are in the maker's hand. If your parent moved to Illinois from such a state, the old will may carry over. Read our page on [out-of-state wills in Illinois](/learn/illinois/is-an-out-of-state-will-valid-in-illinois) before assuming either way.

## What are the risks of a handwritten will even when it is witnessed?

Valid does not mean clear. People who write their own wills tend to leave gaps. Common problems include no backup if a named person dies first, no executor, a gift of "my house" when the house is in a trust, and a signature page that was copied on its own. A beneficiary form on a life insurance policy or IRA also beats the will, so a note leaving "everything to Anna" may not move those accounts at all. Our broader article on [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills) lists these traps. If your assets are modest, try the [will or trust tool](/tools/will-or-trust) to see which tool fits.

## Should you cross out or add words on a signed will?

No. Illinois lets a will be revoked by cancelling or obliterating it (755 ILCS 5/4-7), so a heavy line through a clause could be read as a revocation, and added words are not witnessed. Make changes with a new will or a [codicil](/glossary/codicil) signed with the same formalities.

## What should a family do with a handwritten note found after a death?

Do not throw it away, and do not assume it decides anything. Keep the original paper safe, because anyone who holds a will must file it with the circuit court clerk after a death (755 ILCS 5/6-1), and it is a crime to hide or destroy one. Take it to an attorney, who can tell you if two witnesses signed and whether it could be admitted. If the note is not a valid will, it may still help show what the person wanted, and the heirs can discuss whether to honor it. Our page on [finding a will after a death](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois) explains where to look for a better version, because many people who write a note have also signed a formal will that is on file with a lawyer. If no valid will turns up, the next step is usually to prove who the heirs are, as in [proving heirship in Illinois](/learn/illinois/proving-heirship-in-illinois).
