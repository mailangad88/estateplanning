A neighbor says every smart Chicago landlord uses a land trust. Your estate planning attorney says you need a living trust. The names sound alike, and the person at the bank trust department is happy to sell you one without mentioning the other. Which do you actually need for your house?

This page is a decision guide for Illinois owners, part of our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). It does not repeat the full side-by-side definitions in our article on [Illinois land trust vs living trust](/learn/trusts/illinois-land-trust-vs-living-trust), and it does not cover what a family does after an owner has died, which is in [Illinois land trust when the owner dies](/learn/illinois/illinois-land-trust-when-the-owner-dies). Here the question is simpler: for your property, which tool, and in what order?

## Land trust vs living trust: what is the difference in one minute?

A land trust holds title to a single property. A living trust holds your plan. In a land trust, a bank or title company is named on the deed as trustee, and you, the beneficiary, give it instructions. In an Illinois living trust, you usually serve as your own trustee while you are well, name a successor to step in when you cannot, and say who receives what when you die.

Illinois treats the beneficial interest in a land trust as personal property. That is why you can transfer it with a short assignment instead of a new deed, and why it can end up in probate if nobody planned for it. The [Illinois living trusts](/learn/illinois/illinois-living-trusts) page covers the other document in depth.

## Who is on the deed under each option?

The deed is where the difference becomes visible. Here is how the same Chicago bungalow looks under four setups.

| Setup | What the recorded deed shows | Who controls the house | What happens at death |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owned outright | Your name | You | Probate, unless a survivor or beneficiary is set up another way |
| Living trust only | You, as trustee of your trust | You, then your successor trustee | Passes under the trust, no probate for the house |
| Land trust only | The bank, as trustee, with a trust number | You, by direction to the bank | Depends on the land trust agreement; often probate |
| Both | The bank, as trustee, with a trust number | Your successor trustee, once the interest sits in your living trust | Passes under the living trust, no probate |

The last two rows are the whole story. A land trust changes whose name is on the deed. It does not decide what happens to the house.

## Which setup fits which owner?

Match your situation to the closest line, then read the reasoning.

1. **One house you live in, children as heirs.** A living trust. A land trust adds a bank fee and little else. A recorded [transfer on death instrument](/learn/illinois/illinois-transfer-on-death-instrument) can also work if the house is your only concern.
2. **A condo or townhouse.** A living trust, after you read the association's documents. See the Chicago condo and co-op guide in our [free resources](/free/chicago-condo-and-coop-owners-estate-guide).
3. **A two-flat where you live upstairs.** Either a living trust alone or both. Many owners in this position already have a land trust from the closing. If so, keep it and assign the interest.
4. **Three or more rental buildings.** Usually both: one land trust per parcel, all assigned to one living trust. Each building closes on its own, and there is one plan to administer.
5. **A rental with real liability exposure.** A land trust is not the answer. Talk to an insurance agent about coverage, and ask an attorney whether an LLC fits. Our page on [protecting assets from creditors with a trust in Illinois](/learn/illinois/can-you-protect-assets-from-creditors-with-a-trust-in-illinois) explains why.

Marisol, 62, owns a Logan Square three-flat and a Cicero bungalow she lives in. She is a widow with two adult children. Her three-flat sits in a land trust from 2006 and names no successor. Her bungalow is in her own name. She signs a living trust, assigns the three-flat's beneficial interest to it, and deeds the bungalow to herself as trustee. Her son, the successor trustee, can now manage both without a court. That is typical, and notice that the land trust was kept because it already existed, not because it was needed.

## Does a land trust protect my house from lawsuits?

Do not rely on it. Sources and practitioners disagree on how much, if any, protection a land trust adds. What can be said with confidence is that it makes your name harder to find in the county record. A plaintiff who sues you can still seek your beneficial interest, because it is your property.

A living trust you can revoke does not protect assets from your own creditors either. For a home, real protection comes from adequate insurance. For rentals, it can also come from how the business is structured. If an agent or a friend says a land trust will "protect" the building, ask them to show you the statute that does it.

## How do the two work together, step by step?

The usual order is short. If you already have a land trust, start at step 3.

1. **Sign the living trust** and name a successor trustee and backups.
2. **Create the land trust** if you want one: a trust agreement with the bank, and a deed from you to the bank as trustee.
3. **Read the death-of-beneficiary clause** in the land trust agreement. This is the paragraph that sets what happens when you die.
4. **Sign an assignment** moving the beneficial interest to your living trust, on the trustee's form if it has one.
5. **Deliver the signed assignment to the trustee** and ask for written confirmation that it recorded you as beneficiary.
6. **Update the paperwork** with your lender, insurance carrier and, if you take a homeowner exemption, the county assessor.
7. **Keep one binder** with the recorded deed, the land trust agreement, the assignment and the trustee's contact.

## What should I ask before moving a house into either trust?

Ask your lender first. Federal law generally bars a lender from calling a one-to-four unit home loan when you transfer the home into a trust you benefit from and keep living there, but lenders still want notice. Then ask these:

- **The trustee bank:** What is the annual fee? What does it charge to sign a deed, and how fast does it act on a direction letter?
- **The insurer:** Will the policy name the trustee and the trust? Get the endorsement in writing.
- **The title company:** Does it insure a later sale from the trustee? It usually does, and a quick call confirms.
- **Your attorney:** Does the transfer change a tax exemption, and does the deed need to state a transfer tax exemption? Our page on [how a trust affects homestead and property tax exemptions](/learn/trusts/does-a-living-trust-affect-homestead-exemption-and-property-taxes) covers the usual questions.

## Common mistakes with Illinois land trusts

- **Treating it as an estate plan.** The owner has a land trust and a will, assumes the family is covered, and learns the interest is a probate asset.
- **Losing the trust number.** The family cannot tell which institution holds title.
- **Naming a dead or estranged successor beneficiary** and never updating it.
- **Adding a living trust but forgetting the assignment.** The living trust owns nothing until the interest is assigned to it.
- **Paying for a land trust you do not need,** then paying the bank every year for the privilege.
