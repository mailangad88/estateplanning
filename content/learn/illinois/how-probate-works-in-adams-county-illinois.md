When a parent or spouse dies in Quincy, Camp Point, Mendon or Liberty, the estate is an Adams County, Illinois matter. Adams County is in the 8th Judicial Circuit, and the courthouse is in Quincy. This page belongs to the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). Many states have an Adams County, so check that any form or phone number you find is for Illinois.

It covers where the courthouse and the Circuit Clerk are, how an estate opens, what to do with the original will, self-help, case lookup and remote hearings. It also covers a worry that divides families: a will that may reflect pressure from a caregiver. If you want to talk to an Illinois estate planning attorney, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-adams-county-illinois&topic=probate).

This is general education from official pages read in November 2026, not legal advice. Phone numbers and rules change, so confirm them with the clerk before you go.

## Where is the Adams County Courthouse and the Circuit Clerk?

The Adams County Courthouse is at 521 Vermont Street, Quincy, IL 62301. The Illinois Courts directory lists it in the 4th Appellate District and the 8th Judicial Circuit. It gives courthouse hours of 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and the numbers 217-277-2100 and 217-277-2116.

The Circuit Clerk's page on the county website gives 217-277-2100 for questions about cases. Neither page gives a room number for the clerk or says that the office has a separate probate counter. The directory says the e-filing kiosk and the Self-Help Center are located outside the Circuit Clerk's Office, so look for them as you find the counter.

| Place | What the official page says | What a probate family uses it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circuit Clerk | 521 Vermont Street; 217-277-2100 | Filing the will, petitions and orders; case questions |
| E-filing kiosk | Outside the Circuit Clerk's Office | Electronic filing help on site |
| Self-Help Center | Outside the Circuit Clerk's Office | Forms and finding resources |
| Law Library | West end of the 2nd floor, public computers | Research |

The directory also lists an administrative order on portable electronic devices in the courthouse. Read it before you plan to photograph documents inside the building.

## How does an estate open in Adams County, and what do you do with the original will?

An estate opens when someone files a petition with the Circuit Clerk in the county where the person lived. A named executor asks the court to admit the will and issue letters of office. If there is no will, a relative asks to be appointed administrator. The step-by-step version is in [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois).

The original will comes first. Under 755 ILCS 5/6-1, anyone holding it must file it with the clerk immediately after the death. The page on [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) explains the duty and the 30-day clocks in section 6-3.

A simple order of steps:

1. Keep the original flat and unstapled, and do not write on it.
2. Scan or photograph every page for your own records.
3. Ask the Quincy clerk how to deliver the original, by phone before you go.
4. Ask for a receipt that shows the date and the case or will number.
5. Tell the named executor and the family where the original now is.

Filing the will preserves the document. It does not give anyone power over a bank account or a house. That comes from the court's order and letters of office. The broader process is in [Illinois probate, step by step](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process).

## Can you e-file or appear remotely in Adams County?

Partly confirmed. The courthouse page mentions an e-filing kiosk outside the clerk's office. It does not say which case types must be filed electronically, and it does not describe probate filings. Ask the clerk whether your petition should go in electronically or on paper.

On remote hearings, we found nothing on the official pages we read that sets out a procedure. The judge decides how each hearing runs. If a sibling lives in another state, ask the clerk or the attorney on the estate how to attend, and ask early.

Whatever the answer on e-filing, the original will is the paper that a scan cannot replace. See [what to do if you only have a copy](/learn/illinois/lost-will) for what happens when the original cannot be found.

## What self-help does the Adams County court list?

The courthouse page says a Self-Help Center is outside the Circuit Clerk's office. Illinois Legal Aid Online lists an Adams County Legal Self-Help Center at the same courthouse address, with the same phone, 217-277-2100. It says the center gives legal information and help with some forms, plus referrals. It says it does not provide legal advice.

Neither page mentions probate. Use the center to find forms and resources, not to decide what your will means. ## How do you look up an Adams County case?

The clerk's page tells users to click Search Court Records on the left side menu. It also says records ordered suppressed, impounded or confidential cannot be viewed. So a missing entry online does not prove nothing was filed.

Call the clerk at 217-277-2100 or visit the courthouse for a firm answer. For how estate files become part of the public record, see [is probate public](/learn/probate/is-probate-public) and [how to look up a probate case](/learn/probate/how-to-look-up-a-probate-case). ## What if a relative thinks a caregiver pressured the parent?

This is the situation that turns a quiet estate into a dispute. A daughter in Quincy moved in to care for her father. In his last year he changed his will to leave her the house. His other children were not told. Now the will is in the clerk's file and they are asking whether it reflects his wishes.

Illinois separates two questions. Testamentary capacity asks whether he was of sound mind and memory when he signed (755 ILCS 5/4-1), as the page on [testamentary capacity in Illinois](/learn/illinois/testamentary-capacity-in-illinois) explains. Undue influence asks whether pressure replaced his own choice. A person can have capacity and still be pressured, as the page on [what is undue influence](/learn/basics/what-is-undue-influence-in-estate-planning) explains.

Helping a parent is not undue influence, and neither is an unequal split. The page on [undue influence in Illinois will contests](/learn/illinois/undue-influence-will-challenges-in-illinois) describes the presumption that can arise when a trusted helper arranges the will, and the evidence that can rebut it.

The clock is strict. A petition to contest must be filed within six months after the will is admitted to probate (755 ILCS 5/8-1), and failing to notify an heir does not extend that period. Details are on the page on the [six-month will contest deadline](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-contest-six-month-deadline).

A contest is a court case, and an interested person is the one who files. ## Example: the Brandt family in Quincy

This is an invented example. Walter Brandt, 84, died in March. His daughter Karen, who had lived with him and managed his bills, filed his will with the clerk in April. The will, signed the year before, left Karen the house and split the rest among her brother Paul and sister Lena.

Lena lives in Texas and learned of the will from a phone call. She remembered that her father had told her for years the house would be shared. She also knew that Karen had driven him to the lawyer. Lena called the clerk, learned the date the will was admitted and counted six months forward.

She did not accuse anyone. She asked an Illinois estate planning attorney to review the will, the lawyer's file and her father's medical records from the year before. Karen explained that the lawyer met Walter alone and that he had written a note about why the house was hers. That evidence mattered, and it showed that a suspicion and a case are not the same thing. The family then decided with advice whether a contest was worth filing before the deadline.

## What mistakes should an Adams County family avoid?

- Searching for "Adams County probate" and using a form from another state.
- Waiting for the whole family to agree before the original will reaches the clerk.
- Treating the online record search as proof that a will was or was not filed.
- Counting the contest period from the date of death or the date of a letter.
- Giving a relative the only original without a receipt.
- Distributing property before anyone has letters of office or a court order.
