Illinois families move around. A mother moves to Arizona for her health, a retired couple buys a condo in Florida, an adult daughter in Texas becomes the agent for a father who lives in Peoria. Each time, someone asks whether the power of attorney will travel.

Our general article, [is a power of attorney valid in another state or abroad](/learn/power-of-attorney/is-a-power-of-attorney-valid-in-another-state-or-abroad), covers the basic principle. This page adds Illinois specifics. See our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois) for the other documents.

## Does an Illinois power of attorney work in another state?

Usually it can be used, but the other state decides how to treat it. Illinois law governs how the document was created and what it means. The bank or recorder in the other state follows its own law about whether to rely on it. Many states have adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act, which includes protections for institutions that accept a document in good faith. Illinois uses its own Power of Attorney Act (755 ILCS 45), so the statutory short form language will look unfamiliar to people in states using the uniform act.

In practice, acceptance depends on the people at the bank. A form that is clean, signed with a witness and a notary, and accompanied by a short certification travels well.

## What does Illinois do with a power signed elsewhere?

The Act has a section titled "Power of attorney executed in another state or country; pre-existing powers of attorney" (755 ILCS 45/2-10.6). Read the current text on ilga.gov before relying on it, because the exact tests can change. The section shows that Illinois does not ignore documents from other places. The Act also lets a third party rely on a document that appears validly signed unless the third party knows otherwise (755 ILCS 45/2-8).

If you are the agent under an out-of-state document and need to act in Illinois, bring the original, a photo ID, and a short statement that the power has not been revoked. Ask the Illinois bank for its legal department early.

## What changes for real estate in another state?

Real estate is the hardest part. A deed signed by an agent usually needs the power of attorney recorded with the deed in the county where the land sits. The county recorder follows local rules on acknowledgment, witnesses and the wording of the power. Some states require two witnesses for a power used in a real estate sale. Our article on [powers of attorney and real estate](/learn/power-of-attorney/power-of-attorney-and-real-estate) explains the basics, and [Illinois residents who own property elsewhere](/learn/illinois/illinois-resident-owning-property-in-another-state) covers the estate side.

For an Illinois parcel, ask the recorder in the county. For example, [Cook County](/estate-planning/illinois/counties/cook), [Lake County](/estate-planning/illinois/counties/lake) and [Will County](/estate-planning/illinois/counties/will) may each have their own submission steps.

## What should you do, step by step?

1. List every state where the principal lives, banks or owns property.
2. Check whether the principal still has capacity to sign. If yes, consider a second document.
3. Make several certified copies of the Illinois original.
4. Call the new bank, broker or recorder and ask what they need, in writing.
5. Send a copy ahead with a short cover letter from the agent.
6. If the institution refuses, ask for the legal department. Our Illinois article on [banks refusing a power of attorney](/learn/illinois/bank-refuses-an-illinois-power-of-attorney) applies the Illinois statute.
7. For land in the other state, hire a local lawyer for the deed and recording.
8. Keep records of every transaction, as described in [agent duties and record-keeping](/learn/illinois/illinois-poa-agent-duties-and-record-keeping).

|---|---|---|
| Paying bills from an Illinois bank | Yes, Illinois form | Agent far away; mail delays |
| New bank in the other state | Sometimes | Bank may request its own form |
| Selling land in the other state | Rarely alone | Recording, local witness rules |
| Brokerage account | Often | Custodian may want its form |
| Tax matters | Often | IRS and state use their own forms |
| Health care decisions | Often honored | Hospital may ask for its own form |

## Should you sign a second document?

If the principal has capacity, yes in many cases. A new power for the new state costs less than a guardianship and removes doubts. Signing a second power does not revoke the first unless it says it does, so be clear about which powers apply where, and keep copies of both. Our article on [revoking an Illinois power of attorney](/learn/illinois/revoking-an-illinois-power-of-attorney) explains how a later document can cancel an earlier one.

The same idea applies to health care. Many states honor a health care document that was valid where signed, but hospitals prefer familiar forms. See [advance directives in other states](/learn/healthcare-directives/do-advance-directives-work-in-other-states) and [Illinois health care power of attorney](/learn/illinois/illinois-healthcare-power-of-attorney).

## Example: Gloria moves to Arizona

Gloria, 79, signed an Illinois statutory short form naming her son Marcus, 52, who lives in Texas. She moves to a care home in Arizona. Her Arizona bank accepts a copy but wants an officer's review for two weeks. Marcus sends a certification and a note citing the Illinois Act. When Gloria needs to sell her Illinois house, Marcus records the power in the county and the sale closes. Later, because Gloria still has capacity, she signs an Arizona form as well. ## What should a travel packet contain?

Before the agent has to act in a new state, assemble a packet so a teller or title officer can decide quickly.

- A certified copy of the Illinois power of attorney, with the witness and notary pages.
- A short certification from the agent that the principal is living, the power has not been revoked and the agent is serving.
- A letter of introduction naming the Illinois statute (755 ILCS 45) and, for real estate, a legal description of the property.
- Photo ID for the agent and, if possible, a recent bank statement showing the account holder's name.
- The name and number of the Illinois attorney who prepared the document, in case the local lawyer has questions.

Update the packet whenever the principal moves again. A packet that sits unopened for years is the usual cause of delay.
