The bank accounts are empty, the house is sold, and the checks have gone to the beneficiaries. You might think the work is finished. In Illinois it is not, until a judge signs an order that says the estate is closed and the executor is discharged.

This page covers that last stretch: what the executor files, who has to be told, how a signed receipt from an heir helps, and what changes if the court has been supervising the case. For the earlier steps, see [how Illinois probate works](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process). For the general idea of a receipt and release outside Illinois law, our guide on [final distribution and closing the estate](/learn/after-a-death/final-distribution-receipt-and-release-and-closing-the-estate) is the companion. This is general education, not legal advice.

## The short version

- Closing is a court step, not just a final round of checks.
- In an independent estate, the executor files a sworn report. In a supervised estate, the executor files accounts and the judge approves them.
- Heirs can sign receipts. Each signature shortens the process.
- The estate is not closed until the court enters the discharge order.

## What are the steps to close an independent estate?

Independent administration is the default path, and it closes under 755 ILCS 5/28-11. In order:

1. **Finish the work.** Claims are resolved, taxes are paid or provided for, expenses are paid and the remaining assets are distributed.
2. **Prepare the accounting.** The executor mails or delivers an accounting to all interested persons. It does not go to the court unless someone asks.
3. **Collect receipts and approvals.** Ask each heir and legatee to sign a receipt for full payment, approving the executor's and attorney's fees. Creditors who approve in writing are handled the same way.
4. **File the verified report.** The report goes to the court clerk and is sworn.
5. **Mail the report.** Within 14 days after filing, copies go to everyone entitled to notice who has not waived it, with a statement that the executor will be discharged and the estate closed if no objection is filed within 42 days.
6. **Wait 42 days.** Then apply for the discharge order.
7. **Get the order.** If no objection is pending, the court enters an order discharging the executor and closing the estate.

If an address for an interested person is missing from the report, or the estate was opened on a presumption of death, the executor publishes the notice instead, once a week for three weeks, with the first publication inside 14 days of filing.

## What goes into the closing report?

The statute lists the contents. In plain terms the executor swears that:

- Heirs and legatees were given the notice the Probate Act requires at the start of the case.
- The creditor notice was published, known creditors were told and reasonable care was used to find the rest.
- The inventory and accounting were mailed or delivered as required.
- Every filed claim was allowed, disallowed, compromised, dismissed or barred, and allowed claims were paid in full, or by priority if the estate fell short.
- Death taxes were paid, provided for, or did not apply.
- Expenses and liabilities were paid and administration is complete.
- The remaining assets went to the people entitled to them.
- The fees of the executor and the attorney were approved by all interested persons, unless the report says otherwise.
- The names and addresses of the people entitled to notice are listed.

Notice the dependency. The claims statement leans on the work described in our page on [how creditor claims work](/learn/illinois/how-creditor-claims-work-in-an-illinois-estate), and the death tax statement leans on the tax return decisions made earlier. An executor who skipped a step has to fix it before this report can truthfully be signed.

## How do receipts from heirs help?

A receipt is a signed statement that the heir received a stated amount and agrees with how it was calculated. Under 28-11, heirs and legatees who file signed receipts for full payment do not need the 42-day notice, as long as their receipts also confirm approval of the fees and, where it applies, that they received the inventory and approved the accounting. Creditors who approved the report in writing, or whose claims were paid by priority or an agreed compromise, are likewise left out of the mailing.

If nobody is left who needs notice, the court enters the discharge order and declares the estate closed at once, with no 42-day wait.

| Person | How they can be taken out of the notice list |
|---|---|
| Heir or legatee | Signs a receipt for full payment and approves fees |
| Creditor | Approves the report in writing, or was paid by priority or agreed compromise |
| Anyone interested | Waives notice |
| Trust beneficiary | Not entitled to notice when a trustee is an interested person |

Maria, 64, is executor of her brother Daniel's estate, which has three heirs. Her nephew and niece sign receipts the day they get their checks. Her other nephew, Rob, moves and cannot be reached. His address is not in the report, so Maria publishes the notice for three weeks and waits. Had Rob been found and signed, the estate would have closed weeks earlier. A signed receipt is the cheapest closing tool there is, which is why a good executor asks for it with the check, not months after.

## How does a supervised estate close?

The route is longer. A supervised executor files a verified account with the court that issued the letters. The first is due within 60 days after the first 12 months from the date letters were issued, unless the court grants more time, and later ones are due at the intervals the court orders (755 ILCS 5/24-1). Each account shows the receipts and disbursements since the last one, and the real and personal property still on hand, with proof of the payments if the court wants it. The court can excuse the account if all interested persons file written consents.

Notice of the hearing goes to unpaid creditors and other interested persons as the court directs. If the court approves the account, the order binds those who got notice unless fraud, accident or mistake is shown. People who gave a signed full receipt, or waived notice, are not entitled to the notice (755 ILCS 5/24-2). The final steps depend on the judge's orders in that case, so a supervised executor should ask the court or an attorney what the closing order requires. How supervised cases differ in other respects is in the [independent vs. supervised page](/learn/illinois/independent-vs-supervised-administration-in-illinois).

## What does the discharge order do?

It ends the executor's job, and it limits later claims against the executor by the people it binds. Absent fraud, accident or mistake, the order binds each person who filed a receipt or approval with the report and each person properly notified through the 42-day procedure. A person who was never given the required notice is not bound in the same way, which is the main reason to get the notice list right.

Discharge does not rescue an executor who hid something. Fraud is the usual exception. Keep copies of the receipts, the mailing proofs and the order itself for several years.

## Common mistakes

- Filing the report before the last claim is paid or compromised.
- Missing an heir who moved, then relying on mailing to an old address.
- Treating a text message that says "looks fine" as a receipt. Get a signed one.
- Letting fees be paid without a written approval, then finding an heir who does not approve.
- Forgetting a final income tax question. Our page on [probate accounting](/learn/probate/probate-inventory-and-estate-accounting) shows what to track from day one.
