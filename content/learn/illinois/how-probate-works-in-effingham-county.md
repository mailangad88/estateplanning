Picture a parent who lived near Effingham, Altamont, Teutopolis or Dieterich and has just died. If an estate has to be opened, it is an Effingham County, Illinois matter, heard in the 4th Judicial Circuit. This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois) and is about Effingham County in Illinois, not other counties of that name.

It covers the clerk's office, how an estate opens, filing the will, remote hearings, self-help and case lookup. Then it asks a question many local families face: a parent signed a living trust, so is probate even needed? If you want to talk through your own papers, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-effingham-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the Effingham County probate court and clerk?

The Circuit Clerk is at 120 West Jefferson Avenue, Suite 101, Effingham, ZIP 62401, in the county Government Center. The Illinois courts directory lists the same address and places the courthouse in the 4th Circuit. The mailing address on the county site is PO Box 586.

The clerk lists 217-342-4065 and the email circuitclerkinfo@effinghamcountyil.gov. Hours are Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. The circuit's own site gives a separate main number, 217-342-6166, and links pages for courthouses and circuit clerks.

| Contact | What the official pages list | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Circuit Clerk | 120 West Jefferson Avenue, Suite 101; 217-342-4065 | Delivering the will, filing questions, case files |
| Clerk email | circuitclerkinfo@effinghamcountyil.gov | Written questions |
| 4th Circuit main line | 217-342-6166 | Circuit-level information |
| Legal Self-Help Center | Effingham Public Library, 200 N Third Street; 217-342-2464 | Computers, printing, forms help |

The clerk's page also warns that cell phones and laptops are not allowed inside the courthouse. Print what you need before you go, and ask the clerk before you plan a long wait in the building.

## How does an Effingham County estate open?

Someone asks the court to open the estate. If there is a will, the request asks the court to admit it and name the executor. If there is no will, the court appoints an administrator and the heirs are set by statute.

The Effingham pages list probate only as a civil case category, labelled PR. They do not lay out steps, forms or a local timetable. So the sequence below is the general Illinois process, which the page on [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) explains in more detail.

1. Order certified death certificates. The page on [Illinois death certificates](/learn/illinois/illinois-death-certificates-who-can-order-county-clerk-or-idph) shows who can order them.
2. Locate the original will and deliver it to the clerk.
3. File the petition and the oath and bond papers that apply.
4. Attend the hearing, in person or remotely if the court allows.
5. Receive [letters of office](/glossary/letters-of-office) from the court.
6. Notify heirs, inventory assets and deal with claims over the months that follow.

## How do I file the will in Effingham County?

Whoever holds the original will must file it with the circuit clerk of the proper county. Illinois law sets that duty at 755 ILCS 5/6-1, and the page on [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) covers it along with the 30-day clocks. A photocopy cannot stand in for the original.

The Effingham pages do not say whether the clerk takes a will by mail, whether a receipt is issued, or what ID is needed. Phone 217-342-4065 and ask. Keep your own copy and note who you spoke with.

If you cannot find the will, [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois) lists the usual hiding places. A [lost original will](/learn/illinois/lost-original-will-in-illinois) raises its own problems.

## Can I e-file and attend a hearing remotely?

E-filing is the starting point. The clerk's civil page says the Illinois Supreme Court requires electronic filing for civil cases in circuit court. It points to Odyssey eFileIL and to other providers listed on the state e-filing site, and it mentions a public access computer at the clerk's office.

Remote attendance is less clear. The clerk's page links a Zoom information item, and the 4th Circuit's site links a remote hearings information document. The pages read for this guide did not show how to request a remote hearing or who approves it. The state's remote proceedings page says each circuit follows its own administrative orders and local rules.

So treat remote attendance as something to ask about, not something to assume. Ask the clerk, or the attorney handling the case, whether your hearing can be remote and what link applies. Never post a meeting passcode in a family group chat.

## What self-help and case lookup does Effingham County offer?

The Effingham County Legal Self-Help Center sits in the Effingham Public Library at 200 N Third Street. Its Illinois Legal Aid Online listing names computers, internet, printers, scanners, help finding and preparing online court forms, and referrals. It says plainly that it does not give legal advice.

The clerk's site also links the statewide approved forms. None of the pages read for this guide list probate or will forms specifically. If you plan to go without a lawyer, read about [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) and decide how much you want to take on.

For case lookup, the clerk's site has a Judici quicklink. It does not explain which case types appear or how current they are. Use it to find a case number, then call the clerk for the file. The page on [how to look up a probate case](/learn/probate/how-to-look-up-a-probate-case) explains what a docket summary shows.

## What if my parent left a living trust?

A living trust changes the picture, but only for what the trust actually owns. The trust controls assets titled to the trustee. An Illinois trust is created by a transfer of property to a trustee or a declaration that the owner holds specific property as trustee (760 ILCS 3/401), as the page on [unfunded living trusts in Illinois](/learn/illinois/unfunded-living-trust-in-illinois) explains.

That leads to three common situations in a farm-and-small-town county like Effingham:

- **Fully funded trust.** The house was deeded to the trustee and the accounts were retitled. The successor trustee follows the trust, and the court may never open a file. The [Illinois successor trustee first 90 days](/learn/illinois/illinois-successor-trustee-first-90-days) page shows the early tasks.
- **Partly funded trust.** Some assets are in the trust and some are still in the parent's own name. Those need the will and often the court.
- **Trust signed, nothing retitled.** The pour-over will sends the leftovers to the trust, but usually through probate. See [pour-over wills in Illinois](/learn/illinois/pour-over-wills-in-illinois).

A house is the usual gap. The page on [putting a house in a living trust in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-put-your-house-in-a-living-trust-in-illinois) says the trust document does not move the house, and a signed and recorded deed does.

### Example: Lorraine and the trust that missed one account

Example only, with invented names. Lorraine, 79, signed a living trust and deeded her house to it. Her $64,000 savings account was retitled, but a $120,000 brokerage account stayed in her own name. Her pour-over will names her son Dale as executor and trustee.

After she dies, Dale takes the trust to the bank and starts as successor trustee. The house and savings need no court. The brokerage account is a probate asset in her own name.

| Asset | Titled to | Route | Court file? |
|---|---|---|---|
| House | Lorraine as trustee | Trust terms | No |
| Savings account | Lorraine as trustee | Trust terms | No |
| Brokerage account | Lorraine alone | Affidavit or probate | Maybe |
| Original will | Held by Dale | Filed with clerk | Yes, the will |

The brokerage account is $120,000. The unfunded trust page cites the small estate affidavit for personal property up to $150,000 (755 ILCS 5/25-1), not counting registered vehicles. If that limit is met and the brokerage firm accepts it, Dale may avoid a full estate. The [small estate checker](/tools/small-estate-checker) tests your own numbers. A house in her own name would have changed this, because the affidavit never covers real estate.

Even then, Dale still files the original will with the clerk, because the duty applies to any will. The page on [the small estate affidavit in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) explains who signs and what responsibility comes with it.

## What mistakes do Effingham County families make?

- Giving the trust to the clerk and keeping the will at home.
- Assuming a signed trust means no probate, without checking each title.
- Calling a bank "the court" and a county office "the clerk" and losing a day to the wrong building.
- Arriving at the courthouse with a phone or laptop they cannot take in.
- Using the Legal Self-Help Center expecting legal advice it says it does not give.
- Moving assets informally into the trust after the death, instead of working with the executor.
