If your mother or brother lived in Peoria, Peoria Heights, Chillicothe or Dunlap, the estate belongs in Peoria County, Illinois. The county is part of the 10th Judicial Circuit, which also covers Marshall, Putnam, Stark and Tazewell counties. This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

It covers the courthouse, the Circuit Clerk, how the original will is filed, remote hearings, self-help and case lookup. It also looks at a problem families run into in any county: nobody in the family wants the executor's job. If you want to talk through your own situation, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-peoria-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the Peoria County Courthouse and the Circuit Clerk?

The Peoria County Courthouse is at 324 Main Street, Peoria, IL 61602. The Circuit Clerk's office is inside, in Room G-22. The clerk lists its phone as 309-672-6000, with hours of 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.

The clerk's Civil Division handles probate and wills, along with adoption, chancery, divorce, family, juvenile and small claims. Traffic and criminal cases have their own divisions, so an estate family can ignore those.

Court Administration is a separate office, in Suite 215 on the same address. The Illinois Courts directory lists it with phones (309) 672-6036 and (309) 672-6957, open 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. It is where you go for help beyond the Self-Help Center.

| Place | Where | What a probate family uses it for |
|---|---|---|
| Circuit Clerk, Civil Division | 324 Main Street, Room G-22 | Will filing, probate and wills case files |
| Court Administration | 324 Main Street, Room 215 | Remote hearing questions, help beyond self-help |
| Legal Self-Help Center and law library | 324 Main Street, Room 211 | Forms, computers, e-filing kiosk |

The Illinois Courts directory did not list parking, so ask the court before you go.

## What should the family gather before going downtown?

Start with the will and a rough list of what the person owned. [Finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois) explains where wills hide, from a home file to an attorney's vault.

Then sort the list. Accounts with a named beneficiary and jointly held property usually pass outside court. A house in the deceased person's name alone usually points to probate. The [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter) shows which pile each item lands in.

## How does an estate open and the original will get filed?

The will comes first. The county's e-filing FAQ answers the question directly: the original will still needs to be filed for safekeeping with the Circuit Clerk's office, just as it was before e-filing. The statewide duty is covered in [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk).

The case itself is then e-filed. For a new electronic probate case, the FAQ says you attach a copy of the will, stamped and bearing the will number, to your petition. So the clerk's will number is what ties the paper original to the electronic case.

The clerk's pages direct e-filers to the eFileIL system. They do not list room numbers or hours for will filing, and the county pages do not say what the clerk wants in the way of identification. The clerk's phone line is the place to ask.

A few habits help:

1. Photocopy the will before you leave home.
2. Carry the original flat, without staples or clips.
3. Write down the date of death and the full legal name.
4. Ask what the clerk needs for the stamped copy.
5. Keep any receipt with the estate papers.

After the will is on file, the usual steps follow. [How to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) and the [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) walk through petition, notice, claims and closing.

## What if nobody in the family wants to be executor?

This happens more than people expect. The named executor lives far away, is ill, or has died. The siblings do not get along. Everyone looks at the house and says "not me."

Start with the will. A named executor is not drafted into service. The page on [whether you can refuse to be an executor in Illinois](/learn/illinois/can-you-refuse-to-be-executor-in-illinois) explains that the Probate Act gives a named executor 30 days after learning of the appointment to begin probate or formally decline.

Then look for a backup. Many wills name one, and that person goes through the same 30-day choice. [Who can be an executor in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-can-be-an-executor-in-illinois) lists the basics: at least 18, a U.S. resident, of sound mind, and not barred by a felony conviction or prison status, with one narrow exception.

If the named executor has died and no backup is named, or every named person declines, the court can appoint an administrator with the will annexed. The petition lists heirs and others with equal or higher right to serve, and they are mailed a copy. The page on [what happens if there is no executor](/learn/probate/what-happens-if-there-is-no-executor) gives the longer picture.

Distance alone is not a disqualifier. A relative who lives out of state can serve, though the court may require a bond. The page on a [non-resident executor in Illinois](/learn/illinois/non-resident-executor-in-illinois) covers that.

<!-- visual: whatif id=executor-burden -->

| If this happens | What usually follows |
|---|---|
| Named executor says yes | That person starts probate |
| Named executor declines | A backup named in the will is next |
| Named executor has died, no backup | The court can appoint an administrator with the will annexed |
| Nobody in the family will serve | A person with a right to serve, chosen by the court, steps in |

Telling the family early matters. The court cannot move until someone with a right to serve asks, so silence costs weeks.

## Can I attend a Peoria County hearing remotely?

Sometimes. The 10th Circuit's Remote Hearings page says some proceedings can be held on Zoom at each judge's discretion. You join at zoom.us/join, or by phone at 1-312-626-6799, and enter the Meeting ID from your notice.

You do not need a Zoom account, and there is no charge. You enter your first and last name and wait to be invited in. If you are disconnected, you rejoin with the same Meeting ID.

To ask for a remote hearing, the court's request page says to submit the request at least 3 business days before the proceeding, in writing or in person. You can use the Remote Hearing Request Form or write your own. Send it to the courtroom clerk, or deliver it to the Circuit Clerk at Room G22. The court decides.

An attorney handling the case follows the same steps and must tell the client. The court may also switch a hearing from in person to remote on its own. The pages do not cover Zoom etiquette or recording rules, so call the court at (309) 672-6036 for those.

## What self-help does the Peoria County court list?

The Peoria County Legal Self-Help Center is in Room 211 of the courthouse. Its page lists hours of 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday, and a phone of 309-672-6084.

It provides statewide approved court forms, print materials, computers and online resources. Court navigators help you find them. The page says staff cannot provide legal advice of any kind, and it does not mention probate, so ask whether probate forms are on hand.

The Illinois Courts directory also lists four public computers in Room 211 with free online legal research, and e-filing computers that can send documents to the Circuit Clerk at no charge unless you print. For a person without a computer at home, that is a practical starting point.

The 10th Circuit site links its own forms page and the Illinois Legal Aid Online Peoria page. For handling the case without hiring anyone, read [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) first. For court costs, see [the Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver).

## How do I look up a Peoria County case?

Use the Peoria County Records Portal. The Circuit Clerk's page and the 10th Circuit site both link to it for searching court records. It is an online portal, so no trip downtown is needed to look.

The pages do not describe search fields or which estate documents appear online. If you are an heir and are not sure an estate was ever opened, [how to find out if you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois) explains the next steps. The clerk's phone line is the fallback.

## Example: the Hendricks family in Dunlap

This is an invented example. Margaret, 81, lived in Dunlap and died in October. Her will names her son Paul as executor and her daughter Carol as backup. Paul died two years ago, and Margaret never updated the will. Her other son, Dennis, lives in Tucson and says he cannot take time off.

Carol has a full-time job and a long commute. She nearly declines, then learns she has 30 days to decide. She takes the original will to the Circuit Clerk at Room G-22 and keeps the will number for the petition. Her attorney explains she can serve, and that the court may ask her to post a bond only if the will does not waive one.

Carol agrees to serve if Dennis handles the online accounts from Tucson. If she had declined, the court could have appointed an administrator with the will annexed, after notice to the heirs. Carol's attorney also files a request so Dennis can join the first hearing by Zoom.
