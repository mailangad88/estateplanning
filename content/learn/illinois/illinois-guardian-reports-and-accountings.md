The order is signed, and you are now the guardian. The next surprise is the paperwork. The court that gave you authority expects proof, on paper and on a schedule, that you are using it for the person's benefit. The task is manageable, and it is far easier when you set up good habits in the first week.

This page covers the Illinois reporting duties for an adult guardian. It supports our [guardianship overview](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship). Readers in New Jersey can compare their duties in [New Jersey guardian reports](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-guardian-duties-reports-and-accountings-after-appointment).

## What does the guardian of the person report?

The guardian of the person files a report describing the adult's condition and how their needs are being met. The statute lists the duties to provide support, care, comfort, health and maintenance (755 ILCS 5/11a-17), and requires reports to the court at least annually. A good report covers:

- The person's current address and who lives with them.
- Medical conditions, treatments and any change since the last report.
- Visits you made and the person's own wishes.
- Whether the guardianship is still needed or the powers should change.

Courts often provide a form. Answer it honestly. A report that admits a problem and explains the plan is better than a smooth report that proves wrong.

## What does the guardian of the estate file?

The guardian of the estate files three kinds of documents:

1. **Inventory.** A sworn list of assets and values, due soon after appointment (see Article XIV of the Probate Act for the deadline).
2. **Accounts.** Periodic statements of money received and spent, with a balance of what remains. The Probate Act and local rules set the interval.
3. **Final account.** Filed when the guardianship ends, to close the books and ask to be discharged.

The estate guardian's duty under the statute is to manage the estate frugally and use income and principal for the person's support (755 ILCS 5/11a-18). Each account should show that.

## What records should you keep?

Keep a separate bank account titled for the guardianship and do not mix funds. Save every receipt, pay stub and statement. Write a short note for any unusual item, such as a roof repair or a gift the person previously made.

A sample list that works:

- Monthly bank and brokerage statements.
- Receipts for care, rent, medical bills and supplies.
- Mileage log for trips made for the person, if reimbursement is sought.
- Copies of every court order and notice.

## What happens if the numbers do not add up?

The court or an interested relative can ask questions. A judge may require supporting documents, set a hearing or order the guardian to repay money that was not spent properly. Common problems are mixed accounts, undocumented cash, paying the guardian's own bills and gifts the court did not approve. Early correction is better. If you spot an error, tell the lawyer and fix it in the next filing.

## How do the reports compare?

| | Guardian of the person | Guardian of the estate |
|---|---|---|
| Main filing | Report on the person | Inventory and accounts |
| Timing | At least annually | Inventory early; accounts on a schedule |
| Key content | Health, home, care | Income, expenses, assets |
| Proof | Medical notes, visits | Statements and receipts |
| Statute | 755 ILCS 5/11a-17 | 755 ILCS 5/11a-18 |
| Ends | At discharge | Final account and discharge |

## What is the routine, step by step?

1. Open a guardianship bank account and deposit the person's funds there.
2. Calendar the inventory date, report dates and account dates from the order and local rules.
3. Keep receipts and statements in one folder each month.
4. A week before each deadline, total the income and expenses and compare to the bank balance.
5. Complete the court's form or have your attorney prepare it.
6. File with the clerk and give copies to anyone the court requires.
7. Keep copies, and respond quickly to any court question.

## Example: Priya and her uncle

Priya, 47, is guardian of the estate for her uncle Raj, 85, whose savings total $132,000. She opens a guardianship account, pays his care facility $4,300 a month from his pension and savings, and logs every bill. Her first inventory lists the account, a certificate of deposit and a car. A year later, she files an account that shows $15,000 of pension and benefit income, $51,600 of care costs, $1,900 of medical bills and a balance of $93,500, which matches the bank. The judge approves it.

## What should the first week look like?

A new guardian can do five things in the first week that save months of trouble. First, order certified copies of the letters. Second, open the guardianship account and move the person's funds into it. Third, notify Social Security, the pension payer and the bank of the appointment. Fourth, start the ledger, even a simple spreadsheet with date, payee, amount, category and receipt. Fifth, write down the filing dates in a calendar with reminders a month ahead.

If you also hold a power of attorney from before, keep its records separate. See [revoking an Illinois power of attorney](/learn/illinois/revoking-an-illinois-power-of-attorney) to learn how a guardianship interacts with an older agent's role.

## What happens when the accounts are questioned?

An interested person, such as a sibling, can ask the court to review an account or to compel one. The judge may set a hearing, request more documents or appoint an accountant. Answers should be short, backed by paper and calm. Taking the questions as a chance to clarify, rather than an attack, keeps cases short. Fixed mistakes are far better than hidden ones.
