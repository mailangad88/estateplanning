If your mother or father lived in Belleville, O'Fallon, East St. Louis or Fairview Heights, the estate belongs in St. Clair County, Illinois, not the Michigan or Alabama counties with the same name. This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

It covers where the courthouse and the Circuit Clerk are, how an estate opens, how the original will is filed, remote appearances, the court's self-help resources and how to look up a case. It also follows a family with a tie to Scott Air Force Base. To talk through your own situation, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-st-clair-county-illinois&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the St. Clair County Courthouse?

It is at 10 Public Square, Belleville, IL 62220. The Illinois Courts directory lists St. Clair County as part of the 20th Judicial Circuit, District 5, with phone numbers (618) 277-6600 and (618) 277-1398, open 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The Circuit Clerk lists the same street address and the number (618) 277-6600.

The clerk's own page gives office hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., Monday through Friday. The two hours differ by thirty minutes, so call before a long drive. The clerk also lists an email address, CircuitClerk@co.st-clair.il.us, for general questions.

An older version of the clerk's FAQ placed the clerk's family division window on the third floor and the Law Library on the first floor. It is an older page, so the room and floor for probate are worth confirming by phone. This page does not give a courtroom number for that reason.

Collinsville sits partly in St. Clair County and partly in Madison County. Illinois sets probate venue by where the person lived at death (755 ILCS 5/5-1), so the decedent's actual address decides the county. The page on [probate venue when property is in two Illinois counties](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties) explains the rule.

## What does the Circuit Clerk do in a probate case?

The Circuit Clerk keeps the court file. That means receiving the original will, accepting the petition, setting the case number, issuing letters of office after the judge's order and keeping the docket. The clerk does not give legal advice or choose the executor.

The clerk's Documents and Forms page lists these probate forms:

- Petition for Probate of Will and for Letters Testamentary.
- Petition for Letters of Administration, for an estate with no will.
- Inventory, Annual Report and Report - Current Account.
- Small Estate Affidavit.
- Guardianship petitions, which are a different kind of case.

The page shows no instructions for completing or filing the forms. The Letters of Administration packet itself says the hearing is set at the St. Clair County Courthouse in Belleville, and it asks whether the petitioner wants independent administration. The page on [independent vs supervised administration](/learn/illinois/independent-vs-supervised-administration-in-illinois) explains that choice.

## How does an estate open in St. Clair County?

A person with the original will files it, someone files a petition, the judge appoints a representative, and the clerk issues letters of office. The steps below follow the order a family usually meets them.

<!-- visual: diagram name=ProbateTimeline -->

| Step | What happens | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Find the will | Locate the original and a death certificate | Home, bank box, drafting attorney |
| 2. File the will | Deliver the original to the Circuit Clerk | Courthouse, Belleville |
| 3. Petition | Ask the court to admit the will or appoint an administrator | Electronic filing |
| 4. Hearing | The judge admits the will and names the representative | Courtroom set on the petition |
| 5. Letters | The clerk issues letters of office | Circuit Clerk |
| 6. Notices and inventory | Notify heirs, publish for creditors, list assets | Executor and attorney |

The [petition for letters of office](/learn/illinois/illinois-petition-for-letters-of-office) goes line by line.

First, ask whether a court case is needed at all. The [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter) separates accounts that pass by beneficiary form from property that needs the court.

## How do I file the original will in St. Clair County?

File it with the Circuit Clerk. The clerk's FAQ says wills are filed with the Circuit Clerk's office and, once filed, become a public record that can be viewed. Illinois law puts the duty on whoever holds the original, and it applies right after the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1).

The page on [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) explains the 30-day clocks. The clerk's FAQ also says faxes are not accepted for filing under local rules, so a faxed will would not receive a file mark.

If you cannot find the original, do not file a copy and hope. See [what to do about a lost original will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/lost-original-will-in-illinois).

## Do I need a lawyer to open a St. Clair County estate?

The clerk's site says eFiling is required on all civil case types for attorneys and self-represented litigants, citing Illinois Supreme Court Rule 9. That includes probate. So a person without a lawyer still files electronically, and the clerk's office has three public-use e-filing kiosks according to the Illinois Courts directory.

A small estate may need no case. Illinois allows a small estate affidavit for personal property of $150,000 or less with no real estate, as described in the page on the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit). The clerk's FAQ notes that the affidavit is not filed with the clerk but is given to a bank or similar holder. The St. Clair County form was last updated in February 2026.

A house in the decedent's sole name usually means probate. If the estate is complicated, or heirs disagree, the guide to [handling an Illinois estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) shows where people get stuck.

## Can I attend a St. Clair County probate hearing by Zoom?

Possibly, but confirm it for your hearing. The 20th Circuit's Guidelines for Virtual Courtroom Proceedings, dated May 2020, describe Zoom hearings. Participants give an email address to the clerk's office to receive an invitation, and people without Zoom may join by conference call.

Those guidelines do not mention probate, and the clerk's page lists a separate survey and the guidelines without a probate-specific rule. The Illinois Courts directory also lists remote proceedings information for the courthouse. So ask the clerk or the judge's staff before assuming you can join from home.

The directory adds that interpreters are provided with an order from the judge. It lists a court disability coordinator at (618) 825-2575 and a relay number, 711. Mention any accommodation you need when you call.

## What self-help does the St. Clair County court offer?

The Law Library houses a Self-Help Center, with six internet stations, a printer at each, and one copier, according to the Illinois Courts directory. Illinois Legal Aid Online lists it at 10 Public Square, first floor, phone (618) 825-2634. It says the center provides legal information, not legal advice.

It can help with form instructions, general questions and court procedure. That makes it a sensible first stop for the clerk's fillable forms. It cannot tell you whether to ask for independent administration or how to divide property, which is where an attorney's advice matters.

The [Illinois probate checklist](/free/illinois-probate-checklist) gives you a list to bring along, and [what an executor does in Illinois](/learn/illinois/what-does-an-executor-do-in-illinois) explains the job.

## How do I look up a St. Clair County case?

Use the Court Record Search on the Circuit Clerk's website. The clerk describes it as covering civil and criminal court dates and warns that dates can change when a case is continued, so check again before you go. The page lists a separate Court Dockets tool too.

The site also mentions docket subscriptions through an outside service. It does not say that the search shows full probate files or every filing. For a document you cannot find online, call the clerk at (618) 277-6600 and ask how to see the file.

## What if the family is a military family near Scott Air Force Base?

The court case does not change, but the timing and paperwork do. Scott Air Force Base is in St. Clair County, so many families here have a service member, a retiree or a reservist in the picture. The estate runs through the Circuit Clerk. VA benefits run through the VA.

The page on [estate planning for veterans and military families in Illinois](/learn/illinois/estate-planning-for-veterans-and-military-families-in-illinois) says VA survivor and burial benefits each need their own application and do not depend on whether a will exists. It also says the DD-214 speeds every one. Next of kin can request a deceased veteran's records from the National Archives if it is lost.

The Illinois Department of Veterans' Affairs says its Veteran Service Officers give free help with survivor and burial benefits. The [military family planning guide](/free/military-family-planning-guide) helps a family keep those records in one place. The page on [VA survivor benefits and life insurance claims](/learn/after-a-death/claiming-life-insurance-pension-and-va-survivor-benefits) covers what to claim and when.

## A worked example: Marcus and his father's estate

Example only, with invented names. Walter, 79, a retired veteran, lived in Belleville and died holding a will that names his son Marcus as executor. Marcus, 41, is a technical sergeant at Scott Air Force Base, but he is called to a training assignment out of state.

Marcus's sister finds the original will in Walter's desk and brings it to the Circuit Clerk in Belleville within days. Marcus calls the clerk, asks about e-filing, and hires an Illinois attorney who files the petition electronically and asks for independent administration. He asks whether he can join the hearing by Zoom and gets an answer from the clerk's office.

The judge admits the will and the clerk issues letters of office. Meanwhile Marcus finds Walter's DD-214 in the same desk and sets up a call with an Illinois Veteran Service Officer to ask about survivor and burial benefits. The VA claims and the court case move on separate tracks.

Walter's house is in his sole name, so Marcus's sister handles walk-throughs while the estate stays open. The page on [serving as executor from another state](/learn/illinois/non-resident-executor-in-illinois) covers what can be done remotely.
