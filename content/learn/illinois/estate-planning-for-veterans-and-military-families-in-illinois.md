You retired to Peoria after twenty years, or you are an Air Guard member in Springfield with orders coming. Your family's safety net is spread across VA-run life insurance, a Thrift Savings Plan account, an old will and a discharge paper in a box. Each piece follows its own rules.

This page, part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois), puts the whole plan for a veteran or service member in one place. It covers which forms control the insurance, what to sign before a deployment, how guardians work for military parents, what happens to VA benefits at death, and what your family needs to be able to find. It ends with a checklist and a worked example. To have your own papers reviewed, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-estate-planning-for-veterans-and-military-families-in-illinois&topic=will).

## Does SGLI or VGLI follow my will?

No. Servicemembers' Group Life Insurance (SGLI) and Veterans' Group Life Insurance (VGLI) pay the person named on the form, whatever the will says, as our guide to [claiming life insurance, a pension and VA survivor benefits](/learn/after-a-death/claiming-life-insurance-pension-and-va-survivor-benefits) explains. A Thrift Savings Plan account has its own form too. If your will leaves everything to your wife and your SGLI form still names your mother, your mother receives the insurance.

<!-- visual: diagram name=BeneficiaryBeatsWill -->

Updating happens in different places:

- **SGLI while serving.** The VA says you submit changes through the SGLI Online Enrollment System (SOES), which you reach by signing in to milConnect and choosing "Manage my SGLI."
- **VGLI after you leave.** The VA says you can update beneficiaries online through the Prudential OSGLI portal or by filing the VGLI Beneficiary Designation form (SGLV 8721).
- **Converting at separation.** The VA says you must apply for VGLI within 1 year and 120 days of leaving the military, and that no proof of good health is needed if you apply within 240 days. Check the VA's VGLI page for current rules before you separate.

Name a primary and a [contingent beneficiary](/learn/beneficiary-designations/contingent-beneficiaries) on each form. Naming a minor child directly can mean an Illinois court supervises the money until age 18, as [when a minor inherits money in Illinois](/learn/illinois/when-a-minor-inherits-money-in-illinois) explains, so a trust is often cleaner. The general rules on which document wins are in [which controls: a will, trust, joint account or beneficiary form](/learn/wills/which-controls-will-trust-joint-account-or-beneficiary-form).

## What documents should a service member have before deployment?

Four documents, plus the military's own paperwork. A base legal assistance office prepares simple wills, powers of attorney and health care directives at no charge to eligible service members, and federal law gives those wills special recognition, as [oral, military, statutory and international wills](/learn/wills/oral-military-statutory-and-international-wills) describes.

1. **A will.** Illinois requires a will in writing, signed by you and attested by two credible witnesses (755 ILCS 5/4-3), as covered in [Illinois will requirements](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-requirements). A military will works too.
2. **A power of attorney for property.** The Illinois statutory short form needs your signature, one witness who is not your agent, and a notary (755 ILCS 45/3-3), as explained in [the Illinois power of attorney for property](/learn/illinois/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property). The form covers government benefits, including military benefits. Your agent cannot change a beneficiary designation unless you add that power in specific terms (755 ILCS 45/3-4), so update your SGLI form yourself before you leave.
3. **A health care power of attorney.** The Illinois form needs one adult witness and no notary (755 ILCS 45/4-10). See [the Illinois health care power of attorney](/learn/illinois/illinois-healthcare-power-of-attorney).
4. **A short-term guardian paper, if you have children.** This covers the months you are away, which a will never does.

## How do guardians work for military parents in Illinois?

Two different papers do two different jobs. A will names who raises your children if you die. A short-term guardian appointment covers the time you are alive but away.

Illinois lets a parent sign a short-term guardian appointment in front of two adult witnesses, with no court filing (755 ILCS 5/11-5.4). The default term is 365 days, and a parent on active military duty can set a longer term, covering the active duty period plus 30 days, as our page on [Illinois standby and short-term guardians for children](/learn/illinois/illinois-standby-and-short-term-guardians-for-children) explains. If the child's other parent is living and involved, that parent usually must consent.

Single parents and dual-military couples are generally required to keep a family care plan with their command. That plan tells the military who is responsible, not a school or a pediatrician. Our guide on [who takes your kids in an emergency while you travel or deploy](/learn/guardianship/who-takes-your-kids-in-an-emergency-while-you-travel-or-deploy) covers the school pickup list and medical consent that fill that gap. For the will side, see [naming a guardian for your children](/learn/illinois/naming-a-guardian-for-your-children), and the [guardian picker](/tools/guardian-picker) can help if you have not decided whom to ask.

## What happens to VA benefits when a veteran dies?

The veteran's own benefits end, and the family's possible benefits begin, but only by application. The VA says eligible survivors may receive benefits it owed at the time of death. Do not spend regular payments that land after a death, since those are often reclaimed. Tell the VA promptly.

Survivor programs the VA lists include:

- **Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC)**, for certain spouses, children and parents when the death is tied to service, such as a death on active duty or from a service-connected condition.
- **Survivors Pension**, for some surviving spouses and unmarried dependent children of veterans with wartime service, with income and asset limits.
- **CHAMPVA** health coverage for some survivors.
- **Burial benefits**, covered in the next section.

The VA decides eligibility, so check current amounts on va.gov rather than a remembered figure. The VA also lists [Aid and Attendance](/learn/elder-care/va-aid-and-attendance) as an addition to the Survivors Pension.

Free help is available in Illinois. The Illinois Department of Veterans' Affairs says its Veteran Service Officers give free assistance to veterans and their dependents and survivors, including help applying for federal and state benefits and with burial and survivor benefits.

## Who can get VA burial benefits?

According to the VA, burial in a national cemetery can be available for:

| Person | VA eligibility condition |
|---|---|
| Veteran | Did not receive a dishonorable discharge. For some other discharge types, a VA regional office decides. |
| Service member | Died while on active duty |
| Spouse or surviving spouse | Married to an eligible veteran |
| Minor child | Child of an eligible veteran |
| Unmarried adult dependent child | May qualify in some cases |

A separate burial allowance toward private funeral costs has its own rules. The VA says whoever pays the costs can apply, such as a spouse, child, parent, executor, friend or funeral home, if no other source reimburses them. The veteran must not have received a dishonorable discharge, and the death must fit one of the VA's listed circumstances. The application is VA Form 21P-530EZ, and the VA says most claims must be filed within 2 years after the burial, with exceptions such as a service-connected death. How the estate repays whoever covered the funeral is covered in [paying for a funeral from an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/paying-for-a-funeral-from-an-illinois-estate).

## What should family be able to find?

The DD-214 first, since survivors need it for burial and survivor benefits. If it is lost, the VA says next of kin can request a deceased veteran's records from the National Archives online through eVetRecs, or by Standard Form 180. Next of kin includes a surviving spouse who has not remarried, a parent, a child or a sibling, and they must send a death certificate, another public record of death, or a letter from a funeral home. Keep a copy at home instead.

Put these with the DD-214, and list where each is in a [letter of instruction](/learn/basics/letter-of-instruction):

- The SGLI or VGLI confirmation and the TSP beneficiary statement.
- Your VA file number and any rating decision letters.
- The original will, and both powers of attorney.
- The short-term guardian paper and the family care plan.
- A list of firearms, since Illinois heirs need their own FOID card (430 ILCS 65/2), as covered in [guns and FOID cards after a death](/learn/illinois/illinois-firearms-and-foid-cards-after-a-death).

The free [military family planning guide](/free/military-family-planning-guide) has a deployment timeline and fill-in pages for these records.

## What happens if a veteran has no plan?

The insurance still pays the named beneficiary, but everything else falls to Illinois law. Without a will, the Probate Act sets the heirs (755 ILCS 5/2-1). With a spouse and children, the spouse takes one-half and the children share the other half, as [who inherits without a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-inherits-without-a-will-in-illinois) explains. A judge appoints a guardian for minor children with no nomination to start from. An outdated SGLI form can send the largest single asset to an ex-spouse or a parent.

## Checklist for veterans and service members

- [ ] SGLI or VGLI beneficiaries checked in SOES or the OSGLI portal, with a primary and a contingent.
- [ ] VGLI application calendared before separation, if you want coverage after service.
- [ ] TSP and any civilian retirement account beneficiary forms match the plan.
- [ ] A will signed with two witnesses, or a military will, naming a guardian and a backup.
- [ ] A trust for children if they could inherit insurance or a house.
- [ ] Illinois statutory power of attorney for property, signed, witnessed and notarized.
- [ ] Illinois health care power of attorney.
- [ ] Short-term guardian appointment and school pickup list before a deployment.
- [ ] DD-214 copy, VA file number and benefit letters in one folder your family knows about.
- [ ] The name of a county or IDVA service officer written down for your survivors.

## A worked example: Darnell and Keisha

Example only, with invented names. Darnell, 36, is an Army Reserve staff sergeant in Rockford with orders for a nine-month deployment. He and his wife, Keisha, have two children, 4 and 9. His father served in the Navy and lives with them.

Darnell finds three problems. His SGLI designation, made before he married, names his father. His legal assistance office will names no guardian. And if Keisha were hurt while he was overseas, nobody could sign for the kids.

He updates SGLI in SOES, naming Keisha as primary and a trust for the children as contingent. He signs a new will with a guardian and a backup, Illinois powers of attorney naming Keisha with his sister as successor, and, with Keisha, a short-term guardian appointment naming his sister to take effect if neither parent can care for the children. Keisha also asks the children's school to add his sister to the pickup list.

Last, Keisha puts copies of Darnell's DD-214 and his father's in one folder and writes down the IDVA service office number for burial and survivor benefits later.
