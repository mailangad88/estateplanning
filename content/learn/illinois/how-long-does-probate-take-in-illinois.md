If you are waiting on an inheritance, or you are the executor with a stack of paperwork, the question is how long this will last. Illinois sets several waiting periods in the statute, and they shape the calendar more than the paperwork does.

This page is about the timeline only. For the steps themselves, see [Illinois probate, step by step](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process). For fees, see [what probate costs in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-costs). Our [Illinois guide](/learn/illinois) links to all of it.

## What is a realistic timeline for Illinois probate?

A simple estate with a clear will and no disputes can often be closed in a bit more than six months. The reason is the claims period, which sets a floor. Anything that adds a dispute, a house to sell or a tax return pushes the finish line out.

There is no single legal time limit for an executor to finish. Illinois sets minimum waiting periods and outer limits for specific events. The rest depends on the estate. We do not give an average here, because the statute does not set one, and every county moves at its own pace. For a broader comparison with other states, see our article on [how long probate takes](/learn/probate/how-long-does-probate-take).

## What are the fixed deadlines and waiting periods?

| Event | Time period | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Delivering the will to the clerk | Immediately after death | 755 ILCS 5/6-1 |
| Mailing notice to heirs and legatees | Within 14 days after the order admitting the will | 755 ILCS 5/6-10 |
| Contesting the will | Within 6 months after the will is admitted | 755 ILCS 5/8-1 |
| Creditor claims deadline | Later of 6 months from first publication or 3 months from mailing or delivery of notice | 755 ILCS 5/18-3 |
| Outer limit for most claims | 2 years after death | 755 ILCS 5/18-12 |
| Waiting period before discharge in independent administration | 42 days after the closing report is filed | 755 ILCS 5/28-11 |

These clocks run side by side. The will contest period and the claims period both start soon after the court opens the estate, so they overlap instead of stacking.

## How does the creditor claim period affect the timeline?

The claim period is the biggest piece. The executor publishes a notice once a week for three weeks and mails notice to every known creditor. A creditor must file a claim by the deadline in the notice, which is the later of six months from first publication or three months from mailing or delivery (755 ILCS 5/18-3).

A creditor the executor could not reasonably find still has a limit. Illinois bars most claims two years after death (755 ILCS 5/18-12). A representative who gives proper notice in good faith gets protection from personal liability for creditors who miss the deadline, apart from claims the representative knew about. For which debts come first, see our guide to [debts in probate](/learn/probate/dealing-with-debts-in-probate).

## How does the will contest period affect timing?

Anyone with an interest can petition to contest the will within six months after it is admitted (755 ILCS 5/8-1). Distributing everything before that window closes is a risk, because a successful contest could change who gets what. Executors often wait for the window to end before final distributions. Our article on [contesting a will](/learn/wills/contesting-a-will) explains the grounds.

## What causes Illinois probate to take longer?

Several things add months:

- A will contest, a dispute among heirs or a fight over who should serve as executor.
- Real estate that is hard to sell, or an inherited house that needs repairs. See [selling inherited property](/learn/after-a-death/selling-inherited-property).
- Heirs who cannot be found, which can require extra publication.
- Large or complicated estates, such as a business interest or assets in another state. See [ancillary probate](/learn/probate/ancillary-probate).
- Tax questions. Estates above the Illinois exclusion must deal with the state tax; see our [Illinois estate tax article](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax).
- Supervised administration, where each major step needs a court order. The process article compares it with independent administration.
- A slow executor. Our guide on [removing an executor](/learn/after-a-death/can-an-executor-be-removed) covers what heirs can do.

Court backlogs also vary by county. Ask the clerk or an attorney in your county what to expect for scheduling.

## Can an estate finish faster than that?

Yes, if it does not need formal probate at all. When the decedent left only personal property under a dollar limit, and no real estate, a family member may collect it with an affidavit. There is no creditor-claim clock in the same way, though the person signing the affidavit remains responsible for paying valid debts. See the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) and the [small estate checker](/tools/small-estate-checker).

Assets with beneficiary designations, joint ownership with survivorship, or a living trust pass outside probate on their own timeline.

## Example: Ravi and the house that would not sell

Ravi, 47, is the independent executor for his mother's estate. The court admitted the will in March. Notice went to the two heirs within two weeks, and the claims notice ran that month. By September no claims remained and no one had contested the will. But the house had not sold until November, so Ravi could not make the final distribution until then. He filed his closing report in December and waited 42 days before asking the court to discharge him.

The statutory waiting periods ended in September. The house set the real finish date.

## What does a typical calendar look like?

The table below is a sample sequence, not a promise. It shows how the clocks line up for an estate with no disputes.

| Month | What is happening |
|---|---|
| Month 0 | The will is filed, the court admits it and letters of office issue |
| Weeks 1 to 3 | Heir notices go out, and the creditor notice is published for three weeks and mailed |
| Months 1 to 6 | The executor gathers assets, pays bills and watches for claims |
| About month 6 | The claims period and the will contest period both end |
| Months 6 and later | Final distributions, the closing report and the 42-day wait |

If everything lines up, the earliest realistic finish is a little after the six-month mark. Each delay in the list above pushes the later rows back.

## Does supervised administration add time?

It can. In a supervised estate the judge must approve major steps, so each sale, payment or distribution waits for a hearing date or a signed order. Independent administration lets the executor act without those orders. Neither one changes the claims period or the will contest window. The process article explains how the two types differ and when a court requires supervision.

## What should you do to keep things moving?

1. File the will with the clerk right away.
2. Gather the death certificate, account statements and property records. Our [first 30 days checklist](/checklists/first-30-days-after-a-death) can help.
3. Open the estate promptly, so the notice clocks start.
4. Mail the heir notices on time and keep proof of mailing.
5. Keep clean records of every payment, so the closing report is easy.
6. Ask for help early if a dispute starts.
