A box at the bank sounds like the place where the important papers are. For a grieving family, it is also a closed door. You cannot simply show up with a death certificate and walk out with the contents, and the rules differ depending on what you want to find.

This page separates the two paths. One is a quick look for a will and burial papers. The other is the full removal of contents. For how a will is found and filed, read [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois), and for the larger picture visit our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

## What is the difference between the two paths?

The Safe Deposit Box Opening Act (755 ILCS 15/1) is a narrow tool for finding a will or burial documents. The second path, through the executor or a small estate affidavit, is the general route to everything else.

| | Will-search under the Act | Full removal by the estate |
|---|---|---|
| Who can ask | Certain relatives, a named executor or a former deputy | The executor with letters, or an affiant under a small estate affidavit |
| What may be removed | A will, a codicil, and burial papers | All contents that belong to the estate |
| Court paper needed | No court order; the person signs an affidavit | Letters of office, court order or affidavit under 755 ILCS 5/25-1 |
| Bank must proceed | Not if someone objects or the key is missing | Holder must honor a valid affidavit or letters |
| What happens to a will | Delivered to the circuit clerk | Filed with the clerk, then the case proceeds |

Our page on [finding a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois) explains who counts as an interested person.

## How do you do a will-search opening?

1. Call the branch and ask about its process for a deceased renter. Ask whether the box exists in that renter's name.
2. Bring a certified death certificate and your photo ID.
3. Sign the affidavit stating your interest and your belief that a will or burial papers may be inside.
4. Be present when the bank opens the box.
5. The bank removes any apparent will and delivers it to the circuit clerk of the renter's county, if known. See [filing the original will](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk).
6. The bank may hand burial papers, such as a cemetery deed, to you.
7. Everything else stays in the box until the estate has authority.

The bank is not required to proceed if someone objects or if the key or combination is missing. It must not use this route once it has letters of office, another court order or a small estate affidavit, because those give a different path.

## How does the estate take out the rest?

If a probate case is open, the executor shows certified letters of office and a death certificate. The bank opens the box with the executor present. Banks commonly inventory the contents in front of the executor. The executor then moves valuables to the estate or distributes them as the will directs, and lists them on the [probate inventory](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-inventory).

If no case is open, a small estate affidavit may work for personal property. The statute says the holder must give the affiant access to the box (755 ILCS 5/25-1). That route only fits an estate within the statutory limit with no letters outstanding, no petition pending and no real estate involved. Our [small estate affidavit guide](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) and the [small estate checker](/tools/small-estate-checker) show how to test it. Deeds in the box are real estate documents, so they may signal that probate is needed anyway.

## What if the key is lost or the bank will not talk?

Banks protect customer privacy, so an employee may refuse to say whether a box exists. Start with documents in the home. A rental bill, a key stamped with a number, or a statement showing an annual fee are all clues. Our article on [finding a deceased person's accounts and assets](/learn/after-a-death/how-to-find-a-deceased-persons-accounts-and-assets) lists more places to look.

If you have the right authority and no key, ask for the bank's drilling procedure. The cost of a locksmith or drill is typically charged to the estate. Do not hire a locksmith or take tools to the vault yourself.

## What about a joint box?

If two people rented the box, the survivor often keeps access through the rental contract, but banks treat the death as a change in the account and may ask for a death certificate. The deceased renter's items still belong to the estate. If the survivor removes items, keep a list of what was inside and whose it was. The estate may later need that list.

## What should you not do?

- Do not remove anything beyond a will and burial papers under the Act.
- Do not ask a bank clerk to waive the rules because you are the only child.
- Do not leave a will in the box once you find it. File it.
- Do not guess about ownership of a joint box's contents. Keep a written list.

## Example: Iris and her mother's box

Iris, 57, cannot find her mother's will. She finds a key with a number and a rent notice from a bank in Champaign. She brings a death certificate and signs the affidavit. The box holds the will, a cemetery deed and a coin collection. The bank gives the cemetery deed to Iris, files the will with the circuit clerk, and leaves the coins. After the court issues letters, her brother Marcus, the executor, returns with certified letters, and the coins are listed on the inventory at $2,400. See the [Champaign County page](/estate-planning/illinois/counties/champaign) for the clerk's address.

## What should you bring to the bank?

Call the branch first and ask who handles deceased renters, since many banks route these to a central team. Then bring a certified death certificate, your photo ID, proof of your relationship if you rely on the will-search route, and the key if you have it. For full access, bring certified letters of office, or the court order, or the signed small estate affidavit with the documents the bank asks for. Ask whether you need an appointment and whether a bank officer must be present, and ask what the bank charges for a drilled lock or for an overdue rental fee. Unpaid rent can be deducted from the estate's share once an executor is in place.

If the bank cannot find a box under the name you give, ask it to search by the full legal name and the last four digits of the Social Security number. A box rented in a trust name or a business name may not show under the person's own name.
