You have the signed affidavit and a stack of death certificates. Now comes the part that surprises people: the affidavit does not go to a court, so there is no single office that accepts it. You take it, holder by holder, to every bank, broker and company that has some of the money.

This guide is about that second half of the job. For who qualifies, see our overview of the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit). For the legal duties of the person who signs, see [who signs the affidavit](/learn/illinois/who-signs-an-illinois-small-estate-affidavit). The wider process is mapped in our [Illinois guide](/learn/illinois).

## What does a holder look for in an Illinois affidavit?

A holder checks that the paper tracks the statute, 755 ILCS 5/25-1, and that the person in front of it matches the paper. In practice that means a notarized affidavit with all required statements, a certified death certificate, and valid photo ID. Many holders also want their own cover form and the account number.

The statute protects a holder that pays or delivers property in good faith on a proper affidavit. That protection is the reason holders are careful. A bank teller who is unsure will send the file to legal review rather than guess.

## Which holders will you probably visit?

Different assets need different handling. Use this table as a planning sheet.

| Holder | What to ask for | Common extra requirement | Typical wrinkle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank or credit union | Deceased-customer department | Its own claim form | Funds paid by cashier's check or to an estate-style account |
| Brokerage firm | Estate services unit | Signature verification, account retitling | Securities may be moved to an account in your name before sale |
| Transfer agent (direct-held shares) | Estate or deceased-holder team | Medallion signature stamp | Slow mail processing |
| Employer | Payroll or HR | Final paycheck and unused-time rules | May ask for the affidavit and a copy of the ID |
| Insurance company | Claims department | Own claim form | Policies with a beneficiary do not need the affidavit |
| Safe deposit box | Branch manager | Inventory with a bank employee present | Box may be sealed until the bank is satisfied |

Retirement accounts and life insurance that name a living beneficiary do not use the affidavit at all. The beneficiary collects them directly. Our article on [claiming an account as a beneficiary](/learn/beneficiary-designations/how-to-claim-an-account-as-a-beneficiary) explains that route.

## How do you present the affidavit step by step?

1. Make a list of every holder, with account numbers if you have them and the approximate balance.
2. Call each holder and ask for the deceased-customer or estate department by name.
3. Ask what they need beyond the statute, and whether you can submit by mail, online or in person.
4. Sign enough notarized originals, or ask which copies they will accept.
5. Order enough certified death certificates. Our article on [how many death certificates you need](/learn/after-a-death/how-many-death-certificates-do-i-need) helps you count.
6. Send or deliver the package and keep proof, such as a tracking number or a dated receipt.
7. Follow up in writing after two weeks, and record the name of every person you speak with.
8. When funds arrive, deposit them in an account for the estate's bills and do not mix them with your own money.

Step 8 matters because the signer must pay valid claims before sharing anything with heirs. Our article on [opening an estate bank account in Illinois](/learn/illinois/opening-an-estate-bank-account-in-illinois) is about court estates, but the idea of keeping the money apart applies here too.

## What if the bank wants more than the statute says?

Ask what authority it relies on, politely and in writing. Some banks add requirements such as a waiting period after the date of death or a copy of the will. Others only accept the affidavit if the estate is below a lower internal cap. Our article on [what to do when a bank refuses a small estate affidavit](/learn/probate/what-to-do-when-a-bank-refuses-a-small-estate-affidavit) covers escalation, including how to reach the bank's legal department.

If a holder refuses for a good reason, such as a total estate over the limit, believe it. A refusal is one signal that probate may be the right route.

## A worked example with three holders

Tomas, 49, signs for his father Ruben. Ruben left $22,000 in a credit union savings account, $31,000 in a brokerage account in his name only, and a $2,400 final paycheck. His car is registered with the Secretary of State and is handled separately. The personal estate is about $55,400, well within the $150,000 limit.

The credit union pays in three weeks by check after its legal team reviews the papers. The brokerage asks for a signature verification stamp and moves the securities into an account in Tomas's name as the signer, where he can sell and distribute them. The employer pays the final check within a month. Tomas deposits everything into a separate account, pays the $8,900 funeral bill and a $1,200 utility bill, then splits the remaining $45,300 with his sister, $22,650 each. He keeps a one-page ledger of dates and amounts.

## What should you watch for with brokerage accounts?

Share prices move while the account is being retitled. The value on the date of death matters for the limit and for the tax basis of the shares, which generally resets at death. Our article on [inherited stocks and brokerage accounts](/learn/after-a-death/inherited-stocks-and-brokerage-accounts-selling-retitling-and-tax) explains the basis step-up. If the account has a transfer on death registration, no affidavit is needed. Our article on [transfer on death registration for brokerage accounts in Illinois](/learn/illinois/transfer-on-death-brokerage-accounts-in-illinois) explains why.

## How do you keep records that protect the signer?

Keep a binder or a folder with the affidavit, each holder's response, every deposit slip and every payment. Send the other heirs a short written summary at the end. If anyone later questions the work, the records are your answer. You can sort assets with the [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter) before you start.

## What mistakes slow the process down?

Most delays come from small gaps in the package, not from a legal problem. The same few mistakes appear again and again.

- Mailing the only original of the affidavit to one holder and having none left for the next.
- Sending a photocopy of the death certificate when the holder wants a certified one with a raised seal.
- Leaving an heir off the list, or listing shares that do not add up to the whole.
- Using a nickname on the form when the account is titled in the full legal name.
- Forgetting that a joint owner or a beneficiary on the account means the affidavit is not needed at all.
- Moving money into your own account before the funeral bill and other debts are paid.

A short cover letter helps. It can name the account, cite 755 ILCS 5/25-1, list the attached documents and give your phone number. Holders who process dozens of estates a month appreciate a clean file, and a clean file is less likely to be sent to a second round of legal review.
