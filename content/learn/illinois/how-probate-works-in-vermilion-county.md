When someone dies in Danville, Hoopeston, Georgetown or Westville, the family usually asks the same things. Where does the will go? Who do we call? Can we skip the courthouse entirely if there is not much money? This page covers Vermilion County, Illinois, not the city of Vermilion in Ohio, and it sits inside the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

It walks through where the Circuit Clerk is, what to do with the original will, how remote hearings work, where self-help is offered and how to look up a case. It then adds an angle other county pages skip: a modest estate where the family cannot afford court costs. If you want a plan for your own situation, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-vermilion-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the probate clerk in Vermilion County, Illinois?

Probate filings go to the Vermilion County Circuit Clerk. The Illinois Courts directory lists the clerk at 7 North Vermilion Street, Danville, Illinois 61832, phone 217-554-7700. The county's own site adds that the office is on the 1st Floor and open Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

Vermilion County belongs to the 5th Judicial Circuit. Because estates are opened in the county where the person lived, a family in Hoopeston or Westville uses the same Danville office as a family in Danville. If the person owned land in another county, read [property in two Illinois counties](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties).

We did not confirm separate courtroom numbers or parking details, so call the clerk before you go. Hours can change around holidays.

## What do I do with the original will in Vermilion County?

File it with the Circuit Clerk right away. Illinois law requires anyone who holds a deceased person's original will to file it with the circuit clerk after the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1), and a photocopy cannot replace it. The page on [filing the original will with the circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) explains the duty and the two 30-day clocks.

The clerk's site did not describe a will-drop procedure or an e-filing rule for wills that we could confirm. So treat the clerk's phone number as step one.

1. Find the original, signed will, not a scan.
2. Photocopy it for your own records.
3. Call 217-554-7700 and ask how the office wants the will delivered and what receipt it gives.
4. Deliver it, and keep the receipt with the death certificate.
5. Tell the person named as executor that the will is on file.

If the original cannot be found, do not wait. Read [what to do about a lost original will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/lost-original-will-in-illinois) and [finding a will after a death](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois).

## How does an estate open once the will is filed?

Filing the will keeps it safe. It does not open the estate. The person named as executor, or an heir if there is no will, asks the court for letters of office, which are the document that lets someone act for the estate. The page on [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) lists each paper, and [Illinois probate, step by step](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) shows the stages that follow.

The clerk's Legal Forms menu has a Probate Division category. When we looked, that page contained forms for guardianship of a minor and no estate petitions. So do not assume the clerk's site is a full set of estate forms. Ask the office which forms it expects and how they should arrive.

Local practice also matters. The judicial circuit's page lists Rules of Practice and a county court schedule. An Illinois estate planning attorney will read those before filing, because they can affect when a hearing is set.

## Can I attend a Vermilion County probate hearing by phone or video?

Often you can ask. The county's remote hearing page says you can request to appear remotely by phone or video and should call the clerk's office for information. It says the court will notify you if your hearing is remote, and you must contact the clerk ahead of time if you cannot attend a scheduled remote hearing.

The same page covers practical points.

| Question | What the county page says |
|---|---|
| No computer? | You can ask to appear by phone |
| No phone either? | Ask the clerk what other options exist |
| Cost of the service? | You can ask the court to use a free service or ask for a fee waiver |
| Is it informal? | No, it is an official hearing and may be recorded or viewable by the public |
| Messages to the judge? | If you message the judge directly, include the other party |

The page tells smartphone users to join Zoom with the meeting ID and password the court gives you, and tells computer users to go to zoom.us/join. It does not mention probate by name, so ask whether your estate hearing may be remote.

## Where can I get self-help in Vermilion County?

The Illinois Legal Aid Online page for Vermilion County lists a Legal Self-Help Center at two public libraries.

- Danville Public Library, 319 North Vermilion Street, Danville, phone (217) 477-5220.
- Hoopeston Public Library, 110 North 4th Street, Hoopeston, phone (217) 283-6711.

Both locations list computers, internet, online court forms and printers. The center gives legal information and referrals but states that it does not provide legal advice. That distinction matters in probate, where a wrong form can slow an estate. For how far a do-it-yourself route can go, see [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer).

## How do I look up a Vermilion County case?

The clerk's menu includes a Case Search link. Its public inquiry page lists Clericus Magnus as an online search and notes that it takes a while to load, along with a link to Illinois Legal Aid Online. We could not confirm what the online search shows for an estate file.

For a certain answer, call the clerk with the decedent's full name and approximate date of death and ask for the case number. The Legal Forms menu also lists a Record Search Request, and the clerk's home page mentions paying record search fees online, so ask what copies cost.

## What if the family cannot afford court costs?

This is the situation that stops many families, so start with the cheapest door. The estate may not need court at all. The page on the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) explains that, as of 2026, personal property up to $150,000, not counting registered vehicles, can be collected with a sworn affidavit given to the bank (755 ILCS 5/25-1). No petition is filed. Real estate cannot be transferred this way. The [small estate affidavit guide](/free/illinois-small-estate-affidavit-guide) walks through it.

The signer takes on the debts, so read [who signs a small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/who-signs-an-illinois-small-estate-affidavit) and [using one at a bank](/learn/illinois/using-an-illinois-small-estate-affidavit-at-a-bank) first.

If a house or a bank that refuses the affidavit forces a court case, the second door is a fee waiver. Illinois lets a court waive fees for a person who cannot afford them (735 ILCS 5/5-105), and the clerk's site lists a Waiver of Court Fees page with an application, instructions and an order. The page on [Illinois probate fee waivers](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver) explains that the application looks at the applicant's finances, and that a judge decides whether it fits an estate. A waiver does not necessarily cover newspaper publication or a bond premium.

### Example: Dolores and her father's Danville duplex

This is an invented example. Dolores, 61, lives in Hoopeston. Her father Ray, who lived in Danville, died with a $4,500 checking account, a $7,000 car and a duplex in his name alone. Dolores's income is a small Social Security check.

The affidavit cannot move the duplex, so she cannot avoid court. She files the original will with the Circuit Clerk, then asks about the waiver forms on the clerk's site. She tells the clerk the estate holds little cash.

Her cousin advances the filing cost as a loan to the estate, and Dolores keeps the receipt so the executor can repay it later. She asks about remote options for the hearing and about costs, and she leans on the library's self-help center for printing. Because she is not sure about the creditor notice, she books a consultation before signing anything.

## What costs should I ask the clerk about?

Fees are set county by county, and we do not list dollar figures here because they change. Ask the clerk for the current schedule. The page on [what probate costs in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-costs) lists the categories.

- What does it cost to open an estate and file an appearance?
- Is there a charge to deposit a will?
- What do certified copies of letters cost?
- Can fees be paid by card or only by check?
- How does the office treat a waiver request in an estate?

Creditors matter too. After a case opens, the executor must notice creditors, as described in [how creditor claims work in an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/how-creditor-claims-work-in-an-illinois-estate). That is true whether or not fees were waived.
