Your brother or parent lived in Illinois, you live in Arizona, and the will names you executor. You can accept, and you can do much of the work from home. Illinois law adds a few details for someone who does not live in the state, and they are worth knowing before you file anything.

This page covers those Illinois details. For a state-neutral guide to the logistics of distance, read [how to be an executor when you live out of state](/learn/after-a-death/how-to-be-an-executor-when-you-live-out-of-state). For whether to say yes at all, see our page on refusing to be executor. The Illinois overview is at our [Illinois guide](/learn/illinois).

## Does Illinois let a non-resident serve?

Yes. The qualification section of the Probate Act asks that an executor be an adult, a resident of the United States, not of unsound mind, and not convicted of a felony (755 ILCS 5/6-13). There is a narrow exception for a felony when the will names the person and the court allows it. It does not require Illinois residency.

## What is different about the bond?

The court may require a non-resident executor to give a bond, in an amount and with a surety it sets, even if the will excuses bond (755 ILCS 5/6-13). Think of it as an insurance policy against loss, paid for from the estate. The premium depends on the amount of personal property, and the surety may ask about your credit. Ask the clerk at the start whether a bond is likely, so the application can run while the hearing is being set.

## What can you do from where you live?

| Task | Remote? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| File the will and petition | Yes, through a local attorney or e-filing | The original will must reach the clerk |
| Attend the hearing | Often | Remote appearance or your lawyer appears, depending on the court |
| Sign sworn documents | Yes | Notarize at home; confirm the clerk accepts it |
| Get letters of office | Yes | Certified copies can be mailed |
| Get an EIN and open the estate account | Yes | Online EIN; many banks open accounts remotely, some want a visit |
| Mail notice to heirs and creditors | Yes | Keep proof of mailing |
| Publish the creditor notice | Yes | Through the attorney or newspaper |
| Secure and sell the house | Needs local help | Hire a contact, agent or property manager |
| Pick up mail | Needs local help | Forwarding by the post office |
| Final income tax returns | Yes | See [IL-1041](/learn/illinois/illinois-form-il-1041-estate-and-trust-income-tax-return) |

## What are the steps for a non-resident executor?

1. Call a local Illinois attorney before you leave your home state. Hire someone in the county where the case will be filed.
2. Ask the clerk whether the court will require a bond and whether a remote hearing is allowed.
3. Order certified death certificates by mail or online.
4. Arrange the first trip. Plan to look at the house, meet the attorney and visit the bank in one visit if the bank insists.
5. File the petition and attend the hearing, in person or remotely.
6. Take the oath, handle the bond and collect several certified letters.
7. Set up mail forwarding, insurance on the house and a local helper.
8. Keep a log of travel costs and time so you can ask for reimbursement.

## What about the house and the mail?

The house is where distance hurts. Someone has to change the locks, turn off the water in winter, check the roof and take in the mail. A vacant house may lose insurance coverage after a certain period, so call the insurer. Hire a reliable local contact and agree on a fee in writing.

## Can you be paid or reimbursed?

An executor may be reimbursed for reasonable costs and may be paid reasonable compensation. Travel can count. Our page on how much an executor gets paid in Illinois explains the standard. Keep receipts. Do not pay yourself from the estate account without a record.

## What if you live in another state and the person owned property there too?

Then the estate may involve two states. Illinois letters do not give you power over land elsewhere. See [Illinois residents who own property in another state](/learn/illinois/illinois-resident-owning-property-in-another-state) and [ancillary probate](/learn/probate/ancillary-probate).

## Example: Priyanka in Seattle

Priyanka, 49, is named executor for her father in Rockford. The estate has a $230,000 house, $52,000 in cash and a 2018 sedan. She hires a Winnebago County attorney, learns the court will set a bond of about the value of the personal property, and has her bond approved in nine days. She appears by video at the hearing, signs papers before a Seattle notary and hires a local agent and cleaner for the house. She travels once, for two days, to meet the agent and sort family papers. The estate pays her $1,100 in travel costs, backed by receipts. See the [Winnebago County page](/estate-planning/illinois/counties/winnebago) for the clerk's details.

## What should a distant executor decide first?

Decide who your local team is before you decide anything else. You need an Illinois attorney in the county where the case is filed, a person who can reach the house within an hour, and a bank branch that will open an estate account without a trip, or that you can visit once. Next decide how you will communicate with the heirs: a monthly email with a short status line is enough for most families and prevents the suspicion that distance can create.

Then set a travel budget. Most distant executors make one planning trip at the start and one near the sale of the house. Everything else can be done by phone, mail and electronic filing. Keep a simple log of dates, miles and costs from the first day.

## What records should a distant executor keep?

Keep a single shared folder with scans of the letters, the petition, the order, every notice and proof of mailing, bank statements and receipts. A distant executor cannot walk into the clerk's office to check a file, so the folder is the working memory of the estate. Add a running list of questions for your attorney and a dated log of every call. When the closing report is due, you will build it from this folder, and heirs who live nearby will see that the work was done openly.
