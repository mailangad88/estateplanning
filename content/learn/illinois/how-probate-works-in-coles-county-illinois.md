Maybe your mother lived in Mattoon and kept her papers in a kitchen drawer. Maybe your uncle died in Charleston and the family is not sure where a will goes. Maybe you were just told you are the executor and nobody has said how long you have to act. This page covers Coles County, Illinois, and it sits inside the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

It explains where the courthouse is, what to do with the original will, how estates open, how remote hearings are handled in the 5th Judicial Circuit, where to get self-help and how to check for a case. Then it adds an angle other county pages skip: the clocks an executor faces in roughly the first month and the weeks right after. If you want someone to look at your own papers, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-coles-county-illinois&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the probate courthouse in Coles County, Illinois?

It is the Coles County Courthouse at 651 Jackson Avenue, Charleston, Illinois 61920. The Illinois Courts directory lists the phone as (217) 348-0538, hours of 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and the 5th Circuit as the circuit. The county government site gives the same street address and shows a separate Circuit Clerk department.

Estates are opened in the county where the person lived. That means a family in Mattoon, Oakland or Ashmore uses the Charleston courthouse, not a Mattoon office. If the person also owned land in a neighboring county, read [property in two Illinois counties](/learn/illinois/probate-venue-property-in-two-illinois-counties).

We could not open the Circuit Clerk's own website, so we did not confirm room numbers, a will drop-off process or parking. Call before you drive, and ask which door and which counter to use. Hours often shift around holidays.

## What do I do with the original will in Coles County?

File the original with the circuit clerk right away. Anyone who holds a dead person's will must file it with the clerk after the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1), and a photocopy cannot take the original's place. The page on [filing the original will with the circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) explains the duty in full.

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Locate | Find the signed original, not a scan |
| Copy | Make a full-color copy for your own file |
| Call | Phone the courthouse and ask how the clerk wants the will delivered |
| Deliver | Hand it over or send it as the clerk directs, and ask for a receipt |
| Notify | Tell the named executor in writing that the will is on file |

If you cannot find the original, do not wait for it to turn up. See [what to do about a lost original will](/learn/illinois/lost-original-will-in-illinois) and [finding a will after a death](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois).

## How does an estate open once the will is filed?

Filing keeps the will safe. It does not open the case. The named executor, or an heir when there is no will, petitions the court for letters of office, which prove authority to act for the estate. The page on [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) lists the papers, and [Illinois probate, step by step](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) shows the stages that follow.

Because we could not read the Coles County clerk's forms page, ask the clerk which petition forms the court expects and whether they come on paper or electronically. The 5th Circuit also has local rules, and an Illinois estate planning attorney will check them before filing, since they can affect how a hearing is set.

## Can I attend a Coles County probate hearing by phone or video?

Possibly, and the 5th Circuit's local rule explains how. The Illinois Courts remote hearings page points Coles County to a local rule PDF and lists no separate county page for remote proceedings. The rule covers Clark, Coles, Cumberland, Edgar and Vermilion counties.

| Rule section | What it says, in plain terms |
|---|---|
| III(E)(1) | Sets which appearances may be remote and which must be in person |
| III(E)(3) | Some case types need advance court approval to appear remotely |
| III(E)(4) | Notices for remote-eligible dates must carry Zoom and dial-in details |
| III(E)(5) | Remote appearances use Zoom, and each county has a scheduling contact open weekdays, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. |
| III(E)(6) | A request generally needs a written motion, unless the county or court allows otherwise |

The rule does not single out probate, so ask the clerk's scheduling desk whether your estate hearing may be remote. Illinois Legal Aid Online adds practical advice: send evidence ahead of time, use a quiet room and tell the clerk early if you need an interpreter.

## Where can I get self-help in Coles County?

Illinois Legal Aid Online lists a Legal Self-Help Center at the Charleston Carnegie Public Library, 712 6th Street, Charleston, phone (217) 345-4913. It provides computers, internet, printers, scanners and online court forms, plus legal information and referrals to free and low-cost services.

The center states that it does not provide legal advice. That line matters in probate, where one wrong form can slow an estate. For how far a do-it-yourself route can reasonably go, read [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer).

## How do I check whether a case exists in Coles County?

Call the clerk. Give the decedent's full name and an approximate date of death and ask whether a will was deposited or an estate opened, and for the case number. We could not confirm an online case search for Coles County estates, so we do not describe one.

Costs for copies and filings vary by county, and we do not list dollar figures here. ## What clocks does an executor face in Coles County?

Several, and they start at different moments. Here they are in order, using only deadlines that appear in the Illinois pages linked in each row.

| When it starts | Deadline | Source page |
|---|---|---|
| Right after the death | Holder files the original will with the clerk | [Filing the will](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) |
| You learn you are named | 30 days to start a case or decline in writing | [Filing the will](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) |
| Order admitting the will | 14 days to mail notice to heirs and legatees | [Notice to heirs](/learn/illinois/notice-to-heirs-and-legatees-in-illinois-probate) |
| Order admitting the will | 42 days for an heir to ask for formal proof | [Notice to heirs](/learn/illinois/notice-to-heirs-and-legatees-in-illinois-probate) |
| Order admitting the will | Six months to file a will contest | [Six-month deadline](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-contest-six-month-deadline) |
| Letters issued | About 60 days for the inventory | [Probate inventory](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-inventory) |

Letters of office come before most of this work. After they arrive, the executor gets an estate tax ID number from the IRS, opens an [estate bank account](/learn/illinois/opening-an-estate-bank-account-in-illinois) and starts creditor notice, which means publishing a notice once a week for three weeks and mailing known creditors ([how creditor claims work](/learn/illinois/how-creditor-claims-work-in-an-illinois-estate)). The [executor duties overview](/learn/after-a-death/executor-duties) covers the rest, and the [executor's first 30 days guide](/free/executor-first-30-days-guide) gives a day-by-day list. If you would rather not serve, see [refusing to be executor in Illinois](/learn/illinois/can-you-refuse-to-be-executor-in-illinois).

### Example: Wendell, an executor in Mattoon

This is an invented example. Wendell, 52, lives in Mattoon. His aunt Rosalie died in Charleston, and her will, kept in his desk, names him executor. She left a house in her name alone and a checking account.

On day two Wendell calls the courthouse, then files the original will and keeps the receipt. He tells his cousin in writing that the will is on file. He knows his own 30 days started the day he learned of the nomination, so on day nine he books a consultation and agrees to serve.

After the court admits the will and issues letters, Wendell puts three dates on a calendar: 14 days for the heir notice, 42 days for the formal proof window and about 60 days for the inventory. He opens the estate account before paying any bill. Because the house cannot pass by affidavit, he does not try the small estate shortcut, but he checks it with the [small estate checker](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) first.

## What if the estate might be small enough to skip court?

Check before you file. As of 2026, personal property up to $150,000, not counting registered vehicles, can be collected with a sworn affidavit given to the bank (755 ILCS 5/25-1), and real estate cannot. The [Illinois small estate affidavit page](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) explains who signs and what they take on. If the estate holds a house in the decedent's name alone, plan on a court case, and see [how to pay for probate when the estate has no cash](/learn/after-a-death/how-to-pay-for-probate-when-the-estate-has-no-cash).
