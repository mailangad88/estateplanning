The news says the estate tax only touches the very wealthy, and at the federal level that is mostly right. Illinois families still get tax bills. A person who dies in Illinois with a $5 million estate owes the state about $285,714 and owes the IRS nothing. This page shows why the two taxes give such different answers.

We compare thresholds, portability, rates, forms and deadlines, then run the same estate through both systems. For the Illinois rules alone, read [the Illinois estate tax](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax). For the federal rules alone, read the [federal estate tax exemption](/learn/estate-tax/federal-estate-tax-exemption). The wider Illinois picture is at [our Illinois guide](/learn/illinois).

## How do Illinois and federal estate tax compare?

They are two taxes on the same death, with different rules and different offices. This table sets them side by side.

| Feature | Federal estate tax | Illinois estate tax |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusion in 2026 | $15,000,000 per person | $4,000,000 per person |
| Indexed for inflation | Yes, after 2026 | No |
| Portability to a surviving spouse | Yes, by election on Form 706 | No |
| Tax type | Graduated, top rate 40% | Based on the old federal state death tax credit, with a cap tied to the $4 million |
| Return | Form 706 | Form 700 |
| Filed with | IRS | Illinois Attorney General |
| Tax paid to | U.S. Treasury | Illinois State Treasurer |
| Due | Nine months after death | Nine months after death |
| Marital deduction | Yes, unlimited for a citizen spouse | Yes, with a separate Illinois QTIP election |
| Gift tax | Yes, tied to the same exclusion | None, but taxable gifts count in the test |
| Inheritance tax | None | None |

The Illinois figures come from the Attorney General and 35 ILCS 405. The federal figures come from the IRS. Illinois has no inheritance tax on heirs, only an estate tax, as our article on [estate tax versus inheritance tax](/learn/estate-tax/estate-tax-vs-inheritance-tax) explains.

## Why can a $5 million estate owe Illinois and not the IRS?

Because the thresholds are not linked. Illinois says $4 million. The federal figure rose to $15 million for 2026. Between the two, an Illinois estate owes the state and the IRS gets nothing.

Here is a worked comparison.

| Estate | Illinois tax (about) | Federal tax | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| $3,900,000 | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| $5,000,000 | $285,714 | $0 | $285,714 |
| $10,000,000 | about $926,900 | $0 | about $926,900 |
| $16,000,000 | about $1,747,000 | about $0 (see below) | about $1,747,000 |

The Illinois amounts are estimates from the published formula. The $5 million one is the Attorney General's own example. For $16 million the federal side works out to roughly zero as well, because the federal estate deducts the Illinois tax. $16,000,000 minus about $1,747,000 is about $14.25 million, which is under the $15 million exclusion. A larger estate, such as $20 million, would owe federal tax on the excess after that deduction.

## How does portability change a couple's plan?

On the federal side, a surviving spouse can add the late spouse's unused exclusion by electing portability on a timely Form 706. A couple can then shelter up to $30 million without any trust. Read [portability for married couples](/learn/estate-tax/portability-for-married-couples) for the details.

Illinois does not follow. The Attorney General states that the carry-over of a spouse's unused exemption does not apply to the Illinois tax. So a couple with $7 million who leaves everything outright to the survivor has no federal problem and a state problem of about $565,600. A trust that uses the first spouse's $4 million fixes the state side. We cover that in [credit shelter trusts and the Illinois QTIP election](/learn/illinois/illinois-qtip-election-and-credit-shelter-trusts).

## What steps does an executor follow for both taxes?

1. Value all assets at date of death, including retirement accounts, life insurance and trusts.
2. Add taxable lifetime gifts above the annual exclusion, which is $19,000 per recipient in 2026.
3. Test the total against $4 million for Illinois and $15 million for the IRS.
4. If over $4 million, prepare Form 700 for the Attorney General. If over $15 million, or electing portability, prepare Form 706.
5. Pay each tax to the right office by the nine-month date.
6. Keep both files, and note that a federal return filed for portability does not change the Illinois result.

Our page on [Illinois Form 700](/learn/illinois/illinois-form-700-estate-tax-return) goes through the state steps, and [Form 706](/learn/estate-tax/form-706-estate-tax-return) covers the federal side.

## What about gifts and basis?

Federal law taxes large lifetime gifts through the gift tax system and counts them against the same $15 million. Illinois has no gift tax, but adds adjusted taxable gifts back when testing the estate. The details are in [lifetime gifts and the Illinois estate tax](/learn/illinois/lifetime-gifts-and-the-illinois-estate-tax). Basis follows the federal rules for both, which means assets at death usually get a [step-up in basis](/learn/estate-tax/step-up-in-basis).

## Which one should your plan worry about?

For most Illinois families with estates under $15 million, the answer is Illinois. That changes the plan. Trusts built around a $15 million number can overfund a shelter trust and create state tax at the first death. A will that fits the federal rules may still be too simple for Illinois. Using the [estate tax estimator](/tools/estate-tax-estimator) helps you see where you stand.

Families above $15 million need to plan for both taxes, and the deduction for state tax paid makes the two interact. That calls for a calculation run on real numbers.

## What if the federal exclusion changes again?

The federal exclusion has moved many times. It was $5.49 million in 2017, doubled for 2018 through 2025, and was set at $15 million for 2026 by the 2025 tax law, with inflation indexing afterward. A future Congress could change it. Illinois does not move with it, because the Illinois number is written into state law.

That is a good reason to build a plan that does not depend on one number. A plan keyed to Illinois at $4 million works whether the federal figure goes up or down, since Illinois is the lower of the two. A plan keyed only to the federal figure can leave the Illinois tax unaddressed. Review documents after any change in either law, and after major life events such as a sale of a business or an inheritance. The [plan readiness assessment](/tools/plan-readiness-assessment) is a quick way to see what is out of date.
