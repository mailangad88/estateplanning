A daughter is in treatment, in jail or struggling, and her son has been living with you for months. The school wants a signature, the pediatrician asks who can consent, and you are not sure you are allowed to say yes. You are not alone, and Illinois offers several legal tools, each with a different level of permanence.

This page is for grandparents who are already raising or about to raise a grandchild. If you want to be named in a parent's will, read [can grandparents be named guardian](/learn/guardianship/can-grandparents-be-named-guardian). For the adult side of guardianship, see the [Illinois guardianship overview](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship).

## What authority do you have without any paperwork?

You have almost none. A grandparent who is caring for a child has physical care but not legal authority. Providers and agencies often ask for a parent's consent or a court order, and the answer can differ between a school, a hospital and a benefits office. If the parents are reachable and willing, a signed document is the fastest fix.

## What can a parent sign without going to court?

A parent can designate a short-term guardian in writing for a limited period (755 ILCS 5/11-5.4). Our article on [standby and short-term guardians for children](/learn/illinois/illinois-standby-and-short-term-guardians-for-children) explains the signing rules and the length of time. It works when a parent is in rehab, in the hospital or deployed. A short-term designation does not remove the parent's rights, and the parent can end it.

It also expires. For a stable long-term home, most grandparents eventually consider a court order.

## How does a court guardianship of a minor work?

Under Article XI of the Probate Act, the circuit court can appoint a guardian of a minor when it serves the child's interests. The guardian must be at least 18, live in the United States and meet the statute's other qualifications (755 ILCS 5/11-3). The petition names the child, the parents and the grandparent, and the court gives notice to the parents.

A key limit: if a parent is willing and able to make and carry out day-to-day child care decisions, the court generally lacks authority to appoint a guardian unless the parent consents or does not object (755 ILCS 5/11-5). That makes the parent's attitude the central fact. Our article on [petitioning for guardianship of a minor child](/learn/guardianship/how-to-petition-for-guardianship-of-a-minor-child) walks through the filing.

## What if the parents disagree or cannot be found?

If a parent objects, the grandparent must show that the parent is not willing or able to do day-to-day care, and the case becomes contested. If a parent cannot be found, the petition must show that the court tried to give notice in the way the rules require. Cases involving abuse or neglect may go through the juvenile court and DCFS, a separate system with its own rules. A custody case under the Marriage and Dissolution Act is another route for some nonparents, with standing rules. An attorney can check which applies.

## What are the options, step by step?

1. Talk with the parent and ask whether they will sign a short-term designation.
2. Ask the school and doctor what proof they accept right now.
3. If the arrangement will last, ask the parent whether they will consent to a court guardianship.
4. Get the clerk's forms for a petition to appoint a guardian of a minor.
5. Complete the petition and give notice to both parents and others the rules list.
6. Attend the hearing, and bring proof of the child's home, school and care.
7. Once appointed, keep records and report to the court if the order requires it.

## How do the main options compare?

| | Short-term designation | Guardianship of a minor | Custody case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Court needed | No | Yes (Probate) | Yes (Family) |
| Parent consent | Signed by parent | Needed if parent is willing and able | Not always, but standing rules apply |
| Length | Limited period | Until 18 or ended by court | Until changed by court |
| Ends if parent revokes | Yes | No, parent must petition | No, parent must petition |
| Fits | Temporary crisis | Stable home | Disputed parenting |

## What about the child's money and benefits?

A grandparent guardian does not own the child's money. If the child has an inheritance or insurance payment, the court may supervise it, which may require a guardian of the estate and possibly a bond. Our article on [when a minor inherits money in Illinois](/learn/illinois/when-a-minor-inherits-money-in-illinois) shows the options. Child-only benefits or health coverage may be available through state agencies, so ask your local office.

## Example: Ruth and her grandson Eli

Ruth, 66, has cared for Eli, 8, for seven months because her daughter Tasha is in a residential treatment program. The school asks for proof, and Eli needs dental surgery. Tasha signs a short-term guardian designation for Ruth. Four months later, Tasha agrees to a court guardianship because she expects another year in treatment. Ruth files, Tasha consents in writing and the judge appoints Ruth. Tasha keeps the right to ask the court to change the order later.

Families planning ahead can name a guardian in a will. See [naming a guardian for your children in Illinois](/learn/illinois/naming-a-guardian-for-your-children). The [Cook County](/estate-planning/illinois/counties/cook) page has local starting points, and the [plan finder](/plan-finder) can help sort the paperwork.

## What should you keep in a file?

Whatever route you take, keep a folder. Include the child's birth certificate and Social Security card, immunization and medical records, school records, any parent-signed papers, copies of court filings and orders, and a log of expenses. A folder answers most questions from schools, doctors and benefit offices, and it helps if a parent later asks the court to change the arrangement. If the child has an inheritance or a settlement, see [UTMA accounts and gifts to minors in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-utma-accounts-and-gifts-to-minors).

## What should grandparents plan for themselves?

Raising a child changes your own estate plan. Update your will to name a guardian for the grandchild if you pass away, and consider a standby arrangement. Review your power of attorney and health care directive, and make sure your own beneficiary forms still reflect what you want. Our guide to [paying for a family caregiver](/learn/illinois/paying-a-family-caregiver) may matter if your income is tight. You can also read the national piece on [guardianship versus custody versus adoption](/learn/guardianship/guardianship-vs-custody-vs-adoption).
