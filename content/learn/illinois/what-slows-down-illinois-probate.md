An estate that should take a year runs to two. The executor is not careless. A form was returned, an address was missing, a buyer backed out. Most of the delay in Illinois probate comes from a small set of causes that repeat from case to case.

This page lists seven of them and shows what to do for each. For the base timeline, read [how long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois). If the problem is a person rather than a form, see [what heirs can do about a slow executor](/learn/illinois/what-heirs-can-do-about-a-slow-illinois-executor). The wider guide is at [Illinois estate planning](/learn/illinois).

## Which delays are fixed by law and which are not?

Some waiting cannot be skipped. Creditors have a claim period of at least six months from the first publication, or three months from mailing if later (755 ILCS 5/18-3), and claims are barred two years after death (755 ILCS 5/18-12). A will contest can be filed up to six months after admission (755 ILCS 5/8-1). The independent estate closing report has a 42-day wait (755 ILCS 5/28-11).

Everything else is within reach. That is where the seven causes live.

| Cause | Typical lost time | Who controls it | Prevention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Will not filed promptly | Weeks | Holder of the will | File at once |
| Petition errors or missing heirs | 1 to 4 weeks | Executor and attorney | Complete list before filing |
| Unknown or distant heirs | 4 to 8 weeks | Executor | Start the search on day one |
| Bond or letters not arranged | 1 to 3 weeks | Executor | Ask about bond at filing |
| House not sold or vacant | Months | Executor and heirs | Prepare and list early |
| Family dispute | Months to years | Everyone | Communicate and give notice |
| Late-found assets or taxes | Months | Executor | Search early, check tax needs |

## 1. Is the will filed promptly?

A will that sits in a drawer holds up everything, because the court cannot act without it. The statute says the holder must file it with the circuit clerk immediately (755 ILCS 5/6-1). Filing the original will is covered in its own article. If the will cannot be found, start with [finding a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois).

## 2. Is the petition complete?

A petition with an heir missing, a nickname in place of a legal name or a blank for the property value comes back. Each return costs a week or more. Use the [petition checklist](/learn/illinois/illinois-petition-for-letters-of-office) and check names against the death certificate.

## 3. Are all heirs located?

Notice goes to every heir and legatee within 14 days after the order (755 ILCS 5/6-10). An address that is unknown means publication and more time. Start the search for estranged relatives at the beginning, not after the hearing. See [notice to heirs and legatees](/learn/illinois/notice-to-heirs-and-legatees-in-illinois-probate).

## 4. Is the bond arranged?

If the court requires a bond, letters wait until it is approved. Underwriting can take days or weeks for a person with poor credit. Ask the clerk at filing whether the will's bond waiver will be honored, and start the bond application while the hearing is being set. See [letters and the surety bond](/learn/illinois/illinois-letters-of-administration-and-the-surety-bond).

## 5. Is the house the holdup?

Real estate is the single biggest reason an estate stays open. A vacant house needs insurance, a lawn crew and a listing. Heirs may disagree about selling. Buyers' lenders want certified letters. Our guide to [selling a house in probate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/selling-a-house-in-probate-in-illinois) shows the steps.

## 6. Is there a family dispute?

An heir can ask for formal proof of the will, contest it, or ask the court to end independent administration. Each is a legal right, and each slows the case. Fair, early communication helps. A brief call to explain each step, with written updates, reduces suspicion. See [independent versus supervised administration](/learn/illinois/independent-vs-supervised-administration-in-illinois).

## 7. Are there late-found assets or tax issues?

A forgotten account or an estate that may owe Illinois estate tax turns a clean file into a long one. An Illinois estate tax return is generally due nine months after death for estates over the state's exclusion amount (35 ILCS 405). Check the Illinois estate tax page early. Search unclaimed property at the start, as described in [finding unclaimed property for an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/finding-unclaimed-property-for-an-illinois-estate).

## What is a step-by-step plan to avoid delay?

1. File the will the week of the death.
2. Build the heir and legatee list before the petition.
3. Ask about the bond at filing.
4. Mail notices within 14 days and calendar every later deadline.
5. Open the estate account and send the creditor notice at once.
6. Start preparing the house for sale in the first month.
7. Search for assets and unclaimed property early.
8. Update heirs every month or two in writing.

## Example: Tomas and the 19-month estate

Tomas, 62, is executor for his mother. He files the will in three weeks and the petition in six. The clerk returns it because one grandchild's address is missing. The grandchild turns up in Ohio after a month of searching. The bond takes three weeks. The house, a 1960s ranch, sits vacant for four months because he waits for the claim period to end before listing it. Closing comes at 19 months. A complete petition and a house listed in month two could have saved about six months.

## How do you know which delay you have?

Ask three questions in order. Is anything waiting on the court, such as a hearing date, a bond approval or a ruling? If so, call the clerk and ask what is outstanding. Is anything waiting on a person, such as an heir's signature, an appraiser or a buyer? Then the executor can push that person with a date. Is anything waiting on the calendar, such as the claim period? Then the right move is to use the time to prepare the next step, for example to list the house, gather tax records or draft the closing report. Writing the answers down once a month turns a vague worry into a task list.

## Which delays are worth paying a professional to prevent?

Three are usually worth it: the petition, the bond and the house. A lawyer who files a complete petition the first time saves weeks. An insurance agent who knows sureties can place a bond faster. A real estate agent who has handled estate sales knows what buyers' lenders will ask for. The other delays are mostly about discipline, such as mailing notices on time and keeping heirs informed, and the executor can handle those with a calendar and a checklist.
