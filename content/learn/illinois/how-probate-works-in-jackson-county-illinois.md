When a parent or spouse dies in Murphysboro, Carbondale, Du Quoin or Elkville, the estate belongs to the Jackson County court, which sits in the 1st Judicial Circuit in far southern Illinois. This page belongs to the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). Other states have a Jackson County too, so check that a form or number is for Illinois.

It covers the courthouse and the Circuit Clerk, how an estate opens, filing the will, remote hearings, self-help and case lookup. It then follows a family where someone died with no will and a child had died first. If you want to talk it through with an Illinois estate planning attorney, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-jackson-county-illinois&topic=probate).

This is general education from official pages read in November 2026, not legal advice. Phone numbers and hours change, so confirm them with the clerk before you travel.

## Where is the Jackson County Courthouse and the Circuit Clerk?

The courthouse is at 1001 Walnut Street, Murphysboro, IL 62966. The Illinois Courts directory lists it as Jackson County in the 1st Circuit, 5th District, with hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. and the phone number (618) 687-7300.

The Circuit Clerk's site says the clerk's office is on the main floor, the public entrance is on Walnut Street, and the office does not close for lunch. Department lines run from (618) 687-7301 through 687-7306 if the main line is busy. The site names probate as a department you can ask for, but it does not describe probate steps.

| Item | What the official page says | Why a probate family cares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Address | 1001 Walnut Street, Murphysboro | Where the will and petitions go |
| Hours | 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., Monday to Friday | Plan around the 4:00 close |

| Mailing address | P.O. Drawer 730 | Useful for mail, but ask before mailing a will |

The 1st Circuit covers nine counties: Alexander, Jackson, Johnson, Massac, Pope, Pulaski, Saline, Union and Williamson. A rule you read for Williamson or Saline County may not apply in Murphysboro, so look for Jackson County by name.

## How does an estate open in Jackson County?

An estate opens when someone files a petition with the Circuit Clerk in the county where the person lived. If there is a will, the named executor asks the court to admit it and issue letters of office. If there is no will, a relative asks to be appointed administrator. The steps are laid out in [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois), and the wider timeline is in [Illinois probate, step by step](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process).

The clerk's site links to two e-filing portals but gives no instructions for estates. Ask the clerk whether the petition should go in electronically or on paper, and ask how the original will should be handled either way. Never move money or sell property until the court has issued letters, because the letters are what banks and title companies ask to see.

## What do you do with the will in Jackson County?

Take the original to the clerk. Under 755 ILCS 5/6-1, anyone holding a deceased person's will must file it with the clerk right after the death, and a photocopy cannot be admitted in its place. The page on [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) explains the duty and the two 30-day clocks in section 6-3.

A short order of steps:

1. Keep the original flat and unstapled, and do not write on it.
2. Scan every page for your own records.
3. Call (618) 687-7300 and ask how the clerk wants the will delivered.
4. Get a receipt that shows the date and any case or will number.
5. Tell the named executor and the family where the original now is.

If you cannot find a will, start with [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois). If only a copy turns up, see [what to do with a lost will](/learn/illinois/lost-will).

## Can you attend a Jackson County hearing by video?

Possibly, but the official pages read for this guide do not give a procedure. The Circuit Clerk's site has a Video Court menu item. The 1st Circuit website lists two Jackson County orders, one on remote and limited in-person hearings from November 2020 and one authorizing remote proceedings, but the page shows only their titles.

The Illinois Courts site says you may be able to attend court by telephone or video and tells you to contact your local circuit clerk. It adds that a remote appearance is an official court proceeding, so dress and behave as you would in the courtroom. The judge decides how each hearing runs.

If a sibling lives in another state, ask the clerk or the attorney on the estate early, and not the week of the hearing.

## What self-help and case lookup does Jackson County offer?

Illinois Legal Aid Online lists a Legal Self-Help Center at the Circuit Clerk's office. It says the center offers legal information, help with some forms, computers, printers, scanners and referrals to free and low-cost legal services. It states that the center does not provide legal advice, and it does not mention probate. Printing is listed at 50 cents a page.

The clerk's site also links the Illinois Legal Aid form library, the statewide approved court forms and the clerk's own documents. For what a do-it-yourself executor takes on, read [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer). For a small estate with no house, see the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit).

For case lookup, the clerk's site has a Case Info link to the Judici search for this court. A blank result does not prove nothing was filed, since some records are restricted and new filings can lag. Call the clerk for a firm answer. The pages on [how to look up a probate case](/learn/probate/how-to-look-up-a-probate-case) and [whether probate is public](/learn/probate/is-probate-public) explain what you can expect to see.

## What if someone in Jackson County dies with no will?

Then the Probate Act decides, not the family. The rules in 755 ILCS 5/2-1 split the estate among relatives in a fixed order, as set out in [who inherits without a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-inherits-without-a-will-in-illinois). With a spouse and descendants, the spouse takes one-half and the descendants share one-half. With descendants and no spouse, the descendants take everything, [per stirpes](/glossary/per-stirpes).

Per stirpes matters most when a child has died first. That child's own children step into the parent's place and share that one portion. Nobody has to be named, and nothing has to be signed. Only property in the dead person's name alone passes this way. Accounts with a named beneficiary and joint property follow their own rules.

Someone still has to be appointed. For the person who will run the estate, Section 9-3 gives preference to a spouse or the spouse's nominee, then children and grandchildren, and the person should expect to post a bond. The page on [letters of administration and the surety bond](/learn/illinois/illinois-letters-of-administration-and-the-surety-bond) has the order and the bond rules. The page on [who is next of kin in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-is-next-of-kin-in-illinois) shows why the person who inherits is not always the person who decides.

### Example: the Hollis family near Carbondale

This is an invented example. Ruth Hollis, 79, a widow, died in Carbondale with no will. She owned a house in her own name and an account with no beneficiary. She had three children: Dale, Marcy and Paul. Paul had died four years earlier and left a daughter, Tess, who is 22.

| Person | Relationship | Share of the probate estate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dale | Surviving child | One-third |
| Marcy | Surviving child | One-third |
| Tess | Daughter of Paul, who died first | One-third, taking her father's place |
| Ruth's brother | Sibling of Ruth | Nothing, because descendants exist |

Dale called the Circuit Clerk and asked what to bring. The family found no will after checking Ruth's desk and asking her bank. Dale, as a child, had preference to serve as administrator and asked the court to be appointed. Marcy and Tess received notice, and Tess was surprised to learn she had a share at all.

The house could not be sold until the court issued letters to Dale, and a title company would ask for them. The question of what to do with the house is covered in [what to do when the house is still in a parent's name](/learn/illinois/parent-died-house-in-their-name). If the three had disagreed about keeping it, [when siblings disagree over an inherited house](/learn/illinois/siblings-disagree-inherited-house) shows the options.

## What mistakes should a Jackson County family avoid?

- Using a form or phone number from another state's Jackson County.
- Assuming the Self-Help Center can say what a will means, when it gives information only.
- Waiting for every relative to agree before the original will reaches the clerk.
- Treating a blank online search as proof that no case exists.
- Leaving out a grandchild whose parent died first, when listing the heirs.
- Selling or splitting property before the court has issued letters.
