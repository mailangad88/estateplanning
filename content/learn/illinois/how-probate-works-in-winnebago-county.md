If your mother or husband lived in Rockford, Loves Park, Machesney Park or Rockton, the estate belongs in Winnebago County, Illinois. That is the 17th Judicial Circuit, and it is a different place from the Winnebago County in Wisconsin. Searches for "probate Rockford IL" turn up both, so check the state first.

This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). It covers the Rockford courthouse, how the original will is filed, remote hearings, self-help and case lookup. It also includes a short example of a small estate that may never need court. If you want to talk through your own family, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-winnebago-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the Winnebago County courthouse for probate?

The Winnebago County Courthouse is at 400 West State Street, Rockford, IL 61101. The Illinois Courts directory lists it as part of the 17th Circuit, with a phone of (815) 319-4806 and hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The directory notes free parking, with a map on the court's website.

The Circuit Clerk's office is in the same building. Its location page puts civil filings and payments in Room 108 and lists courtrooms on the first through fourth floors. It does not say which courtroom hears estates, so your hearing notice is the place to find that.

One practical warning. The 17th Circuit's courthouse page mentions a temporary entrance at 403 Elm Street, one block south of the usual entrance. Check that page before you go.

## What should the family sort out before going to the courthouse?

Start with the will and a list of what the person owned. Look in the home, a safe deposit box and the files of any attorney who drafted a will, as described in [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois).

Then sort the list. Accounts with a named beneficiary and jointly held property pass outside the court. A house in the deceased person's name alone usually points to probate. The [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter) helps you see which pile each item falls in.

## How is the original will filed in Rockford?

The Circuit Clerk's Wills page says you file the original Last Will and Testament, plus any codicils, of a deceased Winnebago County resident in Room 108. The clerk files it, assigns it a number and gives you a will receipt for your records.

You must provide the person's date of birth and date of death when you file. The page also says certified copies can be bought at the time of filing. You can reach the clerk at 815-319-4500, and the clerk's phone hours are 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

A few habits help:

1. Make a copy of the will before you leave home.
2. Carry the original flat, without staples.
3. Bring the dates of birth and death in writing.
4. Ask for a certified copy if the attorney will need one.
5. Keep the will receipt with the estate papers.

Filing the will does not open the estate. The law on the duty to file is covered in [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk).

## How does an estate case open and move forward?

The Circuit Clerk's probate page says estate cases receive a PR case number, and that since January 1, 2022, probate uses the PR category while guardianship uses GR. A case is started by e-filing through an approved provider listed at the state's e-file site or through Odyssey eFileIL. You register and set up payment first.

The clerk's page says you will get an email telling you whether a filing was accepted or rejected, and that fees are due upon filing. It also says you must attend court dates and follow the case, or it may be dismissed.

The clerk strongly recommends a licensed attorney to prepare the forms, and says clerk staff cannot prepare documents or give legal advice. The page on [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) explains where families tend to get stuck.

The clerk's location page lists e-filing kiosks on the first floor of the clerk's office, so someone without a computer at home has a place to start. The clerk's probate page states that forms are on its Court Forms page.

After the order admitting the will, the state rules set the pace. [How to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) and the [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) walk through notices, the claims period and closing. [How long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois) explains why even a simple case stays open for months.

## When does a small estate avoid the Winnebago County court?

Sometimes there is no case to file. Picture an estate that is just a car and a bank account. The Illinois small estate affidavit lets a person collect personal property without opening probate, if no letters of office are outstanding and no petition is pending.

The [small estate affidavit page](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) says the personal estate limit is currently $150,000, not counting registered motor vehicles, and that real estate cannot pass this way. It also warns that the person who signs must pay valid debts first and can be personally liable if they get it wrong.

For the car, see [how to transfer a car title after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/transfer-car-title-after-death-in-illinois). For the bank side, [using an Illinois small estate affidavit at a bank](/learn/illinois/using-an-illinois-small-estate-affidavit-at-a-bank) explains what banks ask for. If the estate sits just above the limit, read [an Illinois estate just over the small estate limit](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-just-over-the-small-estate-limit).

A house in the name of the person alone changes the answer. Real estate usually brings the estate back to the courthouse.

## Can I attend a Winnebago County hearing remotely?

Often, yes. The 17th Circuit's virtual court page says people can join through Zoom using a Meeting ID the court provides, or through the Virtual Courtroom Directory, which links directly into proceedings. If you cannot use a computer or smartphone, the page lists dial-in numbers and says to enter the Meeting ID followed by the pound key.

The page summarizes etiquette. You wait in a virtual waiting room until your case is called, stay muted unless speaking, and use your title and legal name as your screen name. Exhibits are shared in advance, and recording a hearing is prohibited. Local Rule 2.10 governs remote proceedings, and the page is only a summary.

Whether your own hearing is remote or in person is up to the court. Read the notice you receive, and ask the attorney handling the case. An out-of-state heir can often follow along this way, as the example below shows.

## What self-help does the Winnebago County court list?

The 17th Circuit's self-representation page lists the Winnebago County Self-Help Center, inside the law library at 400 West State Street, Room 304. The phone is 815-319-4526, and navigators are listed as available 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday.

The center offers court forms, general court information and computer and printing resources. Navigators cannot give legal advice. They can refer people to free or low-cost legal aid groups, and the court's page links Illinois Legal Aid Online. The Illinois Courts directory lists the law library at 815-319-4965.

For court costs, see [the Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver).

## How do I look up a Winnebago County case?

Use the Circuit Clerk's Access Court Records page. It points to Full Court Enterprise, a portal run by Justice Systems, at fce.wincoil.gov. The clerk says the portal offers case information pages, party detail pages and daily and weekly court calendars.

The clerk's Public User's Guide covers searching by case number or by party name, and by citation number. Estate case numbers use the PR category. If you are an heir and are not sure an estate was ever opened, see [how to find out if you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois). The clerk's phone line is the fallback.

## Example: Ruth and her father's car in Loves Park

This is an invented example. Ruth, 52, lives in Madison, Wisconsin. Her father, Walter, lived in Loves Park and died in March. He left a checking account of about $30,000, a registered car and no real estate. His only other heir is Ruth's brother Dan, in Rockford.

Ruth first searches "Winnebago County probate" and lands on Wisconsin results. She corrects the search to Winnebago County, Illinois. Walter's will names Ruth as executor, so she takes the original to Room 108 in Rockford, gives the dates of birth and death, and keeps the will receipt.

Her attorney explains that the estate is small and has no house. No one has filed a petition. They use a small estate affidavit for the account, and the car is retitled separately. No hearing is needed, so the Zoom option never comes up.

Months later, Dan finds an unpaid medical bill. Ruth pays valid debts before splitting what is left, as the affidavit requires. Had Walter owned a house, a PR case would have been opened, and Ruth could have joined hearings from Madison by Zoom.
