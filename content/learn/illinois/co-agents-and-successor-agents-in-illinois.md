Parents often say, "I want all three kids to be in charge." The Illinois forms do not work that way. This page explains what the Illinois Power of Attorney Act allows when you want more than one person involved, and which setup causes the fewest problems.

For the general idea in every state, see [naming more than one power of attorney agent](/learn/power-of-attorney/can-you-name-more-than-one-power-of-attorney-agent). This page covers only the Illinois rules. Our [Illinois guide](/learn/illinois) links the other forms.

## What is the difference between a co-agent and a successor?

A co-agent serves at the same time as another agent. A successor serves only after the first agent can no longer act. The Illinois statutory short form for property says in its notice that you may name successors but not co-agents, and the short form for health care says the same (755 ILCS 45/3-3 and 4-10). The Act also states the point directly: co-agents may be named in other forms, but not in the statutory short forms (755 ILCS 45/2-10.5).

That rule is built into the form. You do not need to ask for it, and writing two names on the agent line puts the document at risk of rejection.

## What happens when you name successors?

A principal may name one or more successors to serve if the first agent resigns, dies, becomes incapacitated, is not qualified or declines to serve (755 ILCS 45/2-10.3). Unless the document says otherwise, a successor has the same authority as the first agent. The form calls for successors to act "alone and successively, in the order named."

Two points matter in practice.

- **Trigger.** The successor takes over when the earlier agent can no longer serve. A bank usually wants proof, such as a death certificate or a signed resignation.
- **Duty to watch.** The statute says an agent is not liable for the acts of another agent, including a predecessor, unless the agent takes part in or conceals a breach (755 ILCS 45/2-10.3). The Illinois Supreme Court has read the duties in that section to reach a person named as a successor. A successor who sees something wrong should act.

## How do you name successors, step by step?

1. Choose a first agent you would trust with every account.
2. Choose a first successor who does not live in the same household, in case an accident affects both.
3. Add a second successor, because the first may also be unavailable.
4. Write the full legal name and address of each person on the form.
5. Ask each person to read the notice to the agent and agree to serve.
6. Have each sign the specimen signature line if the form includes it.
7. Give copies to the bank and each person, and review the list every few years.

## What does the default rule say about co-agents?

When a custom document names co-agents, the Act sets a default. Unless the document or the Act says otherwise, authority granted to two or more co-agents is exercised only by their majority consent (755 ILCS 45/2-10.5). With two co-agents, that effectively means both must agree. Banks often respond by requiring both signatures on every transaction, which can slow bill paying.

A document that names co-agents should say whether each can act alone, whether both must sign for sales of real estate or large transfers, and what happens if one dies or resigns.

| Setup | Allowed on the short form? | Who acts | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| One agent, no successor | Yes | The agent | No backup if the agent cannot serve |
| One agent, named successors | Yes | Agent first, then successors in order | Successor may not know what happened |
| Co-agents, majority consent | No, custom only | Both or most together | Delays and deadlock |
| Co-agents, each acting alone | No, custom only | Either one | Duplicate or conflicting transactions |
| Separate documents, one agent each | Yes, two documents | Each agent for their own job | Roles must be clear |

## When are separate documents the cleaner choice?

Often the family wants two things: one person to handle money and another to handle medical decisions, or two children to share responsibility. Two separate documents can do that. For example, Teresa, 72, names her son Carlos as agent for property and her daughter Maya as agent for health care. Each names the other as first successor. Nobody has to agree with anybody at the bank, and no one can sign for the other's job. The [health care power of attorney](/learn/illinois/illinois-healthcare-power-of-attorney) is a separate form with different witness rules.

## What if a spouse or a trusted person cannot serve later?

Divorce matters here. Illinois treats a former spouse as having died when the court enters a judgment of dissolution or legal separation, so the next successor steps in (755 ILCS 45/2-6). Read our article on [how divorce affects your Illinois estate plan](/learn/illinois/how-divorce-affects-your-illinois-estate-plan). If no successor is named, the power may end.

If siblings are already at odds, naming a neutral first successor, such as a trusted friend, can help. Our [guide to choosing a power of attorney agent](/learn/power-of-attorney/choosing-a-power-of-attorney-agent) lists questions to ask. You can also tell the agent in writing to keep brothers and sisters informed, which avoids many accusations.

## What if the first agent resigns?

An agent who no longer wants the job should resign in writing, as explained in [stepping down as a power of attorney agent](/learn/power-of-attorney/can-a-power-of-attorney-agent-resign-or-step-down). The successor then needs a copy of the resignation to show the bank.

## What should the document say about successors who cannot be found?

Plans fail when the named person is hard to reach. Add a few practical lines when you draft.

- Give each person's current address, phone number and email, and update them when they change.
- Say that a successor takes over when the earlier agent signs a resignation or a doctor or court finds the earlier agent unable to act, so the bank has a clear trigger.
- Tell each person where the original document is kept, as described in [where to keep your power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/where-to-keep-your-power-of-attorney-after-you-sign-it).
- Ask the first agent to keep a ledger from day one, so a successor can pick up the records. Our article on [agent duties and record-keeping](/learn/illinois/illinois-poa-agent-duties-and-record-keeping) shows what to keep.

Review the line-up every three to five years, after any birth, death, move, marriage or divorce, and after a falling out between family members. A good list at signing can be out of date within a few years.
