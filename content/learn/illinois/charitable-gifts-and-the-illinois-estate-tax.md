Many Illinois families already plan to leave something to a church, a school or a hospital. When the estate is near $4 million, that same gift has a second effect: it can reduce or remove the Illinois estate tax. Because of how Illinois sets its tax, the effect is strongest right above the line.

This page shows the numbers, explains which gifts qualify, and lists the steps to carry one out. The general rules for charitable gifts are in our article on [charitable gifts and bequests](/learn/estate-tax/charitable-gifts-and-bequests-gift-tax-estate-tax-and-income-tax). The overview of the tax is in the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois) and [the Illinois estate tax page](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax).

## Does a charitable gift lower the Illinois estate tax?

Yes. Gifts to qualified charities are deductible in computing the federal taxable estate (26 U.S.C. 2055), and the Illinois tax in 35 ILCS 405 starts from the federal calculation. A deductible gift lowers the amount Illinois taxes. The gift can be made by will, by revocable trust, or by naming the charity as beneficiary of an account.

Two cautions. The charity has to qualify as a tax-exempt organization, and the gift must be a binding transfer, not a note in a drawer. Also, the Attorney General says a return is required when the gross estate is over $4 million. Deductions reduce the tax, but we would assume a return is still needed and confirm it.

## What does a charitable gift actually cost the family?

Less than its face value, when the estate is just over $4 million.

Take Helen, 79, a widow in Peoria with a $5 million estate and three children. Without charity, the Illinois tax is about $285,714, the Attorney General's own example for that size. Her children would receive about $4.71 million.

| Plan | To charity | Illinois tax | To the three children |
|---|---|---|---|
| No charitable gift | $0 | $285,714 | about $4,714,286 |
| $500,000 to charity | $500,000 | about $142,900 | about $4,357,100 |
| $1,000,000 to charity | $1,000,000 | $0 | $4,000,000 |
| $2,000,000 to charity | $2,000,000 | $0 | $3,000,000 |

The $1 million gift costs Helen's children about $714,000. The state would have taken $285,714 of that anyway. The $2 million row shows the limit: once the estate is at the line, extra giving costs the family dollar for dollar. The figures are our arithmetic, so run the Attorney General's calculator before deciding.

## Which kinds of gifts work?

- **A fixed dollar bequest in the will or trust.** Simple and clear. Name the charity exactly.
- **A percentage of the residue.** Adjusts as the estate grows or shrinks. Good if you do not know your future numbers.
- **A beneficiary designation on an IRA or 401(k).** The charity receives the account directly. It avoids probate and avoids the income tax that family would pay.
- **A charitable remainder trust.** The trust pays you or a family member for years, then the rest goes to charity. See [charitable remainder trusts](/learn/estate-tax/charitable-remainder-trust).
- **A donor-advised fund.** Gifts go into a fund now and the family recommends grants later. See [donor-advised funds and QCDs](/learn/estate-tax/donor-advised-funds-and-qcds).
- **A lifetime gift.** Removes the asset and its future growth from the estate. Charitable gifts are not taxable gifts for gift tax purposes.

## Why does it help to give the retirement account?

An IRA left to a child is taxed to the child as ordinary income when withdrawn, generally within 10 years under the SECURE Act. The same IRA left to charity is not taxed at all. Other assets, such as a house or a brokerage account, usually get a [step-up in basis](/learn/estate-tax/step-up-in-basis) for the family. So if you will give $400,000 to charity anyway, sending it from the IRA often leaves more for your children than sending it from the house. Naming the charity on the account form is simple, and the account is not probated. Check your forms with the [beneficiary audit](/tools/beneficiary-audit).

## How do you set up a charitable bequest?

1. Pick the charity and get its full legal name and tax ID number.
2. Decide on a dollar amount, a percentage, or an account.
3. Estimate your Illinois estate with and without the gift.
4. Write the gift into the will or trust. For accounts, file the beneficiary form with the custodian and keep a copy.
5. Name an alternate gift in case the charity no longer exists. Our article on a [charity named in a will that no longer exists](/learn/what-if/charity-named-in-will-no-longer-exists) shows what can go wrong.
6. Review the plan every few years, because values and charities change.

## What mistakes cause problems?

Using a nickname for the charity, so the gift is argued over. Leaving the same account to charity and to a child. Forgetting that a gift subject to the family's use, such as a house with a life estate, may be valued differently. And making a large gift when the estate is already far below the line, where it saves no Illinois tax at all. If your estate is $2.5 million, a charitable bequest is a good act, but it is not a tax move.

Married couples add a layer. Gifts to a spouse can qualify for the marital deduction, which defers the tax, and gifts to charity are deductible in full. The tools can be combined with a credit shelter trust, as in [credit shelter trusts and the Illinois QTIP election](/learn/illinois/illinois-qtip-election-and-credit-shelter-trusts).

## What if the estate is far above $4 million?

The percentages change. For an estate of $7 million, the Illinois tax is about $565,600, and the next dollar is taxed at about 11 cents or so, not 28.6 cents. A $1 million charitable gift still reduces the tax by roughly $110,000, but the gift now costs the family about $890,000 after tax. That may still be a good choice if the family wants to give, but it is no longer a bargain.

The most useful moment for charity as a tax tool is an estate between $4 million and about $5.3 million. Above that, giving is mainly about values, not savings. Below $4 million, there is no Illinois estate tax to reduce.
