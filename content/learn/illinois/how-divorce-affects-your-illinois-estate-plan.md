Ruth and Paul divorced in Naperville this spring. The judgment is signed and the house is sold. In her file cabinet, Ruth still has a will that leaves everything to Paul, a power of attorney naming Paul as her agent, a living trust that makes Paul successor trustee and a life insurance form from her old job with Paul's name on it. Illinois deals with some of those documents for her. It does not deal with all of them.

This page takes the whole plan, document by document, and tells you what a divorce changes by itself, what it leaves alone, and what to do. If you want only the will, our page on [changing or revoking a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/changing-or-revoking-a-will-in-illinois) goes deeper, and the power of attorney mechanics are in [revoking an Illinois power of attorney](/learn/illinois/revoking-an-illinois-power-of-attorney). The rest of the state's rules sit in our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

## When does a divorce change your Illinois documents?

At the judgment, not before. Every Illinois rule on this page is tied to a judgment of dissolution of marriage, or in the power of attorney's case also legal separation. While the case is pending, your will still names your spouse, your power of attorney still works, and your beneficiary forms are unchanged. Our page on [what happens if a spouse dies during a divorce](/learn/what-if/death-during-divorce) explains that gap, and [planning during a separation](/learn/life-stages/estate-planning-during-separation-or-a-pending-divorce) covers what to do in the meantime.

## What does divorce do to each document?

| Document | What the judgment does | What it leaves behind |
|---|---|---|
| Will | Gifts, powers of appointment and fiduciary nominations favoring the ex are treated as if the ex died before you (755 ILCS 5/4-7(b)) | Gifts to the ex's family; backup roles that are blank; the rest of the will |
| Property power of attorney | A spouse agent is treated as having died at the judgment of dissolution or legal separation (755 ILCS 45/2-6) | Other agents stay; a bank may still honor an old paper |
| Revocable trust | Provisions favoring the former spouse are revoked unless the trust or judgment says otherwise (760 ILCS 3/605) | Other beneficiaries and trustees; the trust must still be funded correctly |
| Joint tenancy | No automatic change to a joint tenancy | Ex may still survive you as owner |
| Tenancy by the entirety | Converts to a tenancy in common unless the court orders otherwise (765 ILCS 1005/1c) | You and the ex now own shares that pass by will, not survivorship |
| Beneficiary forms | Depends on the account and on federal law | Often the ex stays named |

## Will: what happens to your Illinois will?

The ex is read out of the will, and the document usually survives. Ruth's will leaves everything to Paul and then to her sister Gail if Paul does not survive. After the judgment Paul is treated as having died first, so Gail takes. That works. But if the will also names Paul's brother as trustee for a child's share, or leaves a vehicle to Paul's mother, those gifts to other people stand. A divorce does not rewrite the rest. Make a new will after the judgment, and see [marriage, divorce or a new baby and your existing will](/learn/wills/marriage-divorce-or-a-new-baby-and-your-existing-will) for the general version.

## Power of attorney: who can act for you now?

A spouse named as agent in a property power of attorney is treated as having died at the time of the judgment. If you named a successor agent, that person takes over. If you did not, no one holds authority, and a family member may need to petition for guardianship if you later lose capacity. That is the practical danger. Sign a new power of attorney the week the judgment enters. See [Illinois power of attorney for property](/learn/illinois/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property).

Health care documents follow their own rules. We do not rely on the divorce statute for a health care power of attorney. Sign a new one that names someone you chose, and give copies to your doctors. The Illinois form is explained in our page on the [Illinois health care power of attorney](/learn/illinois/illinois-healthcare-power-of-attorney). If no health care agent is in place, [the Health Care Surrogate Act](/learn/illinois/illinois-health-care-surrogate-act-who-decides) decides who speaks for you, which is a reason not to leave the gap open.

Tell banks in writing. A bank may honor a paper it has on file until someone tells it otherwise. Our page on [whether divorce cancels a power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/does-divorce-cancel-a-power-of-attorney) lists the steps.

## Living trust: does divorce cancel the former spouse's role?

The Illinois Trust Code revokes provisions that favor a former spouse after divorce, unless the trust or the divorce judgment says otherwise (760 ILCS 3/605). Many couples sign a joint trust, and that is where trouble starts. A joint trust may need to be split, funded again, or replaced. Read our page on [Illinois living trusts](/learn/illinois/illinois-living-trusts) for how revocation works while both people are alive. If you hold a house in a trust with an ex, do not assume the statute has sorted out who owns what. The property settlement and the deed decide that.

## Beneficiary forms: where most of the money goes wrong

Forms are the part divorce does not fix reliably. A life insurance policy, an IRA, a 401(k) and a bank account each follow their own contract, and state revocation rules vary by account type. For workplace plans, federal law can require the plan to pay the person named on its form even if state law would remove an ex. Our page on [an ex-spouse who is still the beneficiary](/learn/what-if/ex-spouse-still-beneficiary) explains the court cases and the fix.

Two cautions. First, read the judgment before you change anything. Many judgments require you to keep a former spouse or children as the beneficiary on life insurance to secure support. Second, name contingent beneficiaries, because a blank backup sends the money to your estate. Use the [beneficiary audit tool](/tools/beneficiary-audit) to list every form.

## What should you do after an Illinois divorce?

Work through this list within a month of the judgment:

1. Get a certified copy of the judgment and read the sections on insurance, retirement accounts and property.
2. Sign a new will that names your executor, backup executor and guardians for minor children.
3. Sign a new property power of attorney and a new health care power of attorney.
4. Review the living trust, or replace a joint trust with your own.
5. Change each beneficiary form, subject to the judgment, and keep confirmation.
6. Check deeds, vehicle titles and joint accounts. An entireties home is now a tenancy in common.
7. Send a signed revocation of the old power of attorney to the bank and your advisers.
8. Tell your executor and agents where the new documents are.

Our general guide to [estate planning after a divorce](/learn/life-stages/estate-planning-after-divorce) covers the same ground for any state.

## A common mistake our attorneys see

People update the will, feel finished and forget the retirement account. Years later the account, often the largest asset, goes to the former spouse, and the children learn that the will never controlled it. The reverse also happens: a person updates the forms but leaves a joint account with an ex. The review should be in one sitting, with every document on the table.
