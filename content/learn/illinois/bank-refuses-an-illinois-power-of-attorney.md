You stand at the teller line with your mother's power of attorney, and the manager says, "We need our own form." Or the document is "too old." Or someone in a back office wants to take another week. Your mother's bills are due, and you cannot sign a new form for her.

The general steps are in [what to do when a bank refuses a power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/bank-refuses-power-of-attorney). This page applies the Illinois rules in section 2-8 of the Power of Attorney Act. For the other documents involved, see our [Illinois guide](/learn/illinois).

## What does Illinois law say about refusals?

Section 2-8 of the Act (755 ILCS 45/2-8) deals with third parties who deal with an agent. In plain terms, a person may presume, if they do not know otherwise, that the document was validly signed, and may rely on it. The section then separates refusals into two groups.

| Type of refusal | Examples from the statute | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Unreasonable on its own | Document is not on the bank's own form; a long time has passed since it was signed | The bank should honor a properly signed statutory short form |
| Reasonable | Agent will not give a requested affidavit or certification; the bank knows of a problem | Provide the certification, or expect a legitimate refusal |

The statute lists more items in each group than this table shows, and the lists can change. A 2026 law, Public Act 104-609, amends part of the list of reasonable grounds starting January 1, 2027. Read the current text of 755 ILCS 45/2-8 before you rely on any single item, or have an attorney check it.

## What should you do first, step by step?

1. Ask the bank to state the reason for refusal in writing, with the name and title of the person who decided.
2. Compare the reason with the table above. Is it a documentation request or a rule about its own forms?
3. Gather what the bank can lawfully ask for: the original signed document with the witness and notary pages, the agent's photo ID, and a short sworn statement that the power has not been revoked and the principal is alive.
4. Ask for a supervisor and then for the bank's legal or compliance department. Offer to send a copy ahead.
5. Write a short letter that cites 755 ILCS 45/2-8 and ask for a response by a set date.
6. If the bank offers its own form, read it. Sign it only if it does not narrow the powers or add duties.
7. If the bank still refuses, talk to an attorney about a petition to the circuit court.

## What documents help overcome a refusal?

A few simple documents remove most objections.

- The original or a certified copy of the power of attorney.
- A one-page agent's certification of facts, signed before a notary.
- A short letter from the principal's doctor if the document starts on incapacity. Our article on a [doctor who will not certify incapacity](/learn/power-of-attorney/doctor-will-not-certify-incapacity-springing-power-of-attorney) explains the problem with delayed start dates.
- For real estate, a recorded copy with the county recorder, such as in [Cook County](/estate-planning/illinois/counties/cook).

## Why do banks refuse even a good form?

Usually it is risk and routine. A branch employee has never seen a statutory short form. The bank wants to avoid being blamed if the agent later takes money. Older documents trigger extra review. The Act's protection for third parties who rely in good faith is meant to ease this, but practice varies by bank.

A refusal does not mean your document is defective. It often means the right person has not looked at it yet. Keep calm and polite. Notes with dates and names help if you later need a lawyer.

## When is a court petition the next step?

If the bank will not release money and the principal cannot sign a new power, your lawyer may petition the circuit court under the Act to review the document and give relief. The Act also lets a court order relief when an agent's authority is questioned, as discussed in our piece on [court accounting for an Illinois agent](/learn/illinois/court-accounting-for-an-illinois-poa-agent). A court case costs time and fees, so it is usually a last step.

If there is no valid power at all, [Illinois guardianship](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship) becomes the route. It takes longer and the court supervises the guardian.

## Example: Ruth and the "too old" document

Ruth, 62, holds her father Leonard's power of attorney, signed in 2017 in the statutory form with a witness and a notary. His bank says it is too old and wants a new one. Leonard has moderate dementia and cannot sign. Ruth asks for a written reason, then sends a letter citing 755 ILCS 45/2-8 with a notarized certification. The bank's legal department accepts it within two weeks. Ruth then makes her own copy set so she is ready for the next refusal.

## How can you prevent a refusal?

Send a copy to the bank when the document is signed. Ask the branch to scan it into the file. Use the statutory form and fill it out cleanly, as our [step-by-step guide to the form](/learn/illinois/filling-out-the-illinois-statutory-short-form-poa-for-property) explains. Keep records using the habits in [agent duties and record-keeping](/learn/illinois/illinois-poa-agent-duties-and-record-keeping). The [plan finder](/plan-finder) can help you see whether your documents are up to date.

## What should your letter to the bank say?

A short, calm letter often does more than a long phone call. Keep it to one page and include these points.

1. Your name, your role as agent, and the principal's name and account number.
2. The date you presented the document and the name of the person who refused.
3. The reason given, quoted as closely as you can.
4. A statement that the document is the Illinois statutory short form, signed with a witness and a notary, and a reference to 755 ILCS 45/2-8.
5. What you enclosed: a copy of the power of attorney, your ID and a signed certification.
6. A request for a written answer by a date, usually 10 to 14 days out.

Send it to the branch manager and to the bank's legal or compliance department, and keep the delivery receipt. If the bank answers with a new reason, update your notes. A paper record also helps an attorney or a court see that you acted reasonably.
