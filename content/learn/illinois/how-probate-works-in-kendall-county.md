If a parent lived in Yorkville, Oswego, Plano or another part of Kendall County, the estate is usually a Kendall County matter. The county sits in the 23rd Judicial Circuit, and its Circuit Clerk keeps the probate files. This page is part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

It covers the clerk's office, how an estate opens, the will, remote appearances, self-help and case lookup. It then turns to a question common in a fast-growing county: what happens to a mortgaged, newer-subdivision house while probate runs. For your own facts, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-kendall-county&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where do I file probate in Kendall County?

File with the Kendall County Circuit Clerk. The clerk's probate page for a deceased person's estate lists the office at 807 W John St, Yorkville, IL 60560. It gives 630-553-4183 as the phone and Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., as the hours.

A separate county contact page lists the same street address but shows hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. When two county pages differ, call the clerk before you leave. Neither page lists a room number, so ask where to go inside the building.

| Place or contact | What the county lists | What a probate family uses it for |
|---|---|---|
| Circuit Clerk, probate page | 807 W John St, Yorkville; 630-553-4183 | Forms, filing questions, case files |
| Court Administrative Office | 630-553-4208 | Court scheduling and accessibility questions |
| Courtroom 116 | Listed for probate matters | Where probate hearings are heard |
| Clerk's viewing room | Kendall County Courthouse, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. | Looking at restricted documents in person |

The county pages do not list parking or a courthouse floor plan. If mobility or hearing needs matter, the remote appearance instructions name a Court Disability Coordinator at 630-553-4208.

## How does a Kendall County estate open?

The clerk's probate page sorts it into two paths. If there is a will, the petition is a petition to admit the will. If there is no will, the petition is for letters of administration instead.

The page lists the forms needed to start. They include a new case probate information sheet, an oath of representative, bond and surety forms, and an affidavit of heirship, with or without a surviving spouse or descendants. It also lists petitions to admit a will and proposed orders. It adds about 40 more forms that may come up during the case.

The page also says appearances must show the attorney's or self-represented person's email address. For the steps in order, read [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) and the [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process).

The clerk's general information page adds one timing rule. It cites Local Rule 87-6 and a 9-month status return date on probate cases. The page does not explain more, so ask the attorney or clerk what date applies to your case. Estates that need a long time still follow that early check-in, and the page on [how long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois) shows what stretches a case.

## What happens to the original will in Kendall County?

Start with the statute, not the county. Illinois law makes whoever holds a will deliver it to the circuit clerk after the death. The page on [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) explains the duty, the 30-day clocks and why a photocopy cannot take the original's place.

Kendall County's probate page says you must file a copy of the will with the petition to admit it. The same clerk's forms page lists a Receipt of Will form and a Small Estate Affidavit. The pages do not describe where to drop off an original or when. So call 630-553-4183 first and ask how the original will should be delivered and receipted.

If you cannot find the will, [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois) lists where wills turn up. If the estate is small and has no real estate, the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) may avoid a full case, though a will still goes to the clerk.

## Do I have to e-file, and can I appear remotely?

Probably yes to the first. The clerk's general information page says it is now mandatory to file civil cases electronically, with fees applying. It says the office does not accept filings by fax or email. The clerk's main page says electronic filing goes through eFileIL and warns that emailed filings are rejected under Supreme Court Rules.

Remote appearance is narrower. The county's page says attorneys and case participants can appear by Zoom on some court dates, not all. It calls remote appearance a privilege, not a right, and each courtroom has staggered start times. It refers to Local Rule 2023-01 but does not reprint the rule.

Probate matters are listed under Courtroom 116. The page does not set a separate rule for probate, so ask whether your hearing date allows Zoom. The instructions page adds practical points:

- Create a Zoom account before the hearing if you do not have one.
- Enter the courtroom's meeting ID. The page says there is no password.
- Use your full name as it appears in court documents, or you may not be admitted.
- Wait in the waiting room until the judge admits you, and mute yourself after speaking.
- With no video, call 1-312-626-6799 and enter the meeting ID.
- If you are disconnected and cannot rejoin, call the Circuit Clerk at 630-553-4183.

A remote hearing is still an official hearing, so appear on time. The private chat is not secure, so do not share personal details in it.

## What self-help does the county list?

The clerk's main page points people to Illinois Court Help, described there as a service of the Illinois Supreme Court to assist court patrons with filings. The clerk's general information page says a fee waiver application is available, or you can ask the clerk for an Application to Sue or Defend as an Indigent Person.

The forms page lists a civil fee waiver, along with the Small Estate Affidavit and Receipt of Will. The pages do not say the clerk will fill out forms or give legal advice, and do not describe a probate help desk. Ask the clerk what help exists before you go.

For a person handling an estate without an attorney, read [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer). For court costs, see [the Illinois probate fee waiver](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-fee-waiver).

## How do I look up a Kendall County probate case?

Use the clerk's Online Court Records page. It recommends Chrome or Firefox and lists an eMagnus help desk at 1-800-250-9884 for access trouble. It also lets you sign up for eNotify, which sends text or email reminders about upcoming court dates.

The page does not say which case types or years the search covers, and it does not mention probate by name. It notes that state policy limits which documents are public online. To see restricted documents, go to the Circuit Clerk's viewing room at the Kendall County Courthouse, open 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. on weekdays except court holidays.

If a search shows nothing, treat that as a reason to call 630-553-4183, not as proof that no estate exists. The same goes if you are not sure whether anyone opened a case for a relative. See [how to find out if you are named in a will](/learn/illinois/how-to-find-out-if-you-are-named-in-a-will-in-illinois).

## What happens to a mortgaged newer-subdivision house during probate?

Many Kendall County homes were built in recent decades, and a parent may still owe a large balance. The mortgage does not end at death. As the page on [what happens to a mortgage after death](/learn/probate/what-happens-to-a-mortgage-car-loan-and-student-loans-after-death) explains, it stays attached to the house, and the lender expects payments to continue.

Heirs are not personally liable for the loan just by inheriting, unless they co-signed. The same page says federal law generally stops a lender from calling the whole loan due just because a close relative inherits an owner-occupied home. The page on [what happens to the mortgage when the homeowner dies](/learn/property-and-assets/mortgage-when-the-homeowner-dies) covers the Garn-St Germain rule and the questions to put to the servicer in writing.

Probate adds a timing problem. If the house is in the parent's name alone, the family cannot sell it until the court appoints someone. See [what to do when a parent died with the house in their name](/learn/illinois/parent-died-house-in-their-name) and [can heirs sell the house during probate](/learn/probate/can-heirs-sell-the-house-during-probate).

Meanwhile, bills keep coming. The page on [taking care of a deceased parent's house in Illinois](/learn/illinois/taking-care-of-a-deceased-parents-house-in-illinois) says payments should continue. It also warns that autopay from the parent's account may stop when the bank freezes it. When estate funds exist, payments come from them. A relative who fronts money should keep receipts, since the creditor claim rules covered in [how creditor claims work in an Illinois estate](/learn/illinois/how-creditor-claims-work-in-an-illinois-estate) rank administration costs high.

## Example: the Brennan family in Oswego

This is an invented example. Walter, 74, a widower, died in Oswego. His house is in his name alone, with a mortgage balance of about $180,000 and a home value above that. His will names his daughter Claire as executor and leaves everything equally to her and her brother Evan.

Claire calls the Circuit Clerk at 630-553-4183 and asks how the original will should be filed and receipted. The attorney e-files the petition to admit the will, and Evan, who lives out of state, asks whether Zoom is allowed for his hearing.

Meanwhile, the bank froze Walter's checking account, and the autopay stopped. Claire pays one month's mortgage herself, keeps the receipt, and calls the servicer with the death certificate. Once letters are issued, payments come from the estate account.

The siblings decide to sell. The payoff letter goes to the title company, the lender is paid at closing, and the rest stays in the estate until claims and expenses are handled. Claire never had to take the loan in her own name.
