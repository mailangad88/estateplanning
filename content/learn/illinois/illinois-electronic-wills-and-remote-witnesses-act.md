Priya, 62, lives in Chicago and has just finished chemotherapy. She does not want to sit in a waiting room. Her daughter asks, "Can't you just sign it on a video call?" The answer is yes, in Illinois, but only if the steps are followed.

Illinois passed the Electronic Wills and Remote Witnesses Act so that people can sign wills without being in the same room. This page compares the three signing paths, explains the step order for each, and covers what an executor needs later. Our [Illinois will requirements](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-requirements) page covers the basic rules, and the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois) has the wider picture.

## What does the Act cover?

The Act, found at 755 ILCS 6, governs how electronic wills, paper copies of electronic wills, and wills witnessed by audio-video communication are signed, attested, proved and probated. It came into law in 2021 through Public Act 102-167. Its title now also refers to electronic estate planning documents. A will under the Act must still meet the Probate Act and the common law that the Act does not replace. So capacity, free choice and proper witnesses still count. Check the current text with your attorney, because the General Assembly has amended the Act.

## What are the three ways to sign a will in Illinois?

The path you pick changes who must be present and what your executor must prove. Here is the comparison.

| Path | Where witnesses are | What the signer uses | Main extra step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper, in person | In the room | Pen and paper | None beyond the Probate Act |
| Paper, video witnesses | On a live video call, in the United States | Pen and paper | Illinois named as place of signing; signature pages joined to the will within 10 business days |
| Electronic will | In the room, signing electronically | E-signature on a tamper-evident file | Petition must state it is a tamper-evident electronic record |

For many clients a paper original is still the easiest to probate, because the court sees the same document the maker signed.

## How does a fully electronic will work?

An electronic will is signed with an electronic signature by the maker, or by someone else at the maker's direction and in the maker's presence. At least two witnesses then sign it electronically in the maker's presence after watching the signing (755 ILCS 6/5-5).

Two practical points follow. First, the file must be kept in a form that shows if it was changed, because the petition to probate it must say so (755 ILCS 6/20-5). Second, the maker should tell the executor which company or attorney holds the file. An electronic will that nobody can locate is as useless as a lost paper original. Our page on a [lost original will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/lost-original-will-in-illinois) explains why that is a problem.

## How does video witnessing of a paper will work?

The maker signs a paper will while witnesses watch on a live audio-video link (755 ILCS 6/15-10). The rules include these:

- The will names Illinois as its place of execution.
- There are two or more credible witnesses, each in the United States at the time.
- Each witness confirms the maker's identity and sees the maker sign or acknowledge the signature.
- Each witness attests in the maker's presence, which for a video call means in the video view.
- If witnesses sign separate pages, the signed pages are attached to the will within 10 business days.
- A person who signs for the maker cannot be a witness, a beneficiary, or a spouse or child of a beneficiary.

### What are the steps for a video signing?

1. Test the video and sound link a day early. Agree on a backup plan if it drops.
2. Confirm the final will is printed with the pages in order.
3. The witnesses show photo ID on camera, and the maker does too.
4. The maker states that this is their will, then signs while on camera.
5. Each witness sees the signature, then signs a signature page and an attestation statement.
6. The signature pages go to the person responsible, who attaches them to the original within 10 business days and notes the date.
7. Keep a written record of who attended, from where, and the times.

## What is a certified paper copy, and why does it matter?

A certified paper copy is a printout of an electronic record that someone certifies as accurate. The certifier states facts such as the date the copy was prepared, and makes the statement by testimony, by a written statement certified under the Code of Civil Procedure, or in an affidavit attached to the copy. Where a rule requires an original, the certified paper copy can meet it. This helps with banks, title companies and courts that want paper. Ask the attorney who holds your electronic will to keep a plan for producing certified copies.

## Which path should you choose?

Choose by how you live, not by what sounds modern. A person who can attend an office, and who has a simple family, usually gets the most reliable result with paper. A person who is ill, travels, or lives apart from their attorney may benefit from video witnessing. A fully electronic will suits someone who needs digital storage and has an attorney who runs the process. ## What should your executor know?

After a death the will must be filed with the clerk of the circuit court (755 ILCS 5/6-1). The six-month window to contest a will starts when it is admitted ([Illinois will contest deadline](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-contest-six-month-deadline)). So a clean signing record helps. Keep the attestation statements, the video log, the date the pages were joined and the name of the person who held them. If the family cannot show how the will was signed, expect questions. Changing or revoking an electronic will follows the Probate Act's revocation rules, which our page on [changing or revoking a will](/learn/illinois/changing-or-revoking-a-will-in-illinois) explains.
