A will is only useful if the court accepts it. In Illinois, a will that misses a signing step can be rejected, and then state law decides who inherits instead. The good news is that the rules are short and clear.

This page walks through the Illinois requirements for who can make a will, how to sign it, who should witness it, how to make it self-proving, and how electronic wills and video witnessing work. It is general education, not legal advice. For where wills fit in the bigger picture, see our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois).

## What makes a will valid in Illinois?

Three rules do most of the work. The person must have the right to make a will, the will must be signed the right way, and it must be witnessed the right way.

- **Age and mind.** Anyone who is 18 or older and of sound mind and memory can leave real and personal property by will (755 ILCS 5/4-1).
- **Writing and signature.** The will must be in writing and signed by the testator, or by someone else in the testator's presence and at the testator's direction (755 ILCS 5/4-3).
- **Witnesses.** It must be attested in the testator's presence by two or more credible witnesses (755 ILCS 5/4-3).

The statute does not list a notary. In Illinois, the witnesses are what count. See our article on [how to make a valid will](/learn/wills/how-to-make-a-valid-will) for the general steps that apply in every state.

### What if the person has been under guardianship?

Illinois adds a rule for people who have been found disabled. If a court has appointed a guardian, a will signed after that is presumed invalid. The presumption can be overcome with clear and convincing evidence that the person had capacity when signing (755 ILCS 5/4-1). Doctor notes from the day of signing can help. Our glossary explains [testamentary capacity](/glossary/testamentary-capacity).

## Who can be a witness to an Illinois will?

The law asks for "credible" witnesses. In practice that means adults who can see and understand what is happening and can later say so. The most important point is that witnesses should not receive anything under the will.

Under 755 ILCS 5/4-6, if a gift in the will goes to a person who witnessed it, or to that person's spouse, the gift is void unless the will has enough other witnesses. The beneficiary can still get up to what they would have received without the will. The same section says a person or company is not disqualified from serving as executor or trustee, or from being paid, just because an employee or partner was a witness. Many law firms use staff members as witnesses for this reason.

Here is a quick comparison.

| Person | Safe as a witness? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A neighbor or coworker who gets nothing | Yes | No financial interest |
| A child named in the will | No | Gift may be void |
| The spouse of a person named in the will | No | Spouse's interest is covered by the rule |
| The executor, if also a beneficiary | Risky | Beneficiary rule applies |
| A law firm employee | Yes | Section 4-6 protects the firm's role |

## How do you sign an Illinois will step by step?

Following a clear order helps, because the witnesses may be asked years later what they saw.

1. Gather the testator and two witnesses in one place, with a pen and the final printed will.
2. The testator confirms that this is their will and that they understand it.
3. The testator signs, or directs someone to sign in their presence.
4. Each witness watches the signing and then signs in the testator's presence.
5. The witnesses sign an attestation clause, which states what they saw.
6. The testator keeps the original in a safe place and tells the executor where it is.

Our article on [where to keep your will](/learn/wills/where-to-keep-your-will) covers storage. A handwritten will without witnesses does not meet the rule above. See [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills) for the risks.

## What does self-proving mean in Illinois?

A self-proving will is one the court can accept without live testimony from the witnesses. Under 755 ILCS 5/6-4, the execution of a will is proved when each of two attesting witnesses states that they saw the testator sign, that they attested in the testator's presence, and that they believed the testator was of sound mind.

That statement can be given in court, in an attestation clause signed by the witness and part of or attached to the will, or in an affidavit signed at or after the time of attestation. So a clause or affidavit signed at the signing is what makes the will easy to prove. Our glossary explains the [self-proving affidavit](/glossary/self-proving-affidavit).

The court can still reject a will if there is proof of fraud, forgery, compulsion or other improper conduct (755 ILCS 5/6-4). See [contesting a will](/learn/wills/contesting-a-will) for how those challenges work.

## Does the Electronic Wills and Remote Witnesses Act still apply?

Yes, as far as we could confirm. The Act is codified at 755 ILCS 6. The section on its effective date says the Act takes effect upon becoming law, and we found no repeal or sunset date in the sections we reviewed. Its title now also refers to electronic estate planning documents. Our attorneys can confirm the current text before you sign.

The Act covers two things.

### Electronic wills

An electronic will is signed by the testator, or by someone at their direction in their presence, with an electronic signature. At least two witnesses must sign it electronically in the testator's presence after seeing the testator sign (755 ILCS 6/5-5). When someone wants to probate an electronic will, the petition must state that it is a tamper-evident electronic record (755 ILCS 6/20-5). That extra step is one reason many people still prefer paper.

### Remote witnesses by video

A will can also be witnessed by audio-video communication (755 ILCS 6/15-10). The rules are strict:

- The will must name Illinois as its place of execution.
- It must be witnessed by two or more credible witnesses who are in the United States at the time.
- The witness must confirm the testator's identity, see the testator sign or acknowledge the signature, and attest in the testator's presence.
- If there are separate signature pages, the testator or a person they appoint must attach the signed pages to the will within 10 business days.
- A person who signs for the testator cannot be a witness, a beneficiary, or the spouse or child of a beneficiary.

Take Priya, who lives in Chicago and is recovering from surgery. Her attorney arranges a video call with two firm employees in Illinois. They check her ID, watch her sign the printed will, and sign their pages. Her attorney attaches those pages to the original within 10 business days. Any slip in these steps could cause trouble later, so use an attorney for a remote signing.

## How can a will be changed or canceled?

Under 755 ILCS 5/4-7, a will can be revoked by burning, cancelling, tearing or obliterating it, by the testator or by someone at their direction in their presence. It can also be revoked by a later will or by a signed and witnessed instrument that says so. A divorce cancels gifts and appointments for a former spouse in a will signed before the divorce.

To change a will, sign a new one or a witnessed [codicil](/glossary/codicil). Do not cross out lines on a signed will.

## What happens to the will after death?

Whoever holds the will must file it with the clerk of the circuit court immediately upon the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1). The statute also makes it a crime to alter or destroy a will, or hide it for 30 days after death, and a court can order production of a will.
