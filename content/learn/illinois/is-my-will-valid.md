You are holding a will. It might be a few pages your mother wrote by hand, a printout from an online service, or a form from an office supply store. Someone died, or someone is sick, and the question is whether a court will accept it. This page gives you six checks to run on the paper itself, and then explains what happens in Illinois probate when the answer is no.

It is general education, not legal advice, and it sits inside our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois). If you cannot find the original, start with [what to do about a lost original will](/learn/illinois/lost-will). If you want a lawyer to look at the document, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-is-my-will-valid&topic=review) and bring the original.

## The short version

- Illinois wants a written will, signed by the maker, with two witnesses who watched.
- Handwritten does not excuse the witnesses. Notarized does not replace them.
- Online services give you a form. Your signing day makes it valid or not.
- A failed will is not the end of the story. Other documents, other assets and other wills may still decide the outcome.

## Is a handwritten will valid in Illinois?

Only if it also meets the ordinary signing rule. Under 755 ILCS 5/4-3, a will must be in writing, signed by the testator or by someone at the testator's direction in the testator's presence, and attested in the testator's presence by two or more credible witnesses. The rule says nothing that waives the witnesses for a will in the maker's own handwriting. A handwritten, signed will with no witnesses is the situation our [Illinois handwritten and oral wills guide](/free/illinois-handwritten-oral-and-emergency-wills-guide) calls generally invalid.

A handwritten will that was also properly witnessed is valid. For the general picture across states, see [handwritten, online and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills).

## What are the six checks to run on the will you have?

Go down the list in order. Write the answer next to each one. Stop and call an attorney the first time you cannot answer yes.

| Check | What to look for | If you cannot say yes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Signed | The maker's signature at the end, or a note that someone signed at the maker's direction and in the maker's presence | An unsigned will is a draft. Do not treat it as valid. |
| 2. Two witnesses | Two signatures from people who watched the maker sign | One witness, or none, does not meet the statute on its face. |
| 3. Disinterested witnesses | Neither witness, nor a witness's spouse, is named to receive a gift | The gift to that person can be void (755 ILCS 5/4-6). |
| 4. Capacity | The maker was 18 or older and of sound mind and memory when signing | A guardianship before signing raises a presumption of invalidity (755 ILCS 5/4-1). |
| 5. Attestation | A witness clause or affidavit saying the witnesses saw the signing | The will may still be valid, but the witnesses may have to testify. |
| 6. Format and date | Printed, handwritten or e-signed under the electronic wills law, and the latest version | An old will can be replaced by a later one (755 ILCS 5/4-7). |

### Check 1 and 2: the signature and the two witnesses

These two decide most cases. The witnesses must have seen the testator sign, or seen the testator acknowledge the signature, and then signed in the testator's presence. Witnesses who were handed a signed page and asked to sign later is the most common failure.

### Check 3: who the witnesses were

Take the example of Dolores, who signs her will at her kitchen table with her son Mike and his wife Jen as witnesses. Her will leaves the house to Mike and Jen's two children, and the cash to Mike. Mike is a witness and a beneficiary, so under section 4-6 his gift is void unless the will has enough other witnesses, though he may still receive up to what he would have inherited without the will. The rest of the will is generally untouched. It is a messy outcome for a document that looked finished. Our page on [Illinois will requirements](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-requirements) has the full witness table.

### Check 4: age and mind

The testator must be at least 18 and of sound mind and memory. Capacity is rarely visible on the face of a will. It comes up when someone contests, and the evidence is the doctor's notes and the witnesses' memory. A will signed during a hospital stay can be perfectly valid. It is also the kind most likely to be challenged, which is why a witness statement from the day matters.

### Check 5: a clause that proves the signing

A self-proving attestation is a short paragraph the witnesses sign, stating they saw the testator sign and believed the testator was of sound mind. Under 755 ILCS 5/6-4, that statement, or an affidavit signed at or after the signing, lets the court admit the will without live testimony. Many forms include one, and many were never completed. See the [self-proving affidavit](/glossary/self-proving-affidavit) entry.

### Check 6: online, printed or electronic

An online service does not make a will valid. Its output is a form, and the family finds out later whether the signing was done right. A printed will signed on paper in front of two witnesses meets the statute. A will signed with an electronic signature has to follow the separate Electronic Wills and Remote Witnesses Act (755 ILCS 6/5-5 for the electronic will, 755 ILCS 6/15-10 for video witnessing). Among other things, a video-witnessed will must name Illinois as its place of execution, and signature pages must be joined to the will within 10 business days. If someone says they "clicked sign" and uploaded a PDF, ask an attorney to read the file.

## Does an Illinois will need to be notarized?

No. The statute asks for witnesses and does not mention a notary (755 ILCS 5/4-3). That surprises people who have notarized a deed or a power of attorney. A notarized will with no second witness is still short a witness.

## How does a will get proved in Illinois probate?

Whoever holds the original must file it with the circuit court clerk right after the death (755 ILCS 5/6-1). The court then admits it to probate when it is proved under section 6-4, which the existing guide on [Illinois probate](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) walks through. With a witness clause, the proof is a page. Without one, the court may need one or both witnesses to testify, which is hard if they have died or moved. Take Tom's family: his will is a signed form with two witness lines filled in and no attestation paragraph. Eight years later, one witness is his former neighbor in another state. The family needs her statement, and finding her is now part of the estate.

## What happens in probate if the will is not valid?

The document is not admitted as a will, and the court looks for something else to apply. In order:

1. **An earlier valid will.** If the maker signed an older will correctly, that one controls, unless the invalid one revoked it in a way the law recognizes (755 ILCS 5/4-7). A will that fails on form usually does not revoke anything.
2. **Intestacy.** If there is no valid will, Illinois statutes pick the heirs, starting with the spouse and descendants (755 ILCS 5/2-1). See [who inherits without a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-inherits-without-a-will-in-illinois).
3. **Assets that never needed the will.** Life insurance, retirement accounts, payable-on-death accounts and jointly owned property pass by their own paperwork. A broken will does not change them. See [payable-on-death and joint bank accounts in Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-payable-on-death-and-joint-bank-accounts).

Here is what that looks like. Harold, a widower, wrote on a legal pad that his house goes to his daughter Carol and his savings go to his son Dean. He signed it and his neighbor signed once. His children are Carol, Dean and Beth. If the pad is not admitted, the three share under intestacy, and the house is split three ways even though he named Carol. The pad is not worthless. Carol can show the family what he wanted, and the others may choose to honor it. But in Illinois that is a voluntary family agreement, not an order, and any agreement should be put in writing with an attorney's help.

## Can a family still contest or defend a will?

Yes. A person with an interest can file a petition to contest a will within 6 months after its admission to probate (755 ILCS 5/8-1). The clock runs from admission, not from death. Our page on the [Illinois will contest deadline](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-contest-six-month-deadline) explains who can file. If you are the one who may be left out because the form failed, speak to an attorney well before month six, because gathering the witnesses takes time.

## What should you do with the will you have?

1. Do not mark, staple, fold or add notes to the original.
2. Photograph every page, front and back.
3. File the original with the circuit court clerk where the person lived, or give it to the attorney who will file it. Sitting on a will is a legal risk for the person holding it.
4. Make a list of the witnesses and any lawyer or service involved, with contact details.
5. Note where each asset is held and whether a beneficiary form exists. The [probate asset sorter](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) shows what actually goes through the court.
6. Use the free [DIY will red flags checklist](/free/diy-will-red-flags-checklist) and [Illinois signing requirements cheat sheet](/free/illinois-signing-requirements-cheat-sheet) to compare the paper with the rules.

If the maker is alive, the fix is easy. A new will, signed properly, replaces the old one. The right time to check a DIY will is while the person who made it can still sign again.
