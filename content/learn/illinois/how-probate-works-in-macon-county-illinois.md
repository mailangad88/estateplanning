Say a parent lived in Decatur, Forsyth, Mt. Zion or Maroa and has just died. The estate is almost always a Macon County, Illinois matter, heard in the 6th Judicial Circuit. This page, part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois), is about Macon County, Illinois, and not the other counties of that name elsewhere in the country.

It covers the Decatur courthouse, how an estate opens, what to do with the original will, remote appearances, self-help and case lookup. Then it turns to a worker retired from a plant, whose pension and 401(k) pass by paperwork and not by will. For your own facts, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-how-probate-works-in-macon-county-illinois&topic=probate) with an Illinois estate planning attorney.

## Where is the Macon County probate court and clerk?

The Macon County courthouse is at 253 East Wood Street in Decatur, ZIP 62523. The county's Circuit Clerk page lists the clerk at that address in Room 129. The state court directory places the clerk's office on the first floor and lists the circuit as the 6th Circuit.

The clerk's own site gives 217-624-4442 as the clerk's phone and 217-624-4350 for the Circuit Court. It lists weekday hours of 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The county page shows 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., labelled for payments. When pages differ, call first.

| Place or contact | What the official pages list | What a family uses it for |
|---|---|---|
| Circuit Clerk | 253 East Wood Street, Room 129; 217-624-4442 | Filing the will, case files, questions |
| Circuit Court | 217-624-4350 | Court-side and scheduling questions |
| Probate and Juvenile Division | An email address on the clerk's contact page | Probate questions in writing |
| Language and disability help | A number on the clerk's contact page | Interpreters and access needs |

The clerk's site also links a courthouse map and parking page. Use it before a hearing, because the pages do not describe parking in the text.

## How does a Macon County estate open?

A family member, or the person named in the will, asks the court to open the estate. With a will, the request is to admit it. Without one, the request is for the court to appoint someone, and the heirs are set by statute.

The Macon County pages do not walk through that sequence. They do not list probate forms or a local timetable. So the steps below come from the general Illinois process. The page on [how to open a probate estate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-to-open-a-probate-estate-in-illinois) goes through them in order.

1. Get several certified death certificates. The page on [Illinois death certificates](/learn/illinois/illinois-death-certificates-who-can-order-county-clerk-or-idph) explains where.
2. Find the original will and deliver it to the clerk.
3. File the petition, with the bond and oath papers that apply, through the clerk's e-filing portal if you use one.
4. Attend the hearing, in person or remotely if the judge allows it.
5. Receive letters from the court. The glossary entries for [letters testamentary](/glossary/letters-testamentary) and [executor](/glossary/executor) explain the roles.
6. Notify heirs, inventory assets, and handle claims over the months that follow.

## What do I do with the original will?

Do not keep it, copy it, or hand it to a relative. Illinois law requires whoever holds the original will to file it with the circuit clerk of the proper county after the death. The page on [filing the original will with the Illinois circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk) explains the duty and the 30-day clocks.

The official Macon County pages do not say how the original should be delivered, whether a receipt is issued, or whether mail is accepted. So phone 217-624-4442 and ask. Write down who you spoke with and when, and keep your own copy of the will.

If you cannot find the will, [finding a will after a death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/finding-a-will-after-a-death-in-illinois) lists where wills turn up. If a will exists but is missing, read about a [lost original will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/lost-original-will-in-illinois). A modest estate might use the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit), though a will still goes to the clerk.

## Can I file electronically and appear by Zoom?

The clerk's site links the eFileIL portal under an e-Filing heading and lists its Rules of Practice. It does not post filing instructions for probate. Ask the clerk or an attorney whether your petition should go through the portal.

Remote attendance is narrower. The clerk's Zoom page says remote attendance requires the judge's approval, and that litigants and attorneys who cannot attend in person may request it. The clerk's Rules of Practice page also lists an administrative order on remote hearings and rules on remote court proceedings effective May 1, 2023.

The Zoom page adds these practical points:

- Your Zoom display name must be your full name, because access is granted only when the court expects you.
- You need a computer with a camera and microphone, or a smartphone, and a stable connection.
- Log in at least 10 minutes before your hearing and wait to be admitted from the waiting room.
- Recording any court proceeding, in person or remote, is prohibited without approval.

The page lists links for two courtrooms, 2A and 2B. It gives no instructions specific to probate, so ask which room and link apply to your hearing before the date. Never post a meeting passcode in a family group chat.

## What self-help does the court list?

The clerk's Legal Library and Self Help page says many court forms are already printed and available inside the Self-Help Center. It links the statewide approved forms, Illinois Legal Aid Online and a video guide for people who go to court without a lawyer. The clerk's forms page also links a Macon County Self Help Legal Center and a law school self-help site.

None of those pages lists probate or will forms. So do not assume the Self-Help Center stocks estate paperwork. Call before you go, and read [handling an Illinois probate estate without a lawyer](/learn/illinois/handling-an-illinois-probate-estate-without-a-lawyer) to see what that path involves. A short [Illinois probate checklist](/free/illinois-probate-checklist) can help you gather papers before the call.

## How do I look up a Macon County case?

The clerk's site has a Judici Case Search link for the Macon County court. Use it with a name or case number. The site does not say which case types appear or how fast new filings show up.

Treat the search as a finding aid. The file itself, and anything restricted, sits with the clerk. The page on [how to look up a probate case](/learn/probate/how-to-look-up-a-probate-case) explains what a docket summary can and cannot tell you.

## What happens to a retired plant worker's pension and 401(k)?

Many families have a parent who retired from a plant with a company pension and a 401(k). The key point is that those accounts usually skip the will and probate. They pay the person named on the plan's form, or in some plans the spouse. The page on [which document controls](/learn/wills/which-controls-will-trust-joint-account-or-beneficiary-form) explains the order.

For most private-sector 401(k) plans under federal ERISA rules, a spouse is treated as the beneficiary unless the spouse signs a written waiver witnessed by a notary or plan representative. A defined benefit pension can carry a survivor annuity that a spouse must waive. An IRA has no federal consent rule. See [retirement account beneficiaries and spousal consent in Illinois](/learn/illinois/retirement-account-beneficiaries-and-spousal-consent-in-illinois).

Government pensions follow their own rules, covered in [Illinois pension survivor benefits](/learn/illinois/illinois-pension-survivor-benefits-imrf-trs-surs-sers). The national picture is in [retirement account beneficiaries](/learn/beneficiary-designations/retirement-account-beneficiaries).

### Example: Walter and the old beneficiary form

Example only, with invented names. Walter, 74, retired from a plant outside Decatur. His will leaves "everything equally" to his two daughters, Colleen and Tess. His 401(k) form, signed years ago, names his late wife's sister, Joan. His wife died earlier, so no spousal consent was needed.

When Walter dies, the 401(k) goes to Joan, not to the daughters. Colleen opens the estate in Macon County and files the will with the clerk. The will controls the house and the bank accounts, but the plan follows its form.

| Asset | How it passes | Who gets it |
|---|---|---|
| House in Walter's name alone | Probate, under the will | Colleen and Tess |
| Bank account, no beneficiary named | Probate, under the will | Colleen and Tess |
| 401(k) | Plan beneficiary form | Joan |
| Company pension | Plan's survivor rules | Whoever the plan names or its rules provide |

Walter could have fixed this at any time while alive by updating his forms. The page on [how to change the beneficiary on a 401(k), IRA or life insurance policy](/learn/beneficiary-designations/how-to-change-the-beneficiary-on-a-401k-ira-or-life-insurance-policy) walks through that. The [beneficiary audit tool](/tools/beneficiary-audit) helps list every account and who is named.

If you are the family member in Colleen's position, tell the plan administrator about the death and ask what the plan needs. The estate may also need to list these assets in its [inventory](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-inventory), depending on how they pass. The page on [what an executor does in Illinois](/learn/illinois/what-does-an-executor-do-in-illinois) covers the larger job.

## What mistakes do Macon County families make?

- Keeping the original will at home while the family argues about it.
- Assuming the will controls a pension or 401(k).
- Showing up at the courthouse without calling about the room, hours or the form needed.
- Joining a Zoom hearing without the judge's approval.
- Spending pension deposits that arrive after a death before asking the plan.
- Using a stale beneficiary form after a death, divorce or remarriage.
