If you live in Rockford, Peoria, Springfield, Champaign or Bloomington, are recovering from surgery, or simply cannot get to an office, you may wonder whether you can sign a will or trust over a screen. Illinois allows it, for a will, if a specific set of steps is followed.

This page covers the Illinois rules for remote will signing, how an electronic will differs from a will witnessed by video, what happens with a trust and a power of attorney, and a step-by-step for a video signing. It is general education, not legal advice. It sits inside our [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois), and if you want to talk through your own situation you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-sign-will-or-trust-remotely-in-illinois&topic=will).

## The short version

- A will can be witnessed by live video in Illinois, under the Electronic Wills and Remote Witnesses Act (755 ILCS 6).
- The will has to say Illinois is where it was signed, and every witness must be in the United States.
- A video call does not fix a mistake that a paper signing would also have made, such as a witness who is also a beneficiary.
- A property power of attorney needs a witness and a notary. A health care power of attorney needs a witness and no notary.
- If the only obstacle is distance, a signing at your home on paper is often the calmer route.

## Can you sign a will remotely in Illinois?

Yes. The ordinary rule is that a will must be in writing, signed by the maker, and attested in the maker's presence by two or more credible witnesses (755 ILCS 5/4-3). The Electronic Wills and Remote Witnesses Act adds two ways to meet that rule without everyone standing in one room: an electronic will, and witnessing by audio-video communication. The page on [Illinois will requirements](/learn/illinois/illinois-will-requirements) explains the base rules, and [is my will valid](/learn/illinois/is-my-will-valid) shows how a court checks a signed document. Here is only what changes when a screen is involved.

## What is the difference between an electronic will and video witnessing?

They solve different problems, and people mix them up.

| | Video witnessing | Electronic will |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A will, often on paper, whose witnesses watch you sign by live video | A will that exists as an electronic record and carries electronic signatures |
| Section | 755 ILCS 6/15-10 | 755 ILCS 6/5-5 |
| Who signs | You, and two or more witnesses | You, or someone at your direction, and at least two witnesses who sign electronically after seeing you sign |
| What the witnesses do | Confirm identity, see you sign or acknowledge it, attest in your presence by video | Sign electronically in your presence |
| Main catch | Signature pages must be joined to the will within 10 business days | A petition to probate it must state that it is a tamper-evident electronic record (755 ILCS 6/20-5) |
| Good fit for | Someone who cannot travel but is fine with paper | Someone whose attorney runs an approved electronic process |

For many people the first column is the better fit. The paper will is easy to hand to a probate clerk, and the family can hold it in their hands. Our page on [electronic wills and wills made on a phone](/learn/wills/electronic-wills-and-wills-made-on-a-phone-or-in-an-email) explains why a note or an email is a different thing altogether, and the [online and electronic will decision guide](/free/online-and-electronic-will-decision-guide) helps you decide.

## What are the Illinois rules for witnessing a will by video?

Section 15-10 sets the conditions:

- The will must name Illinois as its place of execution.
- It needs two or more credible witnesses, and each witness must be in the United States at the time.
- Each witness must confirm your identity, see you sign or acknowledge your signature, and attest in your presence.
- If the signature pages are separate, you or a person you appoint must attach the signed pages to the will within 10 business days.
- If another person signs for you at your direction, that person cannot be a witness, a beneficiary, or the spouse or child of a beneficiary.

Everything else about witnesses still applies. A witness, or a witness's spouse, who receives a gift under the will can lose that gift unless there are enough other witnesses (755 ILCS 5/4-6). Nothing in the video rules cancels that.

Consider Hector, 66, who lives outside Peoria and cannot sit for long after a hip replacement. His attorney's office is in another county. He signs a printed will at home while two witnesses at the attorney's office watch him on a video call. The witnesses check his driver's license on camera and see the pen touch the paper. They then sign their own pages, and the attorney collects all three pages, attaches them to the will and notes the date. The set is joined well within 10 business days. Nothing about that is exotic. It is just careful.

## How do you do a video signing, step by step?

The statute sets some of these. Others are habits that make the signing easy to prove years later.

1. **Decide the format first.** Paper with video witnesses, or a true electronic will. Do not decide on the call.
2. **Confirm the will names Illinois as its place of execution.** The video rule depends on it (755 ILCS 6/15-10).
3. **Choose two neutral adult witnesses.** They should gain nothing under the will and should be in the United States on the day. Add an alternate in case one drops off.
4. **Test the connection beforehand.** A frozen screen at the moment of signing is a gap in what the witness saw.
5. **Send the printed will to the signer ahead of time.** The witnesses should have a copy of the same pages.
6. **Start the call and check identity.** Each witness confirms who you are, for example by looking at your photo ID on camera.
7. **State that this is your will.** Say it aloud and have witnesses hear it. A witness who only watched someone scribble makes a weak witness later.
8. **Sign in view of all witnesses.** Keep the whole page and your hand on camera. If you signed earlier, acknowledge the signature on camera.
9. **Have each witness sign and date their page,** with a printed name and address beside it, and sign or attest in your presence on camera.
10. **Collect the pages and join them to the will within 10 business days.** Keep the courier tracking numbers or the email dates.
11. **Store the original** and tell your executor where it is. The [will signing day checklist](/free/will-signing-day-checklist) and the [Illinois signing requirements cheat sheet](/free/illinois-signing-requirements-cheat-sheet) are useful to print.

## What about a trust?

A revocable living trust is a different kind of document, and the Electronic Wills and Remote Witnesses Act is about wills. Trusts are often easier to sign with general electronic-signature tools, because general e-signature law often applies to them, as our page on [signing estate planning documents electronically](/learn/basics/sign-estate-planning-documents-electronically) explains. The practical limit is not the trust itself. It is the deed that moves a house into the trust, which has to be accepted by the county recorder, and a bank or brokerage that wants its own paperwork. Trusts are usually signed with a "pour-over" will, and that will still needs its own two witnesses. For how the trust works in Illinois, see [Illinois living trusts](/learn/illinois/illinois-living-trusts) and [trust signing, notary and witnesses](/learn/trusts/trust-signing-notary-witnesses-and-where-to-keep-the-documents).

## Can a power of attorney be notarized remotely?

The Illinois statutory short form for property must have a witness to your signature and must be notarized (755 ILCS 45/3-3). So a video call can stand in for the room only if the notary and the witness can lawfully do their job that way. Many states allow remote online notarization with identity checks and a recording. Whether a particular notary, the bank that will receive the document and the county recorder will accept it is something to ask before the signing, not after. Our page on [powers of attorney and notarization](/learn/power-of-attorney/does-a-power-of-attorney-need-to-be-notarized) covers why institutions are picky.

The health care form is lighter. It needs one adult witness and, for the statutory form, no notary (755 ILCS 45/4-10), while a living will needs two witnesses. Details are on the page about the [Illinois health care power of attorney](/learn/illinois/illinois-healthcare-power-of-attorney), and the property form is in [the Illinois power of attorney for property](/learn/illinois/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property).

Say Marjorie, 78, lives near Bloomington and wants her son in Texas to act for her. He cannot be her witness. Her neighbor can. A mobile notary comes to her house, and the paper original goes to the bank by mail. That is slower than a screen and far less likely to be refused.

## When is paper at your location a better choice than video?

When anyone's capacity might be questioned, when the family is not in agreement, or when the signer is in a hospital or nursing home. In those cases the person overseeing the signing needs to see who else is in the room. The page on [signing a will with dementia, illness or family pressure](/learn/wills/signing-a-will-with-dementia-illness-or-family-pressure) goes into that. For a simple will and a healthy signer who just lives far away, video witnessing is a reasonable tool.

A short comparison:

| Situation | Usually the better fit |
|---|---|
| Healthy signer, simple will, attorney far away | Video witnessing, paper will |
| Signer in a care facility, family involved | Signing in person on paper, with neutral witnesses |
| Signer comfortable with technology and an attorney-run platform | Electronic will |
| Property power of attorney needed quickly | Mobile notary and one neutral witness |
| Signer who cannot hold a pen | Someone signs at the signer's direction in the signer's presence (755 ILCS 5/4-3), with extra care about who it is |

## What goes wrong with remote signings?

Three things show up again and again:

- **The witness was not eligible.** A son-in-law who was also a beneficiary, or a neighbor who left the call early.
- **The pages were not joined in time.** The 10 business days pass because nobody owned the task.
- **The place of execution is missing.** A form written for another state may not say Illinois.

If your will was signed remotely and you are unsure it meets the rules, have an Illinois attorney read it. The fix, while you are alive, is cheap: sign again properly. If the will was signed by a person who has since died, [is my will valid](/learn/illinois/is-my-will-valid) and the [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) explain what comes next.
