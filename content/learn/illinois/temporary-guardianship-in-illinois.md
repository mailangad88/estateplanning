A parent is in the hospital. The staff says she cannot go home alone, and no one holds a power of attorney. Or a relative discovers that an adult is sending thousands of dollars to a stranger and the bank will not intervene. A regular guardianship case takes weeks or months. Illinois allows a faster, narrower tool for that gap.

This page covers the emergency version under Article XIa of the Probate Act (755 ILCS 5/11a-4). It builds on our [Illinois guardianship overview](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship). Parents choosing short-term care for a child want a different article, [standby and short-term guardians for children](/learn/illinois/illinois-standby-and-short-term-guardians-for-children).

## What is a temporary guardian in Illinois?

A temporary guardian is a person the court appoints for a limited time to protect an adult in an emergency. The petition for a regular guardian must already be filed or be filed with the request. The court may appoint the temporary guardian for the person, the estate or both, based on a showing that the immediate welfare or protection of the person or the estate requires action before the full hearing (755 ILCS 5/11a-4).

The order must be tied to the facts. A judge cannot appoint a temporary guardian because a case is slow. There must be a concrete need and harm that cannot wait.

## What do you have to show the court?

You show specific facts, preferably with documents. Courts look for:

- A physician's statement about the person's condition and the urgency.
- Records or statements that show the harm, such as bank statements, an adult protective services report or a hospital note.
- What you have already tried, such as asking the person to sign a power of attorney.
- The narrow powers you want and why nothing less will do.

Because the person may lose rights even for a short time, the judge expects the request to be modest. Asking to approve one medical procedure is more likely to succeed than asking for open-ended control.

## How long does a temporary guardianship last?

It lasts no more than 60 days after appointment, or until a regular guardian is appointed, whichever happens first (755 ILCS 5/11a-4). The short clock is deliberate. It pushes the case toward a full hearing where the person's rights are fully protected. Our article on [the rights of the person in a guardianship case](/learn/illinois/rights-of-the-person-in-an-illinois-guardianship-case) explains what that hearing includes.

A family should treat the 60 days as a hard limit and ask the court to calendar the regular hearing right away. If the hearing slips, ask a lawyer promptly about your options. Do not assume an extension.

## What powers can a temporary guardian have?

Only what the order lists. Examples that appear in practice include:

- Consent to a defined course of medical treatment or a hospital discharge plan.
- Authority to freeze or move one account, or to stop a particular transaction.
- Authority to make a placement decision to a safe setting for a defined period.

The temporary guardian should keep records, because the judge at the regular hearing will want to see what was done. Spending should be modest and tied to the person's needs.

## What is the process, step by step?

1. Gather the physician's statement and any proof of the emergency.
2. File the regular guardianship petition, or confirm that one is pending.
3. File the request for a temporary guardian, with a statement of the harm.
4. Give notice of the hearing to the person and the relatives the law requires. Notice is shorter than for the regular hearing.
5. Attend the emergency hearing. The person may attend, bring a lawyer and respond.
6. If the judge appoints a temporary guardian, get the order and letters, and use only the powers listed.
7. Ask for the regular hearing date, and be ready before day 60.

## How does temporary guardianship compare with the regular kind?

| | Temporary guardian | Regular guardian |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Emergency gap | Ongoing decisions |
| Length | Up to 60 days | Until the court ends it |
| Notice before hearing | Short | At least 14 days |
| Powers | Narrow, tied to the emergency | As listed in the final order |
| Full hearing first? | No | Yes |
| Statute | 755 ILCS 5/11a-4 | 755 ILCS 5/11a-3, 11a-10 |

## Example: Tom and his sister Joan

Tom, 61, learns that his sister Joan, 64, who has a brain injury, wired $18,000 to a caller promising a prize. The bank says it will not freeze the account without a court order. Tom files for guardianship of Joan's estate and asks for a temporary guardian limited to her checking and savings. The judge hears the matter in a few days and signs a narrow order. The regular hearing is set for week six, and Tom prepares the physician's report in the meantime.

## What are the risks of an emergency request?

Emergency orders move fast, and fast cases can go wrong. A judge who signs a narrow order without a full hearing may revisit it if the facts turn out to be thinner than described. A temporary guardian who overreaches can face questions at the regular hearing, and the family can lose credibility. Practical guards include:

- Ask only for the powers the emergency needs.
- Keep every receipt and decision log.
- Tell the person what is happening in plain language.
- Do not use the order to settle a family dispute.

Where a hospital needs a decision but the person has no agent, Illinois law also lists a surrogate who may decide medical questions without a court. Compare [power of attorney versus guardianship](/learn/power-of-attorney/power-of-attorney-vs-guardianship), and read [the Illinois Health Care Surrogate Act](/learn/illinois/illinois-health-care-surrogate-act-who-decides) before assuming a court is the only way.

## What comes after the 60 days?

If the court holds the regular hearing and appoints a guardian, the temporary order ends and the permanent order lists the powers. If the court declines to appoint one, the temporary order ends and control returns to the person. The temporary guardian's records become part of the file.
