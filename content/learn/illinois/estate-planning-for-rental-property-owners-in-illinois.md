You live upstairs in an Avondale two-flat, or rent out two Evanston condos, or own a six-unit building in Berwyn. Tenants pay you, the plumber calls you, and the bank account is in your name. If you died or had a stroke tomorrow, who would do any of that?

This page, part of the [Illinois estate planning guide](/learn/illinois), compares the ways to hold title to Illinois rentals, shows who can act for tenants during incapacity and probate, and covers reluctant heirs and the Illinois estate tax. The general landlord questions about leases, deposits and loans are on our national page on [what happens to rental properties when a landlord dies](/learn/business-owners/landlord-estate-plan-rental-properties-llc-or-trust). If you already know your buildings need work, you can [book a consultation](/book?from=learn-estate-planning-for-rental-property-owners-in-illinois&topic=trust) about your plan.

## What happens to an Illinois rental the day the owner dies?

The leases keep running, but authority stops. Whether someone can step in this week depends on how the deed reads. A building in your name alone becomes part of your probate estate, and nobody can sign a lease, cash a rent check in your name or list the building until a court issues [letters of office](/glossary/letters-of-office). Our [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) page covers that court step, and [how long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois) covers the wait.

Once letters issue, an [independent representative](/glossary/independent-representative) can manage and sell estate property without a court order (755 ILCS 5/28-8), as our page on [selling a house in probate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/selling-a-house-in-probate-in-illinois) explains. One catch for landlords: if your will leaves a specific building to a named person, the executor cannot sell or mortgage it without that person's written consent, according to the same page. Leaving "the Berwyn building to my son" can tie the executor's hands if the estate needs cash.

## Which way of holding title works for an Illinois landlord?

Each option answers three questions differently: probate, incapacity, and who deals with tenants afterward.

| How the rental is held | Skips probate? | Helps during incapacity? | Who deals with tenants after death | Illinois watch-out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Your name alone | No | Only through your power of attorney | Executor, after letters issue | Weeks of rent with no one authorized to collect it |
| Joint tenancy with a child | Yes, for your share | Partly; the co-owner must sign too | The surviving joint tenant | Deed must state joint tenancy, or it is a tenancy in common |
| Tenancy by the entirety | Yes, to the spouse | No | The surviving spouse | Limited to a homestead, so not a building you do not live in |
| Recorded TODI | Yes, if recorded before death | No | The beneficiary, after a notice of death affidavit | Two or more beneficiaries share with no survivorship |
| Land trust, no successor named | Often no | Depends on the agreement | The executor, as holder of the beneficial interest | The interest is personal property and can land in probate |
| Land trust assigned to a living trust | Yes | Yes | Your successor trustee | The trustee bank needs the assignment on file |
| Living trust holds the deed | Yes | Yes | Your successor trustee | Check the lender and insurer before you record |
| LLC owned by you | No, for the LLC interest | Only if a manager or agent can act | The LLC's manager, if the agreement names one | The heir may get only economic rights |
| LLC owned by your living trust | Yes | Yes | Successor trustee and named manager | The operating agreement must allow trust ownership |

The joint tenancy and entirety rows rest on 765 ILCS 1005/1 and 1c, explained in [Illinois joint tenancy and tenancy by the entirety](/learn/illinois/illinois-joint-tenancy-and-tenancy-by-the-entirety). The LLC rows rest on the LLC Act, under which a dead member's transferee generally gets distributions, not a vote (805 ILCS 180/35-45 and 30-1); see [what happens to my LLC if I die in Illinois](/learn/illinois/llc-owner-dies).

## Where do a land trust and a TODI fit for rentals?

**Land trusts.** Many Chicago-area buildings were bought into a land trust at closing. The bank holds title and you hold the beneficial interest, which Illinois treats as personal property. Without a named successor beneficiary or an assignment to your living trust, that interest can end up in probate. See [land trust or living trust for a Chicago property](/learn/illinois/land-trust-vs-living-trust) and [when the owner of a land trust dies](/learn/illinois/illinois-land-trust-when-the-owner-dies). A lender on a rental may hold a collateral assignment of the interest, so call it before you sign a new assignment.

**TODIs.** A recorded [transfer on death instrument](/glossary/transfer-on-death-instrument) can pass a rental at death without probate, and it costs little (755 ILCS 27). It has three limits for landlords. It does nothing if you lose capacity, and an agent under a power of attorney cannot sign or revoke one for you. It only works on property titled in your name, not on a building held by an LLC or a land trust trustee. And if you name your three children, they take equal shares with no survivorship unless the instrument says otherwise (755 ILCS 27/65), so they become co-landlords who must agree on every lease and repair. The [Illinois TODI page](/learn/illinois/illinois-transfer-on-death-instrument) covers the signing rules, and [TODI or living trust for an Illinois house](/learn/illinois/todi-vs-living-trust-for-an-illinois-house) compares the two.

For a rented condo, also check association rules and assessments in [what happens to a Chicago condo or co-op when the owner dies](/learn/illinois/chicago-condo-and-co-op).

## Who manages the rentals if you are alive but cannot?

Your agent under a power of attorney for property, if the document grants the real estate power. On the Illinois statutory short form, that category covers buying, selling, leasing and managing property, according to our [Illinois power of attorney for property](/learn/illinois/illinois-power-of-attorney-for-property) page. With it, your agent can sign renewals, hire contractors and deposit rent into your accounts.

Three limits matter for landlords:

1. **The agent works for you, not for the family.** The agent must act in good faith and keep records, and cannot give a building to a relative without specific gift authority (755 ILCS 45/3-4). Our page on [whether a power of attorney can sell a house in Illinois](/learn/illinois/can-a-power-of-attorney-sell-a-house-in-illinois) walks through those duties.
2. **Trust and land trust property is not the agent's to sign.** If a living trust holds the deed, the successor trustee manages it. If a land trust holds title, the bank signs at the direction of whoever holds the power of direction.
3. **Everything ends at your death.** The agent cannot collect next month's rent once you die; see [does a power of attorney end at death in Illinois](/learn/illinois/does-a-power-of-attorney-end-at-death-in-illinois).

Name the same person as agent and successor trustee, or make sure the two work together, so one person does not hold the rent account while another holds the deed.

## Who handles tenants and rent while probate is open?

Before letters issue, nobody has clear authority, and that is where landlords' families get into trouble. A relative who tells tenants to pay rent to them personally, or who signs a new lease, is acting without authority, and the money they collect still belongs to the estate. Tenants still owe rent under the lease, and the safest step is to ask them to keep paying the way they always have and to keep receipts until the representative is appointed.

After letters issue, the representative opens an [estate bank account](/learn/illinois/opening-an-estate-bank-account-in-illinois), collects rent into it, keeps the insurance and mortgage current and holds the tenants' deposits as an obligation of the estate, not as money for the heirs. State and local rules on deposits and notices can apply, so the representative should check them before changing any terms. The [Illinois probate inventory](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-inventory) page explains how the buildings and rent receipts are reported to the court.

## What if your heirs do not want to be landlords?

Plan for it now. A child across the country will not want a tenant's furnace call from Cicero, and siblings rarely agree on when to sell; see [siblings who disagree about an inherited house](/learn/illinois/siblings-disagree-inherited-house).

Options to write into the plan:

- **Direct a sale.** The trust tells the successor trustee to sell each building within a set time and divide the proceeds.
- **Give the trustee discretion.** The trustee may keep the buildings, hire a property manager paid from the rent, or sell when the market suits.
- **Leave buildings to the child who wants them.** Equalize the others with cash or life insurance.
- **Keep the LLC.** Heirs can hold LLC interests, with a named manager running the buildings, instead of co-owning deeds.

Heirs who sell soon after a death usually owe little tax on the gain because of the [step-up in basis](/glossary/step-up-in-basis); for heirs who keep renting, see [taxes on inherited rental property](/learn/property-and-assets/inherited-rental-property-taxes).

## Can a few buildings trigger the Illinois estate tax?

Yes. Illinois taxes estates over $4,000,000 of gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts, and spouses cannot share an unused exclusion, according to our [Illinois estate tax](/learn/illinois/illinois-estate-tax) page. The federal exclusion is $15 million per person in 2026, so most landlords owe nothing federally. Buildings count at full date-of-death value whether you hold them directly, through an LLC or in a revocable trust, and a living trust alone does not lower the tax.

The tax rises steeply just over the line. The Attorney General's own example, cited on that page, puts the Illinois tax on a $5,000,000 Illinois estate at $285,714. Married landlords often use a credit shelter trust at the first death so both $4 million exclusions count. The [estate tax estimator](/tools/estate-tax-estimator) gives a rough first number.

## Example: Henry's three kinds of rental

This is an invented example. Henry, 71, is a widower in Oak Park with two daughters, Nina and Claire. He owns (rough values):

- A two-flat he lives in, in his own name: $750,000.
- Two rented Forest Park condos in a 1998 land trust with no successor beneficiary: $300,000 each.
- A Berwyn six-unit building in an LLC he owns alone: $1,400,000.
- Retirement accounts and savings: $1,300,000.

His gross estate is about $4,050,000, over the Illinois threshold. His title would also leave Nina, his executor, with probate for the two-flat and the land trust interest, and an LLC with no one able to sign checks.

Henry signs a living trust naming Nina as successor trustee. He deeds the two-flat to the trust, assigns the land trust interest to it, and assigns his LLC interest to it after amending the operating agreement to name Nina as successor manager. His power of attorney for property gives Nina the real estate power. Claire does not want to manage anything, so the trust lets Nina hire a property manager and sell the condos within two years. His attorney also reviews charitable and lifetime gifts for the estate tax.
