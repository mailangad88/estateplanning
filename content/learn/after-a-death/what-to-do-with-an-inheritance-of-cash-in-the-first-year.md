A check arrives, or a transfer shows up in your account, and the number is bigger than you have ever held at once. For a lot of people there is also grief in it, and a quiet worry that doing the wrong thing with the money would dishonor the person who left it.

You do not have to decide anything quickly. This page covers what to do in the first weeks, what is and is not taxed, the order in which to use the money, and the mistakes we see most often. If you want the tax side in more depth, read [inheritance taxes for heirs](/learn/after-a-death/inheritance-taxes-for-heirs). For everything else that follows a death, start with our [after-a-death guide](/learn/after-a-death).

## What should you do in the first week?

Very little, on purpose. Cash is easy to move and hard to get back.

1. **Confirm it is final.** Check whether the payment is a partial distribution from an estate still in probate. If so, ask the executor whether the estate might ask for money back to cover debts or taxes. Many executors hold a reserve for this reason.
2. **Deposit it where it is insured.** A bank or credit union account is insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per institution, per ownership category. If you received more than that, split it across institutions or ask about other options.
3. **Keep it in your name alone.** Not a joint account, not a family member's account.
4. **Keep the paperwork.** The distribution letter, the receipt and any statement showing where the money came from. You may need them for taxes or for a lender.
5. **Say little.** Telling friends and relatives the amount creates pressure you did not ask for.

Then wait. A few months of quiet is a decision in itself, and it rarely costs anything.

## Do you pay tax on an inherited sum of cash?

Not federal income tax. An inheritance is not income to the person who receives it, and the United States has no federal inheritance tax. If the estate was large enough to owe federal estate tax, which starts above $15 million per person in 2026, the estate pays it before anything reaches you.

A few caveats are worth knowing:

- **Interest and growth are taxable.** The inheritance is not, but what the money earns afterward is. Your bank sends a Form 1099-INT.
- **Some states charge an inheritance tax.** It falls on the heir, with lower rates or exemptions for close family. Our page on [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes) explains who is affected, and the executor usually pays it from the estate before distribution.
- **Foreign money has a reporting rule.** If a U.S. person gets a large inheritance from a foreign person, the IRS requires an information return on Form 3520. There is typically no tax, but there can be a penalty for not filing.
- **Not all inheritances are cash.** An inherited IRA is taxed as it is withdrawn, as covered in [inherited IRAs and 401(k)s](/learn/after-a-death/tax-on-inherited-ira-401k-annuity-and-savings-bonds). Inherited investments have their own basis rules.

You do not report a cash inheritance as income on Form 1040. If you are unsure whether what you received is cash or something else, ask the executor for a breakdown.

## What order should you use the money in?

There is no single right order, and a financial adviser can tailor one to you. This sequence suits most people.

| Step | Why it comes first |
| --- | --- |
| Pay off high-interest debt, such as credit cards | The return equals the interest rate you stop paying |
| Build a cushion of several months of expenses | Protects you from taking on new debt after the next car repair |
| Add to retirement accounts, up to the limits | Tax-advantaged growth that is hard to replace later |
| Fund a clear goal: a down payment, education, a business | Do this after a pause, with a written plan |
| Give gifts or help family, within what you decided | A set amount keeps generosity from becoming a drain |
| Invest the rest and leave it alone | Boring, diversified and patient |

Paying off a low-rate mortgage is a judgment call. Some people sleep better without the loan. Others do better keeping a low rate and investing the difference. It is a values question as much as a math one.

Jorge, 44, inherited about enough to cover a year of his salary. He paid off a car loan and a card, set aside six months of expenses, topped up his 401(k) and left the rest in a savings account for ninety days. At the end, he had a plan and had not been asked for a loan by anyone, because he had not told the family the number.

## Will an inheritance affect benefits, a divorce or a lawsuit?

It can. This is the part people miss.

**Means-tested benefits.** SSI and most Medicaid programs have asset limits, and a lump sum can end eligibility the month it arrives. A special needs trust or an ABLE account can protect the benefit. Read [how an inheritance affects Medicaid or SSI](/learn/after-a-death/does-an-inheritance-affect-medicaid-or-ssi) before you deposit the money, and report it as the program requires.

**Marriage.** In most states an inheritance stays your separate property, until you mix it with marital money. A joint account or a jointly titled house changes the picture. See [whether an inheritance is marital property](/learn/after-a-death/is-an-inheritance-marital-property).

**Creditors and court judgments.** Once the cash is yours, a judgment creditor may reach it. A bankruptcy filing shortly before or after receipt can pull it into the case.

**Disclaiming.** If you do not want the money, or want it to go to your children instead, you may be able to refuse it in writing within a short period, before you accept any of it. Depositing the check usually counts as acceptance. [How to disclaim an inheritance](/learn/after-a-death/how-to-disclaim-an-inheritance) shows the steps.

## Who should you talk to about it?

For a modest sum, your own bank and a free hour with a tax preparer may be all you need. For a larger one, a fee-only financial planner, meaning someone paid by you and not by commissions on products, is worth interviewing. Ask how they are paid before you ask anything else. An accountant helps if the money came with a business, rental or retirement account attached. A lawyer matters if there is a dispute, a benefit at risk, or a trust involved. Be wary of anyone who arrives within days with an annuity or an urgent offer, since inheritances attract salespeople.

## What mistakes do people make?

A few come up again and again.

- **Lending to relatives.** Treat any loan as a gift you will not see again, and decide in advance how much you can lose.
- **Spending before the estate closes.** If a late creditor or tax bill appears, the executor may ask for some back. Read [how long it takes to receive an inheritance](/learn/after-a-death/how-long-does-it-take-to-receive-an-inheritance) to see why the first payment is often not the last.
- **Paying someone's "fee" to release the money.** A real executor or lawyer does not ask you to pay in order to receive an inheritance. Scams that claim a distant relative left you money often start with that request.
- **Putting it in a joint account.** Convenient, and a common way to lose separate-property status.
- **Making one big purchase in the first month.** A vehicle or renovation made in grief tends to be regretted.

The goal is not to be perfect. It is to avoid the few errors that cannot be fixed.
