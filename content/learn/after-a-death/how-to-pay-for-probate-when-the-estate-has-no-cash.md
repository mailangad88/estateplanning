Nadia's father died in October, owning a house, a ten-year-old car and a checking account with a few thousand dollars. She was named executor. Then the practical problems arrived: a court filing fee, a retainer for the lawyer, a notice to run in the newspaper, homeowner's insurance on an empty house, and a utility bill. The checking account is frozen until the court appoints her. She asks the obvious question: who pays for probate when the estate has no cash?

You do, at first, and then you get it back. This page explains how to pay for probate when the estate has no cash, who is repaid, and what to do to keep the costs small. For the general cost picture, see our guide to [probate costs](/learn/probate/probate-costs). It belongs to our [after-a-death guide](/learn/after-a-death).

## Who pays the first bills when the estate has no money?

The person who starts the process, usually the executor or the heir who asks for appointment. The estate cannot pay for anything until the court has appointed someone and that person has opened an estate bank account. The first costs fall before that:

- Court filing fees.
- Certified death certificates.
- A lawyer's retainer, if you hire one.
- Notice to heirs and creditors, including newspaper publication where required.
- Insurance on the property.
- Utilities and basic upkeep for a vacant house.

The executor can pay these personally and later be reimbursed. The key is the paper trail. Our page on [who pays the executor's expenses](/learn/after-a-death/who-pays-the-executors-expenses) explains what is reimbursable and what is not. Keep receipts and write down the date and purpose of every payment.

## What are the ways to raise cash for the estate?

There are five common sources, in the order people usually try them.

1. **Skip court for the cash you can reach.** If the personal property is small and no real estate is involved, a small estate affidavit may release the bank account without a case. In Illinois the cap is $150,000 of personal property excluding registered vehicles. See our [small estate affidavit](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit) guide. A house takes this route off the table, as it did for Nadia.
2. **Open the estate account as soon as you have letters.** Letters testamentary or of administration prove your authority. Then deposit what you can reach. Our page on [opening an estate bank account](/learn/probate/how-to-open-an-estate-bank-account) covers the EIN and bank steps.
3. **Collect what is owed to the estate.** Final paychecks, refunds and a security deposit are all cash. See [what happens to a deceased person's last paycheck and refunds](/learn/after-a-death/what-happens-to-a-deceased-persons-last-paycheck-refunds-and-money-owed).
4. **Sell personal property.** A car, a boat or a collection can be sold by the executor once the court has appointed one. Our [vehicle guide](/learn/property-and-assets/transfer-a-car-boat-or-rv-after-death) shows the paperwork.
5. **Sell the house.** In many estates this is the real source of cash. The sale closes, the mortgage is paid from the proceeds, and what is left flows into the estate account. Our pages on [selling a house in probate in Illinois](/learn/illinois/selling-a-house-in-probate-in-illinois) and [selling an estate house in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/selling-an-estate-house-in-new-jersey) describe the approval steps.

Life insurance and accounts with a named beneficiary are not estate cash. They go straight to the named person. A beneficiary may choose to help with costs, but the executor cannot demand it.

## How do I get repaid after paying out of pocket?

By treating your payments as an administration expense and listing them when you account. The estate account reimburses you when it has funds, and the law ranks these expenses ahead of most debts and well ahead of heirs. The reimbursement is not your executor fee. It is a return of money you spent on the estate.

Two points keep this clean:

- **Pay only proper estate costs.** Court fees, publication, insurance, utilities and repairs needed to protect the house qualify. The funeral may qualify too. Our page on [who pays for the funeral](/learn/after-a-death/who-pays-for-the-funeral) explains the limits. Your own travel or a gift to a relative does not.
- **Do not mix money.** Never deposit estate money in your personal account. If you advance cash, write the amount and date, and ask the estate account to repay you by check.

## Can I ask the lawyer to wait for the fee?

Yes, and many probate lawyers do when the estate is mostly property. The fee is then paid from the sale proceeds or at closing, instead of as a retainer. Ask for this at the first meeting and get it in a written fee agreement. Our guide on [probate lawyer fee agreements](/learn/probate/probate-lawyer-fee-agreements) shows the terms to look for. In Illinois, a lawyer's fees are generally subject to court review in a supervised case, and our page on [Illinois probate costs](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-costs) gives the details.

You can also reduce the bill. Handle the paperwork you can do yourself, such as gathering statements and sorting the personal property, and ask the lawyer to take only the parts that need a license. The [probate cost estimator](/tools/probate-cost-estimator) gives a sense of the range for your estate before you call.

## A worked example

This is an invented example for illustration. Nadia's estate: a house worth $280,000 with a $90,000 mortgage, a car worth $6,000, and $3,100 in a frozen checking account.

| Step | Who pays | Source of repayment |
|---|---|---|
| Filing fee, certified copies, publication | Nadia, personally | Estate account, later |
| Lawyer's retainer | Deferred by agreement | Paid at closing of the house sale |
| Insurance and utilities on the house | Nadia, personally | Estate account, later |
| Sale of the car | Estate, after appointment | Cash into the estate account |
| Sale of the house | Estate, after appointment | Pays off the mortgage, then funds the estate |

After the sale, the estate account holds roughly the proceeds above the mortgage, less costs. Nadia gets her advances back first. The remainder goes to the heirs under the will or, if there is none, under state law.

## What if the estate owes more than it owns?

Then do not keep paying from your own pocket. An insolvent estate pays debts in a legal order, and the executor is not personally liable for the dead person's debts if the work is done properly. Our page on [what happens when an estate owes more than it has](/learn/probate/what-happens-when-an-estate-owes-more-than-it-has) explains the order. Keep your own advances to what you need to protect the property, and talk to an attorney before spending more.

## What mistakes cost executors money?

- Paying the funeral home or heirs before knowing whether the estate can cover costs.
- Leaving a vacant house uninsured. Check our guide on [vacant inherited houses](/learn/property-and-assets/vacant-inherited-house-insurance-repairs-squatters).
- Letting the mortgage go unpaid for months while the case sits.
- Spending estate money without a receipt.
- Treating an advance as a gift to the estate, which means no repayment.
