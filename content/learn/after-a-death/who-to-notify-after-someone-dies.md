In the first week after a death, you get told to notify everybody. Nobody tells you who "everybody" is, which ones can wait, or what to put in the envelope. People end up phoning the same bank three times and still miss the one lender that keeps billing the estate.

This page gives you an order of operations. It covers banks, credit bureaus, card issuers, the IRS, the DMV and the post office, and says what each one actually wants. For the wider list of first-month tasks, start with the [after-a-death guide](/learn/after-a-death) and the [first-steps checklist](/learn/after-a-death/what-to-do-when-someone-dies-checklist).

## Who should you notify first?

Notify the institutions that can lose money or enable fraud before the ones that only send paperwork. Three groups matter in the first few days.

1. **The funeral home.** They file the death record. Most also report the death to Social Security, which stops the benefit. Our page on [Social Security after a death](/learn/after-a-death/social-security-after-a-death) covers the payment that may have to be returned.
2. **The three credit bureaus.** Identity thieves read obituaries. A deceased flag on the credit file makes it hard to open new accounts in the person's name.
3. **Banks, brokerages and card issuers where you know there are accounts.** Not to close them, but to stop fraud and learn what is there.

Everything else follows, roughly in the order below.

| Who | What to send | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Funeral home | Information for the death record | Immediately |
| Equifax, Experian, TransUnion | Letter, death certificate copy, proof you are spouse or executor | First week |
| Banks and brokerages | Certified death certificate, later letters from the court | First two weeks |
| Credit card issuers | Phone call, then written notice | First two weeks |
| Employer, pension, union | Death certificate and claim forms | First month |
| Post office | Request to forward mail | First week |
| DMV | Title paperwork when a vehicle moves | When you handle the car |
| IRS and state tax office | Final returns, Form 56 if needed | By the return due date |
| Insurance companies | Claim forms and certified copies | As soon as you know the policy |

## How do you notify the credit bureaus?

Write to all three. They do not share a single intake, and a note on one file does not reach the others. Each bureau lists a mailing address and an online process for deceased consumers on its site.

Send the full name, Social Security number, date of birth, date of death, last address and a copy of the death certificate. Add proof of who you are, such as a marriage certificate or your letters from the court. Ask for two things: a **deceased alert** on the file and a **copy of the report**.

The report is the part people skip. It is also the most useful. It lists every open credit card, auto loan and mortgage the person had, including accounts the family never heard of. Plenty of estates learn about a second credit card or a store account only from this report.

## How do you handle banks and credit card companies?

Call the bank, tell them the account holder died, and ask what they need. Do not expect to withdraw money on that call. A bank will freeze a sole-owner account once it learns of the death, and it will release the funds only to a person with legal authority. The [guide to what happens to a bank account after death](/learn/probate/what-happens-to-a-deceased-persons-bank-account) explains who gets the money in each ownership setup.

Two quick rules apply. If you are a joint owner, the account usually continues in your name, but you still send the death certificate so the bank updates its records. If the account has a payable-on-death beneficiary, that person claims it directly with a death certificate and ID, with no court involved.

Credit cards deserve more care than most people give them.

- **Do not use the card.** Charges made after death can look like fraud, and the estate may have to repay them.
- **Ask for the balance and the recurring charges.** Mobile phone, streaming, gym and insurance premiums often bill to a card.
- **Check who else is on the account.** An authorized user is not responsible. A joint holder or cosigner is.
- **Do not agree to pay a debt over the phone.** Collectors can talk to the executor, but a family member who is not the executor generally owes nothing from their own pocket for a relative's solo debt. A community property state can change that for a spouse.

Debts come out of the estate before heirs get paid. Our guide to [dealing with debts in probate](/learn/probate/dealing-with-debts-in-probate) covers how the order works.

## Do you have to notify the IRS?

No call or letter reports the death by itself. What you do is file the returns. The executor files the final federal income tax return for the year of death, writes "deceased" with the date across the top, and files a state return where required. A surviving spouse can usually file jointly for the year of death.

Form 56, Notice Concerning Fiduciary Relationship, is a short form that tells the IRS who is acting for the estate. It is worth filing when the estate will have its own tax filings or when you want the IRS to send mail about the person to you. Our page on [final tax returns](/learn/after-a-death/final-tax-returns) walks through the return, and the estate's tax ID is covered in [the trust EIN and 65-day election](/learn/after-a-death/trust-ein-and-65-day-election) article if there is a living trust.

If the person never filed for earlier years, tell the preparer. Back returns can be filed, and the sooner they are, the smaller the problem.

## What about the DMV, the post office and the phone company?

These are the tasks that feel small and then drag on.

**The DMV.** Most states do not demand a report of death, but you will deal with them when a car is sold, retitled or kept. Find out whether the car has a loan, who is on the title, and whether the state allows a transfer by affidavit for small estates. Our guide to [transferring a car, boat or RV after death](/learn/property-and-assets/transfer-a-car-boat-or-rv-after-death) covers that process. Keep the person's driver's license in a safe place or cancel it so it cannot be used for fraud.

**The post office.** Ask for forwarding of the person's mail to the executor. Late statements, a 1099 from a brokerage and a notice from a state agency all arrive this way. If the executor has not yet been appointed, a family member can usually start forwarding with the death certificate and ID. Plan to keep it on for a year.

**Phone, internet and utilities.** Keep the utilities running if the house is occupied or being sold. The phone and email may hold the codes for banking, so keep them active until the accounts are sorted. Our [online accounts checklist](/learn/digital-assets/online-accounts-after-death-checklist) explains how to approach email, cloud storage and subscriptions.

## What proof will everyone ask for?

Expect the same three documents over and over.

1. **A certified death certificate.** Order enough. Our guide to [how many death certificates you need](/learn/after-a-death/how-many-death-certificates-do-i-need) gives a count.
2. **Proof you can act.** For a joint owner, that is ID. For an executor, it is letters from the court, or a certification of trust for a successor trustee.
3. **Identifying details.** Account numbers, the Social Security number and the last four digits of cards.

Arthur, 61, handled his mother's affairs after she died in March. He called her bank and her one credit card, and assumed that was that. In June a collection letter arrived for a store card. A credit report would have shown it in the first week. He ended up requesting the report, finding two more accounts, and writing to the bureaus a second time. A single list kept from day one would have saved him a month.

## How should you keep track of it all?

Keep one log in a notebook or spreadsheet. For each call or letter, write the date, who you spoke with, what you sent and any reference number. Send important notices by tracked mail, and keep a copy.

An executor who keeps that kind of record is in a far better position when it is time to account to the heirs.
