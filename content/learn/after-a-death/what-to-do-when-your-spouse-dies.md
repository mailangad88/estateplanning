Raymond, 71, lost his wife Carol on a Sunday morning in Elgin. By Tuesday he had a stack of paper from the funeral home, a phone full of condolences and a growing worry that he was supposed to be doing something about the bank. He was not, yet. Most of what feels urgent is not.

This page lists what a surviving spouse needs to do in the first month, in an order that makes sense, and what can wait. It is part of our [after-a-death guide](/learn/after-a-death) and a short version of our broader [guide to what to do when someone dies](/learn/after-a-death/what-to-do-when-someone-dies-checklist), written for a spouse who is probably also the executor. Illinois and New Jersey details are included.

## What do I do in the first few days?

1. **Get certified death certificates.** Funeral homes usually order them. Raymond ordered 12, because banks, insurers and retirement plans each keep one. See [how many death certificates you need](/learn/after-a-death/how-many-death-certificates-do-i-need).
2. **Look for a will, trust and recent documents.** Check the home files and ask Carol's attorney. If a will exists, whoever holds the original should file it with the court clerk quickly; in Illinois, hiding a will for 30 days after learning of the death is a felony (755 ILCS 5/6-1).
3. **Ask the funeral home to report the death to Social Security,** and confirm it yourself. See [Social Security after a death](/learn/after-a-death/social-security-after-a-death).
4. **Notify close institutions:** her employer or pension, your bank, the life insurance agent, the health insurer. The [who to notify list](/learn/after-a-death/who-to-notify-after-someone-dies) gives the order.
5. **Secure the house and paperwork.** Check that mail is collected, and that any valuables are locked.
6. **Delay the big decisions.** Do not sell, give away money or sign a contract with someone who calls offering to help.

## Which assets pass to me automatically?

Whether anything must go through court depends on how each asset is titled. This is Raymond's list, with example values.

| Asset | Title or form | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| House, $380,000 | Both names, joint tenants or entirety | Passes to Raymond. Record the death certificate with the county |
| Joint checking, $18,000 | Both names | Passes to Raymond; bank retitles |
| Carol's 403(b), $210,000 | Raymond is beneficiary | Raymond claims it; he can roll it into his own IRA |
| Carol's life insurance, $100,000 | Raymond is beneficiary | Claim form and death certificate |
| Carol's savings, $24,000 | Carol only, no beneficiary | Needs her will and probate, or a small estate affidavit |
| Carol's car | Carol only | Retitle through the state; see [transferring a car after death](/learn/property-and-assets/transfer-a-car-boat-or-rv-after-death) |

Only the last two rows involve a court at all, and even those may qualify for a shortcut. Our [small estate checker](/tools/small-estate-checker) and the [Illinois](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) and [New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-small-estate-affidavit) affidavit pages show when. If you want to sort your own list the same way, use the [probate asset sorter](/tools/probate-asset-sorter).

New Jersey adds one useful point. A surviving spouse is in the lowest-tax class for the state inheritance tax, so assets passing to a spouse are not taxed there. See [New Jersey inheritance tax](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-inheritance-tax).

## How do I claim the money that comes to me?

Ask each institution for its "beneficiary claim" or "death claim" packet. You will usually need the certified death certificate, a claim form and your identification. Retirement plans may take weeks. Life insurance usually pays within a few weeks after a complete claim, but delay is common when the policy is old. Our [claiming an account as a beneficiary](/learn/beneficiary-designations/how-to-claim-an-account-as-a-beneficiary) page walks through the forms. For pensions and veterans benefits, see [claiming life insurance, pension and VA survivor benefits](/learn/after-a-death/claiming-life-insurance-pension-and-va-survivor-benefits).

Before you roll a retirement account into your own name, check your age. A spouse under 59 and a half who rolls everything into their own IRA and then needs the money may owe a penalty on withdrawals, while keeping it as an inherited IRA may avoid that. This is the one place a quick decision can cost money. Read [spousal rollover vs. inherited IRA](/learn/beneficiary-designations/spousal-rollover-vs-inherited-ira) before you sign.

## What should I know about Social Security?

Survivor benefits are not automatic. A widow or widower can usually claim as early as age 60, and a one-time $255 payment is available to a qualifying spouse. You apply by phone or in person. If you are also receiving your own retirement benefit, Social Security pays the higher of the two, not both. The timing of when you claim can change the amount, so ask before you file.

## What about the house?

Update the title. Record the death certificate with the county office, or ask a title company or attorney to prepare the paperwork, so the record shows you as the sole owner. Keep paying the mortgage. The loan does not become due just because a spouse died, and federal law protects a surviving spouse who inherits a home from an immediate call on the loan. See [mortgage when the homeowner dies](/learn/property-and-assets/mortgage-when-the-homeowner-dies).

In Illinois, a surviving spouse is also entitled to a spouse's award of at least $20,000 from the estate for support for nine months (755 ILCS 5/15-1), payable ahead of most other claims if there is a probate estate. See [Illinois spouse rights](/learn/illinois/illinois-spouse-rights-at-death).

Hold off on selling for three to six months. People sell in grief and regret it. If the money is the worry, talk to an attorney and a tax advisor before deciding.

## What about taxes?

A surviving spouse can generally file a joint federal return for the year of death. The return reports your spouse's income through the date of death. You may qualify for joint rates for two more years if you have a dependent child, under a status for a qualifying surviving spouse. Property you inherit usually gets a new tax basis at the date of death, though for jointly owned property in a state without community property only the deceased spouse's half is stepped up. Our pages on [final tax returns](/learn/after-a-death/final-tax-returns) and [step-up in basis for married couples](/learn/estate-tax/step-up-in-basis-for-married-couples-and-joint-property) go further.

## What about health insurance and credit?

If you were on your spouse's employer health plan, ask the plan administrator about COBRA continuation right away, because the election window runs from the notice. If you are 65 or older, you already have Medicare. Remove your spouse's name from joint credit cards and ask for cards in your own name, using your own credit history. Credit cards that were joint remain your obligation; cards held only by your spouse do not.

## When do I update my own documents?

Within a few months. Raymond's will names Carol as executor and sole heir, his power of attorney names Carol, and his beneficiary forms say "Carol." Every one of those needs a new name. See [estate planning after a spouse dies](/learn/life-stages/estate-planning-after-spouse-dies) and [what happens if a spouse dies and the plan is not updated](/learn/what-if/spouse-died-plan-not-updated).

## How state law changes this

States differ in how they treat jointly held property, whether a small estate can skip court, how the surviving spouse's allowance works, and whether community property rules apply. Illinois and New Jersey are not community property states. Start with your state guide, [Illinois](/estate-planning/illinois) or [New Jersey](/estate-planning/new-jersey), and ask about local forms.
