People often put off a power of attorney because they assume it is complicated or expensive. It is neither, compared with the alternative. The question is how much you want to spend on getting it right.

This article, part of our [power of attorney guide](/learn/power-of-attorney), walks through the realistic options, what moves the price, what a flat fee should include, and what happens financially if nobody signs. We do not quote exact prices here, since they differ by state and provider, and we avoid inventing averages. Our firm's own packages are described with an attorney at the consultation.

## How much does a power of attorney cost?

There are four common routes. The ranges below are descriptive, not a survey, so confirm current numbers with whoever you use.

| Route | What you pay | What you get | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| State-provided or library form | Often free, plus a notary fee | A basic statutory form | Wrong fit for your situation |
| Online document service | Usually a modest fee or subscription | Guided form, sometimes with a knowledge base | No one reviews the choices |
| Attorney, stand-alone | A flat fee, typically modest | Custom document and a signing | Higher than a form |
| Attorney, as part of an estate plan | Bundled in a flat fee | Financial and healthcare documents with a will or trust | Cost of the whole plan |

Our overview of the [cost of an estate plan](/learn/basics/how-much-does-estate-planning-cost) covers flat fees and hourly rates in more detail, and the pieces on [the cost of a will](/learn/wills/how-much-does-a-will-cost) and [a living trust](/learn/trusts/how-much-does-a-living-trust-cost) show where a power of attorney fits.

## What drives the price?

Several things push the price up or down.

1. **Complexity of the powers.** A simple "handle my bills" document costs less than one with gifting limits, real estate authority, business interests and tax powers.
2. **Number of agents and rules.** Co-agents, successor agents, a monitor and reporting requirements take longer to draft. See [choosing your agent](/learn/power-of-attorney/choosing-a-power-of-attorney-agent).
3. **Healthcare documents.** Adding a healthcare power of attorney, living will and HIPAA release is normally priced as a package.
4. **Your state.** Some states publish a statutory form with fixed language. Others rely on custom drafting.
5. **Timing.** A hospital bedside signing, a travel visit to the principal or a rush may add cost. A planned signing is cheapest.
6. **Attorney versus self-service.** A review by a lawyer costs more than a template, and it also reduces the chance of a refusal.

## What does a flat fee usually include?

A good flat-fee quote lists the work. For a power of attorney that usually means:

- A conversation about who should serve, what powers to grant and what to limit
- A durable financial power of attorney drafted to your state's law
- Often a healthcare power of attorney and HIPAA authorization
- A supervised signing with witnesses and a notary
- Copies and instructions on where to keep the original

What typically costs extra: real estate deeds, a trust, a plan for a business, tax advice, and court filings. If your situation involves a parent who cannot sign, the work changes from drafting to court, and fees change with it.

## What is the cost of not having one?

The cost shows up in a different form: court. If a person loses capacity with no power of attorney, the family usually petitions for a guardianship or conservatorship. That means court filing fees, attorney fees, a medical evaluation, notice to relatives and often ongoing reports and a bond. The process takes weeks or months, and the court, not the family, chooses the guardian. The details are in [power of attorney vs. guardianship](/learn/power-of-attorney/power-of-attorney-vs-guardianship) and the what-if page on [being incapacitated without a power of attorney](/learn/what-if/incapacitated-without-power-of-attorney).

Consider two siblings. Mark, 66, pays a flat fee and signs a durable power of attorney and healthcare directive. His brother Dale, 64, skips it. When Dale has a stroke, his wife must file a court petition just to pay the mortgage from his individual account, and a judge decides who has authority. The two outcomes do not compare.

A second cost is the bad form. A document that a bank rejects, that lacks durability language or that grants too little authority can force the same court case. Cheap is fine when the document works. ## What questions should you ask about fees?

Before you hire anyone, ask:

1. Is the price a flat fee, and what exactly is included?
2. Does it include a healthcare power of attorney and a HIPAA authorization?
3. Does it include the signing ceremony and the notary?
4. What happens if I move to another state?
5. Will you update the document if I ask within a set period?
6. Who will be the attorney I meet?
7. What is not included, and what would it cost?

Ask for the answers in writing. A flat fee that does not list what it covers is not much of a flat fee. Our guide to [choosing an estate planning attorney](/learn/basics/how-to-choose-an-estate-planning-attorney) offers more questions.

## When is a cheap option good enough?

A free form can be a reasonable choice for a young, healthy adult with few assets who wants something in place this week, and who will revisit it. A single, short-term errand, such as signing papers abroad, may only need a limited document. The cheaper route is weaker when you own a home, run a business, have a blended family, or are worried about a particular family member, because those are the situations where wording matters.

## Does a cheap power of attorney need updating more often?

Often, yes, but not because it is cheap. Any power of attorney benefits from a refresh every several years, after a move, a divorce, the death of an agent or a change in your health or finances. A form completed at home with no attorney review may also be missing a successor agent or durability language, and those gaps tend to show up only when someone needs the document. Budget a small amount for periodic review rather than treating the first signing as the last. Our article on [when to update your estate plan](/learn/basics/when-to-update-your-estate-plan) lists the triggers.

## How state law changes this

Notary fees are regulated by each state. Some states publish an official form that is free to use, and some require witnesses in addition to a notary, which adds a small cost. Recording fees apply only if the document is filed with a county. Your state guide, such as [Texas](/estate-planning/texas) or [California](/estate-planning/california), can tell you what is standard. Our article on [notarization and witnesses](/learn/power-of-attorney/does-a-power-of-attorney-need-to-be-notarized) lists the typical requirements.
