When parents think about "who will take care of the kids," they often picture one person doing everything. The law pictures two jobs. One is raising a child. The other is looking after the child's property. Putting those jobs in the same hands is allowed, but it is not always the wisest choice.

This guide explains each role, how they cooperate, when to split them, and what to put in writing so the people you name are not left guessing.

## What does a guardian do, and what does a trustee do?

A [guardian](/glossary/guardian) makes personal decisions. A [trustee](/glossary/trustee) makes financial decisions under a written trust.

| | Guardian | Trustee |
|---|---|---|
| Main role | Raises the child | Manages the child's inheritance |
| Decisions | Housing, school, medical care, faith, activities | Investments, payments, taxes, record keeping |
| Named in | Will (nomination, then court appoints) | Trust document (living trust or will-based trust) |
| Oversight | Court, in many states | Terms of the trust, beneficiaries, sometimes court |
| Ends | When the child turns 18 (or earlier by court order) | When the trust ends, often at ages the parents choose |
| Standard | Best interests of the child | [Fiduciary duty](/glossary/fiduciary) to follow the trust and act prudently |

The guardian's authority stops at the child's 18th birthday. The trustee's authority can continue much longer, often into the child's twenties or thirties, because parents commonly delay full control of an inheritance.

## Why do many parents split the roles?

Splitting the roles gives you two people, each selected for what they are good at, and it adds a check on the money.

- Skills differ. A warm, steady aunt may be the best person to raise your children and be hopeless with spreadsheets.
- Conflict of interest. A guardian who also controls the money may be tempted to spend trust funds on the household, or face pressure from their own family. A separate trustee avoids that.
- Accountability. A trustee has to keep records and follow the trust. A guardian who must ask for funds has a reason to document needs.
- Longevity. A guardian's role ends at 18, but money may need oversight until 25 or 30.

None of this means you distrust the guardian. It is a structure that protects everyone, including the guardian, who will not be accused of mismanaging the kids' money.

### When might one person do both?

If your estate is modest, the relative you trust most is stable, and the cost of a separate trustee seems out of proportion, naming one person can be reasonable. In that case add protections: require annual accountings to another family member, name a different successor trustee, or let a trust protector replace the trustee if needed. Without a trust at all, a court may require a bond and annual reports for the child's money, which is the situation we describe in [leaving money to minors](/learn/guardianship/leaving-money-to-minors).

## How do the two work together day to day?

The trust should say how the guardian requests money and what the trustee must pay for. A typical arrangement:

1. The guardian sends receipts or estimates for housing, school, health care and activities.
2. The trustee pays the provider directly or reimburses the guardian.
3. The trustee sends the guardian a yearly summary and keeps copies of all records.
4. Large requests, such as a larger home, a car, or private school tuition, require discussion and, if the trust says so, written approval.

Clear standards make this work. The common standard is "health, education, maintenance and support," a long-used phrase that covers the real costs of raising a child. Some parents add extras they care about, such as music lessons, travel to see grandparents, or college.

### Can the trust help with the guardian's housing?

Often, yes, if you say so. A guardian who takes in your children may need a bigger home or more bedrooms. Your trust can allow the trustee to contribute toward a home purchase or addition, usually with the trust retaining an interest or the guardian holding title subject to conditions. These are decisions to discuss with an attorney because they affect taxes and risk.

### Example: Rosa and her brothers

Rosa, a widow with two children aged 9 and 12, names her sister Lucia as guardian and her brother Daniel, a retired bank manager, as trustee. Lucia lives in a two-bedroom apartment, so Rosa's trust allows Daniel to help pay for a larger rental or a modest home purchase. Each year Daniel sends Lucia and a second brother a one-page summary of what the trust spent. Neither sibling controls everything, and both know the rules because Rosa explained them in a letter. If Daniel can no longer serve, a named successor, Rosa's cousin, takes over without a court filing.

## What kinds of trusts hold the money?

There are two basic setups.

- A [living trust](/glossary/living-trust) (also called a revocable trust) that you create now and fund with assets, naming a successor trustee for your children's share. See [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust).
- A [testamentary trust](/glossary/testamentary-trust) written inside your will that springs to life only on your death. It is simpler to set up but goes through [probate](/learn/probate/what-is-probate), so it is often a first step before upgrading to a living trust.

Life insurance can fund either. The policy names the trust as beneficiary so that proceeds are managed under your rules instead of paid straight to a minor. Our guide to [trusts for children](/learn/trusts/trusts-for-children) covers staggered distributions and when each child gets control.

## How do you choose a trustee?

Choose someone who is honest, organized, willing to keep records, and able to say no gently. Financial sophistication helps but is not required, because a trustee can hire an accountant or advisor. Our page on [choosing a trustee](/learn/trusts/choosing-a-trustee) walks through individuals, co-trustees and corporate trustees.

Questions to ask:

- Will this person handle money fairly and calmly under emotional pressure?
- Do they have the time to file tax returns and keep records?
- Do they get along with the guardian?
- What happens if they die, become ill, or move away?

Always name at least one [successor trustee](/glossary/successor-trustee).

## What should you write down for the guardian and trustee?

Give both people a short letter, separate from the legal documents, covering:

- Your values and what you hope for each child
- Who the pediatrician, school, and other key adults are
- Which expenses you consider high priority
- How you expect them to communicate and resolve disagreement
- Where documents and accounts are located

A letter is not binding, but it makes decisions easier and gives both people a voice of yours to rely on.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Leaving money outright to a minor, which forces a court-supervised account
- Naming two people as co-trustees who do not get along
- Forgetting to fund the trust or name it as insurance beneficiary
- Not telling the trustee they were named
- Giving the guardian no way to access funds in an emergency
