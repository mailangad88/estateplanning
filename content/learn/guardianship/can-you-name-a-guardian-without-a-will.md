You have a living trust, or a long email to your sister, or a note in the kitchen drawer that says who should raise your kids. It feels like enough. For the question of who becomes the guardian, it usually is not. A judge looks for a specific kind of document, and most documents parents write do not qualify.

This page sorts the common ways parents try to name a guardian into what works and what does not in Illinois and New Jersey, and gives a quick fix if you have already set something up. The full picture of the topic is in our [guardianship guide](/learn/guardianship).

## Do you need a will to name a guardian for your child?

Yes, for all practical purposes. A will is the document judges look for. In New Jersey, a parent appoints a guardian of the person and of the estate for a child by will (N.J.S.A. 3B:12-13). In Illinois, the statute allows a parent's written designation with at least two credible witnesses (755 ILCS 5/11-5), which a will satisfies, and a stand-alone writing may satisfy if it is executed correctly. The word to notice is "correctly." Illinois courts and clerks work with wills every day and rarely see a stand-alone designation.

Remember what the nomination does. Only a court appoints the guardian, and a will nominates. Our article on [whether a guardian named in a will holds up in court](/learn/guardianship/does-a-guardian-named-in-a-will-hold-up-in-court) explains how much weight a judge gives it.

## Which documents work and which do not?

| Where you put the name | Does it appoint a guardian? | What it is good for |
|---|---|---|
| Signed, witnessed will | Yes, as a nomination | The standard route |
| Pour-over will with a living trust | Yes, as a nomination | Parents who already have a trust |
| Living trust alone | No | Naming who manages the child's money |
| Letter of wishes or instruction | No, but it persuades | Telling the guardian how to raise the child |
| Email, text, video, voice note | No | Evidence of your wishes |
| Beneficiary form or school emergency card | No | Short-term pickup and medical authority |
| Handwritten note | Only if it meets a will's rules | Usually nothing in Illinois |
| Short-term or standby designation | Limited and temporary | A gap while you are alive |

The last row matters because parents confuse it with a long-term nomination. Illinois lets a parent sign a short-term guardian paper, and our article on [Illinois standby and short-term guardians](/learn/illinois/illinois-standby-and-short-term-guardians-for-children) explains how long it lasts. [Temporary and standby guardians](/learn/guardianship/temporary-and-standby-guardians) covers the idea nationally. These fill a hospital stay or a trip. They do not name who raises your child after you die.

## Can I name a guardian in a living trust?

You can write it down, but it does not carry the weight you want. A living trust is a private contract between you and your trustee. It normally never goes to court, and a judge deciding on guardianship looks for a will. The trust is the right place to say who manages money for your children and when they receive it. It is the wrong place to put the one clause a court needs to find.

Tamar and Josh, both 36, have a living trust that says, in its "family wishes" article, that Tamar's brother should raise their two kids. Nobody signed a will. When both die, the trust names the trustee and the money is fine, but the court has no nomination. Tamar's brother has to petition like any relative, and Josh's parents petition too. The trust language might be shown to the judge as evidence of what the parents wanted, which helps, but he starts without the standing a will would have given him.

The fix is a pour-over will. It is a short will that names the guardian and backup, and sends any stray property into the trust. See [pour-over wills](/learn/wills/pour-over-will).

## Is a handwritten guardian nomination valid?

It depends on the state and on how it is written, and the risk is high. New Jersey accepts a will if the signature and the key terms are in the person's own handwriting, even without witnesses, but a court can then require proof the handwriting is yours. Illinois requires the usual two witnesses for a will and does not accept an unwitnessed handwritten one. A handwritten page that says "if we die, Maria gets the kids" and is signed by one parent might be a will in New Jersey and nothing in Illinois. Our guide to [handwritten and DIY wills](/learn/wills/handwritten-and-diy-wills) lists the traps.

A sensible use of a handwritten note: attach it to your real, signed will as a [letter for your guardian](/learn/guardianship/letter-of-intent-for-your-childrens-guardian), covering routines, schools, medical needs and values. It is not the nomination. It is the owner's manual.

## How do I fix it if I have no will today?

A guardian clause takes one document and one signing. Work through these steps.

1. **Choose a first guardian and a backup.** Use the [guardian picker](/tools/guardian-picker) if you are stuck, and ask each person.
2. **Decide who manages money.** The guardian and the trustee can be different people. See [guardian versus trustee](/learn/guardianship/guardian-vs-trustee).
3. **Sign a will that names both.** Two adult witnesses who are not beneficiaries should sign with you. Ask for a self-proving affidavit so your witnesses do not need to testify later.
4. **Match the other parent.** Both wills should name the same people in the same order.
5. **Store it and tell people.** Keep the original where your guardian can find it, and give them a copy.

## What mistakes do parents make when naming a guardian?

Our attorneys see the same four over and over.

- **Naming a couple, not a person.** "My brother and his wife" breaks the day they divorce. Name each by full name and say what happens if they separate.
- **Naming no backup.** The first choice is the one most likely to be unavailable by the time it matters, because children grow up slowly and people move, fall ill or change their minds.
- **Choosing by guilt.** Parents name the grandparents because refusing would hurt feelings, even when the grandparents are 74. Our article on [grandparents as guardians](/learn/guardianship/can-grandparents-be-named-guardian) covers when it works.
- **Never telling the guardian.** A person who learns about the job at the funeral may say no.

## Does a nomination work if I already named someone in an old will?

It works if the will is signed, witnessed and still describes the right people. Wills signed before a child was born often name the wrong guardian, or no one. Marriage, divorce, a move or a falling-out with the person named are all reasons to redo it. Check the date. Anything older than your youngest child deserves a second look, and the [wills guide](/learn/wills) explains how to update without starting from scratch.
