Someone filed to become guardian of your mother. Or a court appointed a guardian for you, and you think it is wrong. Or you are the sibling who was not asked. In each case the question is the same: can this be fought, and how?

It can, at several points. This page covers when and how to contest an adult guardianship in Illinois and New Jersey, what the court looks at, and what to do if the guardian is the problem. It does not cover guardianship of a minor child, which has its own rules; see [how to petition for guardianship of a minor](/learn/guardianship/how-to-petition-for-guardianship-of-a-minor-child). The wider topic sits in our [guardianship guide](/learn/guardianship).

## Who can contest a guardianship?

Three groups can speak up, and they want different things.

| Who | What they can contest | Typical goal |
|---|---|---|
| The adult named in the petition | Whether a guardian is needed at all, and how much power | Keep their rights, or limit the order |
| Relatives and others entitled to notice | The need for a guardian, or who is chosen | A different guardian, or a smaller role |
| The guardian or any interested person | Misuse by the guardian | Accounting, restriction, removal |

The adult is the central party. Both states build in procedural protection for that person. A relative who disagrees with the choice of guardian has a voice, but the question the court decides first is whether the adult needs a guardian.

## How do you contest a guardianship before it is approved?

You act in the case, within the notice period. After the petition is filed the adult must be served, and relatives are notified.

In Illinois, the person receives the petition and summons at least 14 days before the hearing, and listed relatives are notified at least 14 days ahead (755 ILCS 5/11a-10). The person may attend, hire a lawyer and ask for a jury. A doctor's report is filed with the petition, and the court appoints a guardian ad litem to investigate. The court must find a disability by clear and convincing evidence (755 ILCS 5/11a-3). Our [Illinois guardianship article](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship) covers each step.

In New Jersey, the person must be served in person at least 20 days before the hearing, with a notice that says they may oppose the case, bring a lawyer and demand a jury trial. The court appoints a lawyer for the person if none appears (R. 4:86-4). That lawyer interviews the person, searches for any will, power of attorney or healthcare directive, and reports on less restrictive alternatives at least ten days before the hearing. Our [New Jersey guardianship guide](/learn/new-jersey/guardianship-in-new-jersey) lays out the sequence.

Five practical moves help in either state:

1. **Gather the documents.** A signed power of attorney and healthcare directive are the best evidence that the person planned ahead.
2. **Get a current medical opinion.** If you think the doctor's report is wrong, a second evaluation by a doctor who knows the person carries weight.
3. **Offer a narrower order.** Courts can limit guardianship to money or to medical decisions. A proposal for a limited guardian can settle a fight.
4. **Propose someone else.** If the objection is to the choice of guardian, name a better one and be ready to explain why.
5. **Watch the dates.** Hearing notices carry deadlines for responses. Missing them can cost you the right to be heard.

## What does the court need to see?

Both states lean toward the least restrictive option. A bad choice, an odd habit or a family disagreement does not meet the standard.

The court wants proof of a real inability to manage health, safety or money, backed by medical evidence. It also asks whether anything short of guardianship would work. Signed documents answer that question. So do supports like a bill-paying service, a joint account with a trusted person, or a care manager.

Take Walt, 79, whose daughter Beth petitions for guardianship after he lost $9,000 to a phone scam. His son Jim objects. Walt signed a financial power of attorney naming Jim eight years ago, and he still knows what the money is for. The court may decide that the scam is a reason to tighten safeguards and not a reason to remove Walt's rights. It might approve a limited order over his accounts, or it might leave things to the power of attorney with added reporting. The documents shaped the outcome.

## Can a guardianship be ended or changed later?

Yes, though it is harder after the order than before it. Courts want proof that something has changed.

In Illinois the ward may ask the court to change or end the guardianship, and the court must tell the ward of that right in writing (755 ILCS 5/11a-19). The court may end it where the ward's capacity is shown by clear and convincing evidence (755 ILCS 5/11a-20). In New Jersey the person may petition to modify or end the guardianship or to restore rights (R. 4:86-7), and the court looks at current evidence of capacity.

What counts as new evidence? A doctor's report that the person has recovered, as after a stroke or a medication change. A record of sustained good decisions. A plan for support, such as a power of attorney signed now with a capacity letter from the doctor. A court may also prefer a limited order to a full one as a stepping stone.

Before you file, ask what the original order covered. Some orders have review dates, and the court file will show whether the guardian filed required reports. Our article on [guardian reports and accountings in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-guardian-duties-reports-and-accountings-after-appointment) shows what should be in that file.

## What if the guardian is abusing the role?

Contesting the guardian is a different matter from contesting the guardianship. Act quickly if you see any of these:

- Money going to the guardian or to people the ward would never choose.
- The ward being isolated from family or visitors without a stated reason.
- Medical or care decisions made against the ward's known wishes.
- Reports and accountings missing or late.
- Property sold without notice.

The steps are:

1. **Write it down.** Dates, amounts, names. A short timeline beats an angry letter.
2. **Ask for records.** The court file is often public. Bank statements may need a court order.
3. **Contact the court that appointed the guardian.** File a petition describing the problem and asking for an accounting, a restriction on powers, or removal.
4. **Call protective services if there is danger.** Adult protective services exists for neglect and exploitation.
5. **Hire an attorney.** A petition to remove a guardian is a contested hearing.

Our article on [removing a guardian](/learn/guardianship/can-a-guardian-be-removed-or-refuse-to-serve) explains the grounds, and the one on [power of attorney abuse](/learn/power-of-attorney/power-of-attorney-abuse) fits if the problem is an agent under a power of attorney.

## Could a better plan have avoided the fight?

Often yes. Most guardianship contests begin because no one signed a power of attorney in time. A signed [power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/power-of-attorney-vs-guardianship) and healthcare directive usually let a family act without a court, and they let the person choose who decides. If a parent still has capacity, sign now. If capacity is in doubt, see an attorney about whether they can still sign.
