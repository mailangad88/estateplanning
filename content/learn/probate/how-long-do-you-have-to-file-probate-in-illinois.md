Someone has died, a few weeks have passed, and nobody has gone near the courthouse. Is that a problem? Or the other extreme: it has been four years, the house is still in Dad's name, and you want to know whether it is too late. Both are common, and the honest answer is that there is no single "file by this date" rule in Illinois. There are several smaller clocks, and they start at different times.

This page lays out each Illinois clock, so you can tell which have already started in your situation. For what probate is and how the process runs, see our [probate guide](/learn/probate).

## Is there a deadline to file probate after someone dies?

Not one general deadline for petitioning to open an estate. What exists are duties and time limits tied to the will, to creditors and to contests. Think of them as separate clocks:

| Clock | Illinois rule | When it starts |
|---|---|---|
| Delivering the original will | Anyone holding it must file it with the circuit court clerk immediately; hiding it for 30 days after learning of the death is a felony | The death |
| Named executor acts or declines | 30 days to petition for probate or decline (755 ILCS 5/6-3) | Learning of the nomination |
| Notice to family | Heirs and legatees get notice within 14 days | The order admitting the will |
| Contesting the will | Six months | The order admitting the will |
| Creditors | The later of six months from first publication or three months from mailing; all claims barred two years after death at the latest | Publication, mailing or the death |
| Tax | Illinois estate tax return and payment, when required, due nine months after death | The death |

Source details for each are in our pages on the [Illinois probate process](/learn/illinois/illinois-probate-process) and [filing the original will with the circuit clerk](/learn/illinois/filing-the-original-will-with-the-illinois-circuit-clerk).

## What must happen right away?

Very little in terms of court filings, but one thing cannot wait. If you have the original will, deliver it. In Illinois that means to the circuit court clerk in the county where the person lived. That duty exists even when nobody plans to open an estate. A will tucked in a drawer for months is how families end up with accusations.

Everything else can be done on a reasonable schedule, but do not drift. Secure the home, notify the bank and Social Security, get several certified death certificates, and call the attorney. Our [checklist for what to do when someone dies](/learn/after-a-death/what-to-do-when-someone-dies-checklist) puts the first weeks in order.

## What are the first dates that matter after probate opens?

Once the court admits the will and issues letters, the dates arrive quickly.

1. **Day of admission.** The six-month will contest period starts.
2. **Within 14 days.** The executor mails notice to heirs and legatees, with a copy of the petition and the order.
3. **Claim notices.** The executor publishes a notice once a week for three weeks and mails notice to known creditors. The bar date is the later of six months from first publication or three months from mailing.
4. **Nine months after death.** If the estate is large enough to owe Illinois estate tax, Form 700 and the tax are due. Our page on the [Illinois Form 700 estate tax return](/learn/illinois/illinois-form-700-estate-tax-return) explains who files it.
5. **Closing.** An independent executor files a closing report and waits 42 days before asking to be discharged.

You can see how these dates play out on a normal estate in [how long probate takes in Illinois](/learn/illinois/how-long-does-probate-take-in-illinois) and in [what slows down Illinois probate](/learn/illinois/what-slows-down-illinois-probate).

## Can you open probate years after the death?

Usually yes, and people do it all the time. The trigger is almost always a house. A family realizes the deed still names a parent who died in 2019, they need to sell, and the title company wants letters or a court order. They are not penalized for waiting just because the clock ran. But several things become harder:

- **Heirs and witnesses.** Someone who could have signed off is gone. Heirs who moved cannot be found.
- **Creditors.** The creditor window may be long closed in the sense that old claims are barred, but a late opening can raise new questions about unpaid taxes and a lien on the house.
- **Property tax and carrying costs.** The bills kept running and somebody paid or did not. See [what happens if you ignore probate](/learn/probate/what-happens-if-you-ignore-probate) for what accumulates.
- **Disputes.** An estate that sat for years often has an heir who treated the house as theirs. That is a recipe for the kind of conflict described in [siblings inherit a house and disagree](/learn/what-if/siblings-inherit-house-and-disagree).

If the death was a long time ago, an attorney can look at the file and tell you whether a regular probate, a small estate route or a title-clearing proceeding fits. Our article on [fixing title problems on inherited property](/learn/property-and-assets/fixing-title-problems-on-inherited-property) covers the last category.

## What if there is no will?

The same questions apply, with a different first step. With no will, the court appoints an administrator, and the order of priority is set by statute. You can read who gets to ask in [who can open a probate case](/learn/probate/who-can-open-a-probate-case). Nobody is forced to apply, but if the family does nothing, a creditor or another relative can. In Illinois, the court issues letters of administration, often with a surety bond, as our page on [Illinois letters of administration and the surety bond](/learn/illinois/illinois-letters-of-administration-and-the-surety-bond) explains.

## A worked example

Example only: Gloria dies in Du Page County, Illinois, in March. Her son Raymond finds a will in her desk in April and mails it to his sister Sandra, who is named executor, then forgets about it. By October nothing has been filed.

The delay is already a problem. Raymond learned of the death in March and had the will in April. If he holds onto it for more than 30 days after learning of the death, he has crossed the line Illinois law draws. The fix is simple: file the original with the circuit court clerk now. Sandra, as the named executor, also has her own 30-day clock to petition or decline once she learns of the nomination, so she should not let the envelope sit either. She then petitions for letters, and the six-month contest period and creditor notice process begin on normal terms. Nothing was lost except months, but nobody wants to explain the gap to a judge.

## How to decide what to do this week

1. Find the original will and confirm who holds it.
2. If you hold it, deliver it to the court where the person lived.
3. List the assets titled only in the dead person's name. Those are your probate assets.
4. Write the date of death on a calendar and mark the 30-day, six-month, nine-month and two-year dates that apply.
5. Call an attorney if there is real estate, a dispute, an out-of-state asset or unpaid debt.

If everything is small and the family agrees, a [small estate affidavit](/learn/probate/small-estate-affidavit) may avoid a full case.

## What if the family wants to skip probate entirely?

Some estates really do not need a court case. If everything the person owned passed by beneficiary form, joint ownership or a trust, there may be nothing left to probate, and no deadline applies to those assets because they never entered the estate. Our article on [whether probate is always required](/learn/probate/is-probate-always-required) walks through how to tell. The mistake is assuming this without checking. One forgotten account in the dead person's name alone, or a house with only that name on the deed, is enough to need a court case later, and a title company will ask for it when you try to sell.

If you do decide to wait, write down why and when. A short note in the family file saying that all accounts had beneficiaries and the house passed to a surviving owner is useful evidence if a question comes up in five years.
