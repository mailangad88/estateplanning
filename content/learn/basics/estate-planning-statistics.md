Reporters, students and families often ask the same questions: how many Americans have a will, who goes without, and why. This page collects published figures in one place so you can check them. Every statistic names the publisher, the year and a link to the original page. We only include numbers we read on the source itself.

Two cautions apply throughout. Survey results vary with who was asked and how, so we show the survey details next to each number. And a statistic about other people is not a reason for or against making your own plan; for that, start with [what estate planning is and who needs it](/learn/basics/what-is-estate-planning).

## Headline statistics at a glance

| Statistic | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| U.S. adults with a will (online survey) | 24% | [Caring.com, 2025 Wills and Estate Planning Study](https://www.caring.com/resources/wills-survey) |

| Adults with any advance directive | 36.7% | [Health Affairs, 2017 (Yadav et al.)](https://pair.upenn.edu/publication/approximately-one-in-three-us-adults-completes-any-type-of-advance-directive-for-end-of-life-care/) |

## How many Americans have a will?

Estimates range from about one in four to nearly one in two, depending on the survey. The two most cited sources are Caring.com and Gallup, and they measure different things at different times.

| Source | Year | Method | Share with a will |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Caring.com Wills and Estate Planning Study](https://www.caring.com/resources/wills-survey) | 2025 | Online survey with YouGov of 2,500+ U.S. adults, January 3 to 6, 2025 | 24% |
| Caring.com (same study, earlier edition) | 2022 | Online survey | 33% |
| [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/351500/how-many-americans-have-will.aspx) | 2021 | Telephone poll of 1,016 U.S. adults, May 3 to 18, 2021, margin of error plus or minus 4 points | 46% |
| Gallup, historical range | 1990 onward | Telephone polls | 44% to 51% |

Gallup's 2021 report noted that its polling since 1990 has stayed in a 44% to 51% range. Caring.com's own report shows its figure falling from 33% in 2022 to 24% in 2025. The two series are not directly comparable, since sample design and wording differ, so treat each as a trend within its own survey rather than a contradiction.

Caring.com also reported that 13% of respondents had a living trust and 4% had other estate planning documents in 2025. Note that someone can have both a will and a trust, so those shares should not be added together.

## Who has a will? Age, income and demographics

Age and income are the clearest dividing lines. In Gallup's 2021 poll, the share with a will rose steadily with age and with income.

| Group (Gallup, 2021) | Share with a will |
|---|---|
| Adults 65 and older | 76% |
| Adults 50 to 64 | 53% |
| Adults 30 to 49 | 36% |
| Adults 18 to 29 | 20% |
| Household income $100,000 or more | 61% |
| Household income $40,000 to $99,999 | 49% |
| Household income under $40,000 | 30% |
| College graduates | 57% |
| Non-college graduates | 40% |
| White adults | 55% |
| Nonwhite adults | 28% |

Caring.com's 2025 study did not publish percentages by age group on the pages we reviewed. It reported qualitatively that the 35 to 54 cohort, often called the sandwich generation, is the largest group without estate documents, and that many respondents over 55 appear to have plans in place.

The pattern matters for families in practice. The people least likely to have a will include parents of young children, for whom the will is also where a guardian is named. Our guides on [estate planning for new parents](/learn/life-stages/estate-planning-for-new-parents) and [for young adults](/learn/life-stages/estate-planning-for-young-adults) cover those groups.

## Why do people not have a will?

Delay, not disagreement, is the most common answer. The reasons below come from Caring.com's 2025 study.

| Reason (Caring.com, 2025) | Share |
|---|---|
| Respondents without a will who "just haven't gotten around to it" | 43% |
| Respondents who cited low priority and not having enough assets as the reasons they lack a will or trust | 56% |
| Respondents who said "nothing" could motivate them | Nearly one quarter |

Caring.com reported the "nearly a quarter" figure in words rather than as an exact percentage, so we repeat it that way. For the other side of the question, what happens to the property of someone who dies without one, see [what happens if you die without a will](/learn/wills/dying-without-a-will). We did not find a reliable, recent national count of how many estates pass by intestacy or of average probate costs, so we do not publish one here. Probate costs vary widely by state; see [probate costs](/learn/probate/probate-costs) for the factors that drive them.

## How many people have a living will or advance directive?

Roughly a third to nearly half of adults report some form of directive, depending on the source and on whether the figure comes from a survey or a review of records.

| Source | Year | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| [Health Affairs, Yadav et al.](https://pair.upenn.edu/publication/approximately-one-in-three-us-adults-completes-any-type-of-advance-directive-for-end-of-life-care/) | 2017 | Across 150 studies and 795,909 participants, 36.7% had completed an advance directive, including 29.3% with living wills |
| Same study | 2017 | 38.2% of patients with chronic illness and 32.7% of healthy adults had completed one |
| [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/312209/prevalence-living-wills-slightly.aspx) | 2020 | 45% of Americans said they had a living will, up from 40% in 2005 |

Gallup's 2020 poll found the same age and income gradient as for wills: 72% of adults 65 and older had a living will, versus 15% of those 18 to 29, and 59% of upper-income adults versus 28% of lower-income adults. The increase since 2005 was concentrated among college-educated and higher-income respondents.

The Health Affairs figure is lower than Gallup's; the two used different methods, since one pooled 150 studies and the other polled adults by telephone. Details on the documents themselves are in our [living will guide](/learn/healthcare-directives/living-will) and [healthcare directives pillar](/learn/healthcare-directives).

## How many families pay federal estate tax?

Very few. The IRS Statistics of Income program counts estate tax returns, and the numbers have fallen as the exemption has risen.

| Measure | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Estate tax returns filed, 2024 | 7,195 | [IRS Statistics of Income, Publication 5332](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5332.pdf) |
| Estate tax returns filed, 2023 | 9,024 | Same publication |
| Total net estate tax reported on returns filed in 2024 | Just over $23.3 billion | Same publication |
| Estate tax returns filed, 2022 | 8,130 (up 32% from 6,158 in 2021) | [IRS, Publication 5332 (Rev. 10-2024)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/p5332--102024.pdf) |
| Share of 2023 deaths with a taxable return | Fewer than 0.2% (about 4,000 taxable of about 7,100 returns, projected) | [Tax Policy Center, Briefing Book, January 2024](https://taxpolicycenter.org/briefing-book/how-many-people-pay-estate-tax) |

The IRS also reported that stocks and real estate made up nearly 57% of decedents' asset holdings on returns filed in 2024. Note the difference between filing and paying: a return can be required without any tax due. The 2026 federal exclusion is $15,000,000 per person, covered in our [federal estate tax guide](/learn/estate-tax/federal-estate-tax-exemption), so most families owe nothing, though some states tax at lower levels.

## How much wealth is expected to pass to the next generation?

One widely cited projection puts the figure at $124 trillion through 2048. It comes from consultancy Cerulli Associates, reported in the NAPA trade publication in December 2024.

| Measure (Cerulli Associates, projection through 2048) | Figure |
|---|---|
| Total expected transfer | $124 trillion |
| To heirs | $105 trillion |
| To charity | $18 trillion |
| From Baby Boomers and older generations | Nearly $100 trillion (81% of transfers) |
| Millennials' expected inheritance over 25 years | $46 trillion |

Source: [NAPA, "Wealth Transfers Expected to Hit $124 Trillion Through 2048,"](https://www.napa-net.org/news/2024/12/wealth-transfers-expected-to-hit-$124-trillion-through-2048/) December 30, 2024, citing The Cerulli Report on U.S. high-net-worth and ultra-high-net-worth markets. NAPA also reported that over half of the total volume comes from high-net-worth and ultra-high-net-worth households, which make up about 2% of households. This is a forecast built on assumptions, and the average family's inheritance is much smaller than the headline.

What this means for ordinary families is that coordination errors, such as an outdated beneficiary form, cost real money when property does pass. Our article on [common estate planning mistakes](/learn/basics/common-estate-planning-mistakes) lists the ones we see most.

## How we compiled this

We searched for primary sources, opened each page, and recorded each figure with its publisher, year and method as shown on that page. We included a number only if we read it on the source. We did not estimate, round up, or combine figures from different surveys into a new statistic. Where a source gave a figure in words, such as "nearly a quarter," we kept the wording. Figures are as published; some sources may have been updated since we read them, and the IRS posts new filing-year data each year. We did not find a verified national figure for intestate deaths or average probate cost, so we left them out rather than guess. This page is general education and is not legal advice.

If you cite this page, please cite the original publisher and link to its page, since they hold the underlying data.
