Dev, 41, is riding his bike to work when a car hits him. He survives, but he is on a ventilator and cannot speak or sign anything. His wife, Priya, sits in the hospital and learns that the paychecks go to an account only he can use, and his 401(k) is in his name alone. If you are reading this because something similar happened, or because you realized it could, this page explains what the law does and what can still be done.

This page is about a working-age adult after a sudden event. If your concern is an aging parent with memory loss, see our article on a [parent with dementia and no power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/parent-with-dementia-no-power-of-attorney). For the full topic, see our [power of attorney guide](/learn/power-of-attorney).

## What happens by default

Without a signed power of attorney, nobody has automatic authority to manage your money. The law treats your property as yours, even when you cannot act for it. Four things follow from that.

**Your spouse is not automatically in charge.** Being married does not let a spouse sign for you at the bank, change an investment, sell a car titled to you or deal with your retirement plan. Property titled only in your name stays under your control, and no one else can use that control.

**Joint accounts keep working.** A joint owner can generally keep using a joint checking account, because they own it too. That helps with groceries and the mortgage payment. It does not reach accounts in your name alone, and it does not solve a business or a brokerage account.

**Medical decisions often have a default.** Many states have surrogate or family consent laws that let a spouse, an adult child or a parent consent to treatment, usually in a set order. The rules differ, and some states cover fewer decisions than others. There is no general equivalent for money, which is the gap that surprises people.

**Privacy rules can get in the way.** Federal HIPAA rules limit what providers share. Without a signed release, staff may share only limited facts with family, or may decline to talk at all. Families sometimes cannot get records they need for an insurance claim or a disability application.

## What does the court route look like?

When money or property needs to be managed and no document exists, a family member usually asks a court to appoint a guardian or conservator. Some states use "guardian" for personal decisions and "conservator" for finances, and others use one word for both. Our comparison of [power of attorney vs. guardianship](/learn/power-of-attorney/power-of-attorney-vs-guardianship) covers the differences in detail.

The steps usually run like this:

1. Someone files a petition in the county court where you live, asking for a finding that you cannot manage your affairs.
2. Doctors provide evidence of your condition, and the court may appoint a lawyer or visitor to look after your interests.
3. Close relatives receive formal notice and may support or object.
4. A judge holds a hearing, decides whether you lack capacity and chooses who will serve.
5. The appointed person often posts a bond, files an inventory and reports to the court every year. Many states require approval before selling a home or making large moves.

Two points matter to families. First, the judge decides who serves, and that may not be the person you would have picked. A spouse is often favored, but a dispute among relatives can lead to a professional or a different family member. Second, the file is generally public, and the supervision can last for years.

## A realistic example

Back to Dev. Priya can use the joint account, so the mortgage gets paid for now. His employer will not change his direct deposit or his benefits elections without his signature. His disability insurer wants a form only he can sign. The fitness-studio business he owns with a partner needs a decision on a lease renewal.

After a few weeks, Priya's attorney files a conservatorship petition with a letter from his neurologist. Notice goes to Dev's parents, and his brother Marcus objects, saying he should have a role. The hearing is set for the next available date. The judge appoints Priya, requires a bond and orders a first report. The time and cost come from three things: how many people disagree, how fast the doctors respond, and how complicated the assets are.

Had Dev signed a durable power of attorney, Priya would have shown a bank the document the week after the accident.

## What can still be fixed?

**If the person can act, even briefly:**

1. Ask whether they are having clear moments. Capacity is judged at the moment of signing, and a person who is awake and understands can sometimes sign even in a hospital.
2. Contact an attorney right away and say capacity is a question. The attorney should meet the person alone, ask plain questions about what they own and who they trust, and write notes.
3. Sign a durable financial power of attorney, a healthcare power of attorney and a HIPAA release as one set.
4. Consider a short note from the treating doctor written close to the signing.

**If the person cannot act and may not recover soon:**

1. Collect what you can: account statements, a list of bills, insurance policies and any older documents.
2. Pay what you can from joint accounts, and keep receipts and a log.
3. Ask the employer, insurer and bank what their own processes require for an incapacitated customer. Some have simple options.
4. Ask an attorney whether a temporary or emergency appointment makes sense, and whether a full conservatorship is needed.
5. If the person owns a business, review the operating agreement and talk to the co-owners early. See [business owner incapacity planning](/learn/business-owners/business-owner-incapacity-planning).

## How state law changes this

States differ on who can consent to medical care by default. They also differ on the names and standards for guardianship and conservatorship, who gets priority to serve, the bond and reporting rules, and how fast a temporary appointment can happen. Check your state guide, such as [Florida](/estate-planning/florida), and ask an attorney to confirm the local rule.

## How to prevent it

Three documents do most of the work, and a short appointment can cover all three.

- A [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney) lets a person you choose handle money and property, even after you lose capacity.
- A [healthcare power of attorney](/learn/healthcare-directives/healthcare-power-of-attorney) names who speaks for you on medical care, and our [healthcare directives guide](/learn/healthcare-directives) covers the full set.
- A [HIPAA authorization](/learn/healthcare-directives/hipaa-authorization) lets providers share records with the people you name.

Check that your agent knows where the documents are. A power of attorney only helps if the bank will accept it, so ask your attorney about institutions that want their own form.
