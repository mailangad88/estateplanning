People ask the question one account at a time. Can I put a beneficiary on my savings? What about the HSA my wife and I built? Do I name someone on Social Security? The short answer changes by account, so this page is a field guide. Use the table to find your account, then read the notes underneath.

For the main concept, see our [beneficiary designations guide](/learn/beneficiary-designations). If you hold bank and brokerage accounts, the details of payable-on-death and transfer-on-death registration are in a [separate article](/learn/beneficiary-designations/payable-on-death-and-transfer-on-death-accounts).

## Quick reference: who can be named on what

| Asset | Beneficiary allowed? | What it is called | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Checking or savings | Yes | Payable on death (POD) | Bank-specific form, often a single page |
| Brokerage account | Yes, in nearly every state | Transfer on death (TOD) | Registration must be on the account itself |
| IRA, 401(k), 403(b) | Yes | Beneficiary designation | Spousal consent on employer plans |
| Life insurance, annuity | Yes | Beneficiary | Contingent line, irrevocable beneficiaries |
| HSA | Yes | Beneficiary | Spouse vs. non-spouse tax difference |
| 529 plan | Student beneficiary and successor owner | Successor owner | Two roles, two names |
| Pension | Per the payout option | Survivor benefit | Elected at retirement |
| Vehicle | Only in some states | TOD title | Check your motor vehicle agency |
| House | In many states | TOD deed | See the deed rules |
| Safe deposit box | No | Not applicable | Will or trust controls contents |
| Social Security | No | Not applicable | Law sets survivors |

## Can I name a beneficiary on a bank account?

Yes. A payable-on-death designation on a checking or savings account sends the balance straight to the person you name when you die. They bring a death certificate and ID, and the bank pays them. No probate, and your will does not apply to that money.

What happens without one? The account goes to the estate, or to the survivor if it is a joint account. The bank may freeze it until an executor shows court papers. Our page on [what happens to a deceased person's bank account](/learn/probate/what-happens-to-a-deceased-persons-bank-account) describes what families run into, and the [no-beneficiary scenario](/learn/what-if/no-beneficiary-named) shows how a small gap becomes a court case.

You can name several people with percentages, and a contingent beneficiary if the bank's form allows. Not every bank accepts a trust or a charity on a basic POD form. If your revocable trust is the better recipient, ask the bank for its trust-specific procedure or retitle the account to the trust, as discussed in [whether a TOD deed or trust is better](/learn/probate/is-a-tod-deed-better-than-a-trust).

A bank account with a convenience-only child on it is a trap. If Maria adds her son Alex as a joint owner just to help pay bills, Alex legally owns part of the account, his creditors may reach it, and at Maria's death the money may go to Alex alone, not to his sister. A POD form or a power of attorney does the helping job without handing over ownership.

## What about a 529 plan?

A 529 has two roles. The **student** is the beneficiary who the money is for, and the **account owner** controls it. You can change the student to another family member without tax cost, within the plan's rules. When the owner dies, the 529 follows the successor owner named on the plan, if one is named. If not, it usually goes through the owner's estate. Name a successor owner as soon as you open the plan.

## Who gets my HSA when I die?

Whoever you name on the HSA beneficiary form. The tax result depends on who it is:

- **A spouse** becomes the new owner, and the account remains an HSA.
- **Anyone else** receives the fair market value, the account stops being an HSA, and the beneficiary reports the amount as income, reduced by qualified medical expenses paid for the owner within a year.
- **The estate**, if no one is named, which makes the value taxable on the owner's final return.

Because of the tax difference, many couples name the spouse first and an individual contingent.

## Who gets my annuity when I die?

The person named on the contract. Annuity beneficiary rules turn on whether the annuity had started paying out, but the form works like a life insurance form. The gain is taxed to the beneficiary as ordinary income, so check the choices for a lump sum or payments over time. See [taxes beneficiaries pay](/learn/beneficiary-designations/do-beneficiaries-pay-taxes-on-what-they-receive) for the full tax picture.

## Who gets my pension when I die?

Not necessarily who you choose today. A pension pays survivors according to the option you selected at retirement. A single-life payout stops at your death. A joint-and-survivor payout continues, often at a reduced percentage, to your spouse. Under federal law, a married retiree generally needs the spouse's signed consent to pick the single-life option. Some pensions also pay a lump-sum death benefit to a named beneficiary. Ask the plan for your option statement and check it carefully, because most pensions cannot be changed after the first payment.

## Who gets my Social Security benefits when I die?

You do not name anyone. The Social Security Administration pays survivor benefits to eligible family members on your work record: a surviving spouse, minor children, a disabled child and, in some cases, dependent parents. A one-time $255 payment may go to a spouse or child. The benefit paid for the month of death must be returned. Our article on [Social Security after a death](/learn/after-a-death/social-security-after-a-death) covers how to report the death and claim, and ssa.gov has the current rules.

## Can I name a beneficiary on a vehicle title or a safe deposit box?

A vehicle can have one in states that issue TOD or beneficiary titles. Many states do, and the motor vehicle office has the form. In other states, a small estate process or a joint title is the usual route. Our page on [transferring a car, boat or RV after death](/learn/property-and-assets/transfer-a-car-boat-or-rv-after-death) goes through the options.

A safe deposit box cannot have a beneficiary. The box is a rental, and the contents belong to you and pass under your will or trust. Name your executor as a co-renter or deputy if the bank allows, and keep a list of what is inside.

## Can a beneficiary live abroad or be a non-citizen?

Yes. A bank or insurer will pay a beneficiary who lives in another country, though it may require a notarized claim or an apostille, and wire fees add up. For a nonresident alien receiving an IRA, expect tax forms and generally higher withholding, and the payout timing may differ. Tell your attorney about a foreign beneficiary before you finalize a plan.

## Can I name a charity, school or foundation?

Yes, and for traditional IRAs it is often the most tax-efficient gift, because a qualified charity pays no income tax on the money. Use the exact legal name, the tax ID and the address. If you want the gift used for a certain purpose, ask the organization how to word it. Naming several charities is fine; give each a percentage.
