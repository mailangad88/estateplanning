When a parent dies in a manufactured home, the family often discovers the legal question sits between two shelves. Is it a car, because it has a title and wheels somewhere under it? Or is it a house? The answer decides which office you visit, what you sign and how long it takes.

This page explains what happens to a mobile or manufactured home when the owner dies, with the steps for heirs and what an owner can do to make it easy. It is part of our guide to [property and assets](/learn/property-and-assets).

## Is a mobile home a vehicle or a house?

Sometimes both, at different times. Many states title mobile homes through the motor vehicle agency, the way they title a car or an RV. Others treat the home as real estate once it is anchored to a permanent foundation on land the owner owns and the title has been surrendered to the county. A third group has the home as personal property sitting on rented land.

Three situations account for most cases.

| Situation | Treated as | Where the paperwork goes |
|---|---|---|
| Home on a rented lot in a park, original title still active | Usually personal property | State titling agency, plus the park |
| Home on land the owner owns, title still active | Personal property on real property, which can cause confusion | State titling agency and county recorder |
| Home permanently affixed, title retired and the deed covers the home and land | Real estate | County recorder, like any house |

If you are not sure which applies, look for a certificate of title in the deceased person's papers and check the county property tax bill. A home taxed with the land is a good sign that it has been merged into the real estate. Our article on [transferring a car, boat or RV after death](/learn/property-and-assets/transfer-a-car-boat-or-rv-after-death) explains the vehicle-style route.

## How does the home pass if there is a will?

The executor named in the will asks the probate court for letters, then uses them to transfer title. The will can name who gets the home. In Illinois this is a circuit court matter; in New Jersey it runs through the Surrogate in the county. The executor can sign the title transfer or deed to the heir.

If the estate is small and the home is treated as personal property, a small estate affidavit can sometimes replace probate. Illinois sets the limit at $150,000 in personal property, not counting registered motor vehicles. New Jersey's affidavit limits are lower and depend on who is claiming. The affidavit does not cover real estate. See the [Illinois small estate affidavit](/learn/illinois/illinois-small-estate-affidavit) and the [New Jersey small estate affidavit](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-small-estate-affidavit), and then ask the agency which one it will accept for the title.

## What if there is no will?

State law decides who inherits. In Illinois and New Jersey a surviving spouse takes first, then children share, and the shares depend on who survives. Our pages on [who inherits without a will in Illinois](/learn/illinois/who-inherits-without-a-will-in-illinois) and [in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/who-inherits-without-a-will-in-new-jersey) explain the order. A court appoints an administrator who handles the title. Expect the process to take longer than with a will.

## What happens if the home is in a park?

The home and the lot are two different things. The home belongs to the estate. The lot belongs to the park owner, and the heirs use it under a lease or a set of rules.

That makes four questions urgent.

1. **Is there a lot lease, and who is on it?** If the deceased was the only tenant, the park may treat the lease as ended.
2. **Can heirs keep the home on the lot?** Parks often screen new residents or set age or income rules. Ask for the rules in writing.
3. **How long do heirs have to sell or remove the home?** Many parks allow a short window. Ask and put the date in an email.
4. **Who is responsible for rent in the meantime?** The estate is, and unpaid rent can lead to a lien or an eviction.

Do not clean out, sell or tow the home until you know who has authority. If someone does so without it, the title may not transfer to a buyer, and the family may be accused of taking property that belongs to the estate.

## What about the loan?

Mobile home loans are often chattel loans, secured by the home alone without land. The loan does not end at death. The lender can usually demand payment, but a lender that is paid on time often has little reason to push. Heirs who want to keep the home generally keep paying while the estate is settled. Heirs who do not may sell the home, and the proceeds go to the lender first.

Ask the lender about its estate process, and for the payoff figure. Do not agree to re-sign the loan until you know who will own the home. Our overview of the [mortgage when the homeowner dies](/learn/property-and-assets/mortgage-when-the-homeowner-dies) covers federal protections that apply to heirs of real estate loans. A chattel loan on a mobile home may not have those protections, so treat the lender's answer as the rule.

## A short example

Walt, 79, owned a manufactured home in a park in Will County, Illinois, and had no will. His title was still active. His children, Carla and Dean, found the title in his desk and a lot lease in his name only. The park manager said they had 60 days to sell the home or move it, and sent a notice about rent.

Carla asked the court to appoint her administrator. Once she had letters, she could sign the title over to a buyer. The home sold for $38,000, most of it going to the lender who held a $22,000 loan. The remainder went through the estate to the two children. If Walt had left a simple will and listed the loan and the park contact in a letter of instruction, the paperwork would have started a month sooner.

## Are there taxes when heirs sell or keep the home?

Inheriting the home is not income. The heir generally takes a basis equal to the home's value on the date of death, so a quick sale at about that value produces little or no gain. Mobile homes usually lose value rather than gain it, so many sales produce no taxable gain at all, and a loss on an inherited home is generally not deductible when it is a personal residence that was never rented. Property or personal property tax on the home continues, and an unpaid bill can become a lien on the home or the land. Ask the county treasurer or collector for the current balance before you sell.

## How can an owner make this easier?

Put the facts in one place: the title, the loan statement, the lot lease, the park manager's phone and the insurance policy. A will should name the home and the person who gets it. Where the home sits on land you own, it can go into a [living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust), and your attorney can tell you whether your state allows a recorded transfer-on-death deed for it. Compare options in our article on [types of deeds](/learn/property-and-assets/types-of-deeds-quitclaim-warranty-grant-and-trustee-deeds).

Also arrange insurance. A vacant home still needs a policy, and many insurers cut coverage when a home is empty. Our guide to [ending a deceased person's lease, utilities, phone and insurance](/learn/after-a-death/ending-a-deceased-persons-lease-utilities-phone-and-insurance) shows what to cancel and what to keep.
