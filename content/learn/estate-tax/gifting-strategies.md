Giving while you are alive has real appeal. You see the benefit, the recipient gets help when they need it, and a large estate can shrink. But the rules around gifts are less simple than they look. A gift that costs no tax can still cost your child thousands in capital gains tax, or a parent's eligibility for long-term care benefits.

Our [gift tax annual exclusion](/learn/estate-tax/gift-tax-annual-exclusion) article explains the basic mechanics. This article goes further: how different kinds of gifts are treated side by side, which strategies are worth using, and where families go wrong.

## How does the gift tax work in 2026?

The gift tax is paid by the giver, not the recipient, on transfers of money or property for less than full value. Two exclusions limit it:

- **The annual exclusion** is $19,000 per recipient in 2026. You can give that amount to as many people as you like. Spouses can split gifts, treating each as half from each, for $38,000 per recipient.
- **The lifetime exclusion** is $15,000,000 per person in 2026. It is shared with the estate tax, so taxable gifts you make during life reduce what is available at death. See [the federal estate tax exemption](/learn/estate-tax/federal-estate-tax-exemption).

Both numbers change. The annual exclusion is indexed in $1,000 steps, and the lifetime exclusion is indexed for inflation after 2026.

## When must you file Form 709?

You file Form 709, the gift tax return, for the year in which you:

- give any one person more than the annual exclusion,
- split gifts with a spouse, even if all are under the limit,
- give a "future interest," such as a gift to a trust the beneficiary cannot use yet, which does not qualify for the annual exclusion unless it has withdrawal rights, or
- make certain other transfers, such as giving a spouse who is not a citizen more than the special limit.

The return is due April 15 of the following year. For most people no tax is due, because the excess just reduces the lifetime exclusion. Filing creates a record, and in some cases it starts the clock on the IRS's time to question the gift's value.

Gifts that do not need reporting include those under the annual exclusion, gifts to a U.S. citizen spouse, and gifts to charity, plus direct tuition and medical payments.

## Which payments are not gifts at all?

Two payments escape the gift tax entirely, in any amount:

- **Tuition** paid directly to the school for someone's education. The money must go to the institution, not to the student.
- **Medical expenses** paid directly to the provider, including health insurance premiums.

These exclusions are in addition to the $19,000 annual exclusion. A grandparent could pay a grandchild's $60,000 tuition bill directly to the university and still give the same grandchild $19,000 in cash. Only tuition counts, not room, board, or books.

## How do 529 plans fit in?

A contribution to a 529 college savings plan is a completed gift to the beneficiary. It qualifies for the annual exclusion, and the account grows tax-free if used for education.

The special rule is the five-year election. You can contribute up to five years of annual exclusions at once and elect to treat it as made evenly over five years. In 2026 that is $95,000 per beneficiary, or $190,000 for a couple. You report it on Form 709. If you die within the five years, the portion allocated to the remaining years is generally brought back into your estate.

Remember that you keep control of a 529 account as owner. You can change the beneficiary to another family member, which is a feature gifts to individuals lack.

## What about gifts of property like stock or a house?

When you give appreciated property, the recipient takes your basis. This is called carryover basis. When someone inherits property, the basis resets to the market value at death. Read [step-up in basis](/learn/estate-tax/step-up-in-basis) for the details.

Consider Rosa, 79. She owns stock she bought for $20,000 that is worth $220,000. If she gives it to her son now, his basis is $20,000, and selling it triggers tax on $200,000 of gain. If Rosa keeps it until she dies, his basis is $220,000, and a prompt sale produces little gain. With a $15,000,000 exclusion, estate tax is not a concern for her, so the gift saves nothing and costs her son capital gains tax.

The same logic applies to a house. Giving a home to a child while you live puts your original purchase price in the child's hands and may trigger a Medicaid penalty period. A [transfer-on-death deed](/learn/beneficiary-designations/transfer-on-death-deed) or a [living trust](/learn/trusts/transfer-house-to-living-trust) usually keeps the step-up and your control.

The better assets to give during life are generally cash, or assets that have lost value, since the recipient's loss can be recognized on a sale.

## How do gifts affect Medicaid and VA benefits?

Medicaid reviews transfers made within a look-back period before you apply for long-term care coverage. In most states that period is 60 months. A gift made inside it can cause a penalty period during which Medicaid will not pay. Annual exclusion gifts offer no protection here, because Medicaid has its own rules.

Gifts for a parent who may need care soon should be reviewed with an attorney first. See [the Medicaid look-back period](/learn/elder-care/medicaid-look-back-period) and [Medicaid planning](/learn/elder-care/medicaid-planning). Veterans' benefits such as VA aid and attendance have their own look-back rules.

## How should you give to children and grandchildren?

Minors cannot manage large sums, so gifts need a structure:

- A **custodial account** under the UTMA, controlled by a custodian until the child reaches the state's age, often 18 to 21.
- A **trust for the child**, which can delay access and set conditions. A trust drafted to qualify for the annual exclusion for minors, often called a 2503(c) trust, must let the child have access at 21.
- A **529 plan,** which keeps the money for education.

See [leaving money to minors](/learn/guardianship/leaving-money-to-minors).

## What if my spouse is not a U.S. citizen?

Gifts between citizen spouses are unlimited, but a non-citizen spouse has a fixed annual limit, $194,000 in 2026. See [QDOTs and planning with a non-citizen spouse](/learn/estate-tax/non-citizen-spouse-qdot).

## Comparing common gifts

| Gift type | Report on Form 709? | Uses lifetime exclusion? | Recipient's basis | In the Medicaid look-back? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cash under $19,000 | No | No | Not applicable | Yes |
| Cash over $19,000 | Yes | Only the excess | Not applicable | Yes |
| Tuition paid to school | No | No | Not applicable | Possibly; Medicaid has its own rules |
| Medical bill paid to provider | No | No | Not applicable | Possibly; Medicaid has its own rules |
| 529 contribution | If using the five-year election | Only above the election | Not applicable | Yes |
| Appreciated stock | Only if over $19,000 | Only the excess | Your basis carries over | Yes |
| House | Yes | Most of the value | Your basis carries over | Yes |
| Gift to citizen spouse | No | No | Your basis carries over | Generally not for a spouse |

## What mistakes should you avoid?

- **Giving away what you need.** Long-term care can cost hundreds of thousands of dollars. Keep enough for yourself first.
- **Giving appreciated assets that heirs would have received stepped up.**
- **Creating unequal gifts without telling the family.** One child receiving help while another does not can cause conflict that outlives you.
- **Gifting before reading the trust or deed.** If property is held in a trust, you may not have the power to give it.
- **Failing to file Form 709** when it is required.
- **Treating a gift as a loan,** or a loan as a gift, without paperwork.

## Should you keep a gift log?

Yes. For each gift, record the date, recipient, amount or description of property, its value and your basis, and whether you filed a return. In a year with many small gifts, a spreadsheet is enough. The log protects you and your executor if the IRS or Medicaid asks later.

## Do states tax gifts?

Most states do not have a gift tax, but a few add back recent gifts when computing a state estate tax. Rules vary and change. See [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes) and your [state guide](/estate-planning/connecticut).
