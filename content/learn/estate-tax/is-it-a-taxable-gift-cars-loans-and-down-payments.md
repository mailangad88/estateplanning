Dana, 66, bought her son Chris a used truck for his 25th birthday. Last spring she also wrote a check toward his first house and told her daughter that the money she borrowed for a business would "never have to be paid back." Then Dana's accountant mentioned the word gift tax, and she lay awake wondering what she had done.

Almost nothing, as it turns out. The gift tax is one of the most feared and least paid taxes in the system. But a few rules decide whether you must file a form, and a few everyday situations, a loan that is forgiven or a gift with strings, behave differently from what people expect. This article walks through them one at a time. For the full rules on the annual limit, see the main article on the [gift tax annual exclusion](/learn/estate-tax/gift-tax-annual-exclusion).

## The three numbers that decide everything

- **$19,000 per recipient per year** is the annual exclusion for 2026. Give anyone up to that much and nothing needs to be reported.
- **$15,000,000 per person** is the lifetime estate and gift exemption in 2026. Gifts above the annual exclusion are tracked against it, and no tax is due until the running total passes it.
- **40%** is the top rate that would apply above the exemption.

Those numbers change over time, so check the current year, and see our [estate tax guide](/learn/estate-tax) for the wider picture. The structure does not. You report, the exemption absorbs, and in practice very few families ever pay. If you have read about the federal exemption, you will recognize the same pot of money, described in our article on the [federal estate tax exemption](/learn/estate-tax/federal-estate-tax-exemption).

## Who pays, and who reports?

The giver. Always. If Chris gets a truck, Chris reports nothing and owes nothing, and the gift is not income to him. If the gift is large enough to require a return, Dana files Form 709 by April 15 of the following year.

The recipient's tax picture only changes in one way. Property you receive as a gift keeps the giver's original cost for figuring a future capital gain, rather than being reset to market value at death. That is one reason a gift of an appreciated asset can be a costly choice, as discussed in [step-up in basis](/learn/estate-tax/step-up-in-basis).

## A quick guide to common situations

| What you did | Is it a gift? | Reporting |
|---|---|---|
| Gave a child a car | Yes, worth its fair market value | None if under $19,000 in 2026 |
| Wrote a check for a down payment | Yes | None if under the exclusion per person |
| Paid a grandchild's tuition to the school | No, excluded | None |
| Paid a relative's hospital bill directly | No, excluded | None |
| Lent money with a written note and interest | No | None, but interest is taxable income to you |
| Forgave a loan | Yes, for the amount forgiven | Same as any gift |
| Gave to a U.S. citizen spouse | Yes, but unlimited deduction | None |
| Gave to a charity | Yes, but deductible | None |
| Gave to a political organization | Generally not taxed as a gift | Check campaign rules |

The values are measured on the day of the gift. A car is worth what a buyer would pay for it that day, not what you paid. A house or stock is worth its market value, and an appraisal may be needed for anything unusual.

## What about a car or other property?

Treat it as a gift of fair market value. If Dana's truck is worth $14,000, it falls under the annual exclusion by itself. If she adds another gift to Chris during the same calendar year, the two are added together. A total over $19,000 means Form 709, even though no tax is due.

Two practical issues have nothing to do with federal gift tax. The state may charge sales or title tax when ownership of a vehicle changes, with exceptions for family transfers that vary by state. And insurance must follow the new owner. Do not hand over the keys and leave the title in your name, because you may still be liable if the car causes an accident.

## Is a down payment a gift?

Yes, unless it is a loan. Mortgage lenders often require a gift letter that says the money does not have to be repaid, and that letter is a record of a gift. Whether it triggers Form 709 depends on the amount.

Here is where couples can stretch the exclusion. Dana and her husband Ray each have their own $19,000 for each recipient. Chris is married to Leah. Dana and Ray could each give $19,000 to Chris and $19,000 to Leah, for $76,000 in one year with no filing. When one spouse writes the whole check from a joint account, they elect "gift splitting" on Form 709 so the IRS treats it as made half by each. The election requires a return, even though no tax is owed.

For a family helping several children, our article on [gifting strategies](/learn/estate-tax/gifting-strategies) compares gifts, trusts and other options. If you are thinking about adding a child to your deed instead of giving cash, see the article on [adding a child to your deed](/learn/probate/adding-a-child-to-your-deed), because that is a gift of a share of the house with a very different tax result.

## Is a loan to my child a gift?

Not if it is genuinely a loan. The IRS looks at whether the parties intended repayment. Signs of a real loan include:

1. A written promissory note with a repayment schedule.
2. A stated interest rate, at least equal to the minimum rate the IRS publishes monthly for family loans, called the applicable federal rate.
3. Actual payments, made on time.
4. Security, such as a lien on the property, for larger loans.

A loan with no interest or a below-market rate can create imputed interest, which means the IRS treats the lender as having received interest and then gifted it back. There are exceptions for small loans. Ask a tax professional before relying on them for anything significant.

A family loan that was never meant to be repaid is a gift on the day it is made. Informal "loans" with no note and no payments often fall in that bucket in an audit or an estate dispute.

## What happens when you forgive a loan?

Forgiving a loan is a gift of the forgiven balance on the date you forgive it. If Dana's daughter owes her $30,000 and Dana says it is forgiven, that is a $30,000 gift. Dana can use the annual exclusion for part of it, then report the rest. Some parents forgive a loan in installments, up to the annual exclusion each year, and keep a written record of each step.

Forgiveness in a will works differently. The unpaid balance stays an asset of the estate until death, and the will cancels the debt then. That avoids the gift tax filing, but a family should state it clearly in the will, so brothers and sisters do not argue later about who owed what. The same unpaid note, if never mentioned, is something the executor will try to collect.

## What gifts never count at all?

Two kinds of payments are outside the gift tax completely, and they are in addition to the annual exclusion:

- **Tuition paid directly to the school.** The institution must receive the payment. A check made out to the grandchild does not qualify.
- **Medical care paid directly to the provider.** That includes hospital bills and health insurance premiums.

Neither one reduces your lifetime exemption, and neither requires a return. A grandparent can pay a full year's tuition and still give the same grandchild the annual exclusion in cash. The gift to the school is a separate event.

The other exclusions are gifts to a U.S. citizen spouse, which are unlimited, and gifts to qualified charities, which are deductible. Political groups are treated differently. A contribution to a political organization is not subject to the gift tax in general, though the campaign finance rules limit what you can give.

## When should I get advice?

Pick up the phone when the gift is large, when it is not cash, or when it involves property you plan to keep using. Gifts of a house, a business interest or a family cottage have tax and legal strings that a simple check does not have. Also, if you are thinking of Medicaid in the next five years, any large gift can cause a penalty period; the [look-back rules](/learn/elder-care/medicaid-look-back-period) apply separately from the gift tax.
