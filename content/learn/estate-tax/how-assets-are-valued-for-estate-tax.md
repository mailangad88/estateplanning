Executors often assume value is a matter of opinion: what the family thinks the house is worth, what Dad paid for the painting. The tax system has a different test, and it applies to everything from the checking account to the coin collection. An executor who knows the test early saves money on appraisals and avoids arguments with the IRS later.

This article explains how common assets are valued for federal estate tax, what proof to gather, and why the same number matters to the heirs when they sell. For the tax itself, see our [estate and gift tax guide](/learn/estate-tax) and the [federal estate tax exemption](/learn/estate-tax/federal-estate-tax-exemption).

## What is the valuation standard?

Fair market value on the date of death. The test is the price at which property would change hands between a willing buyer and a willing seller, both with reasonable knowledge of the facts, neither forced to act. It is not the price at a fire sale, and it is not the insurance replacement cost.

The executor can sometimes choose the alternate valuation date, six months after death, but only if the choice lowers both the total value of the estate and the estate tax due. It is a tool for falling markets, and estates that owe no tax cannot use it. Our article on [Form 706](/learn/estate-tax/form-706-estate-tax-return) explains the election.

Also remember who is reading the number. The value on the return generally becomes the heirs' basis under the [step-up in basis](/learn/estate-tax/step-up-in-basis). A low value saves estate tax but raises the heir's future capital gain. A high value does the reverse. There is no gaming it safely: the IRS expects a defensible figure and can challenge one that is too low.

## How hard is each asset to value?

| Asset | Usual method | Proof to keep |
|---|---|---|
| Bank and brokerage accounts | Balance on date of death plus accrued interest | Statements for the date |
| Publicly traded stock | Average of the high and low on the date of death | Price history printout |
| Real estate | Qualified appraisal | Appraisal report, photos |
| IRA, 401(k) | Account balance on date of death | Custodian statement |
| Life insurance | Face amount if owned by the decedent | Insurer's statement |
| Art, antiques, jewelry | Appraisal by someone who knows the market | Appraisal, auction comps |
| Cryptocurrency | Exchange price on date of death | Screenshots, wallet records |
| Business interests | Business valuation | See [business valuation](/learn/business-owners/how-a-business-is-valued-for-estate-tax) |

## How is a house valued?

A house is valued by a licensed appraiser who writes a report as of the date of death. The appraiser looks at recent comparable sales, the condition of the house on that date, and anything that affects it, such as a lease or a flood zone.

The best time to order the appraisal is in the weeks after death, while the house still looks as it did when the owner died. Waiting until after repairs, or until the family has cleaned out the contents, makes the report harder to defend.

**Do not use the property tax assessment alone.** Assessments can lag the market by years and are built on mass formulas. A county's number can be a useful cross-check, not a substitute.

### Example: Priya and the lake cabin

Priya's father died in March. The cabin was on the county books at a modest figure, but she hired an appraiser, who found comparable lake sales much higher. Her brother objected that the report would increase estate tax. Their father's estate was well under the exemption, so there was no tax, and the higher date-of-death basis cut the gain when they sold the cabin that fall. The better value helped them both.

If your house is in a trust, the same rules apply. See our guide on [inheriting a house](/learn/after-a-death/inheriting-a-house) for the practical steps that follow.

## How is an IRA or 401(k) valued?

At the balance on the date of death, in full. The fact that the heir will owe income tax on withdrawals does not reduce the value for estate tax purposes, which surprises people. Because retirement accounts count at face value, they can push an estate over the line faster than the owner expects.

The heir may qualify for an income tax deduction for the estate tax attributable to the account, which softens the double tax. Also remember that [inherited IRAs generally must be emptied within 10 years](/learn/beneficiary-designations/retirement-account-beneficiaries) for most non-spouse beneficiaries.

## How are art and collectibles valued?

By an appraiser who knows that specific market. A general antiques dealer is not the right person to value a signed print or a rare watch. Auction results for similar items are strong evidence, and so are gallery sales.

For ordinary household goods, the IRS accepts a reasonable estimate of what they would sell for used. For items of real value, a written appraisal is the safe course, and the instructions for the estate tax return call for attaching appraisals for items above a threshold the form sets.

Some families have jewelry, firearms, wine, or coins that are worth more than the owner thought. Go through the house with a list. Our article on [dividing personal property and family heirlooms](/learn/life-stages/dividing-personal-property-and-family-heirlooms) covers the family side.

Appraisers are paid for their time, not for a result, and a good one will tell you if a piece is worth less than the family hoped. That is useful. A report that holds up under questions is worth more than a high number that does not.

## How is cryptocurrency valued?

Like any other property: in U.S. dollars at fair market value on the date of death. For tokens traded on a major exchange, executors typically use the price at the time of death or an average of high and low for the day, and note which method they used. Thinly traded tokens and NFTs are harder, and may need an appraiser.

The valuation is easy to get wrong because the executor must first find the assets. Our article on [cryptocurrency inheritance](/learn/digital-assets/cryptocurrency-inheritance) explains how to locate wallets and keys.

## What if no appraisal was done at the time?

Order a retrospective appraisal. The appraiser works backward, using comparable sales and any photos, inspection reports, or insurance records from the period. It costs more and carries less certainty than a contemporaneous report, but it is accepted. If the estate owes no tax and the heirs plan to keep the asset, you may reasonably delay, but gather the evidence before it disappears.

Here is a practical checklist.

1. Write down the date of death and what the owner held on that date.
2. List each asset and decide whether it is easy to price or needs an appraiser.
3. Order appraisals for real estate and valuable personal property right away.
4. Save statements, screenshots, and photos from the date of death.
5. Keep every report with the estate records, because heirs will need the numbers for years.

## How state law changes this

States that collect their own estate or inheritance tax usually follow the federal date-of-death standard, though they may require their own forms and have their own filing dates. Some states also tax IRA inheritances differently from the federal system. See our guide to [state estate and inheritance taxes](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes) and your [state guide](/estate-planning/texas) as an example of how to look yours up.
