Grandparents often want to skip the middle step. They want to pay for a grandchild's tuition, set up a college account or leave a trust that will still be around when the youngest child is forty. Then someone mentions a tax with an odd name, and the plan stalls.

The generation-skipping transfer tax is real, but it is narrow. Most families who give to grandchildren will never owe it. This article explains who it applies to, how the exemption works, the gifts that are free, and the places where a mistake gets expensive. It is part of our broader guide to [estate and gift taxes](/learn/estate-tax).

## What is the generation-skipping transfer tax?

The generation-skipping transfer tax, usually shortened to GST tax, is a flat 40% federal tax on certain transfers to a "skip person." A skip person is someone two or more generations below you, such as a grandchild or great-grandchild. It also reaches unrelated people who are more than 37.5 years younger than you.

Congress created it to close a gap. Without it, a wealthy person could leave property to a grandchild and skip the tax that would have applied at the child's death. The GST tax is paid in addition to estate or gift tax, so a taxable skip can face two layers.

The tax has an exemption that mirrors the estate tax exemption. In 2026 each person has $15,000,000 of GST exemption, and the 2025 tax law indexes the amount for inflation with no scheduled sunset. Only the part of a skipping transfer above the exemption is taxed, at 40%. See [the federal estate tax exemption](/learn/estate-tax/federal-estate-tax-exemption) for how the matching amount works.

## Which transfers are taxed?

There are three types, and they work a little differently.

1. **Direct skip.** You give or leave property straight to a skip person, such as a bequest in your will to a granddaughter. This is the simplest case.
2. **Taxable distribution.** A trust makes a payment to a skip person from a trust that has no GST exemption allocated to it.
3. **Taxable termination.** The last non-skip beneficiary of a trust dies or loses their interest, and the property passes to skip persons. For example, a trust pays income to your son for life and then to your grandchildren.

The second and third are why trusts matter. A trust that benefits several generations needs a plan for its GST exemption from the start, because nobody can easily fix it later.

## Do most families ever owe it?

Very few do. The GST tax only bites when the transfers exceed the exemption. A grandmother who leaves $400,000 to a grandchild in her will has a direct skip, but the amount sits well inside her $15,000,000 exemption. In practice the executor allocates exemption on the estate tax return or the gift tax return and no GST tax is due. If the estate is below the filing threshold, nothing may need to be filed at all.

The tax shows up in a few situations. A very large estate leaves money to grandchildren outright. A family sets up a long-lasting trust for grandchildren and later generations. A grandparent makes sizable lifetime gifts to a grandchild that exceed the annual exclusion.

## What gifts to grandchildren are free of GST tax?

Several categories are exempt, and they cover most ordinary generosity.

| Type of gift | GST tax? | What you need |
|---|---|---|
| Up to $19,000 per grandchild per year (2026) | No | Nothing to file if under the limit |
| Tuition paid directly to the school | No | Pay the school, not the student |
| Medical expenses paid directly to the provider | No | Pay the provider directly |
| 529 plan contribution within the annual exclusion | No | Gift tax return only if you use the five-year election |
| Larger gift to a grandchild | Uses GST exemption | Form 709 and exemption allocation |
| Gift to a trust for a grandchild | Depends on trust terms | Attorney review recommended |

The direct payment exclusions are worth knowing. Tuition and medical payments made straight to the institution do not count against the annual exclusion at all. A grandfather could pay $60,000 of tuition for his granddaughter plus give her $19,000 in cash in the same year, with no tax and no return. Paying the student first and letting her pass it along would not qualify.

## How do 529 plans fit in?

A 529 plan contribution is a completed gift, even though the donor often keeps some control over the account. That means the annual exclusion applies to it, and so does the GST exemption if the beneficiary is a grandchild.

Many grandparents want to put in more than $19,000 at once. The tax law lets a donor elect to spread a lump-sum gift over five years. In 2026 that allows up to $95,000 per grandchild from one donor, or $190,000 from a couple. The election is made on a gift tax return. If the donor dies before the five years are up, part of the amount can be pulled back into the estate.

Financial aid is a separate concern. A grandparent-owned 529 is generally treated more favorably than money in the student's own name under current federal aid rules, but private colleges may ask for their own forms, and the rules change. Check the current treatment before committing a large amount.

## Why does portability not apply?

Married couples can combine their estate tax exemptions through [portability](/learn/estate-tax/portability-for-married-couples). The GST exemption is not portable. If your spouse dies without using their GST exemption, it disappears.

Here is how that plays out. Harold and Ruth have a combined $22,000,000. Harold dies first and leaves everything to Ruth. His estate tax exemption can be saved by electing portability, but his GST exemption cannot be saved at all. Had Harold left a trust for the grandchildren and allocated GST exemption to it, that exemption would have been put to use. This is one reason couples with large estates still use trusts at the first death. Our comparison of a [bypass trust and portability](/learn/estate-tax/bypass-trust-vs-portability) covers the larger question.

## What about a long-term trust for grandchildren?

A dynasty trust is a trust built to last for generations, often kept in a state that permits it. If GST exemption is allocated to the trust when it is funded, the trust can pass to grandchildren, great-grandchildren and beyond without further estate or GST tax. Our article on [trust names](/learn/trusts/trust-names-explained-dynasty-credit-shelter-family-and-more) explains how dynasty trusts compare with other designs.

Most of the technical work is allocation. The donor uses Schedule R of the gift tax return to allocate GST exemption. Sometimes the law does it automatically, which can help or hurt. A trust drafted for grandchildren without a clear allocation plan is one of the more common avoidable mistakes in this area.

## Practical steps

If you want to help grandchildren, consider this order:

1. Pay tuition and medical bills directly to the school or provider.
2. Use the annual exclusion for cash gifts and 529 contributions.
3. File a gift tax return whenever a gift exceeds the annual exclusion or you make the five-year 529 election.
4. For anything larger than that, or any trust for grandchildren, ask an attorney how GST exemption will be used.
