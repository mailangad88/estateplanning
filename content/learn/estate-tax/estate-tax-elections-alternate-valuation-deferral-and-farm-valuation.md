Most estates never see an [estate tax](/learn/estate-tax) return. The exclusion is $15,000,000 per person in 2026, and a married couple can shelter twice that. But if you are the executor of a large estate, or a family that owns a farm or an operating company, you may be handed a tax bill due in nine months and an estate made mostly of land and business interests that cannot be turned into cash overnight.

The tax code gives the executor three tools for that situation. They are elections, which means nothing happens unless someone chooses them on the return. This article explains what each one does, who qualifies, and the traps that catch people who treat them as routine.

## What are the estate tax elections an executor can make?

An election is a choice the executor makes on [Form 706](/learn/estate-tax/form-706-estate-tax-return) that changes how the estate is valued or when the tax is paid. The three covered here are listed in the table, along with what each one is for.

| Election | Code section | What it changes | Best suited to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alternate valuation | 2032 | Values assets six months after death | Estates hit by a market drop after death |
| Deferral of tax | 6166 | Lets tax on a business be paid in installments | Estates that are mostly a family business |
| Special use valuation | 2032A | Values land by its farming or business use | Farms and ranches |

None of these removes the estate tax. Two of them lower the number the tax is based on, and one changes the payment schedule. All of them add paperwork, and the IRS looks closely at claims made under them.

## What is the alternate valuation date?

The alternate valuation date is six months after the date of death. Normally everything in the gross estate is valued on the day the person died. If the executor elects alternate valuation, each asset is valued six months later instead, with one exception: anything sold, distributed or otherwise given up before that point is valued on the day it left the estate.

The election exists for one situation. Suppose Walter dies in a month when the stock market is high, and his estate is large enough to owe tax. Six months later the market has fallen by a fifth. Taxing the estate on the higher number would be unfair to the family, so the code allows the lower one.

Two rules limit it.

- **It must lower the tax.** The election is allowed only if it reduces both the value of the gross estate and the estate tax and generation-skipping tax due. An estate that owes nothing cannot use it, whatever happened to the market.
- **It is all or nothing.** You cannot pick the six-month value for the stock that fell and the date-of-death value for the house that rose. Everything moves together.

Assets whose value changes just because time passes, like a term annuity or a patent, are valued on the date of death with an adjustment, not at the later date.

There is one more consequence that surprises people. The heirs' [step-up in basis](/learn/estate-tax/step-up-in-basis) follows the value used on the return. If alternate valuation is elected, their basis is the six-month value. That is a lower basis than they would have had, and it is the price of the lower tax.

## What is a section 6166 election to defer estate tax?

Section 6166 lets an estate pay the portion of the tax that comes from a closely held business over many years instead of in one payment. It was written for a very practical problem. A family can own a successful company or a ranch worth $25 million and have very little cash. Selling the business to pay the IRS defeats the point of leaving it to the children.

In general the election is available when the business interest is more than 35 percent of the adjusted gross estate. The business must be an active trade or business, not just investments. A sole proprietorship, a partnership or corporation with a limited number of owners, or a corporation with a fairly small number of shareholders can all qualify, subject to the details.

If the estate qualifies, the tax tied to the business can be paid like this:

1. The estate pays interest only for the first several years.
2. After that it pays the tax in up to ten equal yearly installments, with interest.
3. A portion of the deferred tax gets a reduced interest rate, which the IRS publishes.

The deferral is not free, and it is not permanent. If the heirs sell or distribute half or more of the business interest, or the estate misses payments, the unpaid balance can become due right away. The IRS can also require a bond or a lien on estate property as security.

Think of Priya, who inherits a regional trucking company that makes up most of her father's estate. Without the election, the estate would have to borrow against the company or sell trucks to pay in nine months. With it, the company's cash flow pays the tax over more than a decade. ## What is special use valuation for farms?

Special use valuation, section 2032A, lets the executor value qualifying farmland or business real estate at what it is worth in its current use, not at the higher "highest and best use" price a developer would pay. For land near a growing town that can be a large difference.

The estate has to pass several tests, and they are specific:

- The land is used for farming or another qualifying closely held business.
- Farm or business assets, counting both land and equipment, make up a substantial share of the adjusted gross estate, and the qualifying real estate itself must be a significant share as well.
- The land goes to a family member of the deceased.
- The deceased or a family member actively participated in the operation for five of the eight years before death.

The total reduction in value is capped by a dollar limit that Congress adjusts for inflation, so for very large holdings it will not wipe out the issue. Check the current figure on irs.gov.

The trade is a long commitment. The heirs have to keep the land in qualifying use for ten years after the death. If they sell it to someone outside the family or stop farming it within that window, the IRS can recapture the tax savings. Each qualified heir signs an agreement to that effect, which is part of the election.

## Which election fits which estate?

Most estates qualify for none, and a few qualify for more than one. A rough sorting looks like this.

| Your situation | Likely election to look at |
|---|---|
| Large portfolio of stocks and funds, market fell after death | Alternate valuation |
| Closely held business is a large share of a taxable estate | Section 6166 |
| Farm or ranch land going to children who will keep farming | Section 2032A, often with 6166 |
| Estate is under $15,000,000 and no state estate tax | None. File only if portability is wanted |
| Estate is under the federal exclusion but state has a lower threshold | Ask whether the state allows the same elections |

The last two rows matter. Many states with their own estate tax have a lower threshold than the federal one. Some follow the federal elections and some do not. Our [state estate and inheritance tax guide](/learn/estate-tax/state-estate-and-inheritance-taxes) lists which states have these taxes at all.

## What mistakes cost executors the most?

The mistakes are rarely in the legal test. They are usually about timing and documentation.

1. **Deciding after the return is filed.** The alternate valuation election must be made on a timely filed return, and late relief is narrow. Appraise the estate on both dates before you file.
2. **Forgetting the basis effect.** A lower tax today means a lower basis for the heirs. On a farm they plan to keep, that may not matter. On stock they plan to sell, it does.
3. **Treating the election as a promise.** The IRS can ask for evidence that the business was active, that a family member worked it, or that the land stayed in use.
4. **Ignoring the heirs' signatures.** Special use valuation needs a signed agreement from every person with an interest in the land. One missing signature can sink the election.
5. **Not budgeting for appraisals.** A qualified appraisal at two dates, plus a business valuation, can cost real money. It is still smaller than the tax at stake, but it should be planned.

## How should a family plan before a death?

Most of this work belongs in the owner's lifetime. A farmer who wants to use section 2032A should be able to show five years of active participation. A business owner who wants section 6166 available should know whether the business will clear the percentage tests, and should keep investment assets from creeping into the company. A will or trust should also say who bears the interest cost of a deferral, because without direction it can become a dispute among the children.

If you are the executor and the estate is near the exclusion, ask for a valuation of the main assets before anything is sold. The way [assets are valued for estate tax](/learn/estate-tax/how-assets-are-valued-for-estate-tax) determines nearly everything else on the return.
