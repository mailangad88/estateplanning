The hearing is over. A New Jersey judge has found your mother, husband or brother incapacitated and has named you guardian. Most families expect the hard part to be behind them. In fact, the court's attention has just shifted from the person to you. The first months decide whether guardianship stays a quiet routine or turns into a series of motions.

This page picks up where [guardianship in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/guardianship-in-new-jersey) stops. That article covers how a guardian gets appointed and the alternatives. Here is what the job requires afterward. It is part of our [New Jersey estate planning and probate guide](/learn/new-jersey). The details come from the New Jersey Courts guardianship self-help page, and the written judgment in your case controls over any general summary.

## What does a New Jersey guardian have to do after appointment?

Follow the judgment, file what it requires and keep records. The Courts say guardians must follow all court rules and file periodic reports, and that the written judgment sets when reports are due and which forms to use. So the first task is the judgment itself. Read it twice. Note whether you are guardian of the person, of the estate or both, whether the guardianship is limited, whether a bond is ordered and what the reporting schedule says.

| Job | Who it applies to | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Post a surety bond, if ordered | Typically guardian of the estate | Within 30 days of the judgment |
| File a Guardian Inventory | Guardian of the estate | Generally within 90 days of the written judgment |
| File periodic reports with a Report of Guardian Cover Page | Every guardian | As the judgment sets |
| File a Report of Well-Being | Guardian of the person | With periodic reports |
| Keep receipts and invoices | Every guardian | Ongoing |

## How does the bond work?

If the judge orders a surety bond, you must post it within 30 days of the judgment to qualify. The bond protects the person's assets, but it does not protect you. You remain personally liable for any misuse of funds. A limited guardian of the estate must also post a bond unless the court relieves the guardian of it, as the statute summary in the [appointment article](/learn/new-jersey/guardianship-in-new-jersey) explains.

Talk to your own insurance agent or a surety company early. Arranging a bond takes some days. Miss the 30 days and you risk not qualifying, which means you cannot act.

## What is the 90-day inventory?

It is the list of everything the person owns and earns. A guardian of the estate generally must file a Guardian Inventory listing all assets and income within 90 days of the written judgment. The Courts say it usually only needs to be filed once. Later estate reports use one of two forms, which the judgment specifies.

Start by collecting statements for every bank account, brokerage account and retirement account, the deed, the vehicle title, insurance policies, pension and Social Security letters, and the last tax return. Include income as well as assets. The inventory is the baseline every later report is measured against, so errors here follow you.

Example only. Walter was appointed guardian of the estate for his father, Leonard, in March. By June he needed the inventory filed. Walter had a checking account, a certificate of deposit, a Social Security deposit and a pension he had never listed. He found the pension on a stray 1099 in a drawer. Listing it from the start meant his later reports matched the income actually arriving.

## What goes in a guardian's periodic reports?

Every periodic report needs a Report of Guardian Cover Page. For the person, the Report of Well-Being describes the overall care and must include a Certification of Examining Professional. For the estate, the reports follow one of the two forms named in the judgment.

The Courts add that reports typically cost $5 per page, payable to the county Surrogate, and filing questions go to the county Surrogate's office. For help completing the forms, the Civil Practice Division lists 609-815-2900, extension 54900.

Write each report as if a stranger will read it, because one will. Volunteers in the Guardianship Monitoring Program review periodic reports. They are looking for gaps: bills paid with no receipt, large transfers with no explanation, living arrangements that changed without notice.

## What records should a guardian keep?

Detailed records with every receipt and invoice. The Courts say these support your periodic reports and that the court may ask for proof of expenses or more detail on spending. The page does not call it an "accounting," but that is what a good ledger is. If you have handled an estate, the [probate inventory and estate accounting](/learn/probate/probate-inventory-and-estate-accounting) article shows the same habit in a different setting.

A workable system:

1. Open a separate account titled for the guardianship. Do not mix the person's money with yours.
2. Deposit all income there and pay all bills from it.
3. Save every receipt in a folder by month.
4. Log each visit or care decision for the person in a short note.
5. Reconcile the account against the statement each month.

## Can a guardian pay themselves or make gifts?

Not on your own say-so. Guardians of the estate may receive compensation based on the estate's total value plus a percentage of any income, and may ask the court to cover out-of-pocket costs, court and required fees, reasonable attorney fees and professional services. The Courts also say guardians cannot take fees or payments from the person unless the court specifically orders it. Gifts from the person's money require prior court approval.

This is where well-meaning children get into trouble. A daughter who pays herself back for groceries, or helps a brother with a car payment "because Dad would have wanted it," has made a decision only the court can make. The safe path is a motion before the spending.

## Who watches the guardian, and what happens if you slip?

The court does, through the reports, the Guardianship Monitoring Program volunteers, and anyone who asks. Friends and family may ask the court to review financial records or your spending. Motions can also change protections, such as reducing a bond or changing reporting requirements, or review the guardian's conduct. If a guardian cannot or will not continue, see [guardian removal and resignation](/learn/guardianship/can-a-guardian-be-removed-or-refuse-to-serve).

## When does the guardianship change or end?

The person under guardianship may petition to modify or end it or to restore rights, under R. 4:86-7, as covered in the [appointment article](/learn/new-jersey/guardianship-in-new-jersey). The guardianship also ends when the person dies. At that point the guardian's authority stops, and the executor named in the will, not the guardian, takes over the estate. Plan the handoff: a final report, a final accounting of the account you kept, and the paperwork described in [executor duties in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/executor-duties-in-new-jersey-step-by-step).

If the person still has capacity and you are reading this to prevent a court case, the [power of attorney versus guardianship](/learn/power-of-attorney/power-of-attorney-vs-guardianship) comparison explains the documents that avoid all of this.
