When a parent dies, the house is usually the largest asset and the most emotional one. In New Jersey it is also the asset that is most likely to be held up on paper. Unlike a bank account, a deed cannot be dealt with just by showing a death certificate.

This article follows a New Jersey house from the day of death to a sale or a new name on the deed. It builds on the state's tax rules in [the New Jersey inheritance tax](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-inheritance-tax) and the court steps in [the New Jersey probate process](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-probate-process), and it sits inside our [New Jersey estate planning and probate guide](/learn/new-jersey). For the national version, see [inheriting a house](/learn/after-a-death/inheriting-a-house).

## Who controls the house right after the death?

The executor named in the will, once appointed, or an administrator if there is no will. Until the Surrogate issues letters, nobody has authority to sign for the estate, and county Surrogates say letters come only after the tenth day following the death. In the meantime the heirs can secure the house, keep the heat on and call the insurance company. The insurer matters. Many policies treat an empty house differently, and our article on [vacant inherited houses](/learn/property-and-assets/vacant-inherited-house-insurance-repairs-squatters) covers what to ask.

The mortgage does not stop. See [what happens to the mortgage when the owner dies](/learn/property-and-assets/mortgage-when-the-homeowner-dies) for the loan side.

## Does the house need a tax waiver before it can move?

Yes, for most New Jersey houses. The Division of Taxation says New Jersey real property cannot be transferred out of a decedent's name without written consent from the Director, and its executor guide says "real estate transfers always require" the waiver. Only the Inheritance and Estate Tax Branch can issue it. It is not a form you fill out and download.

There are exceptions. According to the Division, three kinds of ownership skip the waiver.

- Real estate held by spouses or civil union partners as tenants by the entirety, which is not treated as a transfer to the survivor.
- Property held in the name of a bona fide trust.
- Property owned by a bona fide partnership, or by an LLC or closely held corporation.

Even then, the Division says all property of a decedent must still be reported on the inheritance tax return if one is required.

This is where a [living trust](/learn/new-jersey/living-trusts-in-new-jersey) and ownership by the entirety show their practical value. They take the house out of the waiver step. They do not take it out of the tax.

## How does Form L-9 work?

For a resident decedent whose beneficiaries are all in Class A, the executor, an administrator or a joint tenant can ask for the waiver on Form L-9. The form sets four conditions.

- All beneficiaries are Class A: spouse or civil union partner, children and descendants, stepchildren, parents or grandparents, and certain domestic partners.
- No asset of $500 or more passes to anyone else.
- No mutually acknowledged child relationship is claimed.
- No New Jersey inheritance or estate tax is due.

If the will creates a trust, the Division may require a full return instead. The form is not itself a waiver. It is the request.

Attach a copy of the will and codicils, a copy of the deed, the letters and the death certificate. Mail it to the Division of Taxation, Transfer Inheritance Tax, PO Box 249, Trenton, NJ 08695-0249, and not to the county clerk. Once any beneficiary falls outside Class A, for example a friend or a niece, the executor files the full inheritance tax return instead. The classes and rates are in the [inheritance tax article](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-inheritance-tax).

## How long does it take?

Longer than most families hope. The Division says to plan for "at least several months" after a return is filed, that an audit can add several months, and that about 40 to 50 percent of returns are audited. Returns are handled in the order received. The inheritance tax itself is due eight months after death.

The lien explains why. The Division says the inheritance tax lien covers all the decedent's property for 15 years, unless the tax is paid earlier or secured by bond, and applies whether or not tax has been assessed. A waiver releases it. Nobody wants to buy a house with that lien on it.

## What are the steps from death to the new owner's deed?

1. Locate the original will and apply to the Surrogate for letters after the tenth day.
2. Order several certified death certificates and a copy of the deed.
3. Find out who all beneficiaries are, and what class each falls into.
4. File Form L-9 if all are Class A and nothing is taxable. Otherwise prepare the full return.
5. Mail the 60-day notice to heirs and next of kin after probate. See the [probate process](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-probate-process).
6. When the waiver issues, the executor signs the deed to the heir or the buyer, and it is recorded with the county.
7. Settle the mortgage, taxes and any state claim from the sale proceeds or other estate funds.

The Division's materials do not describe the form of the executor's deed or whether a sale may close before a waiver arrives. Those points belong to your title company and attorney, and we will not guess at them here.

## Should the heirs sell or keep it?

Start with money. A house carries taxes, insurance and upkeep from the day of death, whatever the family decides. Count those costs against what each sibling would actually pay.

Take three siblings, Tom, Alicia and Priya. Tom wants to keep the house, the others want cash. Keeping works only if Tom can buy them out, by refinancing or by taking a smaller share of other assets. Otherwise a sale is the cleanest answer. If they cannot agree, the dispute can be heavy, and our article on [heirs' property](/learn/property-and-assets/heirs-property) shows how co-ownership goes wrong.

Tax basis helps with a sale. Most inherited assets get a step-up in basis, so a house sold soon after death for about its value at death usually produces little capital gain. Our articles on [selling inherited property](/learn/after-a-death/selling-inherited-property) and [whether heirs can sell during probate](/learn/probate/can-heirs-sell-the-house-during-probate) go through that in more depth.

## Does keeping the house affect property tax relief?

Be careful here. The Division of Taxation's property tax relief FAQ says ANCHOR applies only to the qualifying property you owned or rented and occupied on October 1. It tells a surviving spouse to apply as the sole applicant under ANCHOR, and to file Senior Freeze as the "surviving spouse" with a copy of the death certificate. The FAQ does not address adult children who inherit, so we do not assume any benefit passes to them. Ask the Division or an attorney before counting on it.

If the owner received Medicaid, there is another question before the house goes anywhere. [New Jersey Medicaid estate recovery](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-estate-recovery) explains how the state can claim against a home, and what it costs to find out too late.
