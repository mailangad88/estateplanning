When a spouse dies, the house is often already paid for in grief and paperwork. The property tax is the bill that keeps arriving, and New Jersey has several programs that lower it. None of them transfers by itself. Each has its own rule about what a survivor must send, and to whom.

This page lists the programs a widow or widower is most likely to be using: Senior Freeze, ANCHOR, Stay NJ, the senior and disabled deduction, and the veteran deductions. It explains what to refile and what the state says about the survivor. For the house itself, [inheriting a house in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/inheriting-a-house-in-new-jersey) covers the tax waiver and the deed, and our [New Jersey guide](/learn/new-jersey) places both in the bigger picture.

## What property tax relief can a surviving spouse get in New Jersey?

Five programs matter most, split between the Division of Taxation and your town.

| Program | Who runs it | What the survivor does |
|---|---|---|
| Senior Freeze | State | Cross out the spouse's name, print the date of death above it, and enclose a death certificate |
| ANCHOR | State | File as the surviving spouse and leave off the deceased spouse's name |
| Stay NJ | State | Same combined application; the Division's page does not address surviving spouses |
| $250 senior and disabled deduction | Town (assessor or tax collector) | Qualify as a survivor and renew yearly |
| $250 veteran deduction, 100% disabled veteran exemption | Town | Apply on the survivor forms listed below |

The state programs use one combined application. The Division says applicants submit a single application to claim benefits from Senior Freeze, ANCHOR and Stay NJ. The form is Form PAS-1, available online, and the deadline for the 2025 application is November 2, 2026. Because that date is close, ask whether a late filing is possible, or call the Division, if you are reading this after it passes.

## What does a survivor do about Senior Freeze?

Senior Freeze reimburses eligible seniors and disabled persons for property tax increases on their home. The Division's FAQ gives three survivor rules.

- Cross out the deceased spouse's name on the application and print the date of death above it, so the account can be corrected.
- Enclose a copy of the death certificate.
- Meet the requirements for the application year yourself. The program does not prorate, so a widow whose husband died in November does not file for the months he was alive. The home must be owned and occupied on the last day of the year.

Checks go to the surviving spouse if married and to the estate of the deceased applicant otherwise.

For the 2025 application, the income limit was $172,475, combined for a married or civil union couple living in the same home. The age test is that you or your spouse was 65 or older on December 31, 2025, or receiving federal Social Security or Railroad Retirement disability benefits on or before that date. You must have owned and lived in the home since December 31, 2022, or earlier. The Division also describes a one-time exemption when income goes over the limit for a year, which lets you keep your base year. An older survivor should ask about it if a spouse's life insurance or pension payout pushed income up.

## What about ANCHOR?

ANCHOR is a separate benefit for homeowners and renters whose main home was in New Jersey on October 1, 2025. Homeowners cannot have New Jersey gross income over $250,000, measured on line 29 of the NJ-1040. The Division automatically filed for most eligible homeowners under 65, but seniors and people on Social Security or Railroad Retirement disability benefits must file the combined PAS-1 themselves.

On a death, the Division says an executor or surviving spouse or civil union partner may file for an eligible homeowner who died on or after October 1, 2025. Attach a death certificate or similar document. Do not include the deceased spouse's name on the application. For a renter who died before filing, the survivor enters only their own name, even on a joint return, and a personal representative enters the name followed by "estate of," checks the deceased box and signs in an official capacity. When there is an estate, the benefit is issued in the estate's name, and the Division suggests asking the bank or an attorney before closing the estate account.

Stay NJ is one more benefit on the same application. For the 2025 year, it requires age 65 or older, ownership and residence for all 12 months of 2025, and total income of $200,000 or less. Its page does not address surviving spouses, so call the Division if your spouse's death changes the picture.

## Does the $250 senior and disabled deduction continue?

It can. This one is run by your municipality, not the state. A survivor qualifies if the person:

1. was 55 or older when the spouse or partner died, and is still 55 or older on December 31 of the pretax year;
2. owns and lives in the same home on which the deceased received the deduction, for at least a year before October 1;
3. has been a New Jersey resident and citizen for at least a year before that date;
4. has not remarried or entered a new civil union; and
5. meets the income limit.

File Form PTD and your proofs, such as a death certificate, with the local assessor or tax collector. To keep the deduction, file Form PD5 with the tax collector on or before March 1 each year. If the town denies the application, file Form A-1 with the County Board of Taxation on or before April 1 following the denial.

## And the veteran deductions?

Here the answer is narrower than most people expect. A veteran's $250 deduction does not pass on automatically. The Division says a surviving spouse or partner qualifies only if the veteran died on active duty and was honorably discharged, was a New Jersey resident at death, and the survivor owns the property and has not remarried. The form is V.S.S.

A separate 100% disabled veteran exemption has a surviving spouse provision. The survivor must be a New Jersey resident who owns and lives in the home, has not remarried, and can show a current VA certification that the veteran was 100% permanently and totally disabled from a service-connected disability. The form is D.V.S.S.E., filed with supporting documents at the local assessor's office. Denials follow the same A-1 appeal path. Burlington, Gloucester and Monmouth counties have a January 15 deadline instead of April 1.

## A worked example

Example only. Linda, 71, lives in Toms River. Her husband Ray, 74, died in August 2026. They had received Senior Freeze and the $250 senior deduction. Linda will file the combined PAS-1 for the 2026 year when that application opens, cross out Ray's name, print his date of death above it, and enclose the certificate. Because Ray died before the end of 2026, Linda needs to meet the program's own tests for that year.

She also files Form PD5 with the tax collector before March 1 to renew the senior deduction, since she was over 55 when Ray died and has not remarried. She orders extra certified copies first ([how many to order](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-death-certificates-who-can-order-and-how-many-you-need)), because the town, the state and her mortgage servicer each want one.

## What mistakes cost money?

- Missing the March 1 renewal for the municipal deduction.
- Filing the state application with the deceased spouse's name on it.
- Closing the estate account before a state check payable to the estate arrives.
- Assuming a child who inherits the house keeps the parent's relief. The state's published guidance for survivors addresses spouses, so ask before counting on any benefit as an heir.

A house held as tenants by the entirety keeps simple title with the survivor ([joint property in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/joint-property-and-tenancy-by-the-entirety-in-new-jersey)), and the survivor can keep living there while the paperwork catches up ([can I stay in the house after a spouse dies](/learn/after-a-death/can-i-stay-in-the-house-after-a-parent-or-spouse-dies)). Relief programs rarely come up in the estate itself, which is why they are missed. The Division's Property Administration unit takes questions at 609-292-7974 or Taxation.propadmin@treas.nj.gov.
