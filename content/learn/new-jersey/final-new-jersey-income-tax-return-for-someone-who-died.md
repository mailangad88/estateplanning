Someone dies in March. A paycheck stub, a pension statement and a bank 1099 land in the mail. By April of the next year, the family is asking whether New Jersey expects a return, and who signs it.

The short version: the decedent's last New Jersey return is an ordinary NJ-1040, filed by whoever is acting for the person, and it is separate from the New Jersey inheritance tax and from the federal return. This page covers when to file, who signs, how a refund works, and where the estate's own tax return fits. For the executor's whole to-do list, see [executor duties in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/executor-duties-in-new-jersey-step-by-step).

## Do you have to file a final New Jersey income tax return after a death?

If the person had enough New Jersey income, yes. The Division of Taxation's executor guidance tells executors to file the decedent's gross income tax returns when required. The 2025 NJ-1040 instructions set the amount by line 29 of the return, which is New Jersey gross income. You must file if that income is more than:

- $10,000 for a single filer or a married or civil union partner filing separately;
- $20,000 for married filing jointly, head of household or qualifying widow(er).

Those are the 2025 tax-year figures. They can change, so check the booklet for the year you are filing. If the person died in 2026, the return you file in spring 2027 uses that year's booklet.

Do not stop at the threshold, though. A decedent with $6,000 of income and $400 withheld from a pension is owed money, and a return is how the estate claims it. A refund is only paid on a filed return.

## When is the return due?

A calendar-year 2025 return was due April 15, 2026, according to the Division. The same pattern applies to later years, so for a person who died in 2026 the final return is due in April 2027. Federal rules for a decedent's return are covered in our national article on [final tax returns](/learn/after-a-death/final-tax-returns); New Jersey has its own booklet and its own filing channel (NJ-1040 can be filed online through the state's tax portal), so do not assume the federal filing equals the state one.

## Who signs for someone who died?

The executor or administrator signs for the decedent. Before letters are issued, there may be nobody with authority, which is one more reason to get through the [probate steps](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-probate-process) quickly. A surviving spouse or civil union partner can sign a joint return. The NJ-1040 booklet has a dedicated Deceased Taxpayers section, listed in its table of contents, that gives the wording for the signature area and any document to attach when claiming a refund. Read it for the tax year you are filing, since forms change.

For the federal return, the IRS uses its Form 1310 for someone claiming a refund owed to a deceased taxpayer, with exceptions for a surviving spouse on a joint return and for a court-appointed personal representative. Whether New Jersey uses an equivalent attachment for refunds is stated in its own booklet, so look there.

## How does a refund owed to a deceased taxpayer get paid?

The refund is an asset of the estate. If a personal representative files, the refund is paid to the estate, and the estate bank account must still be open to receive it. People close accounts too fast. Our article on [last paychecks, refunds and money owed to the deceased](/learn/after-a-death/what-happens-to-a-deceased-persons-last-paycheck-refunds-and-money-owed) explains the general holding pattern.

State refunds are also not the only money the state may owe. Property tax relief checks can be issued in the estate's name, as covered in [property tax relief after a spouse dies in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-property-tax-relief-after-a-spouse-dies). And dormant bank balances or uncashed checks may sit in state hands, which our article on [unclaimed property for a New Jersey estate](/learn/new-jersey/finding-unclaimed-property-for-a-new-jersey-estate) walks through.

## What filing status can a surviving spouse use?

The surviving spouse can file jointly for the year of death in the usual case. For later years, the 2025 NJ-1040 lists a Qualifying Widow(er) or surviving civil union partner status for someone whose spouse died in the two prior years and who meets the federal requirements. The status uses the $20,000 filing threshold, and the form asks you to fill in the oval for the year the spouse died.

The Division also publishes a tax guide for people losing a spouse or civil union partner. Ask for it if you are the survivor, because pension and insurance income can change what you owe in the year after.

## Does the estate file its own return?

Sometimes. New Jersey taxes the income of estates and trusts. The Division says estates and trusts with income of $10,000 or less for the entire year owe no tax. After death, interest on the estate account, rent on the house or the gain on a sale of property can all produce income. Many estates close before it matters, but a house that sits for a year and is sold at a gain may not. Talk to an accountant before you distribute; our [probate cost guide](/learn/new-jersey/probate-costs-in-new-jersey) lists accounting among the usual fees.

## What is the difference between this and the inheritance tax?

The final NJ-1040 is a tax on the decedent's income up to the date of death. The New Jersey inheritance tax is a tax on what certain heirs receive, and it depends on the heir's relationship to the person who died. Spouses and children pay none. The inheritance tax return, if one is required, is a different form and a different office, covered in [the New Jersey inheritance tax](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-inheritance-tax). Filing one does not satisfy the other.

## A worked example

Example only. Carol, a widow from Montclair, died in February 2026 with a small teacher's pension, interest on a savings account and no wages. Her son Marcus is executor. Her total New Jersey income to the date of death is about $9,200, below the single-filer line used on the 2025 form, but the pension payer withheld $310 in New Jersey tax.

Marcus waits for the 2026 booklet in January, files a final NJ-1040 for Carol, follows the Deceased Taxpayers instructions to claim the refund, and keeps the estate checking account open until the refund arrives. If he had skipped the return because her income was under the line, the $310 would have stayed with the state.

## A checklist for the executor

1. Gather the last W-2, 1099 and pension statements for the year of death.
2. Order certified death certificates so you can attach one when a form asks ([how many you need](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-death-certificates-who-can-order-and-how-many-you-need)).
3. Get the executor's letters from the Surrogate.
4. File the final NJ-1040 and the federal return for the same year.
5. Keep the estate account open until refunds clear.
6. Ask the Inheritance and Estate Tax Service Center at 609-292-5033 if a form is unclear.
