The word guardianship covers two very different situations. In one, a parent is gone or disabled and a child needs a legal caretaker. In the other, an adult can no longer manage money or health decisions and nobody has legal authority to step in. New Jersey handles them with different tools, and mixing them up is the most common confusion families bring us.

This page covers the adult court process, the options that can keep a family out of court, and how to name a guardian for your own children. It sits inside our [New Jersey estate planning guide](/learn/new-jersey). Court procedures change, so the court rules cited here should be checked against the current version before you file.

## How does adult guardianship work in New Jersey?

It is a lawsuit in the Superior Court of New Jersey, Chancery Division, Probate Part. The court may determine that a person is incapacitated and appoint a guardian for the person (N.J.S.A. 3B:12-25). The Surrogate reviews the complaint before it is docketed to confirm venue and that it includes what R. 4:86-2 requires (R. 4:86-3A). Court forms are on the New Jersey Courts site.

Here is the sequence in order.

1. **The complaint.** The plaintiff states his or her relationship to the person and interest in the case, and files with it certifications from two physicians, or one physician and one licensed practicing psychologist. Each must have examined the person personally no more than 30 days before filing (R. 4:86-2). Each states how far the person can still make specific decisions.
2. **An order fixing a hearing.** The court sets the date and appoints a lawyer for the person if there is none (R. 4:86-4). Evaluators related to the person by blood or marriage cannot certify (R. 4:86-3).
3. **Notice.** The person must be served in person at least 20 days before the hearing, with a separate notice that says he or she may oppose the case, appear with a lawyer and demand a jury trial. Next of kin and others, including any attorney-in-fact or health care representative, get notice by certified mail (R. 4:86-4).
4. **The lawyer's report.** The appointed lawyer interviews the person and checks for any existing will, power of attorney or health care directive. The report, due ten days before the hearing, must address less restrictive alternatives (R. 4:86-4).
5. **The hearing.** The person must attend unless the plaintiff and the appointed lawyer certify that he or she cannot because of incapacity (N.J.S.A. 3B:12-24.1). The judge decides if the person is incapacitated and what the guardian may do.
6. **The judgment.** The court appoints a guardian of the person, of the estate, or both (N.J.S.A. 3B:12-57 and 3B:12-43 describe those roles).

That is a significant process, with a court file, hearings, a lawyer's fee and a loss of control for the person. Those are the reasons we steer families toward documents signed in advance.

## Does the court have to take away all of the person's rights?

No. If the person lacks capacity for some but not all of the tasks needed to care for himself or herself, the court may appoint a limited guardian of the person, of the estate or of both (N.J.S.A. 3B:12-24.1). The court must make findings about what the person can still manage. The statute's list of areas includes residential, educational, medical, legal, vocational and financial decision-making. A limited guardian of the estate must post a bond unless the court relieves him or her of it.

That matters in real life. Margaret, 81, has early dementia. She is still sharp about where she wants to live and who she wants to see, but she has been sending checks to phone scammers. A limited guardianship over finances may fit better than a general one that decides everything. People who are subject to a guardianship also keep a path back: they may petition to modify or end it, or to restore rights (R. 4:86-7).

### What about an emergency?

A temporary guardian can be appointed before the full hearing if there is a critical need or a risk of substantial harm. An order entered without notice lasts 45 days unless extended for good cause (N.J.S.A. 3B:12-24.1). This is for a hospital that cannot discharge someone, or an account that is being drained, not for ordinary convenience.

## What can you do instead of guardianship?

The best alternative is the one that is signed before it is needed. If an adult still has capacity, three documents usually do the work.

| Tool | What it covers | Where to read more |
|---|---|---|
| Durable power of attorney | Money, property, bills, taxes | [New Jersey power of attorney](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-power-of-attorney) |
| Advance directive naming a health care representative | Medical decisions and end-of-life wishes | [New Jersey advance directives](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-advance-directives) |
| Living trust with a successor trustee | Assets placed in the trust | [Living trusts in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/living-trusts-in-new-jersey) |

The court rule itself treats these as significant: the appointed lawyer has to search for a will, power of attorney and health care directive, and the court looks at less restrictive options. If a valid power of attorney exists and the agent is doing the job, a family may have no need for a court case. Our article on [power of attorney versus guardianship](/learn/power-of-attorney/power-of-attorney-vs-guardianship) explains why.

For adults with a developmental or intellectual disability, other tools may keep decisions with the person. Supported decision-making means a trusted helper explains choices while the adult decides. I could not confirm a New Jersey statute that formalizes supported decision-making agreements, so ask an attorney how current law treats them. Our guide to [guardianship alternatives for adult children](/learn/special-needs/guardianship-alternatives-for-adult-children) covers the options, and a [special needs trust](/learn/special-needs/special-needs-trust) can hold money without a guardian of the estate.

### What if the person is turning 18?

A law approved on December 23, 2025 (P.L.2025, c.179) amended the guardianship statute. It lets a complaint to appoint a guardian for a minor who is expected to need one at 18 be filed up to 180 days before the 18th birthday. Any judgment cannot take effect before the minor turns 18. The act took effect on the first day of the fourth month after enactment, which is April 1, 2026 by our count. It also amends the developmental disability statute (N.J.S.A. 30:4-165.7) with the same timing.

For parents of a teenager with a disability, that means planning can start before the birthday and before decision-making authority disappears.

## How do you name a guardian for your minor children in a New Jersey will?

In your will. Either parent may, by will, appoint a guardian of the person and a guardian of the estate for children who are under 18 and unmarried at the parent's death (N.J.S.A. 3B:12-13). If the other parent is already gone, or was the guardian, a surviving parent may do so (N.J.S.A. 3B:12-15). The two roles can go to different people. One may be best at raising the child and the other at managing money.

Practical steps:

1. Pick a first choice and a backup, and ask both.
2. Decide whether one person should hold the money, or whether a trust is better. A [guardian of the person versus a conservator of the estate](/learn/guardianship/guardian-of-the-person-vs-conservator-of-the-estate) is a useful distinction.
3. Write the nomination into your will and keep the original safe.
4. Put your wishes in a letter for the guardian, as described in [letter of intent for your children's guardian](/learn/guardianship/letter-of-intent-for-your-childrens-guardian).

A will-named guardian is your direction, not a binding commitment from the court, and the court looks at the child's circumstances. Our article on [whether a will-named guardian holds up in court](/learn/guardianship/does-a-guardian-named-in-a-will-hold-up-in-court) explains that. With no nomination at all, a judge chooses, as described in [what happens if no guardian is named](/learn/guardianship/what-if-no-guardian-named). Our [guardian picker](/tools/guardian-picker) can help you weigh candidates. For the main guide to choosing, see [how to choose a guardian](/learn/guardianship/how-to-choose-a-guardian).
