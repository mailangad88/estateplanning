Most families first meet New Jersey Medicaid at a bad moment. A parent falls, a hospital social worker mentions rehab, and then someone says the words "long-term care." The bill for a nursing home can drain a lifetime of savings in a few years, and the question becomes whether the state program can help and what it will want to see.

This article explains how the program works in New Jersey as of 2026, using the state's own published figures. It does not repeat the national basics, which are in our [Medicaid planning guide](/learn/elder-care/medicaid-planning). It sits inside our [New Jersey estate planning and probate guide](/learn/new-jersey). Figures change every year, so treat the numbers as a snapshot and confirm them with the county welfare agency.

## What is NJ FamilyCare MLTSS?

MLTSS stands for Managed Long Term Services and Supports. It is the part of NJ FamilyCare, New Jersey's Medicaid program, that pays for long-term care for older adults and people with disabilities. According to the state's 2026 brochure, it covers care in assisted living, group homes, nursing homes and your own home. You do not have to move into a nursing home to apply.

Two agencies decide two different questions. The Division of Aging Services decides whether you are clinically eligible, meaning you need a nursing home level of care. The county welfare agency decides whether you are financially eligible. Both reviews run at the same time.

## What are the income and resource limits in 2026?

For a single applicant, the state's 2026 figures are these.

| Test | 2026 figure | Notes from the state |
|---|---|---|
| Countable resources | $2,000 or less | Home and primary vehicle excluded |
| Monthly income | $2,982 | Above this, a Qualified Income Trust is required |
| Self-attestation option | $1,330 a month or less | Fewer documents; resources still checked electronically |
| Life insurance | Face value over $1,500 | Cash value counts; letter from insurer needed |
| Home equity maximum | $1,130,000 | Listed in Medicaid Communication 26-05 |

Resources are measured on the first day of each month. That small rule catches people. If Dorothy has $2,400 in checking on the first, she is ineligible for that entire month, even if the nursing home bill takes the balance to zero on the third.

The income line is the number that surprises people with decent pensions. If your gross income is above $2,982, you cannot simply spend down your way in. You need a Qualified Income Trust, a written trust agreement with its own bank account, managed by a trustee who is not you. All income from a source, such as the whole pension check, goes into it. The county agency and the Division of Medical Assistance and Health Services (DMAHS) approve and monitor it. Our article on the [income cap trust](/learn/elder-care/income-cap-trust-miller-trust) explains the idea, and the state brochure warns that money placed in one may be subject to recovery after death.

## How does New Jersey's five-year look-back work?

The state reviews your income and resources for five years before your first MLTSS application, through the date of your current application. A caseworker is looking for transfers for less than fair market value. The brochure adds that undocumented transfers are treated as gifts and can start a penalty period during which Medicaid does not pay.

The length of the penalty comes from a divisor. DMAHS announced that, effective April 1, 2026, the divisor is $420.67 (up from $402.74). The days of penalty equal the value of the transferred asset divided by the divisor, rounded down, and the clock starts when you would otherwise be eligible. So a $42,067 gift produces a 100-day penalty. A gift of $100,000 produces 237 days.

Two points follow from that. First, a gift to a child does not become safe because it was small, because the penalty is a straight division. Second, the clock does not start on the date of the gift. It starts only once you are in care and otherwise qualified, which is why gifting while still healthy is a different conversation than gifting from a hospital bed. Our national article on the [look-back period](/learn/elder-care/medicaid-look-back-period) goes through exempt transfers, and the [look-back date tool](/tools/medicaid-lookback-date) helps you count back five years.

## How does New Jersey count a married couple?

When one spouse needs care and the other stays home, the state sets aside part of the couple's assets for the healthy spouse. The brochure says only that "some of your marital assets will be set aside." The numbers come from Medicaid Communications 26-01 and 26-05.

- **Resources.** The community spouse resource standard is the greater of $32,532 or one-half of the couple's countable resources, not to exceed $162,660.
- **Income.** The community spouse's monthly maintenance needs base figure is $2,705 as of July 1, 2026, with a shelter standard of $811.50. If the at-home spouse's own income falls short of the allowance, part of the applicant's income can be directed to the spouse.

Take Ruth and Gene. They have $150,000 in countable savings. Half is $75,000, which sits between the floor and the cap, so Ruth, at home, keeps $75,000. If they had only $50,000, half would be $25,000, so the $32,532 floor applies. If they had $400,000, half would be $200,000, but the cap holds her to $162,660.

The state also says a separated spouse does not make you "single" on the application. Spouses who are estranged but not divorced still count.

## What does the home do to eligibility?

The house you live in does not count toward the $2,000 limit, and neither does the vehicle you use. That exclusion is for eligibility only. It does not shield the house from the state after death. Whether and how DMAHS can claim against it is the subject of [New Jersey Medicaid estate recovery](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-estate-recovery), and a house that ends up in an estate is covered in [inheriting a house in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/inheriting-a-house-in-new-jersey).

## What are the steps from first call to approval?

1. Call the Area Agency on Aging at 1-877-222-3737 to schedule the clinical screening, or the number on your plan card if you are already enrolled.
2. Gather five years of bank, brokerage and retirement statements, plus deeds, insurance letters and records of any gift or sale.
3. File with the county welfare agency. The state brochure lists 1-800-356-1561 for the County Social Service Agency.
4. Answer the caseworker's requests for documents quickly. Missing paper is the most common cause of delay.
5. If income is above $2,982, set up the Qualified Income Trust before the month you want coverage to start.
6. Keep resources under $2,000 on the first day of every month once you are applying.

## What mistakes cost the most?

The first is giving the house to a child in a panic. A transfer of the house starts a penalty period, and it can also change the child's tax picture, which is why we usually look at [protecting your home from nursing home costs](/learn/elder-care/protecting-your-home-from-nursing-home-costs) before anything is signed. The second is waiting until the money is gone and then asking what to do. The third is trusting that someone else on the account will "sort it out." A [power of attorney](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-power-of-attorney) that lets an agent handle benefits applications makes the process far easier.
