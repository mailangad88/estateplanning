People expect New Jersey probate to be expensive, and some of it is. But the part that goes to the court is small and fixed. The money that adds up is paid to professionals, and one of the biggest items, executor commissions, can be waived by the family member who earns them.

This page sorts the costs of a New Jersey estate by who gets paid and who decides the amount. It is a companion to our national [probate costs guide](/learn/probate/probate-costs) and sits inside our [New Jersey estate planning and probate guide](/learn/new-jersey). Fee schedules come from county Surrogate pages, so confirm the current numbers with the office where the estate is filed.

## What do New Jersey Surrogate fees cost?

The Surrogate's charges are set by statute, N.J.S.A. 22A:2-30. Morris County's published schedule says its fees are issued under that section, and Middlesex County lists the same amounts for probate, letters, caveats and bond releases. Here are the items most estates meet:

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Probate of a will, up to 2 pages | $100 |
| Each additional will page | $5 |
| Codicil (1 page) | $25 |
| General administration (no will), with bond papers, letters and one certificate | $125 |
| Administration ad prosequendum | $50 |
| Certified copy of administration | $50 |
| Filing or withdrawing a caveat | $25 |
| Certified copy of will with proofs, up to 2 pages | $50 |
| Plain copy of a 2-page will | $10 |
| Refunding bond and release, up to 2 pages | $10 |

Some of these are counter items nobody plans for. Ben, executor for his mother, needs certified copies for three banks and a title company. That is a few hundred dollars of copies, not hundreds of thousands. Even so, a bill introduced in the Senate in November 2025 (S4911) would amend section 22A:2-30 and raise a number of these fees, so the figures can change. Call your county before budgeting. Atlantic County adds that its office takes cash or check only, and other counties accept different payment types.

The court fee does not grow with the estate. A family settling $80,000 pays the same $100 to probate a two-page will as a family settling $4 million.

## Does the executor or administrator need a bond?

A bond is an insurance policy that protects heirs and creditors if the fiduciary mishandles money. It does cost something, but not through the Surrogate.

- **Executor under a will.** Morris County's guide says a will can provide that the executor serves without bond. Many do.
- **Administrator without a will.** Atlantic County's Surrogate says a surety bond is required in most cases, under N.J.S.A. 3B:15-1. The Surrogate sets the amount based on the estate's value and the number of heirs, and the applicant buys the bond from an insurance agency before being appointed.

The premium is paid to the surety company, so the Surrogate's schedule has no line for it. At the end, every beneficiary signs a refunding bond and release, the $10 item above. Our article on [who inherits without a will in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/who-inherits-without-a-will-in-new-jersey) explains why intestate estates end up here.

A well-drafted will that waives the bond is a small way to cut cost and paperwork. That is one reason to have the will [reviewed against New Jersey rules](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-will-requirements).

## What do attorneys charge for New Jersey probate?

The statute does not fix attorney fees, so there is no number to quote. They depend on the work: a simple estate with a self-proving will, no dispute and one bank account is a different job than a house, a business and a quarrelling family. The useful question is how the fee is structured. Flat, hourly, or a percentage of the estate? What is included, and what is billed separately?

We cover this in depth in [probate lawyer fee agreements](/learn/probate/probate-lawyer-fee-agreements) and [whether you need a probate lawyer](/learn/probate/do-i-need-a-probate-lawyer). Our view is that a written fee agreement before the work starts avoids most of the arguments we see later. Many executors do the Surrogate filings themselves and pay a lawyer for review, tax waivers and the sale of real estate.

## What are executor commissions, and are they a real cost?

They are real when taken, and optional. N.J.S.A. 3B:18-14 sets corpus commissions at 5% of the first $200,000, 3.5% of the next $800,000 and 2% above $1,000,000, with an extra 1% for each additional fiduciary. The full explanation and worked examples are in [New Jersey executor commissions and how long an estate takes](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-executor-commissions-and-probate-timeline). The practical point for cost: commissions are income to the executor, and an inheritance usually is not, so a child who is also the main beneficiary often waives them.

## How do inheritance tax waivers add cost and delay?

The Division of Taxation lists no fee for a waiver, but time is a cost when a house is waiting to be sold. Its executor guide says real estate transfers always require a waiver that only the Division can issue, and tells families to plan for "at least several months" after filing a return. It also says an audit may add several months and that about 40 to 50 percent of returns are audited. The Division takes returns in the order received.

If tax is owed, the clock matters more. The tax is due eight months after death, and the Division says late tax accrues interest at 10 percent a year. The rates and classes are in [the New Jersey inheritance tax](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-inheritance-tax). If the estate is all Class A beneficiaries, Form L-9 asks for the real property waiver. Waiting on paper while the mortgage, property taxes and insurance keep running is where families lose money. A house that sits for a year of carrying costs can cost more than every court fee on the schedule combined.

Ask the title company early what paperwork it will want. The sale side of this is covered in [inheriting a house in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/inheriting-a-house-in-new-jersey).

## What else tends to cost money?

These are not set by any New Jersey statute, so budget by getting quotes:

- Appraisals of the house or other property, which also support the tax return and the basis.
- An accountant for the final income tax return and any estate return. See our guide to [final tax returns](/learn/after-a-death/final-tax-returns).
- Upkeep and insurance on an empty house during probate.
- Funeral costs, which the estate often pays first. Medicaid recovery can make the order of payment important; see [New Jersey Medicaid estate recovery](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-estate-recovery).

## Can you avoid some of these costs?

Sometimes. Small intestate estates may use the affidavit in [New Jersey small estate affidavit](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-small-estate-affidavit), where Morris County's schedule caps the Surrogate fee at $50 and no administrator is appointed. Assets with a named beneficiary or held jointly skip the probate fees entirely, but they do not skip the inheritance tax. What fits depends on the property. Our guide to [ways to avoid probate](/learn/probate/how-to-avoid-probate) compares the options.
