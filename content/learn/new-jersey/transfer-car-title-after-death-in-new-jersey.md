A car looks like the simplest asset in an estate, and in New Jersey it usually is. But the Motor Vehicle Commission (MVC) will not retitle it on a phone call or through the mail. You go to an agency, in person, with the right paper for the way the title was held.

This page walks through the four situations the MVC itself separates: a car titled to two spouses or partners, a car with a transfer on death beneficiary, a car in one person's name with a will, and a car in one person's name with no will. It sits inside our [New Jersey estate guide](/learn/new-jersey). For boats, RVs and the national picture, see [transferring a car, boat or RV after death](/learn/property-and-assets/transfer-a-car-boat-or-rv-after-death).

## How do you transfer a car title after death in New Jersey?

You make an appointment at an MVC Vehicle Center and bring the signed old title, a death certificate and whichever affidavit or court paper fits the ownership. The MVC's deceased-owner instructions say transfers must be done in person at a motor vehicle agency and cannot be handled by mail. For questions about a deceased owner, the MVC's customer support line is (609) 292-6500.

Before you leave the house, work out which of these four boxes you are in. The documents are different in each.

| How the car was owned | What you bring | Fee listed by the MVC |
|---|---|---|
| Titled to spouses or domestic partners together | Current title signed by the survivor, death certificate, notarized affidavit (Form BA-62) | $60 title fee, plus $4.50 to transfer the registration to an immediate family member |
| Transfer on death beneficiary named | The owner's TOD beneficiary form, the title, a death certificate, Universal Title Application (OS-SS-UTA) | Not listed on the page |
| One owner, with a will | Old title signed by the executor, Surrogate's Short Certificate, driver's license, EIN if the title goes to the estate | $60, or $85 if financed |
| One owner, no will | Signed title plus a Surrogate-sealed affidavit or an Administrator's Short Certificate | Not listed on the page |

## What if the car was titled to a husband and wife?

The survivor signs the current title and brings a copy of the death certificate and a notarized affidavit on Form BA-62. The title fee is $60, and moving the existing registration to an immediate family member costs an additional $4.50. This is the least painful route. No Surrogate visit is needed, because the car passes by title rather than through the estate.

Get the notary done before the appointment. Banks and many shipping stores notarize, and an unnotarized BA-62 means a second trip. If the couple also owned a house, the rules there are different, and our article on [joint property and tenancy by the entirety](/learn/new-jersey/joint-property-and-tenancy-by-the-entirety-in-new-jersey) explains why.

## What does a transfer on death beneficiary need to do?

The beneficiary presents the form the owner completed, the title, the death certificate and a Universal Title Application (OS-SS-UTA). The MVC says the vehicle is not part of the estate, so no letters of administration are required. That is the whole point of the designation, and it is why an owner who wants the car to go to one child should consider adding one. Our article on [transfer on death options in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/transfer-on-death-options-in-new-jersey) covers how to set one up and what happens if the beneficiary dies first.

## What does an executor need when there is a will?

The executor brings the old title signed by the executor, the Surrogate's Short Certificate, and a driver's license. If the title is going to the estate rather than to an individual, the estate needs an Employer Identification Number first. No EIN is needed if the title goes straight to a person. The fee is $60, or $85 for a financed vehicle.

Two practical points. A Short Certificate is a summary of the letters the Surrogate issues once the will is admitted, and county Surrogates say probate comes only after the tenth day following death, so the car cannot be retitled in the first week. If the executor will drive or insure the car before it is sold or given out, the MVC says a new registration and proof of New Jersey insurance in the estate's name are needed. Our [executor duties guide](/learn/new-jersey/executor-duties-in-new-jersey-step-by-step) sets out the order of the first month.

## What if there was no will and no co-owner?

The amount of the estate decides the paperwork. The MVC's instructions break it into three cases.

1. **Surviving spouse, domestic partner or civil union partner, estate not over $50,000:** the survivor signs the title and brings an Affidavit of Surviving Spouse/Domestic Partner/Civil Union Partner bearing the County Surrogate's raised seal.
2. **No surviving spouse or partner, only heirs, estate not over $20,000:** the next of kin signs the title and brings an Affidavit of Next of Kin with the Surrogate's raised seal.
3. **Estate above those amounts:** an administrator is appointed, and the title is signed by the administrator with an Administrator's Short Certificate bearing the Surrogate's raised seal.

These are the same thresholds as the general [New Jersey small estate affidavit](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-small-estate-affidavit). The catch is that the affidavit measures the whole estate, not the car. A family that has already used the affidavit for a bank account counts everything together. If you are not sure which side of the line you are on, [administering a New Jersey estate without a will](/learn/new-jersey/administering-a-new-jersey-estate-without-a-will) explains who may be appointed.

## What about a car loan?

The MVC says liens on the decedent's title must be paid before a new title is issued, unless the lien is transferred to the sole owner. In practice the lender holds the title, or an electronic record of it, so start with a call to the lender for a payoff figure and its estate paperwork. If the payoff is more than the car is worth, the estate may owe the difference depending on the loan terms, and that difference is a creditor claim. See [creditor claims and paying debts of a New Jersey estate](/learn/new-jersey/creditor-claims-and-paying-debts-of-a-new-jersey-estate) for the order of payment.

## A worked example

Example only. Priya's father, Anand, died in Edison owning a 2018 sedan titled in his name alone, with no loan. He left no will. His only other assets were a checking account of about $8,000 and a few thousand in a retirement account with a named beneficiary. Priya is his only child and next of kin, and the probate estate is well under $20,000.

Priya asks the Middlesex County Surrogate for an Affidavit of Next of Kin, has it sealed, and books an MVC appointment. She brings the title signed in her own name, the sealed affidavit, her license and a certified death certificate. The MVC lists the $60 title fee for a standard vehicle. If Anand had also owned a rental property, the estate would probably cross $20,000 and she would need to be appointed administrator instead.

## Common mistakes our attorneys see

- Signing the title as the dead owner. Sign only in the space for the survivor, executor or administrator.
- Selling the car to a dealer before any court paper exists. Dealers and buyers will want the same Surrogate documents.
- Letting the insurance lapse. A decedent's registration may remain valid until it expires, but an uninsured crash is a family problem, not an estate problem.
- Bringing photocopies of the death certificate when the agency asks for a certified one. See [how many death certificates you need](/learn/new-jersey/new-jersey-death-certificates-who-can-order-and-how-many-you-need).
