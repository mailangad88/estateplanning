Many grandparents never plan to raise a second family. A daughter struggles with addiction. A son is deployed or in prison. A parent dies. The child needs a home that night, and the grandparent says yes. Months later the school asks for a signature the grandparent has no right to give, and the pediatrician wants a parent's consent.

This page covers the legal gap and how to close it, then turns to the plan you need as the person now responsible. It is part of our [life stages guide](/learn/life-stages), and the guardianship terms are explained further in our [comparison of guardianship, custody and adoption](/learn/guardianship/guardianship-vs-custody-vs-adoption).

## Do grandparents have legal rights to a grandchild they are raising?

No, not automatically. Care and legal authority are separate things. A parent holds legal custody until a court decides otherwise, and a grandparent who has the child at home holds only what the parent has handed over or a court has granted.

That gap shows up in ordinary tasks. Schools may require a parent or legal guardian to enroll a child. A doctor may refuse non-emergency care without consent. A passport application needs the parent's signature. In an emergency, hospitals treat the child, but they may ask hard questions about who you are.

## What papers can you get, and how do they compare?

Four options cover most cases, from a quick form to a permanent change.

| Option | Who signs or decides | How long it lasts | Parent's rights |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent's written authorization | The parent signs | Until the parent revokes it | Unchanged |
| Court-ordered guardianship | A judge | Until the child turns 18 or the court ends it | Parent may seek to end it |
| Custody order | A judge | Until changed by court | Reduced, depends on order |
| Adoption | A judge | Permanent | Ended |

The first is the fastest and the easiest to lose. It works when the parent is cooperative and reachable. A signed note on school and medical consent, with the parent's contact information, solves a surprising number of problems. It does not stop the parent from showing up and taking the child back.

Guardianship or custody gives you lasting authority without ending the parent's rights, which is why families choose it when the parent may recover. The process is described in our guide to [petitioning for guardianship of a minor](/learn/guardianship/how-to-petition-for-guardianship-of-a-minor-child). Adoption is final and can reach inheritance, benefits and identity. It is a different decision from the others, and it is usually chosen when the parents will not return.

## What are the Illinois and New Jersey routes?

Illinois law provides a guardianship of a minor in Article XI of the Probate Act. A court may appoint a guardian when it serves the child's best interest. If a parent is willing and able to make and carry out day-to-day child care decisions, the court generally lacks jurisdiction unless that parent consents or does not object (see our [Illinois guardianship article](/learn/illinois/illinois-guardianship)). For short absences, Illinois also has standby and short-term guardians, covered in [our Illinois guide](/learn/illinois/illinois-standby-and-short-term-guardians-for-children).

New Jersey handles guardianship of the person for minors through the Superior Court, and it also has the Kinship Legal Guardianship Act. That law lets a relative or family friend become the child's legal guardian when the parents cannot raise the child, without ending parental rights. Details such as how long the child must have lived with the caregiver are set by statute, so confirm them with an attorney or the court before relying on them. See also [guardianship in New Jersey](/learn/new-jersey/guardianship-in-new-jersey).

Fees, forms and local practice differ by county. A call to the circuit or Superior Court clerk will tell you which forms are used.

## What should you do first? A short checklist

<!-- visual: timeline id=making-a-plan -->

1. **Gather the child's records.** Birth certificate, Social Security card, immunization records, insurance cards, school records.
2. **Get a signed authorization from a parent.** Even if you plan to go to court, this covers the gap.
3. **Make copies and keep a binder.** Include the parent's phone number and any court orders.
4. **Ask the school and doctor what they accept.** Some want a specific form.
5. **Talk to an attorney about guardianship or custody.** Especially if the parent is hard to reach, unwell, or may take the child back at a bad moment.
6. **Check benefits.** Health coverage, school meals and caregiver programs depend on legal status. Ask before you assume.

## How do you plan for the children if something happens to you?

You are now the person the child depends on, and many grandparents are 60 or older with their own health problems. A short plan makes a large difference.

- **A power of attorney and healthcare directive** for yourself, so someone can act if you are hurt and the child still needs care. See [durable financial power of attorney](/learn/power-of-attorney/durable-financial-power-of-attorney).
- **A standby or successor caregiver.** Your will cannot appoint a guardian for a child whose parent is alive, because the parent has the first right. But you can arrange a standby guardian or short-term arrangement, and you can write a letter naming who you trust. Our article on [temporary and standby guardians](/learn/guardianship/temporary-and-standby-guardians) explains the options.
- **A court order that survives you.** If you have been appointed guardian, ask your attorney whether the order lets you nominate a successor, and what the court would do on your death.
- **A letter to the next caregiver.** Routines, medical needs, school contacts, and the child's own wishes.

Take Dolores, 66, who has raised her grandson Mateo, 7, since her daughter entered treatment. She holds a parental authorization and has filed for guardianship. She signs a power of attorney naming her sister, a healthcare directive, and a letter listing Mateo's inhaler, school contact and teacher. She also tells her sister she would be her choice for Mateo. If Dolores is hospitalized the sister can take over the day-to-day and call the court.

## How do you leave money to a grandchild you are raising?

Do not leave it to the child outright. A minor cannot manage property, and a court may need to appoint a guardian of the estate. Better options include a trust, a custodial account under the Uniform Transfers to Minors Act, or life insurance payable to a trust. Our articles on [leaving money to minors](/learn/guardianship/leaving-money-to-minors) and on [leaving an inheritance to grandchildren](/learn/life-stages/leaving-an-inheritance-to-grandchildren) compare them.

If the child receives benefits because of a disability, a direct inheritance can end them, and a [special needs trust](/learn/special-needs/special-needs-trust) may be needed. Pick a trustee who is not you, if you can, so the person who raises the child does not have to ask a court for each expense. Our [guardian fund calculator](/tools/guardian-fund-calculator) can help you estimate what the child may need until adulthood.

## What if the parent comes back?

A parent with legal rights can usually ask the court to end a guardianship or change custody. A judge looks at what is best for the child. A parent who has recovered and shown stability has a real claim, and a grandparent who has been the stable home has one too. Neither of you wins by default.

Keep records of the child's health, school and daily life, and keep the relationship with the parent civil. Courts notice. If you fear the child is unsafe, say so to the court, and call child protective services if there is immediate danger.
