Most of this course has been about what happens after you die. Today is about something people are more likely to face: a time when you are alive but cannot make your own decisions.

That could be after an accident, a stroke, surgery or dementia. It can last a few days or many years.

## What incapacity means legally

Incapacity means you cannot understand or communicate decisions about your money or health. When that happens, someone else needs legal authority to act.

If you have signed the right documents, the people you chose can step in. If you have not, your family may need to ask a court to appoint a guardian or conservator. That can be slow, costly and public, and the court may not choose who you would.

Rules for when someone is considered incapacitated vary by state.

## The documents that cover incapacity

### Health care directive

A [health care directive](/guides/healthcare-directives-and-living-wills) usually does two things. It names your health care agent. It also states your wishes about treatment, such as life support, feeding tubes or comfort care. Some states call parts of it a living will or a health care proxy.

### HIPAA release

This lets doctors share your health information with the people you name. Without it, privacy rules can keep your family in the dark.

### Financial power of attorney

A [financial power of attorney](/guides/powers-of-attorney) lets your agent pay bills, manage accounts and deal with insurance while you cannot.

### A revocable living trust

If you have a [revocable living trust](/learn/trusts/revocable-living-trust), your successor trustee can manage the assets in it if you become unable to. This can be simpler for banks than a power of attorney.

### Medical orders

Some states use a form signed by your doctor, often called a POLST or MOLST. It tells emergency workers what treatment you want. It is usually for people who are seriously ill.

## Questions to think about

There are no right answers. These are personal choices.

- If you were in a coma with little chance of waking, would you want machines to keep you alive?
- Would you want a feeding tube if you could not eat on your own?
- Is staying at home more important to you than every possible treatment?
- Do your religious or personal beliefs shape these choices?
- Who would you trust to make hard calls, even if others disagree?

## Talk to your health care agent

A document helps, but a conversation helps more. Your agent will feel more confident if they have heard your wishes from you.

## A quick example

Daniel is 63. After thinking it over, he writes:

"If I am unlikely to recover and cannot recognise my family, I do not want life support or a feeding tube. I want pain relief and to be at home if possible. I trust my daughter Leah to decide. If my son disagrees, Leah has the final say."

Those five sentences give an attorney what they need to draft his directive. They also give Leah clear guidance.

## Today's task

Write a few sentences about the medical care you would and would not want if you could not speak for yourself. Then add the name of the person you trust to decide for you.
