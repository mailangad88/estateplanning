Today you make a list of what you own and what you owe. It does not need to be exact. Rough numbers are fine.

This list helps an attorney see which documents fit you. It also saves time, and often money, at your first meeting.

## Why an inventory matters

The kind of plan that fits depends on what you have. A renter with a 401(k) needs a different plan than someone with two homes and a business.

How each asset is owned matters as much as its value. Some assets pass by your will. Others pass by a beneficiary form or by joint ownership, no matter what your will says. Your inventory helps sort them.

## What to include

### Real estate

For each property, note the address, rough value, mortgage balance, and whose name is on the deed. If you own property in another state, say so. It can mean a second probate.

### Bank and investment accounts

List checking, savings, CDs and brokerage accounts. Note the bank, the rough balance, and whether it is in your name alone, joint, or has a payable-on-death beneficiary.

### Retirement accounts

List 401(k)s, 403(b)s, IRAs, pensions and annuities, including old ones from past jobs. Note who is listed as beneficiary, if you know.

### Life insurance

List each policy: the company, the death benefit, and the beneficiary. Include policies through work.

### A business

If you own a business, note the type (LLC, corporation, sole owner), your share, and any co-owners. The guide to [business succession planning](/guides/business-succession-planning) explains why this matters.

### Vehicles and valuables

Cars, boats, jewelry, art and collections. Note anything with sentimental value, even if it is not worth much.

### Digital assets

Online accounts, photos, crypto and websites. Today is also a good time to start a separate [digital assets inventory](/checklists/digital-assets-inventory).

### Debts

Mortgage, car loans, credit cards, student loans and any money you have co-signed for.

## How to record ownership

Use simple labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mine | In my name only |
| Joint | Owned with someone else |
| Beneficiary | Has a named beneficiary |
| Trust | Already in a trust |
| Not sure | I will check |

"Not sure" is a perfectly good answer today.

## A quick example

Lisa is 47 and single, with one daughter. Her list takes 20 minutes:

| Asset | Value | Ownership |
|---|---|---|
| House, Elm Street | $380,000, owes $210,000 | Mine |
| Checking, First Bank | $8,000 | Mine |
| Savings, First Bank | $25,000 | Not sure |
| 401(k), current job | $140,000 | Beneficiary: my mother |
| Old 403(b), hospital job | $30,000 | Not sure |
| Life insurance, through work | $100,000 | Beneficiary: daughter |
| Car | $15,000 | Mine |

She realises her 401(k) still names her mother. She also notices the life insurance names her daughter, who is 12. Both are things to raise with an attorney.

## Today's task

List your main assets and debts, with a rough value and how each one is owned. Aim for 30 minutes, not perfection.

Do not write down full account numbers or passwords on this list. The last four digits are enough to identify an account.
