## What is at stake

- **Medical decisions and visitation.** A healthcare power of attorney and HIPAA release name the person who makes decisions and can talk to doctors, regardless of what a relative says. See [living will vs healthcare power of attorney](/compare/living-will-vs-healthcare-power-of-attorney).
- **A contested plan.** If a family has not been supportive, an estate plan that is clear, signed correctly and shows capacity helps reduce the chance of a successful challenge.
- **Funeral and burial decisions.** Many states let you name the person who controls disposition of your remains. If you do not, default rules often give control to a spouse and then to blood relatives. For unmarried partners and chosen family, that matters. [State check]. See the [funeral and burial wishes worksheet](/checklists/funeral-and-burial-wishes).
- **Parentage.** Marriage and a birth certificate are not always enough for the non-biological or non-gestational parent. Many family law attorneys recommend an adoption or a court judgment of parentage for added security, because a judgment is generally recognized across state lines. [State check; VERIFY AT PUBLISH]. We coordinate with a family law attorney. [ATTORNEY: whether the firm handles adoptions]
- **Marriage recognition.** Same-sex marriage is recognized nationwide, and federal law requires recognition of valid marriages. [VERIFY AT PUBLISH: confirm current law]. Couples who never married may have no automatic rights.
- **Unmarried partners and chosen family.** Without documents, a partner of 20 years may have no legal standing. A will, trust, healthcare directive and beneficiary forms fix most of it. See [estate planning for unmarried couples](/blog/estate-planning-for-unmarried-couples).
- **Names and gender markers.** Transgender and nonbinary clients may need documents drafted with the correct legal name, the name they use, and prior names for matching records. We handle that with care.

## What a plan usually includes

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| Healthcare power of attorney, HIPAA release, living will | Names your decision-maker and gives them access. |
| Durable power of attorney | Names who handles money and legal matters. |
| Will or living trust | Names heirs, including a spouse, partner, chosen family or a charity, and an executor or trustee. |
| Funeral and disposition instructions | Names who decides and records your wishes. [State check] |
| Beneficiary review | Accounts, insurance and retirement plans point to the people you intend. |
| Guardian and trust for children | A guardian named in the will, plus a trust for the child's money. |
| Parentage coordination | Referral to or coordination with a family law attorney for adoption or a judgment of parentage. |
| Documents with the right names | Prior and current names recorded accurately. |
| Plan for estrangement | Written statements and a plan to reduce the risk of a successful challenge. |

**What we do not do.** We do not give immigration advice, we cannot promise that any document stops a challenge, and we do not use clients' stories or images in marketing without written consent.

**An honest limit.** A will alone does not settle a parentage question. Some matters belong to a family law attorney.
