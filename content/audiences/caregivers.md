## Where are you right now?

**My parent understands and can sign.** Then the work is simple and fast. A durable power of attorney for finances, a healthcare power of attorney and a HIPAA release cover most of what families get stuck on. An attorney assesses capacity at signing. A doctor's note from the same week helps if there is any doubt. Capacity is not all or nothing: a person with early dementia may be able to sign on a good morning. [State check: capacity standard]

**I am not sure.** Book the consultation and bring what you have. We do not diagnose anyone. The attorney talks with your parent, asks about their finances, family and wishes, and decides whether they can understand what they are signing. If not, we tell you plainly and talk about next steps.

**My parent cannot sign.** A power of attorney cannot be signed after capacity is gone. The remaining options are a court-appointed guardian or conservator, or narrower tools such as a representative payee for Social Security or a bank's own agent forms. [VERIFY AT PUBLISH: representative payee rules]. See [Power of attorney vs guardianship](/compare/power-of-attorney-vs-guardianship).

## What is at stake

- **The bank says no.** Your mother has had a stroke and her account is in her name only. Without an agent document, the bank cannot let you pay anything, even if you are her only child.
- **The hospital wants a decision.** If your father cannot decide and no healthcare agent is named, state law sets a default order of who may decide, often spouse, then adult children. With three siblings who disagree, that default is where fights start. [State check: default order]
- **The house has to be sold to pay for care.** Without authority to sign, a sale waits for a court.
- **You are paying bills from your own account.** Many caregivers do. Without a written agreement it can look like a gift to a Medicaid reviewer, and it can make siblings suspicious.
- **A Medicaid look-back is coming.** Most states look back 60 months at transfers when someone applies for nursing-home Medicaid <!-- verify --> [VERIFY AT PUBLISH: 42 U.S.C. 1396p(c)]. Moving a house or an account to a child in a hurry can cause a penalty period. See [Medicaid and long-term care planning](/guides/medicaid-and-long-term-care-planning) and the [look-back date calculator](/tools/medicaid-lookback-date).

If this is you, you are not behind. You are doing a hard job with partial paperwork.

## What a plan for your parent usually includes

| Document or step | What it does |
|---|---|
| Durable power of attorney (finances) | Names an agent to pay bills, manage accounts, deal with the bank and sell property. Durable means it survives incapacity. |
| Healthcare power of attorney and living will | Names who speaks for your parent medically and records their wishes. See [Living will vs healthcare power of attorney](/compare/living-will-vs-healthcare-power-of-attorney). |
| HIPAA authorization | Lets doctors talk to you. See [What a HIPAA release is](/blog/what-is-a-hipaa-release-and-why-does-it-belong-in-your-plan). |
| Will or living trust check | Confirms the plan matches the family and property today. |
| Beneficiary check | Retirement accounts and life insurance follow the form, not the will. See the [beneficiary designation audit](/checklists/beneficiary-designation-audit). |
| Asset and contact list | The inventory your agent will need. See the [asset and account inventory](/checklists/asset-and-account-inventory). |

**What we do not do.** We do not sign for your parent, and we do not take instructions from you that your parent has not agreed to when they can speak for themselves. If your parent is the client, they are the client.

**An honest limit.** If your parent is healthy and engaged, a short attorney meeting is usually enough. A guardianship or a trust may not be called for, and we will say so.
