## What is at stake

- **Disability.** A population-level risk, not a physician-specific figure. Physicians often buy own-occupation disability insurance. A plan makes sure a power of attorney and a business partner agreement fit with it.
- **Practice succession.** A partner leaves or dies, the practice is sold, or the physician is an employee with a non-compete. Buy-sell terms and entity documents matter. See the [business succession guide](/guides/business-succession-planning).
- **Liability exposure.** Malpractice policies have limits. Personal assets in a spouse's name, in trusts, or in exempt accounts such as retirement plans, a homestead or tenancy by the entirety are treated differently depending on state law. [State check]. Planning done before any claim, for legitimate reasons, is different from transfers made after a claim arises, which courts can reverse. Fraudulent transfer laws can unwind transfers made to avoid known or likely claims.
- **Estate tax is usually not the first issue.** The 2026 federal exclusion is $15 million per person <!-- verify -->. Some states tax lower amounts. [State check]. See [estate and inheritance taxes](/guides/estate-and-inheritance-taxes) and the [estate tax estimator](/tools/estate-tax-estimator).
- **Young physicians and residents.** The first attending paycheck often arrives with a mortgage, a baby and large student loans. A short plan covers wills, a guardian, powers of attorney, beneficiary forms and coordination with term life insurance.
- **Entity and title coordination.** Your practice entity, a real estate entity and personal assets should not contradict each other.

## What a plan usually includes

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| Foundation plan | Wills or a living trust, powers of attorney, healthcare directives and beneficiary review. |
| Practice review | Entity documents, partner agreements, buy-sell, and succession or sale readiness. |
| Disability plan | A power of attorney with practice authority, a backup signer, and coordination with disability insurance. |
| Titling review | How assets are held, what state law protects, and what is simply good housekeeping. [State check] |
| Spousal planning | Whether a spousal trust or other tool is worth its cost and complexity for your numbers. |
| Early-career package | A short, flat-fee plan for families with a baby and student debt. |
| Coordination | With your CPA, insurance agent and financial planner, with your permission and no referral fees. |

**What we do not do.** We do not promise protection from claims, advise on malpractice defense, sell insurance, or help move assets in the face of a known claim.

**An honest limit.** If your estate is below the federal exclusion and you have no practice ownership, a basic plan is usually enough. We will say so, even though a more complex plan costs more.
