## What is at stake

- **Beneficiary forms that never got updated.** Your 401(k), work life insurance and bank accounts may still name a parent, an ex or no one. Those forms usually override a will. See the [beneficiary designation audit](/checklists/beneficiary-designation-audit).
- **No one to act if you are hurt.** Without a power of attorney, a spouse may be unable to access your accounts or sign for you. Without a healthcare directive, doctors may turn to your parents. See [powers of attorney](/guides/powers-of-attorney) and [healthcare directives](/guides/healthcare-directives-and-living-wills).
- **Unmarried partners.** Most states give an unmarried partner no right to inherit and no automatic say in medical care. Your parents or siblings may have both. [State check: unmarried partners]
- **Joint accounts and a joint home.** Joint ownership often passes the whole asset to the survivor. That can be what you want. It can also leave you with one name on a deed and no plan if you both die.
- **Assuming the law sorts it out.** Without a will, state default rules decide who inherits. They do not know your family. [State check: intestacy]

You do not need a large plan for this. You need a few documents and a short list of forms.

## What a starter plan usually includes

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| Simple wills | Name who gets what, and who is in charge, if one or both of you die. |
| Durable power of attorney | Names who can handle money and sign papers if you cannot. |
| Healthcare directive and proxy | Names who speaks for you in a hospital and records your wishes. |
| HIPAA release | Lets your partner talk to doctors and see records. |
| Beneficiary forms | Name the right person on retirement accounts, insurance and bank accounts. |
| Digital access list | Where your passwords and accounts are, in a place your partner can find. |

**You may not need an attorney for every piece.** Updating a beneficiary form is free and you can do it yourself, usually online. Many couples with few assets and no children start there. An attorney helps most when you own a home, are not married, have a child from a past relationship, or expect an inheritance.

**Life insurance.** If someone depends on your income, such as a partner or a future mortgage, term insurance is worth a look. We do not sell insurance. A licensed agent can help you size it.

**What we do not do.** We do not tell you who should be your agent or beneficiary. We help you think it through and put it in writing.

## When life changes, the plan changes

A wedding, a first home and a first child are the usual triggers. Each one is a good reason to check your forms again. See [estate planning after getting married](/life-events/getting-married) and [estate planning when you buy a home](/life-events/buying-a-home).

If a baby is on the way, read [estate planning for new parents](/estate-planning-for/new-parents). If you own a home and have school-age children, see [homeowners and growing families](/estate-planning-for/homeowners-and-growing-families).

**An honest limit.** A starter plan is not a full estate plan. It covers the gaps that cause the most trouble when you have few assets. It will need an update as your life grows.
