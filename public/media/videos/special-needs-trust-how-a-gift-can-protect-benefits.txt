Special needs trust: how a gift can protect benefits

If your child receives benefits like SSI or Medicaid, a direct inheritance can put those benefits at risk. These programs limit the money a person can hold. For SSI, that is two thousand dollars for one person. An inheritance can push someone past it. A special needs trust holds the inheritance instead, and a trustee spends it on extras. Think therapies, travel, a phone, activities. It supplements benefits. It doesn't replace them. There are different types, depending on whose money funds it. The rules differ, including what happens to leftover funds. Many families also write a letter of intent, describing routines, doctors, and what makes their child happy. Every situation is different.
