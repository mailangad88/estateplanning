What a power of attorney can and cannot do

A power of attorney lets someone you trust handle your affairs if you can't. It's one of the most useful documents you can sign. A durable one keeps working if you become incapacitated. That is when it matters most. Your agent can often pay bills, manage accounts, file taxes, and deal with property, within the powers you give. They can't make a will for you, and the authority ends when you die. And they must act in your interest, not their own. Some powers, like making large gifts or changing your trust, usually need to be spelled out. Banks may ask for their own forms, so plan ahead. Pick carefully, and name a backup.
