How a blended-family plan can protect a spouse and children

In a blended family, the usual plan can backfire. Meet Pat, who remarried and has two kids from before. Pat leaves everything to the new spouse, trusting them to take care of the kids later. But once it's the spouse's, the spouse decides. A new will, a new partner, even a fair decision, and the kids may get nothing. The reverse also happens. Pat leaves everything to the kids, and the spouse may lose the home. A trust can split the roles. The spouse gets income, or use of the home, for life. After that, what remains goes to the children. A neutral trustee can reduce conflict. Clear writing helps too, about who pays taxes, repairs, and insurance. Beneficiary forms, a prenup, and titling matter just as much, because they can override the plan. Every family is different. A short conversation can show you the options.
