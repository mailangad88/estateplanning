First 30 days after a loved one dies: a calm checklist

I'm sorry you're here. If someone you love has died, there's a lot to do, but not all of it today. Let's go one step at a time. First few days. Get several certified copies of the death certificate. The funeral home often helps order them. You'll need them for many accounts. Secure the home, pets, and mail. Let close family and key people know. Social Security is usually notified by the funeral home. Find the documents. Look for a will, trust, insurance policies, and account lists. If you find a will, don't change or throw it away, even if it looks old. Week one to two. Speak to an attorney if there's a will or trust, a house, or complicated family. Many offer a first conversation. Keep paying essential bills, like mortgage or rent, utilities, and insurance, so nothing lapses. Keep a log of what you pay and why. Week three to four. Ask for life insurance claim forms, and check retirement accounts for named beneficiaries. Avoid moving money out of the person's accounts yet. Don't rush big decisions. Selling the house, distributing items, or paying heirs early can create problems later. Court filings, if needed, usually start within weeks to months, and deadlines vary by state. A professional can tell you which apply. Look after yourself too. Ask for help. People usually want to. Our checklist keeps all of this in one place. And when you are ready, we're glad to talk.
