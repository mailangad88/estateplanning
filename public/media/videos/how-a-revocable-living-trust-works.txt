How a revocable living trust works

A living trust is like a container you create to hold your assets. You usually wear three hats: the person who creates it, the one who manages it, and, while alive, the one who benefits. Then you move assets into it. This step is called funding, and it is the part people forget. If you become unable to manage things, the successor trustee you chose steps in, without a court. When you die, the trust assets pass to your beneficiaries as you set out, often without probate. Retirement accounts usually stay as they are, with a beneficiary form. A trust costs more up front.
