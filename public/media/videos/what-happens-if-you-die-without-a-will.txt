What happens if you die without a will, in 60 seconds

If you die without a will, you don't get a say in who receives your things. Every state has a default rulebook, called intestacy. In most states, it generally starts with your spouse and children. With no spouse or children, it moves to parents, then siblings, then more distant relatives. An unmarried partner, a close friend, or a charity usually gets nothing. And a judge, not you, may decide who raises your minor children. The court appoints someone to handle things. That process can take months. A will, plus powers of attorney, lets you choose instead.
